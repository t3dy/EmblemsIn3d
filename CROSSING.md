## 1. The boat, from the book

An *exeres* — six-oared — of white sandalwood and citrine with the seams of darker aloewood,
"daubed" not with pitch but with a balm of benzoin, ladanum, musk, amber, civet and storax;
gold nails with gems along the gunwale; thwarts and gratings of blood-red sandalwood
(p. 276). "For its poop the prow, and for its prow the poop" (p. 284): both ends alike.
**No sail** — p. 290 has Cupid "making sail with his spread wings."

Six ivory oars in gold rowlocks with thongs of twisted silk. Six rowers named on p. 277 in
three pairs by their dress: **Aselgia** and **Neolea** in cloth-of-gold on cyan; **Chlidonia**
and **Olvolia** in Babylonian sea-purple; **Adea** and **Cypria** in slashed melledarum with
gold foil — blond and raven by turns. Their names mean what they are (wantonness, daintiness,
of Venus's isle); they are the six-voice choir of p. 285.

At the mast-step a golden spear with a standard of cyan silk carrying, in gems and pearl on
both faces, **an antique vase with a flame in its mouth, the world, and an osier withy
binding them** — which Poliphilo reads: AMOR VINCIT OMNIA (p. 284). Cupid at the prow, wings
"of various and most-pleasant colouring" (p. 285).

## 2. Decisions

- **The reading is on a plaque by the pier, not on the silk.** The cloth carries the signs;
  reading them is Poliphilo's act, and the whole method of the book's hieroglyphs is that the
  reader does the reading. The world does not do it for him.
- **The old prop's sail was simply wrong** and is gone; the rainbow wings are the sail.
- **The crossing itself is a teleport** (digit 0). A ridden voyage — the walker in the poop,
  the boat under way, the island rising — is the obvious next thing and is on the ledger as
  unbuilt: the boat would have to become a mover like the triumph floats, and the island
  group is distance-culled.
- **The hull is white.** The first one was aloewood-dark and read as *absent* against the sea.
