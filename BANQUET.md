## 1. What the book gives, and what stands in the court

The supper is the book's most sustained inventory: fourteen facsimile pages in which every
frame, cloth, flower, stone, dish and sauce is named in turn. It is also a *procedure* — seven
changes of table, each a complete redressing of the room — and the world cannot run a
procedure without making the reader wait. So the procedure is laid out in space instead:

The counts (5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 3) are the book's. Each table stands on the ebony tripod of
pp. 143–144 — lion's-claw feet, a winged child's head on every leg, a garland of leaves and
fruit slung between (plate #28) — with its cloth to the pavement, its gold fringe, a covered
cup and a dish of its own stone, and its flowers half on the cloth and half fallen.

Around them: **the perfuming vessel** on three harpies' feet with its six gilded flying
spirits holding bowls of coals and the candlestick-stem with the seventh (pp. 147–148, plate

diamond (pp. 145–146); **the repository**, a ship of gold with fishes along its side, at the
open east end (p. 150); **the vessel of coals** with a napkin lying unhurt in the fire
(p. 155); **the chalice with the coral tree** flowered with sapphire, jacinth and beryl, and
its bearer (pp. 156–157, plate #31); **fourteen musicians** seven a side of the jasper door
(see [`MUSICIANS.md`](MUSICIANS.md)); **the three that wait** on the Queen's table; and at the
seventh table **the one whose bearing was the resemblance of Polia** (p. 156).

## 2. What was decided, and why

- **Seven tables, not one table seven times.** The book's supper is sequential; the world's is
  simultaneous. Walking round the pavement is walking through the supper, which is the only
  way a reader on foot can be given a sequence without a timer.
- **The asbestos is left a marvel.** Pliny knew *linum vivum* (NH XIX.4); Colonna presents it
  as "the wonderfull straungest of all the rest." The plaque keeps his wonder.
- **The musicians are mute** — the site is silent by standing decision (DECISIONS 2026-09-04).
- **Nobody eats.** The Queen sits, the attendants stand, the dishes are laid. The book's own
  rule is that "not one at that banquet did touch anie thinge sauing onelye the cuppe."

## 3. Still owed

- The pomanders of amber (p. 150) — ten lines, and litter for the court.
- The second great fountain on its axle-tree with the harpies at the angles (pp. 158–160,
  plate #32), which the tour lede has promised since it was written.
- The dish under each cover is a sphere; the fritters, breads and morsels are counted, not
  cooked.
