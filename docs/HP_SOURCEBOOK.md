# The Hypnerotomachia Sourcebook — what the book actually says

> Compiled 2026-08-30 for the v2 fidelity pass. Every fact below is drawn from the
> **1592 English translation** by Robert Dallington ("R. D."), *Hypnerotomachia: The
> Strife of Loue in a Dreame*, London: Simon Waterson — a **public-domain** text.
> Line references are to the Project Gutenberg edition, [ebook #18459](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/18459),
> so any claim here can be checked against the source in one search.

## Rights note — read before adding any more text to the project

Three different things get called "the Hypnerotomachia," and only two are free:

| Source | Status | May we use it? |
|---|---|---|
| Colonna's 1499 Aldine original (macaronic Italian/Latin) | Public domain worldwide | Yes — quote, translate, adapt freely |
| Dallington's 1592 English translation | Public domain | Yes — **quote verbatim**, no limit |
| Joscelyn Godwin's 1999 translation (Thames & Hudson) | **In copyright** | No — not quoted, not paraphrased, not consulted |

A new translation made *from the 1499 original* is a legitimate new work — Ted would own
its copyright and could license it however he likes (CC0 included). The one thing that
would **not** be lawful is producing a "new translation" by rewording Godwin: a
translation is itself a protected work, and paraphrasing one makes a derivative of it.
So the rule for this project is: **work from the 1499 original and the 1592 Dallington,
and never from a modern translation.** Everything below is Dallington.

The other consequence, which is a gift: because Dallington is public domain, the world
can quote him **on screen, at length, verbatim** — the dream's narration can be the
actual Elizabethan English rather than our summary of it.

## 1. The Great Portal (the pyramid, the obelisk, the Medusa door)

*Gutenberg lines 623–1000.* The single most under-built thing in our world. The book's
gate is not a gateway; it is an artificial mountain that seals a valley between two peaks.

**Massing**
- The square plinth under the pyramid is **fourteen paces high** and **six furlongs**
  on a side; Dallington's own sidenote defines the unit: *"A furlong is 16. pole euery
  pole being 16 foote"* — i.e. 256 ft, so a side is ~1,536 ft (~470 m).
- The pyramid's base is the same six furlongs a side, *"which in compasse about euery
  side æquilatered of like bredth, dooth multiplie to 24 furlongs."*
- It rises **1,410 steps** — *"mounting vp laboursomly foote by foote, conteyned 1410.
  degrees or steppes, taking away 10. degrees to make vp the head and gracilament of
  the Pyramides."* So: 1,400 courses, then a finial assembly.
- It is quarried *in situ*: *"not brought from any other place, but euen with plaine
  labour and workemanship hewen out of the selfe same mountaines."*
- It stands clear of the flanking rock by **ten paces** on either side.
- Material: **Parian marble**, dry-jointed — *"The squared stones ioyned togither
  without anye cement,"* the joints so fine that no one *"could deuise to hide the
  point of the smallest spanish needle."* The arrises are vermilion.

**The finial (the part we have entirely missing)**
- Where the last 10 steps would be, a **cube** — *"a huge Cube or foure square stone of
  forme like a dye"* — four paces across the diagonal.
- At each of the cube's four corners, a **harpy of cast metal**, *"their steales and
  clawes armed,"* leaded in, **two paces high**, the four meeting over the diagonal to
  form the obelisk's **socket**, which is dressed with cast leaves, fruit and flowers.
- The **obelisk**: **two paces broad, seven high**, of Theban stone (*Pyrus*), polished
  *"pure and bright shining as a looking glasse,"* carved with Egyptian hieroglyphs.
- On its point, a copper base holding a **turning device** — and on that, a **winged
  nymph weathervane** (Fortuna/Occasio): robe *"blowne abroad with the winde,"* legs and
  thighs bare, two wings from the shoulder blades spread as if to fly, face turned back
  toward her wings, hair flying, crown of the head bare. Her **right hand holds a
  cornucopia stopped up with the mouth downward**; her left is held to her breast. She
  turns with every gust, and grinding on her copper base she **rings like a bell**.

**The way in**
- On the plinth's right, the **head of Medusa** is carved, *"with terrible eyes
  cauernate,"* vipers for hair with **shining stones set in their eyes**. Her **gaping
  mouth is the door**: the spiral stair to the summit begins in it.
- The stair is lit by **loopholes at three levels** (lower, middle, upper), set to the
  east, south and west so that *"euery houre of the day the sunne shined in."*
- At the top, a metal **guardrail** — pillars a foot apart, half a pace high, with a
  battlemented coronet — *"lest that any comming foorth vnawares should fall downe."*
- A brass table gives the dedication (to the Sun) in Latin, Greek and Arabic, and the
  architect signs in Greek: **ΛΙΧΑΣ ΛΙΒΥΚΟΣ … ΩΡΘΩΣΕΝ ΜΕ** — *"Lichas a Libian
  architector set me vp."*
- The plinth's front carries a **Gigantomachy** relief — giants and horses in combat.

## 2. The Elephant and Obelisk

*Gutenberg lines 1231–1330.* Our version is grey and solid; the book's is neither.

- **Black stone darker than obsidian**, *"powdered ouer with small spottes of golde and
  glimces of siluer, as thicke as dust glistering in the sonne"* — the surface is
  polished hard enough to mirror what stands before it.
- A **brass saddle** with two girths buckled under the belly; between them, a quadrangular
  block carrying the obelisk, set so exactly that no plumb-line falls off the diameter.
- The obelisk is **green Lacedaemonian stone**, again **two paces broad and seven high**,
  hieroglyphs on three of its four faces, a shining ball at the point.
- **Tusks of pure white stone**; ears *"like a Fox-hounde flappingly pendent"*; the trunk
  hanging, then curling back up toward the face.
- The breast-strap is inscribed in Latin: **CEREBRVM EST IN CAPITE** ("the brain is in
  the head"). A goldsmith's frontlet hangs over the face, twice as long as broad,
  lettered in Greek and Arabic: **ΠΟΝΟΣ ΚΑΙ ΕΥΦΥΙΑ** (*ponos kai euphuia* — labour and
  natural wit).
- The porphyry base is **12 paces long, 5 broad, 3 high**, hieroglyphs around its sides,
  its outer face cut as a hemicycle.
- **Seven steps** at the rear of the base lead up to it — and under the saddle there is
  **a little door with brazen steps leading up into the beast's body.**
- Inside: an everlasting lamp on copper chains, and a **sepulchre** — a naked crowned
  man in jet, teeth, eyes and nails silvered, pointing with a golden sceptre, holding a
  keel-shaped shield lettered in Hebrew, Greek and Latin: **NVDVS ESSEM, BESTIA NI ME
  TEXISSET. QVAERE, ET INVENIES** — "Naked I would be, had not the beast covered me.
  Seek, and thou shalt find."

## 3. The five nymphs of the senses — and their attributes

*Gutenberg lines 2926–3010.* This is the passage that most changes our cast, because
each nymph is identified **by what she carries**. Aphea speaks for all five:

> "giue mee thy hand, thou art verie welcome. Thou seest at this present here, that we
> are fiue companions, and I am called *Aphea*, and she that carrieth the boxes and
> white cloathes *Offressia*. This other with the shining Glasse (our delightes) her
> name is *Orassia*. Shee that carrieth the sounding Harpe is called *Achol*, and shee
> that beareth the casting bottle of precious Lyquor, is called *Genshra*."

| Nymph (our spelling) | Sense | Attribute, per the text |
|---|---|---|
| **Aphea** | Touch | none carried — she **offers her hand** ("giue mee thy hand") |
| **Osfressia** (Offressia) | Smell | **boxes of sweet perfumes** and folded white cloths |
| **Orassia** | Sight | **a shining glass** (mirror) |
| **Achoe** (Achol) | Hearing | **a sounding harp** |
| **Geussia** (Genshra) | Taste | **a casting bottle of precious liquor** |

Dallington's sidenote is explicit: *"These nimphs were his fiue sences."* They also carry
looking-glasses, gold and jewelled casting-bottles, and *"pure white Vailes of silke."*
Poliphilo offers to carry them and is refused.

**The bath** they lead him to is **octagonal** — *"a marueilous buildyng of a bathe eight
square"* — with paired pilasters at each outer corner, a frieze of naked children holding
bundles of green boughs, an eight-square spire cut through and glazed with **crystal
quarrels**, four ring-seats descending into the water, Corinthian columns of jacinth, and
a lion's head in the cupola holding a ring in its jaws on copper chains, from which hangs
a perfume vessel two cubits above the water. Over the entrance, in Greek: **ΑΣΑΜΙΝΘΟΣ**
(*asaminthos*, "bath"). On its spire is a second weathervane: **a naked boy on a ball**,
standing on his right foot, his head hollowed like a funnel to a **trumpet** at his mouth,
so the wind blows through him and sounds it.

## 4. The Three Doors — we have them in the wrong order

*Gutenberg lines 5327–5470.* The book is unambiguous, and our build contradicts it.

> "The Gate vppon my right hand, had vpon it this word, *Theodoxia*. That vppon my left
> hand, *Cosmodoxia*. And the thirde, *Erototrophos*."

The plate letters all three in Greek, Latin, Hebrew and Arabic:

| Position | Greek | Latin | Keeper |
|---|---|---|---|
| Right | **ΘΕΟΔΟΞΙΑ** Theodoxia | GLORIA DEI | **Thende** — poor, tattered, pale, eyes down, right arm bare and pointing to heaven; six handmaids; a smoky daubed house on a crumbling rock, misty and hard to climb; door painted *Pilurania*. Her gate is *"couered ouer with greene mosse."* |
| **Centre** | **ΕΡΩΤΟΤΡΟΦΟΣ** Erototrophos | **MATER AMORIS** | **Philtronia** — wanton, lascivious regards, wonderfully pleasant; the Mansion-house of Voluptuousness: small herbs, all sorts of flowers, streams sliding over amber gravel, cool shade; six young women. |
| Left | **ΚΟΣΜΟΔΟΞΙΑ** Cosmodoxia | GLORIA MVNDI | **Euclelia** — brown, fierce rolling eyes, lifting a naked glittering sword with **a gold crown and a palm branch crossed** on it; arms brawny as Hercules; six young women. |

**So Mater Amoris is the middle door, not a side door** — and the chapter argument
confirms Poliphilo *"remained"* in *"the middlemost."* Our build currently puts Gloria
Mundi in the centre and calls it "the middle way." That is backwards and should be fixed.

Two more corrections: the doors are **hewn directly out of the living rock** in a barren
place *"without any greene grasse or hearbe"* — not set in a built masonry wall. And
Logistica plays **a lute** (she borrows Thelemia's), sings a **Dorian** tune at the second
gate — *"when labour and trauell is ouer-come, there will be a tyme of rest"* — and at the
third gate, after a long tirade against voluptuousness, she *"cast her Lute vppon the
ground and brake it."* Not a lyre, and she breaks it.

## 5. The Four Triumphs — teams of six, each ridden by a nymph musician

*Gutenberg lines 6142–6560.* Our chariots are drawn by two horses each. The book's are
drawn by **six exotic beasts**, and every beast carries a musician.

- **Europa** — six **centaurs**, *"which came of the fallen seede of the sausy and
  presumpteous Ixion,"* crowned with ivy. Wheels of **Scythian emerald**; the car itself
  of table diamonds set in gold; cornucopias inverted at the four corners; **harpy feet**
  at each corner of the plinth; a five-leaved rose where the axle ends. Six feet long,
  three high. Reliefs: the nymph crowning bulls; the ride over the sea; Cupid shooting
  into the air; Mars showing Jupiter his wound and holding the word **NEMO**.
- **Leda** — six **white elephants**, coupled two and two, traces of blue silk twisted
  with gold and silver in true-love knots, gold poitrels set with pearl.
- **Danaë** and **Semele** follow; the fourth, *"this mysticall triumph,"* is drawn by
  **six leopards** — *"spotted beasts of yealow shining colour"* — coupled with twined
  vine-withes in leaf and cluster, and it moves *"very leisurely."* Its car carries four
  eagles of Persian aetite supporting a great vessel, out of which grows a vine with
  grapes of Indian amethyst and leaves of Persian selenite, shadowing the whole chariot;
  red coral candlesticks at each corner; a young Bacchus crowned with two knotted
  serpents, one white and one black.

On every chariot the six riding nymphs are dressed by rank: **the two nearest the car in
blue silk "like the collour of a Peacockes necke," the middle two in bright crimson, the
two foremost in emerald green** — bearing, respectively, golden topaz censers streaming
fragrant smoke, **gold trumpets with silk-and-gold banners** in three places, and antique
cornets.

## 6. Poliphilo's path — the order of the dream

For checking our station sequence against the book: dark wood (the wolf appears **on his
left**, at the sight of which *"my hayre stood right vp, and I would haue cryed out, but
could not"*) → the valley and the pyramid-portal → the horse, the colossus, the elephant
among ruins → the dragon in the vault → the pleasant country → the five nymphs → the
octagonal bath → Queen Eleutherillida's palace and banquet → the three gates → Mater
Amoris → the torch-bearing nymph (Polia unnamed) → the four triumphs → the temple rites →
the Fountain of Venus → Cythera by Cupid's boat → the awakening.

Note the ruins field before the elephant contains *"the horse, the Colose, and the
Elephant"* as a set — we build only the elephant.

## What this changes in the build

Ranked by meaning-per-triangle, with the ones taken in this pass marked ✔:

1. ✔ **Nymph attributes** (§3) — harp, mirror, perfume casket, casting bottle, offered
   hand. Five props; every sense-nymph becomes legible without a label.
2. ✔ **Three Doors reordered and re-lettered** (§4) — Mater Amoris to the centre, Greek
   titles beside the Latin. A correctness fix, not a decoration.
3. ✔ **The elephant** (§2) — black-and-gold speckled stone, green obelisk, white tusks,
   the two inscriptions, seven steps, the door in its flank.
4. ✔ **The portal's finial** (§1) — cube, four harpies, and the turning winged Fortuna
   with her inverted cornucopia; the Medusa-mouth door; more step courses.
5. ✔ **Triumph teams of six** (§5) — centaurs, white elephants, leopards, with the
   blue/crimson/green nymph musicians.
6. The octagonal bath (§3) — currently a plain pool; deserves its eight sides, crystal
   spire and trumpet-boy vane.
7. The enterable elephant interior (§2) — the lamp, the sepulchre, *QVAERE ET INVENIES*.
   The best candidate for the "Antiquarian's Eye" quest's first find.
8. The horse and the colossus (§6) — the missing two thirds of the ruins field.
9. Dallington's own English in Dream mode — replace summary narration with the 1592
   text where it is vivid, since it is free to quote at length.
