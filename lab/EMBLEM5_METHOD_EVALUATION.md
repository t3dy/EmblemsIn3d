# Emblem V — evaluating the twelve depth treatments

*My own read of each variant's spatial logic, from walking every one of them. Companion to
[EMBLEM5_SPATIAL_ANALYSIS.md](EMBLEM5_SPATIAL_ANALYSIS.md) (the decomposition + depth ladder).
Live: [emblems-in-3d.vercel.app/lab/emblem5.html](https://emblems-in-3d.vercel.app/lab/emblem5.html)*

Your three fixed constraints, used as the scoring axes:

- **Finish** — monochrome "living engraving." *All twelve satisfy this*; not a differentiator.
- **Looks like the emblem** — does the engraved line survive, and is the composition legible?
- **Fully explorable** — can you actually walk *into* the space, or only view it?
- Plus **spatial truth** (does the depth read correctly) and **robustness** (artifact-free).

Scores are 1–5, from my inspection.

## Method 1 — cut-out theatre (the depth-ladder, five ways)

The whole method lives or dies on the ladder, so the five variants probe the ladder itself.

- **1a shadow-box (Z ×0.55).** Compresses everything toward the viewer. Parallax is powerful
  up close, but the town *crowds* the figures — the right house is enormous and near, the
  spires press on the actors' heads. **Spatial read: cramped.** Good for a deliberately
  claustrophobic emblem; wrong for this airy square.
- **1b stage-depth (adopted ladder).** The composition snaps to the original: pair centred,
  gabled house looming at true mid-right, cathedral and town-hall block receding left, the
  staffage small between. **This is the most legible reconstruction of the print.** Depth
  order is right and the spacing is comfortable. The one flaw is shared by all flat theatres:
  no ground contact, so on close inspection the standees hover a hair above the plaza.
- **1c true-depth (Z ×2.2).** Honours the measured scale ratios — the cathedral genuinely
  recedes and the air opens up. **Spatially the most truthful**, but the far town shrinks to
  small flats and the buildings read slightly scattered/floaty; you lose the print's packed,
  decorative back-plane. Realism costs legibility here.
- **1d raked stage.** Toes the flats inward like theatrical wings and pitches the set. From
  the centre seat it reads as a convincing built stage and the flatness is well hidden — but
  the trick only works from one spot, and walking off-axis breaks it. **Least explorable** of
  the theatres.
- **1e grounded + staffage + pier.** 1b, fixed: the figures now cast **real contact-shadows**
  on the plaza (the hover is gone), the two townsfolk + dog sit correctly at mid-depth as their
  own cue, and the dark pier frames the left as a proper *repoussoir*. **The best faithful,
  contemplative reading** — it looks like the emblem and the figures truly stand in the square.

**Verdict on Method 1:** 1e is the benchmark for "looks exactly like the print." Its ceiling
is that the parts are flat — walk 90° around a building and it vanishes to an edge. That is
honest toy-theatre, lovely to look at, but not "walk *into* the world."

## Method 2 — thick cut-outs in a built set (keep the line, add real volume)

- **2a façade-on-mass.** Buildings become solid stone blocks wearing the engraving as a
  façade; the pair is a swept-slice slab. You can walk *between* the masses. **First variant
  that is genuinely a space, not a picture.** The façade stretches a little on the boxes, and
  the sides are blank stone (the engraving is only on the front) — read from behind, a
  building is a plain block.
- **2b extruded silhouettes.** The figures are a stack of alpha-slices swept through depth, so
  the engraved figure becomes a **solid volume you can circle** — the cloak rounds, the body
  catches light — while the town stays as faithful flats. **This is the cleverest single
  idea in the set:** volume exactly where the eye goes (the actors), flatness where it doesn't
  matter (the backdrop). Keeps the line fully. The staffage slices read a touch chunky.
- **2c deep built town + sliced figures.** 2a's masses + 2b's figures: a fully enclosed town
  square with engraving on every façade, a church mass and spire behind, the actors
  volumetric in the middle. **The best match to "fully explorable"** — you stand *in* the
  plaza with architecture around you. Slightly busy, and the blank building backs remain the
  weak point.

**Verdict on Method 2:** this is the family that satisfies *all three* of your constraints at
once — monochrome ✓, engraved line ✓, walk-in space ✓. 2c is the fullest; 2b is the most
elegant.

## Method 3 — carved relief (the print as a stone object)

- **3a single panel.** The whole plate incised into a framed stone slab on a plinth, raking
  light grazing the lines. **As an *object* it is the most beautiful in the lab** — it reads
  unmistakably as the emblem and as carved stone at once. But it is a thing you *approach and
  orbit*, not a space you enter. Spatial "depth" is only the shallow relief.
- **3b layered cameo.** Restores two rungs of the ladder: the pair pops forward as a nearer
  carved cameo standing proud of the town relief, shadowing it. Real parallax returns. **But**
  the figures are still engraved *into* the back panel too, so there's a faint **double-image**
  — fixable by painting the actors out of the back plate, not yet done. Promising, not clean.

**Verdict on Method 3:** the reliquary/altarpiece register. Gorgeous for a *contemplative
per-emblem "monument" view*, but it fails your "fully explorable" requirement by design.

## Method 4 — sculptural rebuild (shown to prove the trade)

- **4a blocky.** Capsules. This is the failure you reacted to: it could be any two blobs; the
  emblem is entirely gone. Included so the loss is visible, not hidden.
- **4b posed.** Skirt, cloak, toad, better proportion — you can read "cloaked man, gowned
  woman" — but it is plain grey clay with **no engraved line at all**. Even done well, it
  stops being *this print* and becomes a generic tableau.

**Verdict on Method 4:** not recommended as the primary treatment. It is the one path that
throws away the thing that makes these images what they are.

## Recommendation

Measured against your own three choices — **monochrome, looks-like-the-emblem, fully
explorable** — only **Method 2** clears all three, and the honest winner is:

> **2c (deep built town) with 2b's extruded-silhouette figures, grounded with 1e's devices
> (contact shadows, staffage, the repoussoir pier).**

That combination is a walk-in monochrome town square, every façade the actual engraving, the
actors given real volume where the eye lands, the line intact throughout. It is the only
treatment that is *both* unmistakably the emblem *and* a space you inhabit.

Two supporting recommendations:

1. **Keep 1e as the "establishing" view.** Its flat-theatre honesty gives the single most
   print-faithful framing; use it as the fixed opening shot / the tour's arrival pose before
   the player is set loose to walk the 2c space.
2. **Offer 3a as an optional per-emblem "monument" mode.** It costs almost nothing (one
   displaced plane) and gives a beautiful contemplative object for the gallery/edition, distinct
   from the explorable world.

### Refinements needed before templating 2c to the other 49

- **Building backs & sides:** wrap a neutral engraving-hatch or a mirrored façade so a block
  read from behind isn't blank stone. (Cheap: a tiled hatch texture on the 5 non-façade faces.)
- **Per-element cut-outs, not one combined figure plane:** for the *sliced* figures, separating
  the man and the woman into two cut-outs would let them stand at slightly different depths and
  read even more dimensionally (right now they're one welded silhouette).
- **Paint the actors out of the town plate** for any relief-cameo use (fixes 3b's doubling).
- **Staffage keying:** re-key from the full-res plate with a tighter threshold so the little
  figures aren't chunky when sliced.
- **Ground:** replace the debug grid with the plate's own paving, projected onto the floor, so
  the plaza underfoot matches the print.

The depth-ladder in the analysis doc is what templates; each new emblem needs only its own
element inventory and one pass of the five depth cues.
