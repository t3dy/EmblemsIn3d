## 2. What a harpy is *for* here

In the *Hypnerotomachia* a harpy is almost never a monster. She is a **support** — a
foot, an angle-figure, a caryatid with wings — and her tail is a **transition**, turning into
acanthus so that a figure can become an ornament without a seam. This is the all'antica
grammar the book teaches: the antique vessel's feet are claws because Roman bronze feet were
claws, and the wings at the corners are there because a corner wants lifting. Build a harpy
as a beast and it is wrong; build her as a bracket and she is right.

## 3. How to build the next one

Plate #32's four angle-harpies are the model: seated, wings up and touching the vessel above,
tails joining and leafing out over the panel between. That is four `cast.figure` bodies with
raised wing-planes, a torus-and-leaf tail run between each pair, and the vessel resting on
the wing-tips — a *carried* load in Masonry terms, so eating a harpy drops the vessel.
