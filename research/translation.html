Poliphilo describes the marvellous artifice of the Venerean fountain standing at the centre of the theatre's floor, and how the little curtain was torn away; and he saw the divine Mother in her majesty, and how she imposed silence upon the singing nymphs, of whom she assigned three apiece to Polia and to him. With the most fitting reverence and the highest honour, the graceful Polia and I knelt devotedly before the mysterious fountain of the divine mother, and I felt an imperceptible sweetness spreading through me and troubling me, so that in that moment I did not know what to do. For what with the delightful place, incredibly pleasing and beyond all belief lovely in its greenness and spring adornment; and the little birds coursing through the purest air and chattering as they flew among the new leaves, most joyfully, to the outward senses; and the hearing of those most beautiful nymphs singing their melodies together with their unfamiliar instruments; and the sight of their divine gestures and most modest movements, which drove me ardently toward the extremity of pleasure; and the careful, curious weighing of a fabric of such dignity of conception and such elegant arrangement; and the greedy drinking-in of a fragrance I had never known — by immortal Jove, I truly did not know to which of my senses I should firmly and steadily apply the object of my attention, distracted as it was by so many different delights, by so excessive and happy a solace and voluptuous pleasing; and I accused myself of not knowing.

And all these beautiful and most sweet things offered me an enticement so much the more gracious and desirable, in that I knew that Polia — heavenly Polia — was placidly taking her share of the delight in them.

In this place, then, and marvelling too at the novelty and excellence of this astonishing fountain.
