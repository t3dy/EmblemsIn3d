The awakening.

POLIPHILO QUIVI FINISSE LA SUA HYPNEROTOMACHIA, DOLENTISE DEL SOMNO CHE NON FUE PIÙ LONGO. ET CHE IL SOLE FUE INVIDIOSO FACENDO GIORNO.

TANTO INOPINABILE DELECTAMENTO surrepto, et dagli ochii mei summoto quel spirito angelico, et subtracto fora dagli somnosi membri il dolce et suave dormire evigilantime, in quel punctulo, Omè Heu me amorosi lectori, tutto indolentime per il forte stringere de quella beata imagine, et foelice praesentia, et veneranda maiestate, lassantime et deserentime tra mira dolcecia, et intensiva amaritudine. Quando dal obtuto mio, se partirono quel iocundissimo somno, et quella diva umbra interrupta et disiecta quella mysteriosa apparitione et sublata. Per le quale fue conducto et elato ad sì alti et sublimi, et penetrabili cogitamenti. Diqué per aventura il Sole de invidia agitato di cusì beato somnio, a depraedare la gloriosa nocte, como publico inimico et Sycophanta della divina Matre, cum gli illuminosi splendori subitoso vene et a dipingere di colore roseo l’albicante Aurora discussa la perpete nocte. Et illustrato et interposito il recentato die, io rimansi stipato et completissimo di dolce, et argutula fallacia. Cogitate dunque quale livore livido, alhora ello harebbe, si io realmente sentisse perfruendo gli proprii et voluptici dilecti, de cusì formosa et diva damicella, et insigne Nympha, che esso non sostene (arbitro perché cum diva non lice) a cconcedermi la longa nocte (da indignatione) di Alcmena. Heu me perché non commutoe egli uno alquantulo della sua celeritudine, cum uno pauculo di secordia, alla mia refecta quiescentia, et praeterire unotantulo il suo statuto? Et perché alhora non mi fue arrogato il Stygio somno della Pyxide della curiosa Psyches? Et quivi Philomela anteluculo flendo promeva, tra gli spinosi rubi operta, et tra boscheti pressi di opaca coma di querculi, involuti della obliquante Periclymeno le violentie dell’adultero et infido Tereo, cum canoro garrito dicendo, Tereus Tereus, eme ebiasato?sospirando emerso et absoluto dal dolce somno repentuscule me lucubrai dicendo. Vale ergo Polia.

Tarvisii cum decorissimis Poliae amore lorulis, distineretur misellus Poliphilus.

.M. CCCC. LXVII. Kalendis Maii.

Poliphilo here ends his Hypnerotomachia, grieving that the sleep was no longer, and that the Sun was envious, and made it day.

So unlooked-for a delight snatched away, that angelic spirit taken from my eyes, and the sweet and gentle sleeping drawn out of my drowsy limbs as I woke — in that instant, alas, ah me, amorous readers, I ached all over from the hard clasping of that blessed image, that happy presence, that venerable majesty, which left me and deserted me between wondrous sweetness and piercing bitterness. When from my gaze there departed that most joyful sleep and that divine shade, the mysterious apparition broken off, scattered, and carried away — by which I had been led and lifted to thoughts so high, so sublime, so piercing.

And then, as it happened, the Sun, stirred to envy by so blessed a dream, coming to plunder the glorious night like a public enemy and an informer against the divine Mother, came suddenly with his shining splendours and painted the whitening Dawn a rosy colour, the long night dispelled. And the new-made day being lit and set between us, I was left crammed and utterly filled with a sweet and subtle deception.

Consider, then, what livid spite he would have had, if I had truly felt, in the enjoying of them, the very voluptuous delights of so shapely and divine a damsel, so matchless a nymph — he who would not endure (because, I judge, with a goddess it is not permitted) to grant me the long night of Alcmena. Ah me, why did he not exchange a little of his swiftness for a little sloth, for the sake of my restored rest, and overrun his appointed hour by just so much? And why was the Stygian sleep out of curious Psyche's box not laid upon me then?

And there Philomela, weeping before daybreak, hidden among thorny brambles and in thickets close with the dark foliage of small oaks wound about with twining honeysuckle, was pouring out the violence of that adulterous and faithless Tereus, saying with tuneful chatter: Tereus, Tereus — [?have I been wronged]. Sighing, risen and released from the sweet sleep, I woke of a sudden into the small hours, saying: Farewell then, Polia.

At Treviso, where wretched Poliphilo was held fast in the most comely little thongs of Polia's love. 1467, on the Kalends of May.

Notes

  • eme ebiasato? — the transcription reads Tereus Tereus, eme ebiasato?
  • Omè Heu me — the Italian exclamation immediately doubled by the Latin.
  • la longa nocte di Alcmena — Jupiter stopped the sun to lengthen the night
  • Tarvisii — Treviso, where the historical Francesco Colonna's Polia is

EPITAPHIUM POLIAE

F œlix Polia, quæ sepulta vivis C haro Marte Poliphilus quiescens I am fecit vigilare te sopitam.

EPITAPHIUM UBI POLIPHILUS LOQUITUR.

VIATOR FAC QUAESO MORULAM, POLIAE NYMPHAE HIC EST MYROPOLIUM. QUAE NAM INQUIES POLIA? FLOS ILLE OMNEM REDOLENS VIRTUTEM SPECTATISSIMUS. QUI OB LOCI ARITUDINEM, PLUSCULIS POLIPHILI LACHRYMUL. REPULULESCERE NEQUIT. AT SI ME FLORERE VIDERES, EXIMIA PICTURA UNIVERSIS DECORITER PRAESTARE CONSPICERES PHOEBE INQUIENS, QUEM INTACTUM URORE RELIQUE RAS, UMBRA CECIDIT. HEU POLIPHILE DESINE. FLOS SIC EXSICCATUS, NUNQUAM REVIVISCIT. VALE.

The text on this leaf is Latin, not Italian. It is given here in the original with an English rendering beneath, since the Latin is itself quotable and the first three lines carry an acrostic.

F œlix Polia, quæ sepulta vivis
C haro Marte Poliphilus quiescens
I am fecit vigilare te sopitam.

Happy Polia, who being buried yet live: Poliphilo, resting from dear Mars, has now made you, who were lulled asleep, keep watch.

The initials set out from the margin spell F · C · I.

Traveller, make a little pause, I beg you: here is the perfume-shop of the nymph Polia. And what, you will say, is Polia? That most admired flower, fragrant with every virtue — who, because the ground is parched, cannot put out shoots again for all Poliphilo's little tears. But if you had seen me in flower, you would have seen me surpass all others in beauty, like an exceptional painting; saying, Phoebus, the one you left untouched by your burning, a shadow has felled. Ah, Poliphilo, have done. A flower dried out like this never lives again.

Farewell.
