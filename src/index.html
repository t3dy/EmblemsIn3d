The Prospect of Cythera

Everywhere else in this dream you are lost, and that is the design: Hunt argues that not being able to place yourself is the first garden experience the book stages, which is why there is no map anywhere in this world. Cythera is the exception, and the book makes it one: Poliphilo describes the whole topography of the island before he lands, and only then explores it. Here, and only here, everything falls into place.

Twenty divisions, by the construction the book itself gives (our translation p. 294). Three concentric claustri, each a third of the radius; the river roofed with a pergola of citrus; six terraces of seven steps; the theatre at the centre. After A. Segre and J. D. Hunt — see GARDENS.md §5–6.
