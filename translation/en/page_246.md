# Page 246 — Chapter XIX

> **D · M · S ·**
> **CADAVERIBUS AMORE FURENTIUM**
> **MISERABUNDIS POLYANDRION**
>
> "To the Gods of the Dead (*Dis Manibus Sacrum*). The Polyandrion, for the wretched corpses of those raging
> with love."

This noble and much-admired fragment, in one solid frust (chunk) — and a little portion of its fastigium, or
frontispiece — still retained itself egregiously delineated. In the triangular plane of which, two figments I saw
carved, and not entire: a decapitated bird (I judged it to be of an Owl), and an antique lamp, all of perfect
alabaster. Thus I interpreted them:

> **VITAE LETHIFER NUNTIUS** — "The death-bringing messenger of life."

Come then into the middle part of the temple, somewhat immune and unoccupied of rubble I found it. Where, again,
the consuming time, to one illustrious work of note — all of rubicund porphyry — had only pardoned. Which was
sexangular (hexagonal), with the bases upon a solid stone of Ophite, of the same figure, fixed in the pavement;
and six little columns, distant one from the other six feet, with the Epistyle, Frieze, and Cornice, without any
lineament and sign, but simply terse and pure. Which were, on the exterior, imitating the (hexagonal) form; but
the interstices in a circled figure. Where, upon the flat of the cornice, was born a cupola of a single and solid
rock, marvellous artifice; which thinned at the summit, where a pervious funnel, streaked and mirror-like, covered
a subterranean vacuity, illuminated by a circular opening of egregious grating, impeded (closed) of metal casting.
Which noteworthy ciborium, of the utmost polish, thus I found.

## Notes

**Confidence: high** (the two inscriptions are secure and read from the woodcut); medium for the ciborium-
description. The Polyandrion's **dedication inscription** — *D. M. S. / CADAVERIBUS AMORE FURENTIUM MISERABUNDIS
POLYANDRION* ("To the Gods of the Dead: the Polyandrion, for the wretched corpses of those raging with love") — and
the **owl-and-lamp emblem** decoded *VITAE LETHIFER NUNTIUS* ("the death-bringing messenger of life"); then the
hexagonal red-porphyry **ciborium** at the temple's centre, over a grated shaft into a subterranean crypt. All
secure.

- **The Polyandrion named on its own gate.** The dedicatory frieze gives the ruin its identity in Roman funerary
  form: *Dis Manibus Sacrum* ("sacred to the shades"), the **Polyandrion for those who died raging with love**.
  This is the world's polyandrion's founding inscription — a tomb consecrated to love's casualties. Prime text to
  carry on the world's ruin (with the concord-motto, p. 244).
- **"the death-bringing messenger of life."** The owl (bird of ill omen) and the funeral lamp decode to *vitae
  lethifer nuntius* — the herald that announces death within life. A dark emblem fit for the tomb's pediment; a
  ready device for the world/novel's polyandrion.
- **the hexagonal ciborium over the crypt.** At the temple's centre, a six-columned red-porphyry canopy under a
  single-block cupola, pierced by a grated oculus, tops a **subterranean vacuity** — the crypt Poliphilo descends
  into (p. 247). A buildable centrepiece for the world's polyandrion: a hexagonal porphyry baldachin over a grated
  shaft into the tombs below.
- **the whole ruin reads as a text** — obelisk, gate-frieze, pediment-emblem: every surface of the Polyandrion is
  an inscription to be decoded. Poliphilo is *reading the graveyard*, the chapter's method.
