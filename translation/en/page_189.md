*The Nymph leads the loving Poliphilo through other fair places, where he saw countless Nymphs keeping
solemn festival, and cheerfully keeping the feast about a sacred altar with the triumph of Vertumnus
and Pomona. Then they came to a marvellous temple, which he in part describes, and its builder's art.
And how in the said temple, at the warning of the priestess, the Nymph with much ceremony put out her
torch, showing herself to Poliphilo to be his Polia. And afterwards, having entered with the
sacrificing priestess into the holy shrine, before the divine altar she called upon the three Graces.*

I could no longer contend against the heavenly and violent armour; and, the most elegant Nymph having
lovingly gained an irrevocable lordship over wretched me, further on with her (I following her measured
steps), driving me as her equal, she led me towards a spacious shore. Which bordered upon the
flower-bearing and hilly valley, where the adorned little mountains ended at this shore, and the
vine-bearing hills, with their ways shut, enclosing round about this golden country, full of incredible
delight.
