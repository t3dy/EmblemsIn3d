But at once returning to my fixed and accustomed object, weeping for the loss of two things so much to
be desired — Polia, that is, and precious life — invoking her diligently, with a sighing and sobbing
voice sounding through that thick air enclosed beneath huge vaults, and in that hidden place, I said
within myself: if I die here so wretched and grieving, and altogether unconsoled, who shall be the
deserving successor to so great and so prized a gem? Who shall possess so inestimable and talented a
treasure? What serene sky shall regain so clear a light?

O most wretched Poliphilo, where are you going, most lost? Where do you direct your attempted flight?
Where do you still hope to see again any longed-for good? Behold, abruptly cast down and interrupted
are all your gracious pleasures built by sweet love in your entangled mind. Behold, in a moment cut off
and annihilated are all your loving and so lofty thoughts.

Alas, what unjust lot and malign star has led you so perniciously into these troublous darknesses, and
cruelly exposed and cast you down to copious and mortal languors, and destined you to perish by the
most savage voracity and sudden gluttony of this terrifying Dragon? That, alas, I should be thrown
whole into its foul and most filthy and dunged bowels to putrefy, and thence afterwards be cast out to
an end not to be thought of?

O lamentable and unaccustomed destruction; O pitiable ending of my life! Where are those eyes so
barren, sucked and drained and deprived of moisture, that they did not all melt into thickest falling
tears? But behold me dying, for I feel it at my shoulders. Who ever saw turned upon himself a more
atrocious and deformed savagery of fortune?

Behold unhappy and insolent death, and the supreme hour and accursed moment at hand, in this dark
gloom; and that my body and my human flesh should be the sating of this terrible beast! What ferocity?
What rage? What more monstrous misery could mortals suffer — that the sweet and friendly light should
be taken from the living, and the earth denied to the dead? O how much more ghastly a calamity and
enormous misery, so sorrowfully and so importunately desired, in abandoning my most welcome and most
perfect Polia. Farewell, farewell then, excellent light of virtue and of every true and real beauty,
farewell.
