## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The junction where **dome meets pier**: the gilt scaled roof (surpassing the
Capitol and the Pantheon), the relieving arches, the **niches with statues** on the pilaster-faces, the
dome springing from a denticulate/ovolo cornice with its own drainage-channel feeding the piers, and the
scrolled cartouche (with bean-pods and an artichoke leaf) at the cupola-to-pier volute — ending in a
**compass-construction of the volute-spiral**. All secure; the geometry-of-the-scroll is a genuine drawing
recipe.

- **The dome-to-pier detail, fully worked.** Colonna resolves the hardest architectural joint — where the
  round dome lands on the piers — with relieving arches, a cornice that both crowns and drains, and a
  scrolled cartouche masking the transition. The dome's rainwater runs off through this cornice into the
  piers (continuing p. 202's system). Buildable to the last moulding.
- **statue-niches on the piers** — each pilaster-face carries a niche (*solium*) with "a most-noble statue,
  with manifold acts": another rank of statuary (below the Muses of p. 201), lining the interior with
  figures. The temple is populated with sculpture at every level.
- **the volute drawn with compasses** — the page ends with an actual step-by-step compass method for
  striking the scroll's spiral (continued p. 204). Colonna hands the reader the draughtsman's technique, as
  he handed the setting-out geometry on p. 197: the book teaches how to *make* the building, not just how it
  looks.
- **surpassing the Capitol and the Pantheon** — the two most famous gilt/domed Roman roofs (Catulus gilded
  the Capitoline temple; the Pantheon's dome was the standard of engineering); Venus's temple exceeds both.
  The surpassing-topos, on domes.
