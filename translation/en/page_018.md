# Page 18 — Chapter II

Now, like an animal that for the sweet bait does not weigh the hidden trap, setting aside my natural
need, I went without delay, hastening vehemently on the way, after that more-than-human strain. And
when I reasonably judged that I had come to it, I heard it in another quarter; and when I had hurried
to that place, it appeared to have settled somewhere else. And so, as it changed its places, so
likewise it changed to a voice more sweet and more delightful, with heavenly harmonies.

Therefore, by this empty labour, and having run so far with troublesome thirst, I so weakened myself
that I could scarcely sustain my weary body. And my panting spirits being no longer able to bear up
the body, gravely wearied as it was — what with the terror now past, what with the urgent thirst,
what with the long wandering search, and also with my heavy anxieties and with the hot hour — I was
left defenceless and abandoned by my own powers, desiring and craving nothing whatever save quiet
rest for my weakened limbs.
