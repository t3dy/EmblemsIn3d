Which wooded and cool grove was pleasant to look upon and desirable for its spaciousness, and joyful
with fronds, full of little woodland and mountain birds. Beyond the bridge it stretched somewhat
further towards a pleasant plain, everywhere sounding with sweet chattering. There leapt the restless
Squirrels and the sleepy Dormice, and it was inhabited by other harmless little animals.

In this manner, then, was shown this wooded district, shut about by the tree-bearing mountain, much to
be gazed at by the eyes; and the plain covered everywhere with a variety of herbs. And the most limpid
little rivers flowed murmuring down through the feet of the sloping mountains into the valley. Adorned
with the flowering and bitter Oleander, and with Periwinkles, and Coltsfoot, and Loosestrife; shaded by
tall black and white Poplars, and the riverside Alder and Manna-ash. And upon the mountains I saw the
tall single-stemmed Fir, and the weeping Larches, and Silver-firs, and other noble kinds of like
foliage.

For which cause, considering the place so pleasant, and a most convenient station and welcome retreat
for shepherds — a place certainly inviting one to sing bucolic songs — I stood not a little stupefied,
and suspended in mind, seeing a country so kindly yet deserted of people and uncultivated.

And then directing my eyes to the adorned plain, anxiously passing by the aforesaid place, I beheld a
marble building appearing among the trees, and above the tender tree-tops its pinnacle. Whereupon,
made all cheerful, judging that I had at last found a habitation and some refuge, I came to it joyfully
without delay.

I found an eight-sided building with a wonderful and excellent fountain. Which also offered itself not
in vain as a sweet invitation to my thirst, so long held back and not till then satisfied and quenched.
