# Page 298 — Chapter XXI

In this pleasant and delightful enclosure, between the greenest myrtle-grove and
this flowery orange fence encircling, innumerable and mingled animals, shut in,
roamed loosely — however nature, unlike, repugned — harmless and tame. They wandered
there in mutual friendship. And first the goat-born satyrs with their pendent and
twisted little horns; the two-horned fauns, with the other sex. Then the half-wild
stags, and the rock-seeking roes, and the timid does, the spotted fawns, the leaping
kids; the long-eared hares; the timid rabbits; the cat-kind, white and yellow
weasels, and the lying *gallante*; restless squirrels, and drowsy dormice; the
fierce unicorns, and *tragopan* and *tragelaphus* [goat-stag]; every leonine kind
without any fierceness, but playful; the long-necked giraffes, swift gazelles; and
infinite other animals, intent on the solaces of nature.

Beyond this cloister, then, toward the centre, I found the magnificence of an
excellent orchard, or delightful pleasure-ground — such as never could humans, not
only order, but, I persuade myself, so much as think. It is easy, then, that the
second workers act according to the first worker. And I too consent to this, that
no wit so fecund is found that could singly avail worthily to publish somewhat, by
report, of so many most excellent works of this sacred place. Let the hanging
gardens built by King Syrus yield here. Wherefore I rightly judged that by no
other, but only by a divine artificer, was so most exquisite a conception, for the
contemplation of the fostering goddess of nature, produced to such order and effect
most becomingly.

Which most admirable garden, stretched toward the centre, of 166 paces and a half,
divided into meadows. Which division stood by passages straight to the centre, and
circularly transverse, five paces wide. The primary meadows, in the first line of
their squaring toward the enclosure, with the lateral ones, were 50 paces. But the
fourth line, toward the centre, decremented; and from this line took its dimension
the first line of the second meadow, and in the same manner the third meadow was
squared. Because the force of the lines tending to the centre caused the narrowing,
and the straitness of these meadows and of the streets; and therefore the squaring
was deformed, the transverse ones remaining in their integrity.

Which streets were pergola'd, and in each ridge was a little dome upon four Ionic
columns, the shaft or their tallness being of nine diameters of their lowest
thickness. And here and there of the passages, or streets, were tomb-shaped
platforms of finest marbles, with most becoming lineaments. And every four
diameters stood one of the aforesaid columns. Such columniation, and
intercolumniation, was observed throughout.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The tame-beast catalogue is secure and vivid; the garden
geometry that follows is technical and rendered literally, including Colonna's key
admission about the shape of the compartments. This is the middle ring — the
*prati* — with its measurement of 166½ paces, the *semitertio* Segre's whole
reconstruction depends on.

- **The tame animals in mutual friendship** — the peaceable-kingdom motif of the
  King's-garden *bosco* (Segre §the bosco): satyrs, fauns, stags, hares, giraffes,
  lions "without any fierceness," even unicorns, roaming loose among the trees.
  Colonna makes the whole outer island a menagerie of harmless beasts, a status
  symbol of the highest-rank garden.
- **"166 paces and a half"** — the width of the middle ring, the *semitertio*
  ("half of a third," a third of the radius), the exact figure Segre uses to scale
  Cythera's three concentric zones (GARDENS.md §5). The 240 fruit-trees-and-meadows
  of the world's middle ring come from here.
- **the *quadrati illegitimi***, stated outright — "the squaring was deformed…
  because the lines tending to the centre caused the narrowing." This is Colonna
  admitting that his radial compartments are not true squares but trapezes,
  distorted by the circular plan (GARDENS.md §5, "illegitimate squares"). He knows
  the geometric cost of a round garden and accepts it. The world's island wedges
  reproduce exactly this trapezoidal narrowing toward the centre.
- **"the hanging gardens built by King Syrus"** — the Hanging Gardens of Babylon
  (attributed here to a "King Syrus," i.e. Semiramis's line); the one built garden
  Colonna's invented one is meant to surpass.
