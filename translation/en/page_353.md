In the middle of which Area, at the centre, was the most-holy and delicious fount of the divine Genetrix

The gradation of which began from the area of the most-lucid stone. Not solid, nor massy steps, but
inane — that is, hollow — four, with the stepped ascent, one above the other, like the gradation of the
benches. In the height of the seat, six palms; and in the breadth, two feet and a half, or in the
opening. Which were, in their encompassing circuit, surrounded by boxes stuffed with diverse flowers,
which did not exceed the middle of the following little box. The fourth and supreme step levelled its
opening, with the lips, to an interposed little walkway, five feet wide. Which, encircling, was covered
by a little pergola, a pace and a half high.

Beyond then the summit of the inflexure of such pergola, the other order of as many steps followed in
order. The first, lower, began so much higher in the band, or wall, encompassingly at the inner part,
that the sublime incurvature of the pergola did not occupy the seeing of the front of that first step.
Such symmetry was directly observable in the subsequent. Then the fourth gradation of this second order,
and so of the third — like the first — was of walkway, and of pergola, and of uniform commensuration.

The boarded walls, or the sides of the said pergolas in circuit, were of blackish stone of mirror
sheen. The first wall of the lower pergola was boarded of *spartopolia* stone; the second of
*hieratites*; the third, supreme, of *cepionis*. The sheen of which, under the pergolas, simulated an
opening of the serene air, that shone within it, and not a closure.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The theatre's seating explained: the **cavea is built of tiers of hollow flower-
boxes** (four hollow steps, each a planter of flowers, six palms high), interrupted by walkways under low
**pergola-galleries**, in three superposed orders; and the gallery-walls are of **mirror-black stone** so
polished they seem to open onto sky rather than close the space. All secure; a wholly original theatre-
form (a *planted* auditorium).

- **The planted theatre — a cavea of flowers.** Colonna's amphitheatre has no ordinary stone seats: each
  "step" is a hollow box (a *capsa*) filled with flowers, tier upon tier, with narrow walkways under
  arched pergolas between the three orders. The audience-slope is a garden. This is a striking, buildable
  idea for the world's theatre: concentric planted tiers under three rings of pergola, not bare benches.
- **the black-mirror gallery walls.** The pergola-galleries are backed by walls of mirror-black stone
  (spartopolia, hieratites, cepionis) so lustrous they "simulate an opening of serene air, not a closure"
  — the same illusion as the obsidian floor (p. 352), lifted to the walls. The whole interior multiplies
  itself in black mirror. A powerful rendering cue: dark reflective surfaces that dissolve the enclosure.
- **the Fountain of Venus named, then deferred.** Colonna announces the "most-holy fount of the divine
  Genetrix" at the exact centre, then postpones it to describe the theatre first — the fountain is the
  subject of ch. XXIII, the world's climax-station. Here it is placed: dead centre of the obsidian mirror.
- The three pergola-pavements (perfumed paste, coral, lapis) are described on p. 355.
