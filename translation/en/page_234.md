# Page 234 — Chapter XVIII

Now, no sooner did I taste the miraculous and sweetest little apple, than without delay I felt in me refresh
and renew the rude and gross intellect, and the anxious and mourning heart all recreate itself, anointed in
amorous joy — neither more nor less than one who, overwhelmed in the deep sea, and to the bottom immersed with
shut lips, without draught of breath, from above returns, most-avidly taking the fresh and most-pleasant airs,
and vivifies himself. For which thing, incontinently, in me more amorous flames began to burn; and with a more
sweet torment of new qualities of love it appeared to me to be transmuted. And therefore I began evidently to
know, and effectually to feel, what the Venerean graces are, and of how much efficacy they present themselves
to the earth-born, and how great a reward joyously they attain, who — through the delightful realms, intrepidly
soldiering, and in the amorous, pertinacious battles — arrive at them. Ultimately, after the devout and sacred
refection of the fatal fruits, taken without hesitation, that divine germ (bush), vanishing from the eyes,
disappeared. The libant Priestess went out of the holy sacello, and Polia paired with her, and I, and all.

With such and so-made order, terminated and defined the mystic sacrifices, and libations, and immolations, and
the divine cult — both, having deposed the sacred vestments, and stripped off, with choice veneration, all
those mysterious and pontifical instruments, with domestic and templar obsequies, in the sacristy reverently
they re-deposited.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The aftermath of the miracle: Poliphilo eats the fruit and feels his whole being
renewed ("like one who returns from the deep sea and breathes"), his love transmuted into "new qualities"; the
rose-bush vanishes; and the rite is closed — the vestments and sacred instruments put away. All secure.

- **The fruit's effect: love transfigured.** The communion-fruit does not sate desire but *transmutes* it into
  "new qualities of love" — a higher, sanctioned ardour. Poliphilo now "knows what the Venerean graces are" and
  what reward awaits those who "soldier intrepidly through the delightful realms." The miracle has changed the
  nature of his love, not ended it: the fidelity-thread elevated. The drowning-and-surfacing simile is the
  book's image for this rebirth.
- **the rite closed.** The sacrifice ends cleanly — bush vanished, vestments and instruments returned to the
  sacristy: Colonna's characteristic orderly wind-down (cf. the drainage-mindfulness, the scrupulous
  immersions). The mystery performed, the temple returns to rest.
- The priestess's blessing and the lovers' departure follow on p. 235, leading to the ruined **Polyandrion**.
