The fourth wall the palace made, with a distribution equal to those hinted at, save that the door took
up the middle space. The other six, with a ruled correspondence and harmony with the rest, in their
jewels (opposite the planets, and in a symmetried meeting) held the virtuous operations belonging under
those, expressed in the form of elegant Nymphs with the titles and signs of their effect.

The seventh, in the middle of the frontispiece, or gable, of the door, was placed directly over against
the seventh jewel, which held the planet Sun. Which was raised higher than the others by the placing of
the Queen's throne. For which cause every part most accurately answered — in material, in number, in
form to the line, and in every least part and placing most evenly and to the level; and thus mutually
the right side with the left, and here and there, agreed in a most exquisite meeting. Of which
surpassing place each wall was extended twenty-eight paces.

By such an elegant disposition this open court was made even, covered round about all in the most
perfect gold, a work wonderful and unspeakable.

The little pilasters, or half-squares, at a distance of four paces one from another, with a just
sevenfold partition (a number most welcome to nature), were of the finest and eastern lapis lazuli,
with a most joyful fullness of its colouring, adorned with a most charming scattering of minute sparks
of gold. The front of which, between the closing wavings, were marvellously carved with candelabra,
with a most notable company of fronds, horns of plenty, little monsters, heads maned with foliage,
figures ending in scrolls, birds, and horns, and little vessels of pomegranate flower, with
distinguished invention and thought, from the lowest footing to the topmost shaft, in a relief almost
torn free of its flat ground. The thickness of which was measured from its own rise. These, then, in a
sociable and most welcome assembly, made among the golden plates the interruption of the most seemly
spaces.

The capitals were of a making suited to the other workmanship. Above stretched the straight beam with
the chased lineaments required, with little cylinders, or rounds, the flattened whorls set between them
two by two. And following above it its adorned frieze. Which held alternately such expressions as
these: heads of oxen, with their horns knotted with fillets, flowing with hanging berries sewn on, with
two branches of myrtle, looped in the middle section with a flying selvage; and with dolphins, with
their gills leafed and likewise their fins, the outermost part passing into foliage; and at the whorl
certain little boys with their hands seizing the whorl, which ended in an antique flower. The dolphin's
