I consenting, we went out from this one too; and the brazen valves being bolted, Thelemia struck at the
third and middle door, and the bar being removed, without delay it was opened. And being let in, there
came to meet us a notable lady, whose name was Philtronia. With wanton and unsteady glances, her most
joyful look at the first sight forced me to love her and drew me to it.

A dweller in a voluptuous place, its ground green with soft grass and clothed with flowers, abounding
in solace and pleasant idleness, flowing with springs of the clearest fountains and little streams,
running with a sounding gush, watered to the greatest pleasure; sunny fields, and the shades of the
leafy trees cool and fresh.

She likewise had with her six mistress-like and most shapely girls of her own age, and most charming in
their look, with a most welcome elegance and amorous ornaments, trapped out, seemly with an ambitious
beauty; of whom the name of the first was Rastonelia, the next was named Chortasina, the third Idonesa,
and the fourth was called Tryphelea. And the fifth was called Etiania; the last Adia.

Such and so made presences showed themselves exceedingly welcome and delightful to my intent eyes. For
this the sincere Logistica, seeing me readily disposing myself and already abruptly bent to the love of
her, and addicted in a servile manner, said with a sorrowful voice:

"O Poliphilo, the painted and feigned beauty of this woman is lying, insipid and savourless; for if you
would look discussingly upon her back you would grasp, with loathing, how much unseemliness lies
beneath, and how despicable and stomach-turning and abominable they are with their foulness, standing
high upon a great heap of filth. For that which flees is fleeting and vanishing, and the pleasure
passes, and shame with repentance, with vain hopes, with the briefest cheerfulness, with perpetual
weeping and anxious sighs, the toilsome life remains behind.

"O sweetness adulterated with misery, holding in itself so much bitterness, like the honey in Colchis
dripping from the leaves. O death most dreadful and foul, how you are clothed in a sweet poison; with
how many hazards and mortal perils and cares are you sought by blind lovers, unadvisedly and headlong.
Present and before their eyes you stand, and the wretches do not see you. O of how many sorrows and
bitter pain and torment you are the bearer. O depraved, impious and execrable appetite; O detestable
madness; O senses defrauded — by you thus slipperily, with that same beastly pleasure, wretched mortals
are ruined. O sordid love. O most absurd frenzy. O disordered and empty desire, nesting and wounding in
the hearts you have touched with so many errors and torments. O wicked and deadly destruction of
manifold good. O huge monster, how easily and slyly you veil and cloud the eyes of your unhappy lovers?
