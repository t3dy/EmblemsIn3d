For more evident demonstration: the circuit of this delightful and most pleasant
island, by measurement round, stood at three miles. The figure of which gave, for
its diameter, one mile. Which, divided in three parts — one third contained 333
paces, one foot, and two palms and somewhat more — from the outer lip of the shore-
banks up to the orange-cloister occupied the measure of one semitertio, 166 paces
and 10 palms. From this boundary began the meadows, tending toward the centre,
another semitertio as much. Thus, one whole third being suitably distributed, there
remains one semitertio to dispense up to the very middle, 166 paces and 10 palms.
From the aforesaid peristyle, somewhat of space was granted, remaining for the
contraction of the aforesaid meadows, to avoid the straitness of the squarings —
which did not have their boundary up to the completion of the third; and this
shrewdly happened, to proportion somewhat the last square by the lines drawn to the

## Notes

**Confidence: high.** This is Colonna's own scale-plan of Cythera, given both as a
labelled diagram and as a prose gloss, and it is clear arithmetic. **This is the
single most important page for the world's reconstruction of the island.**

- **The measured plan of Cythera, in the book's own numbers.** The island is a
  circle three miles round, one mile in diameter, divided into three concentric
  *tertii*. The diagram labels the zones from shore to centre: the shore, the myrtle
  enclosure, the grove enclosure, the peristyle, then the **bosco (20 paces wide)**,
  the **paving (14)**, and the central **theatre (25)**; the three meadow-orders at
  **58, 48, 38 paces**; the **semitertio at 166½**; the orange-cloister; and the
  **river**. Segre's reconstruction (GARDENS.md §5), which the world's
  `_buildCytheraIsle` is built from, is a direct transcription of this diagram —
  and here at last is Colonna's own label for every ring the island renders.
- **the *semitertio*** — "half of a third," a third of the radius, 166 paces 10
  palms: the width of each concentric zone, and the figure quoted on p. 298 and
  p. 292. Now shown to be exactly one-sixth of the diameter, with the arithmetic
  worked in the open.
- **the trapezoidal meadows again** — the plan confirms that the meadows "did not
  have their boundary up to the completion of the third," being contracted "to
  avoid the straitness of the squarings" — the *quadrati illegitimi* of p. 298,
  stated a third time. The design's one geometric compromise, fully owned.
- This page should be treated as the canonical reference for the island's
  proportions; the world's ring radii can be checked against it directly.
