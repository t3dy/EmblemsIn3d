# Page 305 — Chapter XXI

Following in order, the description also of the third meadow toward the centre,
which in its middle had a chest of round content, raised three feet, by diameter
two paces the opening, with the accessory lineaments. Out of it exalted itself an
artificed box, of the following topiary composite. The chest was of finest
litharge.

The stock a foot and a half; above sat an onion-shaping, somewhat exceeding the
round of the chest — which was hollow, and open at the top by diameter a pace and a
half. Above the lips of which opening a columniation of six greening stocks turned
with little arches, four feet high. Then a metal-shaped summit, or shaped into a
chalice-foot, ascended; on the summit of which lay a most exact pommel, three feet
in its thickness.

Upon the lowest end of the domed summit, at the perpendicular of each stock, there
coiled the tail of a serpent, with the belly bent and the spine arching, as much as
was the projection of the onion-bulk; adhering with its neck to the pommel existing
above, with the head outstretched and jaws open, and through most hidden pipe-like
passages it leapt, pouring most fragrant water from the mouth, with the feet
extended toward the head, with the wings spread — six in number.

Out of the top of the pommel sprang three branches, three-forkedly divided, and
raised two feet bending. Each of which, then, at its summit sustained a little
rounded altar, or a little tube, with exquisite diligence attributed above the
little coronet, and below the fitting throats, with most even clipping. The height
without the lineaments three feet. Above the flat sat an antique water-jar, four-
handled, raised three feet. From each of which singly grew a box with two steps of
box rounding. The lower exceeded in circuit the bulk of the underset water-jar,
raised from the orifice above the stock one foot; the other, upper, stood off from
this somewhat lessened, as much again. Beyond this raised, as much again, was a
pommel of the vase's compass. Out of which, at equal height, one standing opposite
the other with a triangular separation, rose for each a most straight stock — one

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The third meadow's topiary — box trained on a chalice-domed
stand ringed by winged serpents, then up through jars and arches — is secure in
scheme though intricate in detail; the winged serpents are the clear and vivid part.

- **The third ring's centrepiece: winged serpents.** Where the first ring had the
  hydra-fountain and the second the box giant, the third's is a domed box structure
  ringed by **six winged serpents** — snakes with spread wings and feet, coiled at
  each stock, spouting water from open jaws through hidden pipes. The escalation
  continues: architecture, then a giant, now a ring of dragons. All in living box
  and stone with concealed plumbing.
- **litharge and the fragrant corners** — the chest is *litharmeno* (litharge, lead
  monoxide, a glassy yellow), continuing the run of ever-rarer materials for the
  three rings' planters (jasper, amber, now litharge). The species catalogue for
  this ring's corners follows on p. 306.
- The trained-box structure is described part by part again; where p. 300 and 303
  became unreadable, this one stays followable because the serpents anchor it.
