# Page 270 — Chapter XIX

Not far distant from this — somewhat moved to little sighs by the preceding unhappy case read — I found another
noteworthy and most-worthy monument, with two fluted little columns, one to a side, semicircle-worked out of the solid
rock, of whitest marble, with little bases and capitals, little architrave, little frieze, little cornices, and little
fastigium; in the three-sided (pediment) of which two whitening turtledoves drank together in a little vase. In the
content between the fluted (columns), of low relief carved, was an arched soffit, evenly distributed into little
coffered squares, where, in each, a cinquefoil occupied, by the optic norm (in perspective), the linings diminishing
with the content (depth). Under which an artful ark somewhat stood forth, fixed with two little doors — in one of
which entered —

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** A new **whitest-marble tomb**: fluted flanking columns, a pediment with two **turtledoves
drinking from a vase**, and a coffered vault rendered in optical perspective (the cinquefoil-coffers "diminishing with
the depth"), over an ark with two little doors. All secure; the epitaph itself follows on p. 271.

- **Perspective in the coffering.** Colonna notes the vault's coffers are carved "by the optic norm" — in linear
  perspective, the lines shrinking with depth: a knowing reference to the new science of perspective (Alberti, della
  Francesca), applied to a carved ceiling. The Polyandrion's art is technically up-to-date. A buildable illusionistic
  vault for a world tomb.
- **the drinking turtledoves** — Venus's birds "drinking together" from a vase in the pediment: the emblem of paired,
  faithful love (cf. the doves of pp. 219, 233), fit for a couple's tomb. The epitaph (p. 271) proves it a marriage-
  monument, though a tragic one (a jealous wife's suicide).
- **the ark with two little doors** — a double-tomb (two chambers), its doors the threshold between life and death
  Poliphilo keeps reading. The chapter's tombs increasingly pair the dead; the Polyandrion is a place of couples.
