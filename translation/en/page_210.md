In the aforesaid summit of the just-narrated cupola, again eight little Pilasters, with the height of two
squares, rose straight, with an ewer-vase, with the opening inverted, above them. The ascent, then, of the
whole, with exquisite commensuration, and with an object mathematically proportioned to the inspectors.

Upon the bottom of the ewer-vase (so I interpret it, for its form), roundabout, most-beautifully scored of
melon-scoring, was impressed a stalk of the proper metal, which began from the breadth of the bottom,
moderately slender ascending. And by as much as was found the middle of the vase, the stylus, ascended,
sustained a huge hollow triangle, together with the stylus artfully cast. In the summit of which gaped a
round opening; and in the low, corpulent (part), in four locations, it was bored — conjecturing, I
pondered, the shrewd device of the provident artificer for this: that water let in by rain, or conceived
frost, should not occupy it from its office, and to avoid the weight. Through the upper Opening of the said
sphere, free of the lips, the said stylus, or spindle, passed, sharpened at the tip. From the outlet
upward it was as much as from the bottom of the vase to the sphere.

In this fastigium a bronze moon was fixed, eight-day (crescent), as much as she shows, with the horns to
the sky. In the gulf, or sinus, of which sat an Eagle with outspread wings. Under the moon, on the stylus,
were four solid and firm grapples, in which four little chains of the aforesaid metal were retained, and

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The temple's crowning **lantern**: eight hollow fluted columns carrying a scaled
cupola, each column topped by a **weathervane figure of one of the eight winds** (winged, pivoting to face
the blast); and the finial — a stalk carrying a great hollow bronze triangle, a horned bronze **moon**
cradling an **eagle**, and four chains (whose sounding bells are on p. 211). All secure.

- **The eight-wind weathervanes.** The lantern is ringed by eight pivoting winged wind-gods, each turning
  to face the wind that blows — a working weathervane-crown, one per wind of the classical rose. A
  distinctive, buildable finial-ring for the world's temple; it also rhymes with the calendar/zodiac
  windows (p. 200) — the temple measures both the year and the winds.
- **the moon-and-eagle finial.** The very top is a horned crescent moon holding an eagle, on a triangle:
  lunar (Venus/night) and solar/Jovian (eagle) signs crowning the temple of generative Venus, with the
  triangle (the number of generation). A precise, symbolic finial.
- **the hollow triangle bored to drain** — even the finial is engineered: pierced so rain and frost drain
  and do not add weight (continuing the drainage-mindfulness of pp. 202–203). Colonna's practicality
  reaches the summit.
- The founder's virtuosity (casting the whole in one) leads into p. 211's account of casting an unbroken
  chain, and the wind-rung bells.
