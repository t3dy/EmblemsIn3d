## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The prayer ends (the virgins' "So be it"), and the **sacrifice of the swans**: the
priestess scatters roses and oyster-shells and asperges the altar with sea-water, cuts the two swans' throats
(reserving their blood with the doves' in the *praefericulum*), has them cremated and their ash boxed under the
altar, and then **signs arcane characters in the blood** on the pavement, Polia repeating the act. All secure.

- **The Venerean sacrifice completed.** Swans (Venus's chariot-birds) join the turtledoves; roses, oyster-
  shells and sea-water (her flower, shell, and element) dress the altar. The blood is reserved and used to
  trace "arcane characters" — the same ash-and-blood writing as p. 227, love-magic in the sacrifice's remains.
  Every element is precisely Venerean.
- **the doubled blood-writing** — the priestess signs characters in the blood, then has Polia do the same:
  the initiate repeats the celebrant's act, as with the ashes (p. 227). The rite is teaching Polia the mystery
  even as it performs it. The characters stay undescribed — the secret is kept.
- **the prayer's close** — "eternally live with thee in the supernal delights and glorious triumphs": the
  petition ends on the promise of the lovers' eternal life in Venus's realm (Cythera's triumphs). The whole
  oration (pp. 229–231) is answered by the miracle on p. 233.
- The earthquake and the miraculous rose-bush follow on pp. 232–233.
