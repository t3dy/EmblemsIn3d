# Page 302 — Chapter XXI

This arrangement, in all four platforms placed at the corners of this first
meadow, showed itself all clothed with flowering *chamaedaphne*. The opening of the
topmost step was a foot in diameter, and in each was planted a most noble and
fecund fruit-tree, led uniformly topiary-wise. And first they were apple-trees: in
one corner I saw most fragrant Appian apples; in the other, Claudian apples; in the
third, Paradise apples; in the fourth, the little Decian apples. But in each meadow
of this first order the species of the said fruit varied. Trees here fecund with
apple-bearing offspring, the fragrance diffusing everywhere — with such beauty of
colour, and producing such sweetness of taste, as the tree of Hercules of Gades did
not produce, nor such trees as Juno commanded to be grafted in her gardens; whereby
they might be called *pomarii aphyracori* [orchards of golden apples].

The topiary shaping, and the circled thickness, was of a crown, with the winding
toward the watch-tower. The parapets, or fences, of this stepped platform stood of
most beautiful mirror-jasper, oversown with golden sparks, and mingled with yellow
mottling, and with cyan veins serpentining, and reddish ones running, and confusedly
dappled with a chalcedonic waving, squared off with most gratifying little wave-
lines.

Beyond, then, from this described first order of greenery toward the island's
centre — in the second order, in the middle of the area, in place of the watch-
tower — I beheld a most admirable device of box, in artful topiary. A stone ark set
of precious chalcedony of the colour of soapy water, with most becoming lineaments,
its height three feet and in length three paces, destined to the line of the
transverse streets. From the one and the other end, less a foot, was set a box in
the form of an antique vase, both two equal and uniformly egregiously conducted,
with the little foot, bulk and orifice, raised a pace, without handles. Upon their
mouths a giant three paces tall, on this side and that trod with the foot, with legs
apart. Clothed in a rounding down to the cap of the knees, girt, with the arms

harmony. He was helmeted; with his arms he sustained two towers, one per hand, four

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The corner fruit-tree topiaries and the second-ring's box
giant are secure in scheme. The apple-varieties are Pliny's (Appian, Claudian,
Paradise, Decian) and match the ones the *Untangling the Knot* study lists for the
*prati* (GARDENS.md §5). The giant-holding-towers topiary continues on p. 303.

- **The second ring's centrepiece is a box giant.** Where the first ring's meadows
  had the fountain-under-topiary, the second ring's have a box figure of a giant,
  three paces tall, legs astride two vase-shaped box stocks, holding a tower in
  each hand. Living-box sculpture again, escalating from the first ring's
  architecture to a figure. This is the "different types of knot gardens and
  topiary" Segre's plan gives the inner zone.
- **the apple varieties** — Appian, Claudian, Paradise, Decian, all real Roman
  cultivars from Pliny; A. Segre's garden study cites exactly these for the first
  order of the *prati*. The world's fruit-tree corners come from here.
- **"the tree of Hercules of Gades… Juno's gardens"** — the golden apples of the
  Hesperides (Gades = Cadiz, near Hercules's western limit; the apples were Juno's
  wedding gift). The orchard is named *aphyracori*, of golden apples, surpassing
  even those.
- **mirror-jasper parapets** — the stepped platforms are faced in figured jasper,
  described with a lapidary's precision (golden sparks, cyan and red veins,
  chalcedonic dappling). Colonna's stones are always this exact.
