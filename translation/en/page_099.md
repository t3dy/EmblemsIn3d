Divine, and of a marvellous magnanimity in her aspect. Sumptuously clothed in cloth rewoven with
forced gold in the thread.

Her majestic head she held adorned with an ambitious ornament, of a silken and purple mitre, matronly
and royal, of a heap of uncommon and light and burnished pearls, which went round the wide forehead in
the fringe of the mitre. Which pressed down her blackest hair — shining more than Indian amber — that
descended with a comely difference, waving over her snowy temples. Then, from the back of the head, her
overflowing hair was parted into two tresses neatly plaited. The one here and the other there, they
passed above the little ears, and then met again in a wonderful agreement. At the top of the crown they
were bound in a knot, or tuft, of great and roundest pearls, such as the Indian promontory Perimula
does not bring forth.

And out of the knot flowed the remainder of the long and most abundant locks, covered by a most thin
veiling, with a golden parting-pin held by the said knot or tuft, which descended flying to her
delicate shoulders. In the middle of the mitre, at the parting above the crown, there stood out a most
precious little shape. And her rounded throat, suffused with a snowy whiteness, was encircled by an
inestimable necklace, with a pendant towards the fork of her milky breast, of an incomparable diamond
in a tablet of half-moon form, or oval, sparkling all over, monstrous in size, in a golden setting of
fine-wrought ornament.

Besides, from her ears there were most richly hung, exquisitely in the piercing, two inestimable
drops, of two great pure carbuncles, flashing without equal. She had also her feet shod in green silk,
and the little slippers with golden loops twisted at the straps, adorned with many jewels. They trod
upon a footstool, or rather a support, of a soft cushion swollen with a heap of down, of crimson
velvet, with a border going round of eastern pearls, such as are not to be found in Arabia in the
Persian gulf. With four hanging tufts at the four corners, covered with flashing gems, with their
little beards, or fringes, of twisted threads, and mingled hanging things of gold and of vermilion
silk.

On the right and left side, upon the sandalwood benches, the ladies of the court sat in a well-ordered
manner, with an honest and homely gravity, clothed in cloth of gold, with an incredible adornment of
dress, such as never was seen in the world's judgement. In the midst, then, sat this renowned and
sovereign Queen with pomp and the utmost state, and an unthinkable adornment. The borders led round her
ambitious garments were filled with most opulent jewels of alternating colour in such plenty that you
would say nature had hailed down there all the finest manners of shining gems, in a wanton array.
