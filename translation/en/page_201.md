# Page 201 — Chapter XVII

The remaining walls of the temple, with manifold and various-form Emblemature, were laboriously
incrusted of precious marbles — as best the much-knowing Architect, imagining, could often apply to so
great a magnificence of structure; nor, perhaps, was such made for Ammon. Above the order of the
Corinthians, of most-perfect sculpture, the eloquent Apollo resided, lyring. And above each of the
others, in circuit, upon a fit Altar, of the whole entire, a Muse of *pilates* stone — at the opportune
gestures and office, with the utmost praise of the Statuary most-exquisitely carved — set above the
aforesaid, above, of the encompassing binding.

The huge cupola showed the greatest token, rather of divine operation than of human ostentation. But if
human, not without stupor and accusation of the ambition of so much attempted of the founder's-art by
human wit — for, considering so great a vastness, in one sole and solid casting and pour of metal (as I
judged), I remained most-highly marvelling and hallucinated, damning it almost to impossibility.
Nonetheless, this such Bronze-work was all of Vine springing out of most-beautiful vases, of the bronze
material, standing at the perpendicular and order of the columnation — diffusing the branches, vine-
shoots, or twigs, and Tendrils, dizzily entangling themselves, with most-pleasing conjugation, in
observance of the formation of the convex of the said cupola, one with the other with decent density;
with leaves, clusters, little infants climbing and plucking, little Birds fluttering, Lizards creeping,
in emulation of nature, most-excellently worked out, and all the rest passable (open).

The described works (were) so directly conducted, that, proportioned, in the area they artfully appeared
as much as the natural. Of most-perfect gold, all shiningly gilt. The openings — that is, the void of the
foliation, fruits, and animals — most-fittingly presented themselves stopped with crystalline plates, of
diverse colouring tinged, like translucent gems.

To the congruity of the structure opportune is the integrity of the harmony; for every girdable inner
binding demands the outer girdle. The external piers continued the *areobate* footing, with the three
uneven steps, binding all the base of the temple — as much librated from the area as the inner pavement
was raised — above the *areobates*, or *stylobates*, or Scabelli, in place of a Base: an ornament, with
Tori, Alvei, Fasciae, Gulets, and quarter-circles circling round, and again round the sacello becomingly
circled. Its lowest projection received (worn) from the human foot; and above, the Pilasters. Which, in
the interstice, were pierced through, or tubed, and perforated — that is, the guttering, or compluvium of
the precipitate waters, through the Tubes let down to the ground, were —

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The temple's walls (incrusted in emblematic precious-marble panels), its
sculpture programme (**Apollo lyre-playing over the Corinthians, a Muse over each other column**), and its
crowning marvel — **the great cupola cast as a single bronze vine**, its foliage, clusters, climbing
putti, birds and lizards gilt, and its openings glazed with coloured crystal like gems. All secure; the
vine-dome is one of the book's supreme architectural inventions.

- **The bronze vine-dome.** The whole cupola is a single colossal bronze casting in the form of a living
  vine growing from vases at the column-tops, its shoots woven into the dome's curve, populated with
  grape-plucking infants, birds and lizards, gilt all over, and its interstices filled with coloured
  crystal that glows like gems. Poliphilo cannot believe it was cast in one pour. This is a spectacular,
  buildable centerpiece — a golden vine-lattice dome glowing with coloured light — for the world's temple.
- **Apollo and the nine Muses.** The statue-programme crowns the columns: Apollo lyring over the
  Corinthians, a Muse (in appropriate gesture) over each of the other columns. A temple of Venus crowned by
  the god of harmony and the Muses — generation under the sign of music and art. A ready statuary set.
- **the areobate base and its engineering** — the outer piers stand on a three-stepped stylobate, and the
  pilasters are hollowed as *downpipes* ("tubed… the compluvium of precipitate waters"): the temple's rain
  drains through its own columns to a cistern (detailed pp. 202–203). Colonna designs the plumbing.
- **"nor perhaps was such made for Ammon"** — the surpassing-topos: not even the temple of Zeus Ammon
  (Siwa) had such marble-work. The habitual heaping of famous buildings to be exceeded.
