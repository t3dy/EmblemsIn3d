# Page 170 — Chapter XIV

The fourth triumph four wheels carried, of iron-like Arcadian asbestos, once kindled refusing to be put
out. The rest of the four-square tabling, in the manner aforesaid, was of flashing carbuncle of
Troglodytis, not fearing the thick darkness, with most polished chasings, long to speak of distinctly.
But such workmanship ought to be considered in what place, and by what maker, it was wrought.

The right-hand face, then, showed excellently this history. A venerable matron with child. To whom the
highest Jupiter appeared divinely (as he is wont with the goddess Juno) with thunders and lightnings,
so that, kindled, she was burnt to ashes; and out of the burnt body they drew a most noble and divine
infant.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The fourth car, and Semele burnt by Jove's lightning. Both stones are
correctly identified and glossed, and one of them corrects an earlier page: *Asvesto Archado* is
given plainly as Arcadian asbestos, once kindled never quenched — which is the same material the
banquet's fireproof napkins were made of on p. 110, where the English left *Bysso di Carysto*
unexplained. *Carbunculo tragoditano*, the carbuncle of Troglodytis that does not fear the dark, is
right. Medium because Semele is not named, and because the page breaks off after one panel, so its
confidence rests on a short sample.

*Confidence stated 2026-09-20 by a second reader, not by the translator (ticket tr-confidence-unstated-180).*
