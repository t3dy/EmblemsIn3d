Likewise, beneath her straight nose, a most charming little valley followed, bordering on her small
mouth of a courteous shape; the little lips of which, not swollen but moist, and painted with a purple
tincture, covered the even ranks of her small and ivory teeth, not one standing out above another, but
set evenly in order. Among which Love unceasingly composed a breathable fragrance. For, thinking upon
it, I presumed that in those gracious lips there was nothing else than, for the milky teeth, shining
pearls, and for the fragrant breath, the warmest musk, and for the sweet voice, Thespis with her nine
daughters.

By all which things being exceedingly enticed, among my kindled feelings and my disordered and burning
appetite, there was straightway born a great sedition and a bitter contention, such as was not in the
presences told of before, nor among the most ample and various riches. For my various and thieving eyes
commended one part as far fairer than the others. But the appetite, carried off to another part of that
divine little body, argued and preferred that one to the rest.

And of this the whole ill beginning of so troubling and contentious a commotion were my unsated and
most troublesome eyes, which I felt sowing and stirring up so great and such a harmful quarrel in my
sad and wounded heart. By the stubbornness of which I then lost it altogether; and none the less
without them I could in nothing at all satisfy them. The raging appetite then extolled her most
delicious breast without equal. The eyes, consenting with delight, said: if only we could uncover it
all. So that those unsteady ones, thence violently lifted from her most comely face, matched every
extreme pleasure in it.

And here the appetite, strengthened and unfittingly forward, murmured: who would easily persuade me
that ever at any time there was a head so plentiful, with such native tresses and voluptuous ornament of
weaving, and of twisted hairs in a distinguished heaping, and with a joyful winding, adorning so fair
and so shining a brow. Such shavings of fir, always deserving to be in vine-curled circles.

That never such and so notable a Hesperia pleased Aesacus with her flowing hair, nor appeared to him;

rays, saw; wounding me deeply in my heart like a dart let fly by an angry Cupid. So that, concluding, I
