## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The gate's architecture — its whole order (base, capital, architrave, frieze,
cornice, pediment) of the golden *litharmenon*, its arch of ophite with alternating ophite/porphyry
columns and matching altars, and two great gem-vases (sapphire and emerald) flanking the entry; then
**Cupid ("the lord Archer") descends** at the gate, and the amphitheatre proper is introduced. All
secure.

- **The alternating ophite/porphyry order.** Colonna specifies a strict green-and-red rhythm: arch of
  ophite (green serpentine), first columns porphyry (red), then alternating, with capitals, bases and
  altars all switching in counterpoint. A precise polychrome-stone scheme for the world's theatre-gate —
  green and red columns in alternation, as the terraces alternated red and black (p. 315).
- **the sapphire and emerald vases** — two colossal gem-vases flank the gate, "like the vases at the
  temple of Jove in Athens." Blue and green, they answer the red/green stone: the gate is a composition
  in coloured minerals. Buildable as two great glowing urns at the theatre entrance.
- **"the lord Archer descended."** Cupid dismounts from the serpent-car at the gate — the triumph reaches
  its destination and the god goes in on foot. The amphitheatre's own structure (double arcade of
  alabaster and gilt bronze) is described from here through p. 349.
- **refusing steel** — the golden marble is so hard it "refuses the tenacious steel": Colonna's insistence
  that the whole theatre is worked in intractable precious material, "not makeable by earth-dwellers."
