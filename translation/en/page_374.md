## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The completion of the cameo-Venus and the Latin distich
are secure; the couplet is elegant and its sense plain. One gesture-word
(`tatula`, a diminutive verb of touching/patting) is uncertain and marked.

- **Venus nursing Cupid over the tomb** — the statue resolves: the *puerpera*
  Venus of the previous page is shown giving the breast to her son, but the
  couplet turns it: he sucks not milk but the bitter tears she weeps for Adonis.
  The image of maternal love over the lover's grave is pushed to its sharpest
  point — the child of love feeding on grief.
- **the drill-work tresses of translucent sardine vein** — the carver has used the
  clear band of the stone for Venus's hair, drilled into openwork curls. The
  reference is to the famous emerald(?) intaglio of **Polycrates'** ring, which
  Pliny says Augustus dedicated in the temple of Concord in a golden horn:
  Colonna's cameo outshines even that most storied gem.
- **`tatula dextra`** — "with her right [?patting] hand," the gesture of a nursing
  mother; the verb is a Colonna diminutive and its exact force is uncertain.
  Marked for the facsimile.
- The couplet is one of the book's genuine small poems, and being Latin it is
  quotable verbatim; given here with a plain English gloss beneath, as with the
  other 1499 inscriptions.
