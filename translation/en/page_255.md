# Page 255 — Chapter XIX

> **HAVE**
> **LERIA,**
> **OMNIUM**
> **AMANTISS(IMA),**
> **VALE.**
>
> "Hail, Leria, most-loving of all — farewell."

Who (the old satyr), with the tumefied cheeks, sounded two rural Tibiae, and (was) propped upon a knotty trunk of
*Dendrocissus* (tree-ivy), all hollow of old age, with pervious cracks and rami discordant, rare of leaves, with the
hair (foliage) unkempt and un-fronded. Between these two leapt a nude boy. On the other side was one who, upon the
robust shoulders, bearing a leaky *Armillum* (wine-jar) with the orifice inverted, over the horned head scattered the
unmixed wine. Beside him was a matron, with the hair let down, dishevelled — and she, and the porter of the wine-jar,
nude — and tearful, holding a torch with the kindled part downward. Between these two appeared a little Satyr-boy, who
in the hands squeezed a serpent much involved. There followed then a rustic old woman, basket-bearing, clad over the
nude in a flying cloth, girt over the flanks; on the inelegant head, over the bald (crown), she had a *cesticillus*
(pad); and above she bore a wicker *cista* full of fruits and of fronds, and in the other hand held a vase of oblong
clayey orifice. These figures most-excellently were carved, and roughly (in high relief). On the little altar thus was
inscribed. Excited most-highly by so much comeliness of monuments, questing, to me a most-elegant Roman epitaph,
inscribed on a stone, offered such a most-pleasant dialogue, and with such ornaments:

## Notes

**Confidence: medium** (the *Have… Vale* epitaph is secure). The little tomb-inscription **"Hail, Leria, most-loving
of all, farewell"**; the historiated Bacchic sepulchre described in full — an old satyr piping on twin *tibiae* against
a hollow tree-ivy trunk, a leaping boy, a wine-pourer with a leaky jar, a dishevelled tearful matron with an inverted
torch, a satyr-boy strangling a serpent, and a basket-bearing old woman; and the introduction of the **dialogue-
epitaph** (its text on p. 256). All secure.

- **"Have… Vale" — the shortest, purest epitaph.** *Hail… farewell*, framing "Leria, most-loving of all": the Roman
  tomb-formula of greeting and parting the passer-by, at its most distilled. A perfect, tiny inscription for the
  world's polyandrion (address and dismissal, the traveller greeted and sent on).
- **the Bacchic funeral-relief.** The carved scene — piping satyr, wine poured from an inverted jar, mourning matron
  with a reversed (extinguished) torch, serpent-wrestling boy, fruit-bearing crone — is a Dionysiac *thiasos* of death:
  wine, grief, the snake (renewal), the harvest-basket. The **inverted torch** is the antique sign of a life
  extinguished. A ready carved programme for the world's tombs, iconographically exact.
- **the dialogue-epitaph announced.** Poliphilo, "excited by so much comeliness of monuments," is now fully in the
  antiquarian's delight — the graveyard has become a gallery. The Nevia dialogue-epitaph (p. 256) is the chapter's
  finest inscription.
- **rustic detail** — the crone's *cesticillus* (the pad worn on the head to carry loads) and clay vase: Colonna's
  precise, humble realism even in a funeral relief.
