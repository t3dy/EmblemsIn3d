## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The theatre's interior climax: the **mosaic soffits historiated with "all the
deeds of Cupid,"** the surpassing of every ancient wonder (Ephesus, Colosseum, Verona), the entry of the
inner party alone (the outer nymphs excluded), and — on entering the Area — the **mirror-black obsidian
floor** so lustrous that Poliphilo, stepping on it, feels he is falling into an abyss. All secure; the
obsidian mirror is one of the book's great images.

- **The obsidian mirror-floor — the theatre's central marvel.** The whole Area is a single slab of
  polished obsidian, black and bottomless as calm sea, in which everything is reflected. Poliphilo's right
  foot, at first step, seems to plunge into an "abyss." This is the floor on which the Fountain of Venus
  stands (ch. XXIII), and a spectacular, buildable feature: the world's theatre centre should be a black
  mirror-plane reflecting the fountain and the sky-dome. (The book's own next lines describe what is seen
  reflected in it.)
- **the vaults painted with all Cupid's deeds** — the ceiling-mosaics narrate the complete mythology of
  Love: the theatre is an iconographic programme, "all the effects done by Cupid," over the mirror that
  reflects them. A ready subject-list for the world's dome or vault decoration.
- **the party narrows to six** — only Cupid, Psyche, Poliphilo, Polia, and the two binding-nymphs pass the
  Royal gate; the whole triumphal throng is left outside. The revelation of Venus (ch. XXIII) is for the
  initiated few. A narrative gating the world can honour (the climax-station admits only the walker).
- **surpassing Augustus's elephants and Menelaus** — the closing surpassing-topos of the chapter, heaping
  the Roman wonders to be exceeded. Chapter XXII ends inside the theatre, on the obsidian; ch. XXIII opens
  at the Fountain of Venus at its centre.
