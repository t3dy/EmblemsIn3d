# Page 217 — Chapter XVII

Not so soon did I do this holy command, than she, taking the precious hyacinthine *lepista*, and with a
little cord of gold and of crimson and green silk (deputed to such office), infusing it into the cisternal
water, drew out of the blessed water, and, with religion, offered it to the Nymph alone. And she, with prompt
devotion, drank it. Immediately then the hieratic Priestess, with the golden little key, diligently re-shut
the covering of the Cistern; and, somewhat reading over the holy and efficacious prayers and exorcisms, she
enjoined the Nymph to say, three times, these words toward me:

> "May the divine Cytherea hear thee, to thy vow; and, propitiated in me, may she nourish her son."
> Responsory from the virgins: "**So be it.**"

The said ceremonies religiously terminated, the Nymph, in that instant, reverent, at the sandalled feet of
purple re-woven with gold, ornated with manifold gems, of the Priestess, having prostrated herself — of a
sudden she made her rise up, and gave her a holy kiss. And to me then the Nymph, boldly turned, with her comely
presence placid, full of pious semblances, with a sigh come warmly from the base of the inflamed heart, thus
said to me:

## Notes

**Confidence: high.** The rite completes: the priestess draws **blessed water and gives it to Polia alone** to
drink, reseals the well, and has Polia say thrice "may Cytherea nourish her son"; the virgins answer "So be
it." Polia kisses the priestess's feet, is raised and given a holy kiss, and then **turns to Poliphilo and
begins her declaration** — that his love has "stolen her from the chaste college" and made her quench her
torch. All secure; the union begins.

- **Polia drinks; the vow is sealed.** Only Polia drinks the blessed water — she is the one being received
  into "the amorous realm of the Divine Mother." The blessing ("may Cytherea nourish her son") makes Poliphilo
  the goddess's foster-child, the beloved she will nurture. The rite converts private desire into a sanctioned
  bond.
- **"stolen me from the chaste college."** Polia was among the temple's chaste virgins (the "college"); Poliphilo's
  love has drawn her out and made her "extinguish her torch" (surrender her chastity to love). Her declaration —
  the turning-point of the whole first book — begins here and completes on p. 218. This is the reciprocation
  Poliphilo has begged for since the crossing (p. 288).
- **the torch quenched / the torch extinguished** — the rite's quenched torch (p. 216) and Polia's "extinguished
  torch" here are the same image: the cold, chaste flame put out so that love's fire may take its place. Colonna
  binds the liturgy to the emotional event.
- The declaration and the lovers' first kiss (the "pledge," *arra*) are on p. 218 — the emotional climax of
  Book I's later half.
