Let us return, then, to that vastest Pyramid, beneath which lay a huge and solid Plinth, or block, or
square set under it, of fourteen paces in height and in extension, or length, six stadia. Which made
the footing of the lowest step of the massive Pyramid. And this I shrewdly judged had not been
brought there from elsewhere, but carved out of the mountain itself by human labours into that figure
and Scheme, and reduced to so great a mass in its own place. The remainder of the steps was made
compositely of separate blocks.

Which immense square did not adhere to the flanking mountains of the valley, but was set apart and
separated on the one side and the other by ten paces. On the right-hand side, as I went, of the
aforesaid Plinth, in the middle of it, the viperous head of the fearful Medusa was fearsomely and
perfectly carved, shown in furious act, crying out and snarling; with terrifying eyes, hollowed
beneath the pressed-down brows, and with wrinkled forehead, and the mouth gaping open. Which,
excavated with a straight passage, its top vaulted, penetrating as far as the centre — or as far as
the middle perpendicular central line of the topmost Cap of that portent-bearing Pyramid — made a
most ample entrance and approach.

To which opening of the mouth one ascended by way of her twisted hairs, expressed with an
inconceivable subtlety of understanding, and art, and studied thought of the craftsman; with such
rule and reduction that they aptly made climbing steps up to the open mouth. And in place of curling
tresses I gazed astonished at vipers and twisted serpents, in living and huge coils, wrapping
themselves confusedly about the monstrous head with the swiftest turnings. Whereupon the face and the
scaly quarrelling serpents were so exactly counterfeited in the working that they struck no little
horror and terror into me. In the eyes of which were most conveniently enclosed the brightest little
stones — so much so that, had I not been certain the material was marble, I should not have dared
approach so easily.

The above-named passage, carved within the solid rock, led to where the stairs were, with a winding
course, in the centre, by spiral turnings; by which one climbed to the highest summit of that Pyramid,
onto the surface of the square Cap. Upon which was founded the eminent Obelisk.
