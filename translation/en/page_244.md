>
> "By peace and concord small things grow; by discord the greatest decrease."

## Notes

**Confidence: high** (the two mottoes are secure — the hieroglyphs are given with Poliphilo's own Latin, and both
are famous). The obelisk-base's two hieroglyph-panels decoded: the first a **dedication to Julius Caesar** by the
Egyptians ("for his clemency and liberality"); the second the celebrated **ant-and-elephant concord-motto** —
*by peace and concord small things grow, by discord the greatest decrease* — read from the caduceus, the ant
growing to an elephant (and elephants shrinking to ants), and the fire-vase and water-shell. All secure.

- **The ant-and-elephant hieroglyph — the world's polyandrion device.** This is the exact source for the
  animated ant/elephant hieroglyph on the world's polyandrion obelisk (`_buildPolyandrion`, `this._hiero`): a
  caduceus flanked by an ant swelling into an elephant (concord, growth) and elephants dwindling to ants
  (discord, decline), with fire and water balanced between. Poliphilo's Latin — *pace ac concordia parvae res
  crescunt, discordia maximae decrescunt* — is the motto the world can carry verbatim. One of the most-copied of
  all the book's hieroglyphs (Erasmus and the emblem-books took it up).
- **the obelisk of Caesar.** The first panel is a hieroglyphic dedication to **Julius Caesar** "ever Augustus,
  governor of the whole world," raised by the Egyptians — hence the world's naming of the monument "the obelisk
  of Caesar." Colonna reads the balance/dog/serpent/sword/crown and the eye/wheat/globe/rudder as this imperial
  encomium (justice, vigilance, world-rule, clemency).
- **hieroglyphics as the book's signature.** Here is the Hypnerotomachia's method in its purest form: pictures
  decoded into Latin sentences. The two mottoes — imperial praise and the concord-maxim — set the obelisk as a
  monument of *good rule and harmony* at the heart of the tomb of love's discord. The Polyandrion's dead died of
  love's *discord*; the obelisk preaches concord over them.
- Both mottoes are prime material for the world/novel's polyandrion (the concord-motto especially, already used).
