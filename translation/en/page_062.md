Alas, a thing to terrify warlike and corsleted Mars, and to make tremble the terrible and evil-averting
Hercules with all his brawny and Molorchian Club; and to call back Theseus from his attempted
enterprise and rash beginning; and to terrify the giant Typhon more than the gods above were terrified
by him; and to make lose whatever shaggy, obstinate and impenetrable heart there ever was. Alas, a
thing to draw back heaven-bearing Atlas from his office — let alone a young man and small of soul,
finding himself alone among unknown places, unarmed and fearing peril.

And perceiving that it was smoke-vomiting, of foul breath, and deadly, as I rightly suspected —
mistrusting any escape whatever, and of saving myself from mortal peril, almost without breath, in my
little courage I devoutly invoked, trembling and terrified, every divine power.

And without delay, turning my shoulders and entering into the darkness, I committed myself to swift
flight, anxiously carrying my already hastened feet to flee. With the greatest nimbleness, unknowing,
penetrating swift-footed into the inner parts of that dark place, escaping through divers and oblique
turnings and windings of passages.
