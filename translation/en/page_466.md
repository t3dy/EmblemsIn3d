# Page 466 — Epitaphium Poliae

*The text on this leaf is Latin, not Italian. It is given here in the original
with an English rendering beneath, since the Latin is itself quotable and the
first three lines carry an acrostic.*

## Polia's epitaph

> F œlix Polia, quæ sepulta vivis
> C haro Marte Poliphilus quiescens
> I  am fecit vigilare te sopitam.

Happy Polia, who being buried yet live:
Poliphilo, resting from dear Mars,
has now made you, who were lulled asleep, keep watch.

*The initials set out from the margin spell* **F · C · I**.

## The epitaph in which Poliphilo speaks

> VIATOR FAC QUAESO MORULAM, POLIAE NYMPHAE HIC EST MYROPOLIUM.
> QUAE NAM INQUIES POLIA? FLOS ILLE OMNEM REDOLENS VIRTUTEM SPECTATISSIMUS.
> QUI OB LOCI ARITUDINEM, PLUSCULIS POLIPHILI LACHRYMUL[IS] REPULULESCERE NEQUIT.
> AT SI ME FLORERE VIDERES, EXIMIA PICTURA UNIVERSIS DECORITER PRAESTARE
> CONSPICERES, PHOEBE INQUIENS, QUEM INTACTUM URORE RELIQUERAS, UMBRA CECIDIT.
> HEU POLIPHILE DESINE. FLOS SIC EXSICCATUS, NUNQUAM REVIVISCIT. VALE.

Traveller, make a little pause, I beg you: here is the perfume-shop of the nymph
Polia. And what, you will say, is Polia? That most admired flower, fragrant with
every virtue — who, because the ground is parched, cannot put out shoots again
for all Poliphilo's little tears. But if you had seen me in flower, you would
have seen me surpass all others in beauty, like an exceptional painting; saying,
*Phoebus, the one you left untouched by your burning, a shadow has felled.* Ah,
Poliphilo, have done. A flower dried out like this never lives again.

Farewell.

---

## Notes

**Confidence: high.** This leaf is Latin, not macaronic Italian, and it is
straightforward funerary Latin at that. Two abbreviations and one line-broken
word had to be expanded, all noted below and none of them doubtful.

- **`MYROPOLIUM`** — literally an unguent- or perfume-seller's shop
  (μυροπώλιον). Colonna is calling the tomb a perfumery because what is stored in
  it is fragrance: the *flos omnem redolens virtutem* of the next line. Kept
  literal, since flattening it to "tomb" loses the conceit that the whole epitaph
  turns on.
- **`LACHRYMUL.`** — the source abbreviates; expanded to *lachrymulis*, "little
  tears," the diminutive Colonna uses throughout for Poliphilo's weeping.
- **`RELIQUE / RAS`** — broken across a line in the transcription; read as
  *reliqueras*.
- **`PHOEBE INQUIENS`** — vocative: the dead flower addresses the Sun, who
  spared it and could not save it from shade. This is the same envious Sun that
  ends the dream on the previous page, which is surely deliberate.
- The three-line acrostic **F · C · I** is not the book's famous
  thirty-eight-chapter acrostic (POLIAM FRATER FRANCISCVS COLVMNA PERAMAVIT) but
  a small separate one on this leaf.
