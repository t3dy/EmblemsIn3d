It was aptly founded upon a distinguished shaft of jasper, of straggling mixtures, the one beautifully
adulterating the other, and cut across by translucent chalcedony of the colour of troubled sea-water,
brought to a most noble making. Raised of ewer-like vessels, one above another, with the parting of a
choice knot-work. Which stood upright, fixed in the centre of a rounded plinth of green ophite. Which
round was raised from the level pavement by a fifth part, with the surrounding porphyry, which was
curiously lined with well-polished little waves.

Round about the shaft, set beneath the basin, four harpies of gold with clawed and rapacious feet stood
upon the flat of the ophite. Which, with their hinder parts towards the shaft, the one directly
opposite the other, and with unfolded wings, held up beneath the violet lip, or basin, with maidenly
faces. Their locks flowing loose down over their shoulders from the neck. And with their heads not
reaching under the basin. With their serpent tails coiling into a globe, and passing at the end into
antique foliage. They made with the lowest ewer-vessel of the shaft a not ungrateful but a friendly
knotting and joining.

Within, at the middle navel of the vessel, above the order of the shaft beneath, there was raised
proportionately out of the amethyst vessel itself an oblong chalice turned upside down, lifted as much
as the vessel was hollowed, its middle standing clear of the surrounding lips of the basin. Above which
was raised an artful little altar, set beneath the three naked Graces. Of the finest gold, of a common
tallness, the one cleaving to the other. From the nipples of whose breasts the rising water dripped
finely, appearing like little rods of burnished and streaked silvered ash. And as though it had been
distilled through the whitest pumice of Tarragona.

And each of them held in her right hand an all-bearing horn of plenty, which rose somewhat above her
head. And then all three openings came elegantly together in one rounding, their mouths meeting equally
together. With fruits and various fronds hanging out of the rims, or lips, of the twisted horns,
abundantly filled.

Among the fruits and foliage six little pipes projected a little, set in order and flowing, from which
the water rose in the finest thread. And then the most skilful founder, so as not to hinder one elbow
with the other, had the statues hide with the left hand, as a sign of modesty, the part worthy of
covering.
