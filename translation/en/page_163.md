# Page 163 — Chapter XIV

The following triumph was no less marvellous than the first. For it had its four turning wheels
entirely, and the spokes, and the hub, of dark agate, lovelily straggled with white little veins. Nor
certainly did King Pyrrhus carry such a one, with the nine Muses and Apollo in the midst playing,
stamped there by nature.

The axle and the form of it like the first, but the tablets were of dark blue eastern sapphire,
sprinkled with little sparks of gold, most welcome to magic, and by far most acceptable to Cupid in his
left hand.

In the right-hand tablet I beheld carved a notable matron who had given birth to two eggs, set in a
royal bed of a marvellous palace, with the midwives astonished, and many other matrons and Nymphs
standing by; out of one of which there came a little flame, and out of the other egg two most notable
stars.
