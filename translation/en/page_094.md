*How great and notable was the majesty of the Queen, and the condition of her dwelling and its
wonderful furnishing, Poliphilo tells as best he can. And the kind and affable receiving of him. And
how she marvelled at him. And how wonderful and splendid was the banquet, exceeding the reach of human
knowledge, and the place where it was held (without comparison), he somewhat describes.*

Being come to the first doorkeeper, she saw me not without astonishment; and having greeted her
seemly and duly reverenced her, and being given by her a familiar invitation to enter and a courteous
welcome — and in like manner by the following curtain-keepers — I saw a lofty portico, as long as the
compass of the palace. Whose golden vaulting was painted with green foliage, with distinct flowers and
entwined fronds and flying birds, choicely embroidered in mosaic work. The cleanest paving, like that
outside in the enclosed court. The walls crusted with sumptuous stones artfully set in fine work.

At the last curtain that matron Mnemosyne very affectionately urged me to doubt of nothing, but to show
myself a strenuous follower of the Queen's royal urging and wholesome counsel, and a persevering doer
of it, because then the outcome would come out pleasant without fail. And entry being particularly
granted — behold, there presented themselves to my eyes things rather divine than human. A most
ambitious furnishing in a stupendous and spacious court, beyond the palace and adjoining it, opposite
the other, perfect in its square.
