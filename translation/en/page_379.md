## Notes

**Confidence: high.** Three lines and the book-division. The Italian colophon
reads *FINIS DEL PRIMO LIBRO DILLA HYPNEROTOMACHIA DI POLIPHILO*.

- **The end of Book I.** With Polia settling to speak, Colonna closes the first
  and longer book — Poliphilo's dream of the journey through the wood, the portal,
  the palace, the doors, the triumphs and the fountain, ending at the tomb of
  Adonis where the woman he has won is about to tell her own side. Everything
  Dallington translated, and everything in this project's chapters XVII–XXIV,
  belongs to Book I.
- **What comes next.** Book II (chapters XXV onward, from p. 381) is Polia's
  first-person narrative: her ancient Trevisan lineage, the plague that made her
  vow herself to Diana, her cruelty to Poliphilo, his death in the temple, and her
  relenting. It is the shorter book and the mirror of the first — "two dreams told
  toward each other," as the awakening (ch. XXXVIII) will call the whole machine.
- **Page 380 is blank** in the source (a leaf-turn before Book II's title page),
  so there is no page_380 in the translation.
