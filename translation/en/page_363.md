# Page 363 — Chapter XXIII

And within the inner circuit of the most sacred fountain, clear of the join of
the sumptuous steps, flowering, purple Adonis sprang up among its kindred
leaves, purpling, kept just clear of the water; and on the left side likewise
*thelygonon* flowered with its pale grape-clusters, and on the right
*arsenogonon* — most admirable herbs, and always in flower. And in a circle
about the goddess certain small white doves fluttered, dutifully and decorously
ministering: dipping their golden bills in the most spotless waters, they
mysteriously bedewed the little Cytherean body, and the droplets showed on her
translucent flesh as nothing other than orient pearls set there. Close by stood
the nymph Peristeria, promptly and even boldly ready, attending with a whole
mind upon the services of Venus.

Likewise outside the fountain, upon the stone floor where Peristeria stood, at
the right side, three other divine girls stood naked in this manner,
inseparably embraced: two of them, Eurydomene and Eurymone, showing their
maiden faces toward us; the third, Eurymeduse, turned with her whitest
shoulders to us, her hidden hips veiled by the long outpouring of her most
golden hair. These were the gracious daughters and handmaids, prompt to the
effect of the goddess mother. And she held, lastly, in her divine hands an
opened oyster-shell packed with fresh spring roses, and in the other a burning
torch.

Now from the topmost step, upon which the columns stood, six little steps more
went down to the lip of the fountain, of dark agate; and the flat floor beneath
the water was wave-worked with the loveliest and most graceful milky veining,
variously spiralling — as delightful an object as could ever be set before the
senses. The fountain's water reached to the edge of the fourth step; the others
stood clear of it.

And above the upper step there sat at his ease a wanton figure in the shape of
a man, the god Nyctelius, whose look was that of a pert and striking girl, most
witty, the breast bared by the parting of his garment. His horned head was
bound with a vine-band of writhen tendrils, adorned with savoury grape-
clusters. He leaned upon two most swift tigers.

And on the left in the same manner sat, at her ease, a most beautiful and
nourishing matron, her broad and curled brow wreathed with a blond garland of
wheat-ears. That renowned lady rested upon two scaled serpents. And each of the
two held a spherical ball of some thin and yielding stuff in their laps; and
with these, at measured intervals, through a cunning and fateful nippled
opening, they dripped into the fountain, drop by drop, a most sweet, foaming

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The tableau is secure — every figure is identifiable —
but the botanical and mythological vocabulary is dense and two renderings are
interpretive: `amnice fronde` taken as "kindred leaves" (from *amnicus*,
river-kin, is also possible: "riverside leaves"), and the syntax of the oyster
and torch sentence, whose subject the page leaves implicit (it is Venus's
attribute pair; assigned to the goddess). **A reviewer should start with the
Adonis sentence.**

- **Adonis at the fountain's rim** — the purple flower is the anemone, sprung
  from Adonis's blood; planting him at Venus's water is the myth made
  horticulture. *Thelygonon* and *arsenogonon* are from Pliny: herbs supposed
  to cause the conception of girls and of boys respectively. At the fountain of
  generation, Colonna plants the two sexes as botany.
- **The Three Graces** — Eurydomene and Eurymone facing, Eurymeduse turned away:
  this is exactly the canonical pose of the ancient Three Graces group, two
  frontal and one from behind — Colonna is describing a statue type his readers
  knew. The names are his own coinages on Greek roots (wide-ruling, wide-
  wandering, wide-guarding).
- **Peristeria** — περιστερά, the dove: the dove-nymph attends the doves.
- **Nyctelius** — an epithet of Dionysus (of the night rites). Bacchus with the
  look of "a pert and striking girl" is the androgynous Dionysus of Hellenistic
  sculpture. His tigers are canonical.
- **The matron on the serpents** is Ceres — wheat crown, and the serpents that
  draw her chariot. **Bacchus and Ceres together at Venus's fountain enact the
  adage *sine Cerere et Baccho friget Venus* — without bread and wine, love
  grows cold.** And the annotator Hand E read precisely this pairing
  alchemically (`hp.db` folio_descriptions c6v): "Bacchus et Ceres id est sol
  [et luna]" — the two donors are Sol and Luna, and their dripping of "potent
  liquor" into the fountain is the feeding of the bath of the chemical wedding.
  The world's Chymica layer should quote this.
- **`orificio papillato`** — a nippled opening: the spheres are breasts in all
  but name, and the liquor is milk in all but name. Kept at the text's own
  level of euphemism.
