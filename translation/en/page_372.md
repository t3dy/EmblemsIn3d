The tessellated pavement was laid level over all the enclosed ground. Which was
on every side green without any bareness, and all tressed with tiniest fragrant
thyme, of which a single leaf did not stand above another, with a gratifying
denseness clothing right up to the banks of the fountain, springlike, with an
even shearing.

Here beneath I beheld a venerable work, which these divine nymphs and we devoutly
reverenced. A marvellous sepulchre, full of mystery, stood there appointed: five
feet in length, and in breadth as much raised — ten inches — excluding the socle,
with the little cornice, which was a quincunx; which tomb the nymphs said to be of
the hunter Adonis, slain in that place by the tusked boar. And in this place
likewise the holy Venus, coming naked out of this fountain, tore in those
rose-bushes her divine calf, to succour him who had been beaten by jealous Mars,
with a woeful and indignant face, and with anguish of soul.

This little history was seen perfectly carved on one long side of the sepulchre;
and the son Cupid then gathering the purple blood into an oyster-shell — adding
that that divine gore was laid up in that sepulchre, placed with the ash, with
every holy rite. Whereupon on the front of our entrance to the sepulchre it was
hollowed out in a circle to the size of the square, and then stopped up with the
precious stone jacinth, of a transparent vermilion colour, with great coruscation
of flaming splendour, burning unsteadily by the light set opposite, so that I
could scarcely fix my eyes on it for the wavering.

On the other long side of the sepulchre I saw likewise Adonis, carved with some
shepherd-hunters, among some little shrubs, with dogs and the dead boar, and he
slain by it; and Venus falling sorrowfully weeping into the pitiful embraces of
three half-swooning nymphs, clothed in finest cloth, weeping together with the
goddess; and the son, with a little bundle of roses, I saw wiping the maternal
eyes, tearful with flowing tears.

There, between the one sex and the other, in a garland of myrtle, I saw thus
inscribed: **IMPURA SUAVITAS**. In like manner on the other little history, in
Greek, was thus expressed: **ΑΔΩΝΙΑ**. So exquisitely feigned in sculpture did all
these things present themselves, that I was moved to a sweetness of pity.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The two carved histories on the tomb are secure and
vivid; the measurements of the sepulchre (`pedi quinarii`, `dextante`, the
quincunx cornice) are a technical vocabulary rendered by standard equivalents.

- **The tomb of Adonis**, the goal of chapter XXIV. The two long reliefs tell the
  myth: on one side Venus rushing naked from the fountain and tearing her calf on
  the thorns to reach the dying Adonis, Cupid gathering the blood into an
  oyster-shell (from which the anemone springs — cf. p. 363, Adonis flowering at
  the fountain's rim); on the other, the boar-hunt and Venus mourning in the arms
  of three nymphs. The tomb is thus the origin-story of the flower that grows at
  the fountain of the previous chapter — Colonna closes the loop.
- **the jacinth glowing in the tomb-front** — a red transparent stone set over a
  lamp, so the tomb appears to hold a burning coal: the blood of Adonis kept
  alight. A theatrical relic-effect, and the same glowing-stone trick as the
  Medusa-door's viper eyes (docs/HP_SOURCEBOOK.md §1).
- **IMPURA SUAVITAS** — "impure sweetness," between the sexes, in a myrtle garland
  (Venus's plant): a motto on the double-edge of desire, love as sweetness that is
  not clean — the shadow-side of the *coniunctio* just celebrated. **ΑΔΩΝΙΑ** — the
  Adonia, the festival of mourning for Adonis.
- **`dextante`, `septuncio`, `semiasside`, quincunx** — Roman fractional measures
  (ten-twelfths, seven-twelfths, a half-as, five-twelfths). Colonna measures even
  a tomb in the duodecimal *as*. Rendered as the fractions; a reviewer with the
  facsimile could confirm the exact values.
