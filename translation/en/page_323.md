triangle caused by the rounding and by the angles of the inner bands, was for each a little
circle of the herbaceous colouring of those enclosing; the excluded, of butcher's-broom. In

myrtle, with a two-foot stalk, and of the rest.

The other quadrangle — bands, circle, as is now recounted. In the circling were two birds:
on the one part an Eagle, and on the other, facing, a Pheasant, with beak straight to beak.
Upon the lips of a vase they footed. Which had a scrap of a foot-stalk, and from the back
the wings of both raised. Between the volutes of the bands, on the eagle's part, in the

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The first eagle-inscription completes — **ALES MAGNA DICATA OPTIMO
IOVI** ("A great bird, dedicated to Jupiter the Best") — read from the letters planted in the
beds; then its full planting-key (rue bands, thyme eagle, marjoram letters, myrtle-sphere
finials); then a second roundel with an **eagle facing a pheasant** on a vase, whose beds
begin spelling a second motto (SUP-ERN-AE… LIT-IS… BENI-GNI-TAS). All secure; letters read
from the woodcut.

- **The garden spells its dedication.** The inscribed parterre reads **ALES MAGNA DICATA
  OPTIMO IOVI** around a spread-winged eagle — the whole ring is a living dedicatory
  inscription to Jupiter, the herbs themselves forming the majuscules. The world's inscribed
  ring can carry this exact text.
- **the second roundel: eagle vs. pheasant** — a heraldic confrontation (Jupiter's eagle
  faces Juno's bird) on a shared vase, its beds spelling the motto completed on p. 324:
  *SUPERNAE ALITIS BENIGNITAS*.
- **"forbidden to proceed to the destiny of fecund nature"** — the herbs are kept clipped so
  they never run to seed and blur the letters: horticulture arrested at the point of legibility.
  Colonna's image of art holding nature at the exact instant of perfection.
- The planting-keys here are as build-ready as pp. 320–321: each glyph and field named by species.
