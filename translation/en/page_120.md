If the sound held one measure, those eight of one kind used that measure in passing to another square.
They could not go back, save deservedly for having climbed untouched upon the line of the squares where
the King had his seat; nor go straight forward, except by a diagonal line.

A secretary and a knight passed three squares in one measure: the secretary by a diagonal line, the
knight by two equal-sided straight ones and one turning off the line; and they could move to every
side. The keepers of the citadel could pass straight and freely over many squares. So that in one
measure they could run over three, four, or five squares, keeping the measure, and hastening their
step.

The King could climb upon whatever square was not blocked, or held by a garrison — indeed he may take
it; and that square is forbidden him where others may climb, and if the case be fitting he must yield,
with a warning going before. But the Queen upon every square of her own colour, where she first set her
seat. And it is well that she always follow her husband closely on every side.

Whenever the officers of the one or the other King found one of the other without keeping and garrison,
they made him prisoner, and both kissing one another, the vanquished went out.

In this manner they made a most famous game in a most elegant dance, dancing and merrily playing with
the measure of the sound, in such a way that the silver one remained the winner, with cheerfulness,
solace and applause. This solemn festival lasted, by its contests, flights and garrisons, for the space
of an hour, with such measured circlings, reverences and pauses, and modest stayings, that so great a
delight came over me that I not undeservedly thought myself carried up to the supreme delights of the
highest Olympus, and to a newest happiness.

The first game in dance being ended, all returned again to their appointed square. And in the same
manner as they had done at first, so the second time, having all alike returned in order to their
places, the players tightening the measure of the time, so the movements and gestures of the playing
dancers moved more busily — but keeping the measure with the sound, in so most apt a manner and with so
approved a gesturing and art that it was not fitting to say anything.
