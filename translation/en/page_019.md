Resting upon my left side, lying there, I drew in the fresh airs with my thinned spirits, more
constantly with my parted lips than the weary Stag that has been driven and bitten in the flanks by
biting and fierce hounds, and wounded in the breast by the arrow, and that at the last, propping his
heavy head with its branching horns upon his weakened back and no longer able to stand, sinks down
dying and weary upon his buckling knees.

Whence, lying in this like agony, I ran over anxiously in my mind the most tangled quarrels of
infirm fortune, and the enchantments of the maleficent Circe — whether by chance I had been knotted
into her verses, or the magic wheel had been used against me. To these and so many attendant terrors:
alas then, where could I find here, among herbs so various, the Mercurial Moly with its black root,
for my help and my medicine? Then I said: this is not that; but what is it, if not a malign deferring
of the death I long for?

Standing thus in these pernicious agitations, my powers dwindled little by little, and I could
think of no other Safety than to draw in and receive frequently and diligently the fresh airs, and,
having warmed them in my breast where a little vital heat still gathered and beat, to breathe them
out again through my parched throat. Finding myself therefore in no other way than half alive, for a
last refreshment I took the moist dew-laden leaves kept beneath the leafy oak, and offered them to my
pale and harsh lips, sucking them dry with the greediest avidity, licking at them ravenously, and
cooling a little my thirsty palate. Desiring then that Hypsipyle might show me too the spring of
Langia, as she did to the Greeks. For I anxiously suspected that perhaps in the vast wood I had been
bitten unawares by the serpent Dipsas, so unbearable was my thirst.

At last, renouncing and proscribing this wearisome life, whatever might come of it, astonished and
estranged by the gravest cogitations, and reeling as though in madness, once again beneath this
oaken shade, wanton with the spreading darkness of its branches, I was overcome by an overpowering
sleep; and the sweet drowsiness being spread through my limbs, it seemed to me that I slept again.
