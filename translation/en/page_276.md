## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** Cupid finishes his invitation (only he, as "navarch and ferryman," may carry them to Cythera);
the lovers **board the boat**; and the vessel and its **six rowing-nymphs** are described — an *exeres* (six-oar) coated
not in pitch but in a **fragrant balm** (benzoin, ladanum, musk, amber, civet, storax) and built of sandalwood and
aloewood, gold-nailed and gem-studded, its ivory oars in gold rowlocks; the six maidens dressed in wind-revealing sheer
cloth, blond and raven-haired. All secure.

- **The boat to Cythera.** Cupid's six-oared vessel, smeared not with tar but with solid perfume and built of scented
  woods, gold-nailed and gemmed, rowed by six nymphs — this is the boat of the crossing (chapter XX, already translated:
  the *Amor vincit omnia* standard, p. 284; the six singing nymphs, p. 285). Here is its full material description: an
  aromatic, jewelled sandalwood skiff. A vivid, buildable ferry for the world's digit-0 sailing.
- **only Cupid may ferry them.** The god insists none can reach "the maternal realm and destined island" without him as
  pilot: Cythera is reachable only under Love's own conduct. A narrative gating the world honours (the crossing is Cupid-
  led). It also explains why the boat of ch. XX carries Cupid at the prow (p. 285).
- **the six nymphs — the rowers who will sing.** These are the six *nautical nymphs* who rest the oars and sing in
  chapter XX; here they are introduced (blond and raven, wind-revealed, pearl-bound). Their number (six) matches the
  six-voice part-song of p. 285. The world's crossing can carry both the boat and its six singer-rowers from these pages.
- **fragrance as material** — the hull *is* perfume (cf. the perfumed pavements of Cythera, p. 355): Colonna builds the
  vessel of scent and precious wood, so it "renders the heart exhilarant." The crossing is sensory before it is musical.
