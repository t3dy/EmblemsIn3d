# Page 235 — Chapter XVIII

And there, weighingly, the high Priestess, with special majesty, thus familiarly said to us: "My children, now
by me expiated and blessed, to your amorous undertaking and voyage go. I pray, again, that the divine mother
show herself auspicious and affable, and to your amorous conceits, intents, and cases, be pitiful, favourable,
and propitious. And, in the present, encloister the profound and frequent sighs; the lamentings postpone and
leave; put to flight every mourning. For already, by my instance, this present hour will be to you salutary and
favourable. Then, to these my salubrious admonitions and profitable commands let your soul attend, so that she,
with her pious and sweet affect, grant you happy success."

After the sacred monitress with bland speech had said (this), we — immortal thanks saying — from all reverently
took leave, with sweetest and mutual salutes, indicating (by) the wet face that our departure almost troubled
them. Nonetheless, bidding farewell, out of the magnificent and superb temple we went; and, my
*chrysocari* (golden-graced) Polia admonished of our way and itinerary, finally we departed.

O comitation so long desired, so most-pleasing, and happy, and prosperous exit of the transacted sadnesses! Now
my heart, breathed-upon with internal sweetness, and the noxious fire perfused with celestial dew, no longer
wavers me — but firmly and evidently this is my so most-desired Polia. My tutelary goddess. The genius of my
heart. To whom, well-deserving, due gratulation I owe, of so great her service to the divine mother, and of so
great a showing of love in this most-pleasant comitation. These and like words low-voiced saying, Polia, in that
instant aware of my depressed speech, regarded me with two festive eyes kindled with love — more clear than most-
lucid stars, without the horned Cynthia, in the serene sky coruscating; not otherwise than as the ignited steel
upon the anvil, malleated, scintillates, so, in my smitten breast, those (eyes) frequently scintillated. And, in
speaking, comely, with angelic accents, with the purpling mouth (hiding-place of every fragrance, apothecary of
oriental pearls, most-rich seminary of enucleated and sweet little words), timely, soothing, she delineated
(dissolved) all my mental inquietude. A speech, without doubt, to make pleasant the terrific aspect of Medusa,
and to mitigate the horrendous atrocity of the inflamed Mars, and to despoil him of his bloody armours; to steal
the most-beautiful Ganymede from the hooked talons of the supreme bird; and, from tenderness, to burst and
minutely cleave into tiniest fragments the hardest marbles, and whetstones, and crags, and roughest rocks, and
abrupt (cliffs) of Persia, and of the pathless and cloudy Mount Atlas in the Ocean-part; and, quieting, to tame,
or make gentle, the most-savage beasts of Libya; and to vivify every dusty and ashen dead. Thus she said:

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The **priestess's blessing** and the lovers' tearful departure from the temple; then
Poliphilo's joyful certainty ("firmly and evidently this is my Polia, my tutelary goddess"), and a great praise
of Polia's speech — soft enough to soothe Medusa and Mars, cleave marble, tame beasts and revive the dead. All
secure; the transition out of the temple.

- **The priestess sends them to love.** Her blessing — "go to your amorous voyage… leave the sighs, put to
  flight every mourning" — releases the lovers from grief into joy: the temple has done its work, and love is
  now sanctioned and light. It sets up the coming of the *Polyandrion* (p. 236) as a deliberate contrast — from
  the blessed temple of the living to the ruined tomb of those whom love destroyed.
- **Polia confirmed, finally and fully.** After a whole book of doubt, Poliphilo declares without wavering that
  this is Polia, "my tutelary goddess, the genius of my heart." The recognition (begun p. 216, sealed p. 218) is
  now his settled certainty. The anvil-spark simile for her love-kindled eyes is one of the book's vivid images.
- **the praise of Polia's speech** — a virtuoso surpassing-catalogue: her words could gentle Medusa, disarm
  Mars, steal Ganymede back, shatter marble, tame Libyan beasts, revive the dead. Hyperbole as devotion; the
  register the "beautiful and appropriate" passages the project prizes. Her mouth as "apothecary of pearls,
  seminary of sweet words" is characteristic Colonna.
- The ruined Polyandrion is reached on p. 236.
