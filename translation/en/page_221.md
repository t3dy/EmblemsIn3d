# Page 221 — Chapter XVII

The demonstration of which Altar, succinctly saying, I beheld a conspicuous device of unwonted make. The
lowest of the said, upon the stepped and marble footing, was a round little slab. Upon which undulated an
auricular foliage, most with polish jagged, and choicely cauliculate, ending its point at a little cord, or
knot, or fillet, contained upon the little slab. And likewise, at the birth of the encompassing foliage,
sat above another little cord; and between this and another was pressed a *trochlea* (pulley-moulding)
moderately channelled, and then a little cornice. Upon the bracket, or plane, of the said Trochlea, over-
stood another regulated rotundity; then, somewhat gulleted, it contracted itself toward the flat and
unencumbered surface. In the middle part of which stood forth a striated stylus, more stretched in the lower
part, upon the flat, with a proportioned base. The diameter, then, of the inner rotundity of this stylus
divided: one partition was consigned to the encompassing projection; the upper head had two portions, with
turned gulets, and the lowest also duly reserved.

The aforesaid stalk covered an inverted flat rotundity, turning in projection as much as the extreme outlet
of the lips of the subjacent Trochlea, ornated in the upper part, in the extreme circuit, in a sloped
demonstration, with a Sima of noteworthy foliage, from an egregious little cornice most-polishedly being
born. In the circled content, then, of which little cornice, beautifully occupied the opening of an elegant
flower, in balustered lineament fashioned, with the calyxed lips over the flat lambent, and quadripartite
into most-pleasant acanthine foliage, gaping. Under which, in the jagged discrepancy, subsided another
leaf, artfully carved. Upon the point of which, after the due lineaments, egregiously rounded a knot of
exquisite expression. To which, fixed, set, dilatedly stood forth an antiquarian platter of purest gold,
with the lips broadish, and a little lacunate (hollowed). In the flat rim of the lips, alternately, stood
forth incomparable Diamonds and Carbuncles, with outstanding pyramidal fashioning, in the circuit
marvellously disposed, of incredible thickness. Let yield here the *Scyphus* of most-strong Hercules, the
*Cantharus* of pleasant Bacchus, and the *Carchesium* dedicated to immortal Jove.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** A dense architectural description of the **central altar of the Adytum** — a turned,
moulded pedestal (slab, auricular foliage, pulley-moulding, cornice, fluted stem) rising to an inverted
dish carved with acanthus and a balustered flower, crowned by a **golden platter rimmed with pyramidal
diamonds and carbuncles**, surpassing the famous cups of Hercules, Bacchus and Jove. All secure; a
goldsmith's-and-architect's altar, described moulding by moulding.

- **The altar-of-the-flame, buildable.** Colonna gives the altar as a turned column of mouldings topped by a
  gold offering-dish set with alternating diamonds and carbuncles (clear and red fire-stones) around the rim
  — on which the "divine flame" (p. 220) burns. A precise, renderable centrepiece for the world's sanctuary:
  a jewelled gold fire-dish on a carved plinth.
- **the acanthus-and-flower capital** — the altar's head opens as a balustered flower with four-part acanthus
  leaves "lambent over the flat": the whole object is vegetal-architectural, of a piece with the temple's
  living ornament (the vine-dome, p. 201; the acanthus-girls, p. 207).
- **surpassing the cups of the gods** — the *scyphus* of Hercules, the *cantharus* of Bacchus, the
  *carchesium* of Jove: the three most-famous mythological drinking-vessels, all exceeded by this altar-dish.
  The surpassing-topos, on sacred vessels (cf. the surpassed cups of Bassus and Antony, p. 354).
- **the ornament is the point.** This is one of the book's purest passages of *ekphrasis-as-inventory*:
  Colonna dwells on the altar's profile as lovingly as on the whole temple, because for him the object's
  design *is* its meaning. Rendered with the technical mouldings (trochlea, sima, cauliculate) kept.
