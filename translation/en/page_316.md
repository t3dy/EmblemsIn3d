# Page 316 — Chapter XXI

From this much-admired colonnade up to the following steps, it was levelled into a
circling marble path, six feet in breadth. Where immediately began another seven steps
to climb, with the aforesaid work, measure, and material, and colouring — and all this
was kept entire in what follows.

Upon the topmost was a box-like excavation, four feet in opening. The hollow of which
was sufficiently deep, and so in those that follow. In which grew a hedge of box, of a
glassy sheen, most pleasingly greening. And, level with the bridges and roads, I beheld
a tower of the said greenery, raised nine feet, and five wide, with an open door
gaping three feet, and six high, and so the following ones.

This first hedge, of three feet's thickness, and six high — and so those that follow —
was seen of densest foliage, and was so disposed of the feathering of its own
tree-growth. Between one tower and the other I saw excellently made a triumph, with
horses drawing a chariot, and preceding the one who triumphs certain sword-bearing
soldiers, and with triumphal spears artfully compacted, varying most beautifully the
works. In another space between towers stood forth a naval Enyo. Between two other
towers, a fleet-battle on land. Between two others, a hunt, and antique fables of love.
With most diligent relief, and most exquisite fashioning. Such an order going round,
varying in its sequences.

Within this first rampart, after an encompassing path — like the lower one, between the
colonnade and the steps, of marvellous tessellation, which offered itself to great
admiration, and an excessively delightful artifice, without doubt to weary all human
sight and sense. Which, at first aspect, I readily judged to be *charaine* carpets laid
out and spread flat upon the level, with all manners of colouring, which for such a
show it fittingly served to express — in the manner of a gracious painting, conducted
into more varied and manifold groupings and figures and signs, with the fit diversity
of colourings, of little pot-herbs, to the required distinction of the feigned work.
Some full of colour, others of dark colouring, some middling; some clearer and gayer,
some leek-green, others of a pale verdure, some less so, and of a somewhat reddish
colouring, with a most pleasant reconciling. The principal figures, containing in
themselves manifold designs, were: between two rhombs, a circle; and a rhomboid
between two circles, alternating continuously in a ring — except in that part where the
ways were providently left to intercalate. Which paths always passed between two
uniform figures.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The second seven-step flight, the box-hedge rampart with its
topiary towers and — between the towers — topiary *triumphs* (a triumphal chariot, a
sea-battle, a land-battle, a hunt, love-fables) clipped in living box; then the
carpet-like mosaic of coloured pot-herbs (rhombs and circles) that carpets the ring.
All secure.

- **The topiary triumphs — Cythera's living reliefs.** Between the box towers,
  Colonna sets *triumphs shorn in living box*: a triumphal chariot with sword-bearing
  soldiers, a naval battle (Enyo, war-goddess), a land-battle, a hunt, and love-fables
  — the same triumph-subjects as the four Triumphs of ch. XIV, but here rendered in
  clipped greenery. A future terrace pass could render these as hedge-relief panels
  between the box towers.
- **the box-hedge rampart, in numbers** — hedge three feet thick and six high; towers
  nine feet high, five wide, doorway three by six. Concrete dimensions for the world's
  terrace hedges and their tower-gates.
- **the *charaine*-carpet mosaic of pot-herbs** — the ring between colonnade and steps
  is planted as a living mosaic of coloured herbs (leek-green, pale, reddish) in a
  repeating pattern of rhombs-and-circles, so exact it looks like laid carpets. This
  is the *broderie* parterre in its earliest literary form — the world could carry it
  as a patterned ground-texture on the terrace rings.
- **the sevenfold terraces confirmed** — a second flight of seven steps, "and all this
  kept entire in what follows": the terraces repeat, ring within ring, up to the
  central theatre.
