# Page 301 — Chapter XXI

In the middle intersit of the levelled, most solid inlay of the peristyle was a
fountain, founded within a rounding somewhat shell-like hollowed, from the centre
of which rose an inverted baluster, two feet. Above sat a platter, the gap of its
opening having by diameter four feet; in the very middle of which, three straddling
tails of three golden hydras, through the bottom outlandishly three-forked, then
tightly intertwining in a most beautiful knot, divided by the belly one from the
other in a bag-like form, with an arching discrepancy, and in their throats
snake-wise knotted, with the head three-forked apart, vomiting the most fragrant
water into the shell — equally sustained upright, two feet, a vase of ovate figure.
Of which, in the part above the surface, were fixed eight little gold pipes, from
which a most slender thread of water leapt forth. And through the gaps or intervals
of the box columns it issued, dewily moistening the whole meadow. The interstice of
which watch-tower was clear and pervious. The stone-work was all of finest jasper,
reddening and lustrous, adulterated drop-wise with infinite spots of manifold
colouring, with elegant and exquisite carving at the fitting places.

Now in each corner of the quadrangled meadow, with a proportioned distance from the
little chests, was disposed a platform of four steps, in square form. The first
step, most solid, in front appeared two feet; in the flat, or mouth, a side of a
foot and a half; and they were hollow platforms. Then followed the other above, in
graded order, tall as the open breadth of the first; and so the third, and so the
fourth. In the first, fragrant herb germinated; so in the following. Here, then,
were crisp and tiniest basils, citron-scented and chervils, which did not overpass
the middle front of the step, the fronds kept even; the like stood uniform
throughout. The other had fragrant and small thyme, gracious to the honey-making
bees. In the third, the small and bitter *glyciacon*, or nectary, or southernwood,
such as the Sicilian does not offer. In the topmost, Celtic spikenard, of pleasing
odour.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The central hydra-fountain and the four-step herb platforms
are secure in their scheme; one anatomical clause of the three intertwined golden
hydras is dense but the image (three snakes knotted, spouting from their three
forked heads into a shell basin) is clear.

- **The hydra-fountain at the peristyle's centre** — a shell basin holding a
  jasper vase supported by three golden many-tailed hydras knotted together,
  spouting water from their forked throats, with eight little gold pipes above
  throwing fine jets that dew the whole meadow. This is the true fountain at the
  heart of each first-ring meadow, beneath the box-topiary canopy of the previous
  pages.
- **the four-step herb platforms at the corners** — stepped square planters, each
  step a different fragrant herb: basil and chervil, thyme "gracious to the
  honey-making bees," southernwood, and Celtic spikenard at the top. A scented
  garden in miniature at every meadow-corner, kept shorn even. The whole *prati*
  ring is, on this evidence, as much a garden of *smell* as of sight — which
  matches Segre's reading of the inner gardens as organised by fragrance.
- **`glyciacon`** — an obscure herb-name (perhaps from γλυκύς, sweet); glossed by
  Colonna himself as "nectary, or southernwood," so the identification is his.
- The catalogue of the *prati*'s planting continues on the following pages.
