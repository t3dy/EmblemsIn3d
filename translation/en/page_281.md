Navigating, then, with the light and sweet appulse of the outstanding breezes, I, with scrutinising and perspicacious and
inconstant regard — intently, not being able to affirm (fix on), now the one, now the other speculating — but of this I
could not discern, nor delimit, the disparity of the two (Cupid and Polia), save (in) the divinity. And there, strongly by
an inexcogitable sweetness compelled, to both I freely repudiated (surrendered) my soul: commending it to the power of one
(Cupid), who fittingly could condone to it his amorous salvation; and to the will of the notable Polia, that she too
benignly should lend her consent. But yet, undoubtedly, I esteemed, confident and reliant, that to such majestic presence
and venerable aspect no other effect and exit could breathe than ardent love, and that she now could no more flee her
triumphal boat, nor retreat; and much more I hoped for the sequel of my extreme desire, by the saying of the amorous
hieroglyphs of the fluttering standard (*Amor vincit omnia*) of this glorious little boat of the divine and most-potent
Cupid — where, exulting to be conducted into such dignification, most-blessed, and glorying to be adorned with so excellent
a companion and amorous reciprocation (that Apollo cannot so glory of the ornament of his quiver and cithara of Peneian
fronds, nor Polycrates of the recovery of his ring, nor the Great Alexander of the attained victories and elevated trophies)
as I judged myself glorious, finding myself in so great a triumph. Nonetheless, beyond belief marvelling, by what mode, or

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** Poliphilo's **rapture at possessing Polia**, in a great surpassing-catalogue (Endymion, Paris,
Jason, Theseus, Antony, Apelles — none gloried in their loves as he in Polia); his surrender of his soul "to both" Cupid
and Polia; his confidence that the *Amor vincit omnia* standard guarantees her love in return; and the beginning of the
meditation on Love's cosmic fire. All secure; the emotional summit of the crossing.

- **The surpassing-catalogue of lovers.** Every famous love — Endymion's moon, Paris's Helen, Jason's Medea, Theseus's
  Ariadne, Antony's Cleopatra, Apelles's Campaspe — is exceeded by Poliphilo's joy in Polia. Colonna's habitual topos, here
  at its fullest, marking the crossing as the fulfilment of the whole quest. The *Amor vincit omnia* standard (p. 284) is
  named as the pledge that seals it.
- **"the disparity of the two, save in divinity."** Poliphilo cannot tell Cupid and Polia apart except that one is a god:
  Polia is all but divine to him. He surrenders his soul to both — to the god's power and the beloved's will. The love-story
  and the theophany merge on the boat.
- **the reciprocation hoped and assured** — he trusts that "no other effect can breathe" from such presence "than ardent
  love," and that Polia can "no more flee." The crossing carries the certainty the temple-rite promised (p. 218): the union
  is now secure, borne by Love's own boat to Love's own island.
- The meditation on Love's fire — which "inflames the universe" — completes on p. 282, ending chapter XIX.
