## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The famous **lament for lost architecture** ("O unhappy times… how is so beautiful
an invention ignored by the moderns"), then the temple's centrepiece: a **well-head over a cistern ringed
by carved alabaster nymphs**, above it the metal vine-dome closing on a cast-bronze **Medusa-head** at the
apex, from whose mouth a **golden knotwork chain** descends to a suspended lamp-plate held by four chains
and four monster-girls. All secure; the Medusa-and-hanging-lamp is the temple's inner focus.

- **The manifesto for antique architecture.** The page opens with the Hypnerotomachia's most-quoted
  cultural lament — that Colonna's own age has forgotten the ancients' mastery of the orders and of marble-
  polishing (*xesturgy*). It is the theoretical heart of the whole temple-ekphrasis: the building is an
  argument that classical architecture must be recovered. Worth surfacing as the book's architectural creed.
- **The centre of the temple: well, Medusa, hanging lamp.** At the exact centre is the cistern's well-head,
  ringed by flying-veiled alabaster nymphs; overhead the bronze vine-dome converges on a **Medusa-head** at
  the oculus, from whose mouth a golden chain drops a suspended lamp-plate borne by four monstrous girls.
  This vertical axis — well below, Medusa above, lamp between — is the temple's focus, answering Cythera's
  fountain-on-obsidian (ch. XXII). A spectacular, buildable centrepiece for the world's temple.
- **the Medusa at the oculus** — cast in the same bronze as the vine-dome, her serpents merging with the
  vine: the apotropaic gorgon guarding the sacred well, and the point where the golden chain (and its lamp)
  hangs. Colonna fuses the dome's vine with the myth's snakes.
- **"in default only of the spirit"** — the carved nymphs lack only breath to be alive: the standard topos
  for supreme sculpture (cf. the Cythera reliefs), here for the alabaster well-chorus.
