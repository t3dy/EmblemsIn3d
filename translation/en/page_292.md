# Page 292 — Chapter XXI

With these amorous triumphs, and the euphonious voices of the sailor-girls, and
beyond by the sea-deities, the serene air was filled, and the cerulean deep, with
confused clamours, and a multitude resounding — the waves, and feasts, and dances,
and especial gesticulation and festive gladness, and devout veneration — as,
gliding to the most pleasant place, we put in. So kindly and most gratifying, so
delightful and beautiful, offering itself to the senses with singular tree-set
ornament, as ever the most excellent and voluptuous thing could be beheld with the
eyes. For every fertile tongue would accuse itself of poverty of expression; and
by such a likeness the things commemorated before would be too unbecoming and
abusive a comparison to this. For beyond estimation it was a most solacing place,
and of delights — all planted and adorned as a kitchen-garden and herb-garden, and
a fertile orchard, and a pleasant pleasure-ground, and a gracious tree-plot, and a
most delightful shrubbery. Which was a place not of trackless and disused
mountains, every roughness done away, but flattened and even right up to the
circling steps toward the marvellous theatre at its heart. The trees were of most
sweet odour, most fertile of yield, of the widest spread of branches. A garden of
incomparable delight, affluent, of the largest fruitfulness, of most delightful
flowers, and stored, free of impediments, and of ambushes all adorned, of flowing
fountains and fresh rivulets. The sky not rigid, but temperate, most wide, clear
and bright; not of the horrid shades of infernal places; immune from variable and
inconstant weather that might offend with the ambushes of tainted winds harassing,
nor with troublesome and wintry frosts; nor the swelter of an importunate sun, nor
an invaded and torrid place of the dryness of heat; nor scorched with horrid
freezing. But all springlike and salubrious — such as the air is not to the
Egyptians who look to Libya — of long health and salubrity, and destined for
eternity. A place planted with green things, of admirable density of leafy trees,
of most gratifying show, with most lovely presentation of greenery; and everywhere
through the liquid air an incredible breathing of flowery fragrance, with all the
grassy ground steeped in fresh dew, and flowering meadows; and beyond the imagining
of every pleasure, teeming with natural goods — with coloured fruits among the
perennial green foliage, in stable cohesion and consent, with paths marked out
among the plants, and arched over with manifold roses. Let the watered and
tree-set field of Themiscyra yield place here.

Wherefore I judge it more than arduous, and reckon it difficult, to wish — even
with keen wit — to narrate it. But as much as my grasping, retentive, yet arid
memory, set upon its praise, shall recall to me, so much briefly I will adapt
myself to write.

This holy place, dedicated to nature — witty to mortals, and pitiable — nursling
of the gods and their station, and lodging of the blessed spirits, encircled (as I
could rightly conjecture) three miliaria; and on every side an interflowing of

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** A great ekphrastic hymn to the island, secure in sense;
the difficulty is only its unbroken length and the piled Latinate garden-terms.
This page carries the measurement on which the whole reconstruction of Cythera
rests.

- **"encircled three miliaria"** — the sentence Segre builds the island's plan
  from (GARDENS.md §5): Cythera is a perfect circle three *miliaria* (Roman miles)
  in circumference, and this is the figure that scales the whole reconstruction we
  built. The description of the concentric rings begins here and runs for the next
  several pages.
- **"flattened and even right up to the circling steps toward the marvellous
  theatre at its heart"** — confirms the island's inward rise to the theatre of
  Venus, the terraced approach the world's Cythera renders.
- **"a place not of trackless mountains"** — Colonna states outright that first
  nature (wilderness, mountains) is *excluded* from Cythera, exactly as Hunt reads
  it: the island is pure third nature, garden art, with no share of the wild.
- **Themiscyra** — the plain of the Amazons by the river Thermodon, a classical
  byword for a rich watered field; Cythera outdoes it. The catalogue of garden-
  types (*horto olitorio, herbario, pomario, viridario, arborario, arbustario*) is
  the same taxonomy Segre maps onto the concentric rings.
