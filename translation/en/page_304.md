# Page 304 — Chapter XXI

In the corners also of the present, and second, meadow toward the centre, as in the
first were constituted the chests of four steps, with all the regulation, dimension,
and placing that the other platforms of the first meadow have — except of the stone,
which was of blackest amber, or *ambrum*. Never did the Phaethontides beside
Eridanus pour out such in their weeping, nor would such be found in the Electrid
isles, nor does the temple of Ammon produce such — of mirror-smoothness, drawing the
straw when rubbed. Which chests were figured circular.

In the lowest germinated the fragrant cassia; in the next grew odoriferous nard. In
the third was Mint, the nymph, betokening the fierce hatred of Proserpina. In the
fourth grew the unlucky royal Amaracus, extinguished amid his own odour — and such
Cyprus does not produce.

In the upper middle likewise, planted, sat a fruit-tree for each. But dissimilar in
fruit, and in topiary form, to the first. For [?…] they were four, of a spherical
figure most beautifully; they produced kinds of pears — one, musk pears; the other,
Crustumian; the third, [?…] and succulent Syrian; the last, tenderest little
*curmunduli*. [?…] in this secondary order of meadows, the others [?…] varied the
species of the fruit, with [?…] colour, with most gratifying odour, and with [?…]
taste. The ground alone clothed with fragrant and tiniest [?…], and the platforms
of [?…] simples.

## Notes

**Confidence: low.** The corner herb-and-fruit scheme of the second ring is
recoverable, but **the source transcription of this page is itself damaged**: the
Wikisource text carries several `{{Pt||}}` markers and dropped words in the last two
paragraphs (marked `[?…]` in the translation), where the fruit-catalogue and the
closing sentence have lost material. **A reviewer must restore this page from the
facsimile before it can be trusted; the gaps are the transcription's, not the
translation's.**

- **The corner planters of the second ring** repeat the first ring's four-step
  form, but faced in **black amber** rather than jasper, and planted with the four
  fragrant herbs: cassia, nard, mint, and marjoram (*amaracus*). Each carries a
  myth — **Mint** was the nymph Minthe, trampled by jealous Proserpina and turned to
  the herb; **Amaracus** a royal youth "extinguished amid his own odour," turned to
  marjoram. The scented-and-storied planting continues.
- **the black amber** — *electrum/succinum*, amber, "which draws the straw when
  rubbed" (its static charge, straight from Pliny) and wept by the **Phaethontides**
  (Phaethon's sisters, turned to poplars weeping amber into the river Eridanus/Po).
  The Electrid isles were the legendary amber-islands.
- **the pear varieties** — musk, Crustumian, Syrian, *curmunduli*: the second order
  of the *prati* is pears where the first was apples, matching Segre's sequence
  (apples, then pears, then plums, GARDENS.md §5). The exact list is damaged in the
  source.
- This page and p. 300, 303 are the low-water mark of the chapter's legibility; the
  rest of XXI is cleaner.
