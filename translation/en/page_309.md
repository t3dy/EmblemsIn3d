find for turning columns. A work certainly sumptuous, proud, precious, and elegant.

The Ionic columns with their capitals, with the echini within the lanceolate — which,
clothed with a bark-like enwrapping, shone with bases of finest gold, such as the
gold-bearing Tagus does not produce in Hesperia, nor Padus in Latium, nor Hebrus in
Thrace, nor Pactolus in Asia, nor Ganges in India. The frieze was adorned with antique
foliage convolved on itself, egregiously carved. And the lattices hedged between the
little altars were of finest electrum — such as was not that of which, in the form of
a breast, in the isle of Lindos in the temple of Minerva, was dedicated by Helen.

Upon the flat of the little wall, for each interval between one column and the other,
was set an ancient vase of fitting work to the rest recounted, with most cunning
polish, varied of stone and of colour — of *sphragis*, of *colorites*, of chalcedony,
of *coaspites*, of agate, and of many other precious and most gratifying stones — in
its burnish simulating any object most cunningly, most polished and expressed, with
lineaments I thought not human. Out of which were seen most admirable simples and
plantlets, heaped in varied topiary shaping. Here marjoram, the aromatic and crisp
*sentonica*, southernwood, myrtles, and others than which no more delightful object
could be offered to the content of the eyes.

From the aforesaid peristyle to the banks, right up to a river, the nearest ground
was occupied, grassy, with dewy grass. Here, with gracious dispensation, was the
flowering *xiphion*, lavenders, marjorams, fleabane, white marjoram, Mint the nymph —
who received the beautiful gift from Pluto — and there flowered also the tears of
Helen, called *helenion*, salubrious to the face, and of the holy Mother
Conciliatrix; and innumerable other most celebrated plantlets, aromatic, and of most
acceptable fragrance; and the whitening, and cerulean hyacinths, and purple — such as
in Gaul is not produced.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The stone-catalogue of the columns, the goldsmith
comparisons, and the flower-planting down to the riverbank are secure; the mineral
names are Pliny's, several in Colonna's Greek forms.

- **The columns' seven stones**, in order: *hexacontalithos* (green, sixty-coloured),
  *hieracites* (hawk-black), *galactites* (milk-white), chrysoprase, and *atizoe*
  (silvery) — a coloured colonnade, each pair a different gem, "alternating
  inlay-wise." The same jewelled-column impulse as the fountain of Venus's seven
  gem-columns (ch. XXIII), here on the *prati*'s enclosing peristyle.
- **the five gold-bearing rivers** — Tagus, Padus (Po), Hebrus, Pactolus, Ganges: the
  classical rivers of gold, none of whose gold matches the columns' bases. And the
  **electrum breast dedicated by Helen at Lindos** (Pliny: Helen dedicated an amber
  cup molded to her own breast in Athena's temple) — the lattices outshine it.
  Colonna's habitual surpassing-the-famous-object.
- **the riverbank flowers** — this is the ground running down to **the river**, the
  ring-river that separates the middle *prati* from the inner terraces (GARDENS.md
  §5). The flowers carry their myths: **Minthe** (Mint, Pluto's love trampled by
  Proserpina — cf. p. 304), **helenion** (elecampane, sprung from Helen's tears).
- **the description reaches the river**, the next major feature; the terraces and the
  theatre lie across it.
