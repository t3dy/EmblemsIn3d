Most excellent forms, kindling dress, girlish movements, biting glances, a cleanest adornment. No part
feigned, but all perfected by nature, with an exquisite polish; nothing misshapen, but all a most
fitting harmony. Fair heads with the blondest tresses and sunlike hair, so many and so beautifully
folded together with little cords, or braids, of silk and twisted threads of gold, that they wholly
surpassed human work, wrapped about the head with a distinguished ordering and held with hair-pins; and
the forehead wooded with curling ringlets, hanging over with a wanton unsteadiness. And with elegant
garments of manifold invention for pleasure, all fragrant, musky, breathing an unknown scent.

Their speech of itself able to steal away and force any reluctance and hardness of whatever wild and
ill-disposed heart, and to corrupt all sanctity, to bind everything loose, and to fit every awkward
rusticity, and to crumble every flinty hardness. By which things my soul, being wholly inflamed with a
new longing, and already proscribed in the utmost burning of concupiscence, and every headlong and
slippery appetite of mine stirred to love and plunged in desire, suddenly I saw myself invaded and
infected with a fiery contagion, and made hot with such a kindling; and it growing within me, the
lovely damsels, without my noticing, left me alone, so kindled, in a most pleasant plain.

*An elegant Nymph comes to meet him in this place, where he was left alone and deserted by the wanton
girls. Poliphilo lovingly describes her beauty and her dress.*

My tender heart being excessively struck with amorous prickings, I do not know whether I raved, that I
stayed thus astonished at the manner in which so suddenly the most welcome company vanished and
disappeared from my eyes. So that, almost out of myself and as if carried off, raising my eyes a
little, behold, before me I see only an artful arbour of flowering jasmine, with a tall arching, painted
all over with its fragrant little flowers of the three colours mingled. Entering beneath this, heavily
anxious at the unlooked-for loss, and thinking again upon the various and great and stupendous things
