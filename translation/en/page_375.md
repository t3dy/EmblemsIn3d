# Page 375 — Chapter XXIV

Having done and duly performed this honorary and devout ceremony, we went out of
the sacred little pergola. The illustrious nymphs with affable eloquence said to
us: Know that the present place is mysterious, and most celebrated with the
greatest veneration; and every year, anniversarily, on the day before the Kalends
of May, the divine Mother comes here with her beloved son in a divine pomp of
lustration, and with her all we her subjects, of our own accord at her command,
with observed attendance and with proud solemnity, assemble. Come here, then,
with sweet tears and little sighs, she commands us that all the roses of the
pergola — and the lattices too be stripped of them — and over the alabaster
sepulchre, with high-sounding invocation, ritually to strew them, and heapingly to
cover it. Then in the said order and process she first departs. On the following
calendar day the despoiled rose-bushes reflower to the same number of glowing
roses.

And on the Ides another time the goddess, returning in the former manner —

## Notes

**Confidence: high.** Plain expository prose — the nymphs explaining the annual
rite of the Adonia — with no obscure vocabulary. The one detail worth a note is
the date, which ties the tomb to the frame of the whole book.

- **"the day before the Kalends of May"** — that is, **the last of April**, the
  eve of May Day. This is the exact date of the whole dream: the book ends
  "1467, on the Kalends of May" (ch. XXXVIII, translation/en/page_465.md), the
  first of May. So the yearly mourning-rite for Adonis at this tomb falls on the
  very eve of the morning Poliphilo wakes. The dream is dated to the Adonia, and
  the reader who holds both dates sees that Poliphilo's whole vision of winning
  and losing a beloved is folded inside the festival of a goddess losing hers.
- **the roses stripped and reflowering "to the same number"** — the miracle of the
  rite: the rose-bushes are stripped bare to cover the tomb and bloom again
  overnight, undiminished. The rose that springs from Adonis's blood (p. 372) is
  renewed each year, death feeding flower — the same cycle the anemone at the
  fountain of Venus makes (p. 363).
- **`pridiano dì delle calende di Magio`** — kept as "the day before the Kalends
  of May" rather than modernised to 30 April, so the Roman-calendar form (which
  matches the book's own colophon) stands.
