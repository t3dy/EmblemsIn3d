Let us return to the surrounding girdle of the most precious vessel, or bordered band. In the empty
space left between the tails, I saw two histories worthy of the greatest admiration in such carving.

On the front face of that vessel I beheld, excellently cut, high-thundering Jove. He held in his right
hand a cutting golden sword of a vein of Ethiopian chrysolite, shining. In the other a flashing
thunderbolt of a ruby vein. And he with a threatening aspect of a vein of galactite, crowned with
sparkling stars like the thunderbolt. Standing upon a sacred altar of sapphire.

In whose divine and dreadful majesty I looked upon a festive chorus of seven Nymphs, white in their
dress, showing that they sang religiously, with a worshipping applause. Who then were transformed into
green trees of an emerald clearness, filled with shining dark blue flowerets. And they bowed devoutly to
the highest godhead. Not that all the Nymphs were changed into fronds, but the last being wholly turned
into a little tree, and her feet into little roots; and the next with her feet gone; and the third from
the girdle upward, with the beginning of her arms; and each of the rest thereafter. But at the top of
their maidenly heads they showed the metamorphosis that was to follow upon them all in turn.
