And above all to win the precious love, and to gain the long-desired good will of this woman: fairer
beyond comparison than Cassiopeia, more shapely than Castiamira — alas for me, who carries my dying and

least that it might be eternally granted me by a special gift and privilege and favour to gaze upon
her steadily and intently.

And then, speaking to myself, I said: O Poliphilo, if the burdensome and troublesome heaviness of this
amorous weight should by chance frighten you, the sweetness of the fruit invites you, a lover of labour,
to any sad toil; and if the toilsome perils frighten you, yet the hope of the patronage and help of such
a Nymph ought to make you fierce and persuade you.

Then, my varied wavering having departed from here without delay, I said: O gods above and greatest,
and you sovereign goddesses, powerful over mortals — if this is that most acceptable Polia whom at
present I see, whom without interruption I have carried and keep most preciously stamped in my burnt and
tenacious heart, cleaving with an everlasting glue, from the first years of love until now, I am content
with everything, and beyond her I ask nothing else; but only this I beseech as a suppliant: bind her
likewise to my fervent loves, and that she, with that same burning fire in which I so hardly feed
myself for her and burn consuming, may burn equally, and both of us mailed together; or else loose me
alone.

For which cause I can no longer feign and pretend the bitterness, to hide the strengthened burning. I
am forever dying while living, and living I feel no life; I am cheerful while grieving, and I do not
grieve, and I go on suffering; I consume myself, feeding in the flame, and the overflowing flame grows;
and burning like gold in the strong crucible I find myself solid ice.

Alas, wretched me: this so heavy love troubles me more than heavy Inarime troubles Typhon; it scatters
me more than the ravening vultures the coiled entrails of Tityus; it tangles me more than the
labyrinth's winding; it disquiets me more than the storm-bearing winds the quieted sea; it presses me
more than the biting hounds pressed Actaeon in flight, and more than horrible death troubles sweet
living, it troubles my spirits.
