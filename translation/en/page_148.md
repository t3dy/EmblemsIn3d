No sooner had her amorous aspect and most welcome presence slipped through the service of the eyes to
my inmost parts than remembering memory awoke, stirring the heart within. Presenting to it and offering
her who has made of it a plentiful workshop, and a quiver packed with her sharp arrows, and a familiar
and treasuring dwelling of her sweet likeness: it knew her again, and her who has at length used up my
tender years in her warm, first and strongest loves. For I felt it, now leaping out of place, and
without delay beating steadily upon my hurt breast like a hoarse drum.

And although in her comely and most acceptable aspect, and by her blonde tresses, and in her tendrilled
brow with its crisp and quivering locks playing above, there appeared to me that golden-haired Polia
extremely loved — and for whom my life has never for a moment been able to move itself out of the
burning flames, nor, tossed as it is, to be tempered — nevertheless the proud and unaccustomed nymphlike
dress, and the unknown place, made me remain greatly in suspense over it, doubting and of two minds.

Who, with the snowy arm of her left side, resting upon her breast white as snow, carried a kindled and
shining torch, standing a little above her gilded head, holding the slenderest end of it with a
clenching fist; and shrewdly offering her free arm, whiter than ever was that of Pelops. In which
appeared the fine cephalic and the basilic vein, like sandalwood lines drawn upon the cleanest papyrus.
And with her delicate right hand, taking mine in a well-ordered way, with a wide and splendid brow, and
with her laughing mouth fragrant of cinnamon and her dimpled cheeks, and with her most adorned coaxing
speech, she pleasantly said:

"O Poliphilo, come safely with me, and do not hesitate at all." I then felt my spirits astonished,
marvelling how she knew my name. And all my inward parts, laid low, encircled by a burning amorous
flame, and my voice taken from me, shut in between fear and a reverent bashfulness. And thus,
unawares, I knew not what I might worthily answer her to that, nor how otherwise to reverence the divine
young girl, save that I readily offered her my unworthy and unfitting hand.
