Whence perhaps this divine Nymph is reserved for her Genius, or for some other lofty hero, and I,
unworthily attempting such a sacrilege, might deservedly stir him to indignation against me.

Weighing such a reason in good time, then, I thought that those who lightly make themselves secure also
lightly perish, and that for such men deceit and failure are not hard to come by. And that among the
bold (as it is wont to be said) all deceiving and mocking Fortune does not stand. And besides this, it
is hard to search out another's heart. For which cause, as Callisto, feeling with shame her swollen
belly grow heavy, withdrew herself from the presence of chaste Diana, so, stirred by shame, I drew
myself back from such an impulse, bridling my delighting and unseemly desires.

But steadily, with a lynx's eye, and unceasingly, I examined with the utmost pleasure and with a
wonderful affection the most beautiful Nymph, disposing myself wholly to her most welcome love, with an
unfailing, obstinate and most settled mind.

*Polia, still unknown to her lover Poliphilo, graciously and wittily makes him secure. Who, by her
wondrous beauties, gives himself to love in his mind; and both of them drawing near to the triumphs, he
saw countless young men and girls keeping festival with much delight.*

Cupid the archer being strongly and most aptly set in my captive heart as a presiding tyrant, and
having bound me strongly with the solidest chains of love, I felt him prick hurtfully, and the raw and
urgent bite force me violently, being already put under the privilege of his hard but pleasant laws.
