Out of which broadish orifice, flying and crackling, with a most-pleasing pop, sparks leapt out,
running lucent through the air; which then, extinguished, fell as ashen sparks.

The other companion, **Capnolina** (Smoke-girl), bore a little goatish, or earthen, vase, with a
narrow orifice, raised somewhat high and thinning at the bottom; and above its dilation, under the
handles, it had circularly, with even distance, thirteen most-measured Greek letters diligently
impressed:

> **ΠΑΝΤΑ ΒΑΙΑ ΒΙΟΥ** (PANTA BAIA BIOU)
> — "All the things of life are little [trifling / short-lived]."

With many other ornaments and obliquing little channels. Which, from the letters toward the orifice,
was polished-smooth. Out of which, and through the vent-holes, leapt forth a misty and sweet-smelling
smoke, resolving into nothing through the air.

## Notes

**Confidence: high** (the Greek inscription and its sense are secure); medium for the two vessels'
ornament. The last of the matrons: **Vrachivia** with an emerald urn that shoots sparks that die to
ash, and **Capnolina** ("Smoke-girl") with an earthen vase inscribed **ΠΑΝΤΑ ΒΑΙΑ ΒΙΟΥ** — "all the
things of life are little/fleeting" — from which fragrant smoke rises and dissolves into nothing. All
secure.

- **"PANTA BAIA BIOU" — the memento-mori at the heart of the triumph.** Amid Love's parade of pomp,
  Colonna sets a smoking vase inscribed *all the things of life are trifling and brief*, its incense
  "resolving into nothing through the air," paired with sparks that "fall as ashen sparks." The triumph
  of Love carries its own vanitas: pleasure and life alike are smoke. This is the philosophical answer
  to QUIS EVADET / NEMO (p. 328) — none escapes Love, and none escapes transience. A key inscription
  for the world's triumph.
- **the two smoke-and-spark vessels** — Vrachivia's emerald urn throws sparks that die; Capnolina's
  earthen vase breathes vanishing incense. Together they stage the brevity of all things as a
  perfumed, sparkling spectacle — beauty and mortality in one image, the book's characteristic
  doubleness.
- **the procession's shape** — the named nymphs have presented, in order, bow, flowers, quiver-and-
  arrows, balls, blindfold, the nude sphere, torch, spark-urn, and smoke-vase: the full regalia and
  the full meaning of Love, ending on transience. The triumphal car itself follows.
