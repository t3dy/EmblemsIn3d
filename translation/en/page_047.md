# Page 47 — Chapter V

POLIPHILO, HAVING MADE SUFFICIENT DEMONSTRATION OF THE SYMMETRY OF THE GREAT GATE, GOES ON TO DESCRIBE
EXCELLENTLY ITS MOST POLISHED AND MOST SKILFULLY WROUGHT ORNAMENT, AND HOW MARVELLOUSLY IT WAS
COMPOSED.

LET IT NOT VEX THAT NOBLE COMPANY who give themselves continually to pleasing love, that I have
lingered somewhat in what is narrated above — which perhaps to them is not in the least gracious,
being desirous, as they treat of it, to understand that thing in which (however bitter it be in
itself) they nourish themselves with a patient mind, placing their festive heart in it.

Whereupon, human affection being naturally variable, let it not for this cause be laid to their
charge: bread grateful to an unhurt palate is sometimes displeasing to a depraved one, but is rather
kindly welcomed by him to whose taste it is pleasing. For, having in some part made mention of the due
end of building — which is that excellent invention, to acquire by measure the solid body of the
edifice — thereafter, that being freely invented, the Architect reduces it by minute divisions;
neither more nor less than the Musician, having invented the intonation and the measured time in one
greatest note, then, proportioning it in minute Chromatics, neatly carries it back upon the solid.

By such likeness, after the invention, the chief rule peculiar to the Architect is the squaring. And
distributing this into the smallest parts, there is offered to him the harmony of the building and its
measured proportion, and to his principal the fitting corollaries.

For which cause, this gate being for excellence most beautiful by its admirable composition and
invention, and there being added to it so much especial elegance and with so amended a distribution
that no part accused itself as needing correction in the least cutting-back — I therefore now think it
worthy to describe its perfect completion.
