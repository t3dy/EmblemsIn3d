In the square above the order of the columns on the left, of unaccustomed carving, was contained the
history of how the disconsolate Cyparissus lifted his tender limbs high into the air, for the Hind
struck by his arrow; and how Apollo wept hard for it.

The third square, that which stood placed next above the resisting columns, offered such a most
beautiful carving: Leucothoe, impiously slain by her own father, changed her white and maidenly flesh
into tender bark and moving fronds and drooping twigs.

In the fourth squaring was shown the unwilling Daphne, in no way yielding her virgin flesh to the
burning desires of the long-haired Delian, sorrowfully transforming herself towards the warm heavens
into eternal greenness.

Now, in successive order, above the Cymatium — which of any lineament is called the topmost line — of
these histories described, there rose extended a dentilled cornice, and one of ovolos with the
thunderbolts, or little darts, set between the gape of one ovolo and another; and foliage, and the
tiles, with the whorls and Nicolo-stones and other Seals, and other choice workings expressed; and
modillions with the Astragals, without any defect; and last the Cyma foliated with Acanthus with a
most polished spacing between. Of which things so excellent a carving was afforded that, of works
sharply hollowed out, not the least mark of the gnawing Auger appeared.

Returning duly to the frontispiece, or pinnacle, in the disposition of which (as I have said before)
there are repeated in it all the cornices set beneath, in all the parts of the workings, answering to
the perpendiculars — the drip of the topmost cornice being set aside, denied to this member.

At present it falls to me to dispatch the triangular flat of the said temple-like part, in which,
not without admiring contemplation, there presents itself to be considered: as much as the extremities
of this inserted figure could contain, so much was fitted in it a crown of divers fronds and fruits
and stalks entwined, and diligently unfolded, of most green stone, drawn tight in four parts, in knots
of thongs plaited through the little bundles. Held by two Scyllas, half human and the lower part
fish-like, which with the arm above and the other below aptly embraced it. From the one and the other
of the lying angles above the cymatium of the cornice, they stretched out their sea-beast tails,
