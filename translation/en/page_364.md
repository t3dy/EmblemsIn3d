— they took care to dip their little rosy feet in the salt fountain; and of
their ready little toes one stood out beyond the rest, the one nearest the

By this divine arrangement, then, the most holy majesty of the goddess sat
voluptuously in the very middle of the fountain; and the part of the divine
body that stood among the waters showed neither more nor less than a ray or
splendour of the sun shining through the most polished crystal.

Kneeling there and devoutly persevering, my mind wavered, gazing beyond all
measure and to excess, unable to look fixedly upon the divine spirit breathing
everywhere. And no less thoughtfully I turned over the cause — by what sweetness
of fortune and faith, by what means and merit, such mysteries were granted me
to see clearly, and with my eyes unfit for such an object. But I judged it to be
only the free will of the immortal gods, and the kind consent and faithful
prayer of Polia. Above all things it displeased me that among so many celestial
and divine persons I alone stood there contemptible and foreign, in worn and
threadbare clothes, unlike them in every way, most abject and most poor; and I
would then most gladly have had some means of covering my deformity — as
Erichthonius had, to hide his viper feet. And so, stupefied in my mind with
incredible wonder, I praised the divine benignity to the utmost, that it had
allowed an earth-born man openly to contemplate the works of the gods and the
treasure of fermenting nature.

Whereupon those illustrious nymphs, who beneath the pergolas rejoiced with their
applause and songs and sweet harmony for the prey that winged and rash Cupid —
keener-sighted than Lynceus and eye-covered Argus — was vigorously ready with
his weapons to take in triumph: after a little hour had been spent by the mother
goddess, and the celestial sounds and songs were broken off a while into
silence, to both of us, with divine eloquence and soothing charm, in a gentle
address, that most holy mouth uttered such sweet-speaking words — words shrewd
and beyond doubt able to lull to sleep the wakeful guardian of the fatal
treasure of Colchis, to call back to a kindly shape Aglauros the daughter of
Cecrops, to restore Daphnis of Ida to human form and to the grateful flock, and
to recall Cadmus and Hermione from the hissing voice and the scaled body — to
Polia thus saying:

Lovely Polia, my worshipper, your holy libations, your assiduous observances —

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The narrative frame is secure; the difficulty is a chain
of learned mythological allusions in the description of Venus's eloquence, each
identified below, and one alchemical phrase (`thesoro dilla fermentosa natura`)
that is doctrinally loaded and kept literal. **A reviewer should check the
Colchis / Aglauros / Daphnis / Cadmus series against the Italian word order.**

- **`fermentosa natura`** — "fermenting nature." Fermentation is an alchemical
  operation, and Colonna's phrase treats the fountain-tableau as a chemical
  process on display. Kept literal so the Chymica reading survives.
- **Erichthonius** — born of Hephaestus's seed on the earth, serpent-footed or
  serpent-legged, and hidden away for it. Poliphilo, ashamed of his shabby
  mortal clothes among the gods, reaches for the one mythological figure who
  also needed to hide his lower body.
- **The four things Venus's eloquence could do** are a rhetorical tour: lull the
  sleepless dragon guarding the **Golden Fleece at Colchis** (as Medea's charm
  did); soften **Aglauros**, whom Mercury turned to stone; restore **Daphnis**;
  and call **Cadmus and Hermione** back from the serpent-shape they were given.
  Each is a myth of a charm undoing a transformation — fitting for a speech that
  is about to bind two lovers.
- **Lynceus and Argus** — the keenest-sighted of the Argonauts, and the
  hundred-eyed guardian: Cupid outsees both.
