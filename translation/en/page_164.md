## Notes

**Confidence: low.** **The oracle on this page is untranslatable as the English stands, and it is
the point of the panel.** Apollo's answer is given in the Italian as two Latin clauses that look
almost identical and mean differently — *Uni gratum mare. Alterum gratum mari.* — and Colonna says
in the same sentence that it was *perplexibelmente* and an *ambiguo responso*. The English prints
"the one is pleasing to the sea; the other is pleasing to the sea", which is the same clause twice,
so the reader is told an answer is ambiguous and then shown one that is merely repetitive, and the
Latin that carries the ambiguity is dropped. The rest of the page (Cupid drawing the constellations
with his arrow, Jove appointing the shepherd judge) is secure; the oracle must be restored from the
facsimile and re-Englished with both readings marked.

*Confidence stated 2026-09-20 by a second reader, not by the translator (ticket tr-confidence-unstated-180).*
