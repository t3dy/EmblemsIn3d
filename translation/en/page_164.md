In the other slab, the curious parents, ignorant of the new prodigy, devoutly asked in Apollo's temple
of the divine image, by an oracle, the cause and the outcome. To whom the kindly godhead answered thus
perplexingly: *the one is pleasing to the sea; the other is pleasing to the sea.* By such an ambiguous
answer they were kept safe by their pious parents.

On the front face there was seen a most beautiful boy Cupid, raised into the upper air, and with the
cutting point of a golden arrow violently painting in the starry heaven various figures of animals
four-footed, creeping and flying; and on the earth men stood marvelling at so great an effect from one
frail little arrow. On the hinder one, great Jupiter set a skilful shepherd in his own place as judge,
