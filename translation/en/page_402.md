# Page 402 — Chapter XXVII

"And, at the present striking, there came many hunting dogs, bristle-haired, famished, with fervid barkings and dissonant,
intoning (such as the Albanian King did not give in gift to the Great Alexander); and rabid Lions, and roaring; and
raging wolves; and, in the air, rapacious Eagles, and fasting Kites; and hissing Vultures — to the warm blood and
nefarious feast they presented themselves. And the boy — from him every humanity removed — the stricken girls (their last
weeping and voice proffered) beating-down, into two partitions cut (them); and, unsealed their female breast, the living
heart he drew out, and that to the fierce fowls threw, and the fuming viscera to the savage Eagles; and the rest of the
pallid bodies, quartered, to the rabid animals cast. And there he watched the spear-like Lions, devouring, assaulting, and
gluttonously avid, into the human flesh their toothed jaws to put, and to un-nerve; and, with the taloned claws, to
split, and, lacerating, to fragment; and, in the purple blood, dyeing their tawny manes, to make (them) cruent; and to
make, ultimately, a terrific *laniena* (shambles) and tearing of the divulsed and dissipated members of the two damsels,
of tender and soft age compelled, immature, their extreme spirits to breathe out. Ah, cruel spectacle! Alas, horrend mode
of burial!

"O me — being (consider, piteous Nymphs) frightened at the aspect of so much evil and cruent immanity — I found myself, of
every counsel, indigent and naked, and greatly terrorised, and beyond belief affrighted; I stood lurking and hidden among
a biting thicket, enveloped with thorny sloe-bushes, and dog-roses, and of pricking wild-pear, and, with valid prickles,
the harsh Christ's-thorn (paliurus). And under this brushy density, and nemoral shade, thus standing over-covered, I
doubted, and, without measure most-terrified by such-made a vision — putting myself in a greater fright than the horrible
simulacrum of Clytemnestra to the matricide Orestes, armed with serpents and with ardent fire — fearing lest the untamed
and smell-tracking beasts, in the secret and thick wood, (against me) sole and unarmed, and, of sex and of age, fragile
and simple, and without hope of aid, should not, on me, make a similar laceration. And within myself I said, tremulous:
'Alas — should I ever be, like Iphigenia, hither by winds reported, among the most-cruel Tauric strangers, to be made a
victim? Alas — what Caucasus, what Hyrcania, what interior Libya, or Agisymba, so immense, so sanguinary beasts nourished,
that could be equalled to so incredible a cruelty?'"

---

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The vision's grisliest turn: Cupid **cuts the girls in two, tears out their living hearts** and
feeds them (and the viscera and quartered bodies) to a pack of dogs, lions, wolves, eagles, kites and vultures; Polia,
hidden in a thornbrake, watches "beyond belief affrighted," fearing she too will be torn like an Iphigenia-victim. All
secure; the punishment complete.

- **The heart torn out and devoured.** The culmination of the *contrapasso*: the heartless are literally de-hearted, their
  hearts thrown to beasts. It is the exact horror Polia earned by killing Poliphilo's "amorous heart" (p. 397). The vision
  spares her nothing — she must see the full wage of cruelty before she can renounce it.
- **Polia hidden in the thorns.** Cowering in a thicket of sloe, dog-rose, wild-pear and Christ's-thorn (paliurus), Polia
  is the terrified witness — not yet punished, but shown her fate. Her fear "greater than Clytemnestra's ghost to Orestes"
  measures the vision's force. This terror is what finally cracks the frozen heart (her lament and dream follow, p. 403+).
- **the thorn-botany** — even in horror Colonna names the exact thicket-plants: his herbarium persists into the nightmare
  (cf. the Polyandrion's shore-herbs, p. 236). The wild wood is precisely observed.
- **Iphigenia among the Tauri** — Polia fears becoming a human sacrifice like Iphigenia among the Taurians (Diana's cruel
  cult): the Iphigenia-motif returns (cf. pp. 227, 388) — she who vowed to Diana now fears Diana's own savage rite. The
  chastity-goddess's dark side closes on her.
