# Page 324 — Chapter XXI

> *(The letters of the second roundel spell, between the eagle and the pheasant:)*
> **SUPERNAE ALITIS BENIGNITAS**
> — "The benignity of the celestial bird."

Within the circuit, inwardly, beyond the bounds of the figuring, all was of mountain
*polium*; the pheasant of *laurentia*; the eagle of groundsel; the vase of asarabacca. In the
gap between the encompassing lips, of butcher's-broom. The outer bands, with all their course,
of periwinkle. The other band, of *trinità* (heartsease). The circles in the intruded angles,
of marjoram-gentle. What is excluded and intruded of these, of *digitello*. The letters, of
thyme; the spaces, of maidenhair. The fields of the round band-work, of *santonica*. In the
centres of the circles, between the triangles, was a sphere — two of sweet-smelling southernwood
and two of lavender, raised a foot and a half upon its stalk. In the rest, alternately, a sphere
of savin and one of juniper, three feet. All the herbs with most comely thickness, and freshest
verdure, and most pleasant look. A wondrous work of exquisiteness, of pleasantness, and delight.
Irrigated by narrowest little pipes, orderly distributed, vomiting a most tenuous and droplet-like
sprinkling.

FOLLOWINGLY, with the kept rule, ascended other seven steps, upon the topmost of which a
much-admired railing encompassed the whole, of red and most illustrious jasper, with elegant
open-work, agreeing concordantly to most pleasing patterns, of a sextant's thickness. This
railed hedge, and the following, was without any opening, but continuous; and here ended the
roads tending straight to the island-centre — but it stood passable only in the triumphal road,
and so the following.

In this voluptuous cloister I beheld a grove, conspicuously shady of density, of most-celebrated
shrubbery. Here were the two female terebinths, tenacious to old age, of choice and blackish
splendour, of pleasant odour; bdellium with oaken foliage; the *malus medica* (citron), perennially
apple-bearing. Precious ebony, the pepper-tree, clove, nutmeg, the threefold sandalwood, cinnamon,
the most-praised silphion — which would not be found in the vale of Jericho, or in Egypt at
Matarea. Here the whitening costus, which the isle of Patale does not produce. And the shrub nard,
with its ears bearded, most praised of spike and of leaf. And the aloewood, of unspeakable
sweetness, which the headless Nile does not carry down; and storax, and stacte. And —

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The second inscription completes — **SUPERNAE ALITIS BENIGNITAS** ("The
benignity of the celestial bird") — with its planting-key; then a **red-jasper railing** crowns
the seventh terrace, sealing every radial road except the one triumphal way; and within opens the
**grove of aromatics** (terebinth, bdellium, citron, ebony, pepper, clove, nutmeg, sandalwood,
cinnamon, silphion, costus, nard, aloewood, storax). All secure; the spice-catalogue is Pliny's
and the *materia medica*.

- **The two paired eagle-mottoes together** — *ALES MAGNA DICATA OPTIMO IOVI* (p. 323) and
  *SUPERNAE ALITIS BENIGNITAS* (here): the garden's rings are dedicated to Jupiter under the sign
  of his eagle, "the benignity of the celestial bird." A Jovian theology written in herbs, just
  inside Venus's island.
- **the red-jasper railing seals the rings.** From here inward only the one *triumphal road* passes;
  the other radial roads all stop at this fence. This is the threshold between the terraced gardens
  and the sacred grove-and-theatre at the centre — a real gate in the plan.
- **the grove of aromatics** — the innermost planting is not flowers but the great spices and incense-
  woods, "surpassing" Jericho, Egypt, Patale, the Nile, Saba. The approach to the centre is scented,
  not merely coloured: the garden intensifies toward the holy middle.
- **the misting irrigation** — "narrowest little pipes… a most tenuous droplet-like sprinkling":
  Colonna even specifies a fine-mist watering system for the parterres. A concrete atmospheric detail.
