Beyond this first binding followed, ascending in order, a similar order of columns, in every thing
conformable, and in no part discrepant. And although the building-art appetites that superposed columns
ought to be shorter by a quarter than those set below — of which the perpendicular came, without the
altar-work, above the centre of the columns beneath, with the sequence, and the third (order) a fifth
(shorter) — nonetheless in this elegant and symmetrical building this was not observed, but of one
height, the overstanding with the underlying. The third quadrangular ones obeyed in the third order.
These superseated columns had also the encompassing binding, like the subjacent. Beyond this girdle,
the quadrangular ones, or little pilasters, furrowed, rose. And between one and the other, a third of it
emerging from the solid, in the evened intercapace gaped a window — not quadrangular in the temple-
manner, but as is requisite in profane buildings, obliqued, or arched.

Supremely, in a turned orb above the square columns, the royal cornice, without projections, with every
ornament and most-requisite lineament, and with the special due drip-moulding in it, with every harmonic
proportion, went round. Beyond the said, raised, circled a clean and unencumbered band, a pace and a half
high.

All this celebrated, illustrious, superb, and supremely-approved building, of finest Indian alabaster, of
glassy sheen, artfully constructed, most-excellently decorated, and egregiously perfected — without any
smearing of lime-paste, or cement, but with stable cohesion and contracted consent, perfect. Which pre-
shining material was not disdained by staining smoke, nor paling by the anointment of oil smeared on, nor
muddied by the infusion of vermilion wine, but in every part unliable, without any infection, kept in its
superb, luculent brightness.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The upper storeys and the wall's finish: the second and third column-orders (kept,
unusually, at **one height** rather than diminishing), the arched profane windows between fluted pilasters,
the royal cornice, and the whole built of **mortarless Indian alabaster** so pure that smoke, oil and wine
cannot stain it. All secure; the note on column-diminution is a knowing Vitruvian aside.

- **Columns of equal height — a deliberate rule-breaking.** Colonna knows the Vitruvian rule (upper columns
  a quarter shorter than lower) and states that this theatre *breaks* it: every order is the same height, for
  the sake of "elegant and symmetrical" effect. A specific instruction for the world's theatre — stack equal
  orders, not diminishing ones.
- **mortarless alabaster.** The whole cavea is translucent Indian alabaster, dry-jointed ("without lime… with
  stable cohesion"), and stain-proof against smoke, oil and wine. A glowing, seamless, self-cleaning shell —
  the world can render it as a luminous alabaster material with no visible joints.
- **arched windows "in the profane manner"** — Colonna distinguishes sacred (square-headed) from secular
  (arched) fenestration, and gives the theatre arched windows: it is a *profane* building, a place of
  spectacle, not a temple. A precise stylistic cue.
- **the bulls dissolving into foliage** — the frieze-relief's bulls turn, at the panel-ends, into scrolls of
  acanthus: the antique "inhabited scroll," figures growing out of ornament. The theatre's carving is
  continuous, scene flowing into pattern.
