"Let us go for a walk to the other garden, no less delightful and filled with delights: the glass one
adjoining the right wall of the proud, great and royal palace." And having entered there, I remained
wholly dazzled and exceedingly full of wonder to see a workmanship hard not so much of belief as of
telling. Which was of equal compass with the glass one, with a like disposition of raised beds, with
adorned lips of cornice-work and a golden socle, save for the workmanship and material of the enclosing
walls — for it was all made most excellently of silk: the box-trees and cypresses of silk, their stems
and branches of gold, not without a most apt sowing among them of gems; and the standing beds most
thickly filled with the simples that the mother of them envies, with a most joyful and most longed-for
flowering, with every exquisite colouring, fragrant, and like the other glass ones.

But the surrounding walls, of a wondrous workmanship and of incredible cost, were all of a covering of
pearl — that is, I saw all the faces covered with the brightest pearls, heaped and gathered into one,
and with a thick cohesion, joined together of a middling thickness; and above them most beautifully,
sprouting out of the boxes, a straggling and greenest ivy with its foliage somewhat raised from the
pearls and hanging, with its stems of gold most artfully creeping, with slender little roots wandering
among the pearls, with the utmost and most exquisite polish. And berries of jewels, knit to the
clusters, with an excellent division, headed by the golden squares, with a masterly and required
following of beam, frieze, and cornice of gold.

The faces of the beds rewoven in tapestry stitch with little histories of love and of hunting, in
threads of gold and silver and silk, feigned with so most fitting a painting that nothing could equal
it. The ground of the most level area was seen most welcome, of green silk pile, like a most notable
meadow; and in the middle of the area there stood a round enclosure, with a raised dome of little rods
of gold, distinguishedly covered with manifold flower-bearing rose-trees of the said workmanship.
