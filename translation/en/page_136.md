# Page 136 — Chapter X

And behold, there presented herself to us a woman far on in years, of an unwedded aspect, who came out
of a wattled little cottage with a smoky roof and smoked walls, by its small door (which had noted
above it PYLURANIA), coming with a modest matronliness; her little chapel set in a solitary place, and
in a shady cliff, crumbling with bare and friable stone; torn, squalid, lean, poor, with her eyes fixed
upon the ground; Theude her name.

And with her she had six companions and inseparable serving maids, meanly enough clothed and heavy. Of
whom one was named Parthenia. The second Edosia, and one Hypocolinia. The fourth Pinotidia. And next
was Tapinosa, and the last Ptochina. Which venerable matron, with her right arm bare, pointed to high
Olympus.

She dwelt at the entrance of a stony road, difficult to go along, hindered with thorns and briars. The
place appearing rough and displeasing, with the sky rainy and troubled, and darkened with a cloudy
gloom, and a most narrow path.

So that Logistica, taking note that at the first sight I shrank from such a thing, said to me, almost
sadly: "Poliphilo, this path is not known until the end." And thus of this venerable and holy woman
Thelemia, sharp-witted, quickly said to me: "O Poliphilo, the love of such a laborious woman is not for
you now." I, nodding shrewdly to Thelemia, we came forth from there. And the door being closed again,
they knocked at the left-hand one.
