# Page 199 — Chapter XVII

In the pier of the Arches, opposite, behind the Corinthian, a third of a fluted quadrangular (pilaster)
stood forth, with the base upon the pavement, standing in the face of another (in the band under the
arch that divided the vaults, fixed) wholly similar. The interval of these, from the one to the other,
the radii drawn to the circumstance made, set upon the line of the outermost Piers. Upon the semi-
capitals of which went round a circum-ligation of gracious workmanship.

The half-Tubes and half-Altars were of bright Alabaster, with curving ropes, or bundles, of manifold
fronds and fruits — of *lacterii* (spurges), of medlars, and stalks of Poppy, growing heavy in their
middle descent, of wavering shoots ensnared, suspended and knotted in certain rings comelily adorned.

Under that circum-ligation a little above narrated, between one and the other striated half-quadrangular
(pilaster), in the primary wall was a quadrangular window of one square and a half, as is required in
antiquarian temples. The opening, or lighting, of which artfully occupied a mirror-like, or diaphanous,
plate of *sogobrina* stone, not fearing old age. Whence, in sum, eight windows there were — because one
part the door of the temple occupied, and, directly opposite the Pronaos in front, another part (the
door, with the golden valves, of the rear chapel, or the sacred Adytum, the description of which will,
in another place, be more fittingly treated).

Over against the above-recited quadrangular columns, fixed in the first mural circuit, the piers
projected outside, worked out from the wall of as much thickness as that of the wall — to which their
breadth the lines of the radii of the division, from the centre to the circumference, gave. Which
breadth divided: one portion was for the breadth of the pilaster; the other portion, again deduced into
a twin partition (one here and one there, the other attributed to the collateral Pier), were for an
arching in the solid of the wall, or vaulted, between one pilaster and the other. Of which Piers the
projection, or outlet, divided by a third, usurped the prominence of the vault from the plane of the
scarped wall; and the two thirds the Piers stood forth, in a solid arched wall-and-Piers. This exquisite
observance the elegant Architects praised, so as not to give the wall a rude thickness so great that the
windows should accuse themselves obtuse (deep-set), with conspicuous respect of the rude and superfluous
solid; and for exterior adornment, which vaults in a ring turned themselves, conjoined most-excellently
one to the other with the same thickness, with due binding, from the Piers, carried round upon the wall,
throughout beautifully girt. Not otherwise, between one and the other contrasting arch, opportunely it
stood.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The temple's wall-structure: fluted pilasters answering the columns, alabaster
half-altar sub-bases with fruit-and-poppy festoons, **eight windows** glazed with translucent *sogobrina*
stone (two bays taken by the door and the rear sanctuary), and the outer piers whose thickness is
governed by the radial lines and lightened with wall-arches. All secure; a lucid (if dense) account of a
buttressed, thin-walled rotunda.

- **Eight windows and the axis.** The ten wall-bays hold eight windows plus the entrance door and, on the
  opposite axis, the door of the **rear sanctuary (the Adytum)** — whose description is deferred ("in
  another place… more fittingly"). This fixes the temple's plan: a central axis from door to hidden
  sanctuary, eight windows lighting the ring. The Adytum is the temple's secret heart (like Cythera's
  fountain), promised for later.
- **translucent stone glazing** — the windows are closed not with glass but with sheets of *sogobrina*, a
  diaphanous stone "not fearing old age": alabaster-like translucent panes. The whole interior is lit
  through stone, a soft mineral glow. A distinctive rendering cue (cf. the alabaster theatre, p. 350).
- **the wall lightened by arches** — Colonna praises the architects for hollowing the thick wall with
  relieving arches so the windows are not buried in "rude and superfluous solid": a real structural
  refinement (a thin, arcaded drum), not mere ornament. The temple is engineered, not just decorated.
- **poppy-and-medlar festoons** — the alabaster sub-bases carry garlands of spurge, medlar and poppy-heads:
  fertility-and-sleep plants fit for a temple of generative Venus. The ornament carries meaning.
