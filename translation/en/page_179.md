Then I saw in flight a fleeing quail, and a hook-clawed eagle following. Thence too Erigone I saw, who
had her sparkling breast packed with savoury grapes. And the daughter of King Collus pleased with a
strong bull. And the wife of Eripeus gracious with her changed husband. And her born of Alpus quietly
delighting in a shaggy and woolly ram. And the maiden Melantho with the swimming beast. And Philyra,
daughter of ancient Ocean, with the father of Chiron.

Afterwards I saw law-giving Ceres, her brow bound with yellow ears of corn, embraced in a delightful
pleasure with the scaly serpent. And the most shapely Nymph Lara of the Tiber delighting with the
slayer of Argus. And the fair Nymph Juturna. And many others, long to tell of.

Now, with an excessive delight, while ignorant and astonished I carefully contemplated with an applied
mind the heavenly troop and the divine triumphs hedged about with such dances, and the delicious fields
— what they were was wholly unknown to me, save that they were amorous mysteries — the divine Nymph, my
faithful companion and guide, taking note of my dullness, with an excellent face and adorned and
sweetest little words (I not asking her) providently said:

"My Poliphilo, do you see that one?" (showing me who in the fleeting age they had been). "By high Jove
she was ardently loved. And likewise that other beloved one. And this was such a one, and those
godheads were taken with her sweet love." And in this manner besides making known to me her noble and
royal lineage. And the name I did not know she affably, all solacing, told me, pointing.

Then she showed me a venerable crowd of young girls. To whom three holy matrons stood before with
divine bearing, going ahead of so great a delight. Adding (changed somewhat in her angelic face), she
said lovingly: "My Poliphilo, I would have you understand that no earth-born creature could enter here
without a torch kindled — either by ardent love and with the utmost labour, such as you see me carry
burning now, or by the safe company of those three matrons." And sighing from her heart she said: "The
present torch it will behove me, for your love, to quench at the holy temple (as you shall see),
offering it."

when she called me thus: "My Poliphilo." Because I was made wholly suspicious that without fail she was
Polia. For which cause I felt myself, from head to foot, inwardly altered and refreshed with the
