Moreover the water was copious with manifold fishes, of form beautiful, and various,

and there twisting, and willing to do the women's bidding, one drawing the other.
Heaped together were the white swans, with the voice sent forth with the tears poured
out for beloved Phaethon; and otters and beavers, with other water-animals, all most
joyously beneath the topiary covering made festival to voluptuous delight, without
other pondering — so that nothing arose to cause molestation to their pleasure and
solace, nor anything displeasing to oppose it — a silent desire sprouting again in
my soul. In this happy place I would willingly still, with my goddess Polia, wish to
dwell eternally. But then, every other roving desire extinguished and repudiated,
without delay I fell back to my most firm intent, in the unique love of my most
beloved Polia. Nonetheless I judged this without doubt a thing super-excellent, and
super-delightful, above any delight, above every sweetness of pleasure.

Likewise, in the first circular and wooded enclosure of the groves, creatures of the
one and the other sex, ever within that same hedge, dwelt with those animals
appointed to pleasure. In the greening meadows within the peristyle, by another
turning, I saw innumerable youths and outstanding girls, intent on solaces, on
sounds, and songs, on dances, and delightful conversations, and pure and sincere
embraces, given over to adornment and personal cultivation, to compose muse-songs,
and to various works the studious and devoted damsels — where I judged virtue to be
more affected than any other lascivious pleasure.

Beyond then this most clear and delightful river, with a continuous circling, was a
grassy meadow as great as that which stretches from the peristyle to the river. This
river was passable by symmetrical bridges, built with wondrous and exquisite skill,
delineated of most excellent marble, alternately one of porphyry and the other of
serpentine, shining with splendid brightness, preserving the lineament of the roads
tending toward the middle navel of that mysterious island, verdant with every
fecundity of delights.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The end of the boating-game (fish that carry the girls,
swans, otters), Poliphilo's moralised recollection of Polia, and then the crossing:
the river's porphyry-and-serpentine bridges, and the start of the seven-step
terraces. All secure.

- **The porphyry-and-serpentine bridges over the ring-river.** The roads that run to
  the island's centre cross the river on alternating porphyry / serpentine bridges,
  "preserving the lineament of the roads." Segre's plan carries these radial
  bridges; the world's twenty radial roads could gain them at the river-crossing.
- **the seven-step terraces begin here.** "After the meadow began seven steps… in
  height seven feet" — the start of the *seven concentric terrace-flights* rising to
  the theatre (GARDENS.md §5), the feature the world's Cythera leaves flat. Pages
  315–316 detail them: the colonnade on the top step, the topiary towers with
  triumphs. This is the material for the deferred terrace pass.
- **Poliphilo turns from the pleasure-island back to Polia** — the recurring moral
  beat: he admits the place is "super-excellent… above every sweetness," then
  "extinguishes every roving desire" and returns to "the unique love of my most
  beloved Polia." Colonna keeps the erotic garden inside a frame of fidelity.
- **swans weeping for Phaethon** — the Heliades, Phaethon's sisters, turned to
  poplars (here conflated with swans); another metamorphosis among the water-fowl.
