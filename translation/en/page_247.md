## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** Poliphilo descends through an ivy-choked broken door into the **crypt** beneath the
ciborium: a round marble-vaulted subterranean chamber on dwarf columns (six, under the six above), damp and owl-
haunted, with a sectile floor; at its centre a hollow **brass sacrificial altar** with a grate and a stoke-hole,
which he reads as the furnace where the funeral victims were burned and their ash drawn. All secure.

- **The crypt of the Polyandrion.** Below the hexagonal ciborium (p. 246) lies a round vaulted crypt on six dwarf
  columns — the burial-level of the tomb-temple, where the sacrificial furnace-altar stands. Colonna gives it fully:
  damp, natron-strewn, owl-fouled, marble-clean. A buildable underworld for the world's polyandrion (a descent
  from the obelisk-court into a lamp-lit crypt).
- **the descent as transgression.** Poliphilo enters "seduced by too scrutinous desire, inconsiderately" — the
  same rash curiosity that will, deeper in, read the Rape-of-Proserpina relief and panic (the chapter's climax,
  pp. 280+). His antiquarian appetite leads him into the tomb's dark; the descent is a small *katabasis*.
- **the furnace-altar.** The brass altar is the crypt's focus — the fire where the love-dead's bodies were burned
  (cf. the swan-holocaust of the Venus-temple, p. 231, its dark twin). The whole Polyandrion is an engine of
  cremation and memory. A striking central object for the world's crypt.
- **natron and owls** — *aphronitum*/*baurach* (natron, the Egyptian embalming-salt) strewn in the crypt, and
  night-owls fouling the floor: precise, grim atmosphere. Colonna's realism serves the mood — the tomb is genuinely
  sepulchral, not merely picturesque.
