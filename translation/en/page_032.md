Upon this courtyard, ten paces in from the beginning towards the gate, I saw a prodigious horse and
winged Vaulting-steed, with its wings spread, of bronze, of excessive magnitude. The hoof of which
occupied, upon the level of the base, in the outermost line of the rounding of one shoe, five feet.
And from this outermost circuit of the hoof up to beneath the breast I found it, by due reckoning,
nine feet high.

With its head loose and unbridled, with two little ears, the one thrust forward and the other drawn
back. With waving and flowing mane hanging down over the right of the neck. Upon which many striplings
were attempting to ride astride. Not one of them was able to hold himself firmly upon it, because of
its unchecked swiftness and hard jolting. Whereupon some were falling, some stood on the point of
falling headlong. Some on their backs, and some thrown over backwards, and others were climbing up
leaning on it; some, tumbled over, held vainly to the long hairs caught in their gripping hands.
There were some fallen, and then in the act of rising, slipped beneath the body of the shaker-off.
