# Page 401 — Chapter XXVII

"Which (girls), errant and ignorant, most-intent on flight, through devious and pathless places, and through dense thorns
forced to go, and by the lacerating and mortal friction, and by the ardour of the fiery chariot, many times, exorbitant,
scrabbling through the shrubs — from head to feet lacerated, and, of blood, the shattered members rained, and the torn
flesh; and the vermilion and fuming blood copiously to scatter itself through the barbed briars, and through the earth, I
saw. And disorderedly, through the thick and pointed brambles, by furious rage driven, now here, now there, badly the
ardent and ponderous chariot they drew — which still cruelly burned the soft and delicate little flesh; which not only
were cooked, but, like a burnt hide, cracked. And so, with 'wah!' and cries, miserably afflicting themselves, in high
clamours and weepings, and miserable ululations, they exclaimed, turned into a greater fury than Orestes. Whence the
scabrous and most-wooded place all resounded of the piteous accents; and, henceforth, the mandibles restraining
themselves, and the tired and consumed voice made raucous, no more availed the dolorous, lifeless ones to endure. Then, a
little hour after, many most-cruel animals joined them. And the executioner and unmild boy, after long and cruent
butchery and immanity of the ill-fated and wretched adolescent-girls — like one cruent and exercised in such butcheries —
of the ardent vehicle suddenly descended; with a *soliferrum* (all-iron javelin) and cutting *rhomphaea* (broadsword),
(the girls) loosed from the molesting yoke and grievous drawing, through the middle of their pulsing heart, he — despoiled
of any pardon and miseration, with rigid and uncontaminated severity — suddenly transfixed (them)."

---

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The graphic climax of the vision: the two girls, dragged bleeding through the thorns by the fiery
chariot ("not only cooked but cracked like burnt hide"), fill the wood with their cries; then Cupid dismounts and **runs
them through the heart** with sword and javelin. All secure; deliberately harrowing.

- **The punishment of cruelty, unflinching.** Colonna does not spare the horror: the cruel women are burned, torn, and
  stabbed through the heart by the very god they scorned. It is the *contrapasso* — those who gave love mortal wounds (as
  Polia did Poliphilo, p. 395) receive them. The violence is the measure of Polia's guilt, shown to her as a mirror. The
  Nastagio-vision (p. 400) plays out in full.
- **the heart pierced.** Cupid drives the blade "through the middle of the pulsing heart" — the organ that would not feel,
  now destroyed. Polia's own "stony breast" (p. 396) is the target the vision threatens. The bodily logic is exact: the
  hard heart is the thing punished.
- **the register.** Book II's plainer voice turns, here, to full Gothic horror — the most violent passage in the
  Hypnerotomachia. It is the necessary dark before Polia's conversion: terror, not persuasion, breaks her. (The feeding
  of the hearts to beasts follows, p. 402.)
