These choice and ready columns aforesaid the sustaining bases of bronze subdued, with the Tori, or
mouldings, of oaken fronds with the fruits expressed, closely girt about with a winding binding,
resting above upon the Plinth set beneath. The capitals set above, of the material of the bases, of
work fitting and requisite to all the harmony — such as Callimachus the Cunning Craftsman did not see
in the sprouted Acanthus from the Basket upon the buried Corinthian maiden, nor made to express his
comely ornament.

Covered by the curved Abaci, or bent lids, decorated with the lily in the middle. The vase of which
was excellently clothed with two orders of eight Acanthus leaves in the Roman and Corinthian manner.
Out of the said leaves came the lesser Helices, meeting in the middle of the vase, and produced the
lily most beautifully fitted into the curve of the Abacus. From which then the little stalks twisted
themselves beneath the projection of the Abacus. Such as Agrippa approvedly set in the Porch of the
wonderful Pantheon, attributed for its height one entire diameter of the base of the column, with
observable Symmetry in each of its parts and accessories.

Speaking now of the threshold of the gate: a huge green stone made it, degenerate with a seed-bed of
whitening, black and yellow markings, and of other various and imperfect mottlings, most hard. Above
this stood the straight Antae, free and shining in the aspect of the space between; as broad as the
breadth of the threshold set beneath, one pace, so broadly they stood; but the outward face was
notably carved. Without sign of hinges in the threshold, nor yet in the lintel; nor did there appear
any trace of the iron catches of the cramps holding the half-capitals of its stone.

Thence, then, arching the arch-beam, or half-circle, with the requisite lineaments and measured bands
of the beam — that is, little balls or berries, and with tenth-part spindles, as though threaded in a
row made equal, and dog's-ear mouldings, and with waving down-lying work antiquely curved, or
jagged, with the little stalks. The keystone of which, or clasp, or Wedge, worthy of admiration, and
of a subtle and daring feigning, and of elegant polish, offered itself thus most notable.
