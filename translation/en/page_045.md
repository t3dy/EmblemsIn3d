Finally the frontispiece, with exquisite correspondence sharing the lineament of the elegant cornice,
in its first order then usurped the flat of the projected square; and last, a part of dentilled

The aforesaid gate was most diligently wrought in a levelled polish of cut stone panelling, the waving
figures conforming themselves in the joining of the panels. With a pleasing fitness of the inserted
works, and the material bright and gracious.

On the one side and the other, separated by two paces from the compass of the gate, there stood still
unmoved two great and proud columns, buried up to their footing in the roughness of the ruin. From
which, removing the ruin as far as I could, I laid bare the bronze bases, uncovering them; and of like
material were the Capitals, excellently cast. And measuring for pleasure the thickness of one Base and
doubling it, it expressed the entire diameter of the lowest thickness of the column. By which
measuring I found its height more than eight-and-twenty cubits.

The two near the gate were of finest Porphyry and of gracious Ophite; the other two channelled, or
fluted, or grooved, and most excellently ready. Beyond these, then, on the left and the right hand,
stood others in orderly fashion, paired in company, with modest swelling, of hardest Laconian stone.

The half-diameter of the lowest circuit of the column made the thickness of the Base, which consisted
of Tori, of a roundel, or Scotia, or Trochilus, and a Plinth. That half being divided by three, the
Plinth usurped one for itself, its breadth one diameter and a half. The two partitions being divided
again into four, one took the topmost Torus. The three being distributed into twin parts, one took the
lowest Torus, and the other was afforded to the hollowed Trochilus, with the edges taking a seventh
part of it. Such measurement I found elegantly observed by the skilled craftsmen.

Above the ruled capitals of the aforesaid columns there extended an excellent Beam, or Architrave,
with the lowest band adorned with rounded whorls, or berries; and the second with an association
lengthwise of truncated spindles, with two whorls pressed down in a row set between the one and the
other. The third, with approved comeliness, was clothed with Cymas turned like little ears, in noble
foliage with stalk-work of most approved expression.
