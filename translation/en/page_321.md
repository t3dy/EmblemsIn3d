## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The completion of the nine-square/octagon knot and its full
planting-key (cyclamen in the free squares, butcher's-broom bands, thyme in the cross,
and nine named herbs — laurel, tarragon, yarrow, groundsel, buckthorn, sowbread, marjoram,
maidenhair — one to each octagon), plus the detail that whitest-marble edging-plates fixed
in the ground wall each herb-cell. All secure.

- **Marble edging-plates set in the ground** — the herbs are kept to their cells by thin
  white-marble plates fixed edgewise in the soil, "walling round the simples." This is the
  construction detail that makes the knot read as clean coloured figure: mineral edging, not
  clipped box, holding each colour. A concrete rendering cue.
- **the second parterre fully keyed** — cyclamen fields, butcher's-broom and mountain-polium
  bands, thyme in the cross-cells, and a different herb in each of the nine octagons. With
  p. 320's key, both alternating parterres of this cloister are now fully specified.
- **"A show, by Jove, conspicuous"** — Poliphilo's exclamation; the parterres are the visual
  climax of the garden-rings, and Colonna wants the reader to register them as the marvel
  they are.
- The text now turns (p. 322) to the **altar and vase** at this parterre's centre, and then
  to the next cloister, whose beds spell out letters.
