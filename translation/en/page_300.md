## Notes

**Confidence: low.** This is the hardest page yet: a single continuous description
of one topiary-and-vase-and-fountain structure, in Colonna's most compressed
technical Italian, with several words that are either coinages or corrupt (`gampsado`,
the "haired" *pili*, `strongyla`). The overall shape is recoverable — box trained up
out of vases into arches, balls, an oval finial, and eagles — but individual clauses
are provisional, and I have rendered them literally with bracketed glosses rather
than smoothing. **A reviewer should treat this whole page as unsettled and work from
the facsimile woodcut, which shows the structure.**

- **The topiary fountain-tree in full.** The box is trained out of the antique
  vases and up into an elaborate tiered structure: arches ("triangles pierced"),
  hanging balls, a jewel or ring at the top, then six more stocks, a little cupola,
  an oval finial with four openings, and **a flying eagle perched at each corner**,
  crowned with a cone or globe. It is a piece of living architecture in clipped
  box, and the most virtuosic topiary in the book after the Hercules-labours grove.
- **`pili`** — "hairs," rendered literally, but here meaning the thin trained
  shoots of box drawn up in an even line; the whole vocabulary of the trained plant
  is borrowed from anatomy (hairs, belly, head), which is characteristic but makes
  the mechanics hard to follow.
- **the eagles "in the act, beak in prospect"** — poised as if about to fly, beaks
  forward. Left literal.
- This is the page most in need of the woodcut; the marked-low confidence is
  deliberate.
