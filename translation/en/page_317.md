## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The paving of the cloister-roads (a seven-band section: three
of black touchstone, one milk-white, two red, with tessellation), and the topiary in
the parterre-figures (cypress in the circles, pine in the rhombs, savin on the
friezes); then the gardeners tending it, surpassing Alcinous's gardens. All secure.

- **The paved radial roads, in banded stone.** Each road is a seven-band strip: three
  central bands of mirror-black touchstone, flanked by milk-white (Murano glass) and
  red (coral) bands, tessellated at the joints — "continued through all the following
  cloisters." A concrete paving-pattern for the world's radial roads.
- **cypress in the circles, pine in the rhombs, savin on the friezes** — the topiary
  scheme of the parterre: a tall cypress rises from every circular bed, a pine from
  every rhomb, and savin (*Juniperus sabina*) marks the friezes. The "divine mother
  who hid the calumny" with savin is Venus (or Smyrna/Myrrha, hidden as the myrrh-tree
  — Colonna's usual botanical myth).
- **surpassing the gardens of Alcinous** — the Phaeacian king's ever-bearing orchard
  (*Odyssey* 7), the classical touchstone for a perfect garden; Cythera's gardeners
  out-do it. The habitual surpassing-the-famous topos, applied to horticulture.
- This closes the **first** garden-cloister; the second (with orange-tree towers)
  begins on p. 318 after another seven steps.
