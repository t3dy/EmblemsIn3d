Upon the topmost step of the blackest stone stood forward an encompassing and elegant
little colonnade of *pycnostyle* spacing, its continuity interrupted, or intervalled,
by the rule of the breadth of the bridges, which observe that of the roads. These
excellent bridges were duly roofed by the immaculate pergola over the river; and
although the roads tended directly to the centre, the steps were not on that account
cut through from their whole circling. Then, as just said, above the ascent of the
steps there was, of the little columns, as much opening as the width of the paths
afforded. But the royal way tending straight to the gate of the wondrous circus,
opportunely excluded from the narrowing of the lines toward the centre, stood alone,
even, and uniform in its breadth, and its rise over the steps sloped most conveniently
walkable; and so in this place the stairs were necessarily found discontinuous.

Which pycnostyle, with its doubled little columns, the plinths of the bases — of
which, by an ant-like distribution, one made mutual contact with another at the angles
by a diagonal line, set into the straight faces with successive coupling — between
every two, one of alternated colouring of most shining outstanding jasper, and between
every seven, one square of red, capped with a translucent gilt-bronze sphere; and the
others likewise, bound and girt with a little architrave, frieze, and cornice of the
material and colour of the square one, with most decent lineaments. Above the little
columns' capitals, the interspaces were of chalcedony, six in number, one between two
of green jasper spotted with manifold flecks, with due and companionable distance.

Upon which most pleasant pycnostyle I saw, wantonly running about and pausing,
innumerable peacocks — whitening, red, and some fanned with their own colour, and
others with their much-admired feathers let down — and there, intermixed here and
there, all the species of parrots, with no mediocre adornment and delightful show upon
the said work.

The fronts of the steps were seen carved wonderfully with noble and Assyrian
knotwork; and for its greater relief, the intercarving was gracefully stuffed full of
material, in the red ones azure, and in the black ones with a whitening filling.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The technical description of the terrace-steps (red and black
marble), the pycnostyle colonnade crowning the top step, its doubled jasper and red
columns with gilt-bronze sphere finials, the peacocks and parrots, and the Assyrian
knotwork inlaid on the step-fronts. All secure; the architectural vocabulary
(*pycnostyle*, *dodrans*, plinth) is Vitruvian.

- **The colonnade on the terraces, and how the roads cut them.** The top step of the
  seven-step flight carries a *pycnostyle* (closely-spaced) colonnade of paired
  columns, broken only where the radial bridge-roads pass through. The one straight
  "royal way" to the central theatre-gate keeps a constant width and a walkable ramp;
  the other roads narrow toward the centre, so the steps there go discontinuous. This
  is precisely the ringed-terrace-plus-radial-road geometry of Segre's plan.
- **the gilt-bronze sphere finials and jasper columns** — every seventh column a red
  square topped with a gilt-bronze globe, the rest jasper: a rhythm of 1-in-7, the
  island's sevenfold number again (seven steps, seven stones, seven terraces).
- **peacocks and parrots on the colonnade** — the Juno-bird (peacock) and the
  many-coloured parrot crown the terrace, the aviary continuing up the rings.
- **Assyrian knotwork inlaid red-on-azure / black-on-white** — the interlace band on
  the step-fronts; a concrete pattern the world's terraces could carry as an inlay
  texture (cf. the world's `_knotTexture`).
