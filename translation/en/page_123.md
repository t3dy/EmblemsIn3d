"And when the most ancient doors are opened to you, in each of them she will present herself before
your eyes as one to come; and yet you will not know her, unless moderating prudence somewhat sees her
in a riddle, and with an upright and sincere judgement, and rather considers her — because in habit and
in aspect she changes herself ambiguously. And by this two-headed watching, man is very often deceived
without remedy of what he expected.

"That, then, Poliphilo, which these two girls of mine, entrusted and committed to you, shall counsel
you by their suggesting — and at which door you ought to enter and to stay, and which of them shall
most please you — you may hear and listen to, and freely assent to it, by my most excellent and
gratuitous gift and the leave I grant, because these have some knowledge of that." And this said, she
made a sign to the two, Logistica and Thelemia, who without waiting made themselves humbly her
servants. And I then, in the act of speaking — not daring, nor knowing how, before so lofty a presence
— gave her thanks for her great munificence.

The two appointed companions took me festively with a homely readiness and maidenly gestures, the one
by the right hand and the other by the left; and leave being reverently obtained first chiefly from the
Queen, and then from all. Going out by the same curtains and door, still eager and unsatisfied, I
turned back to the notable door to gaze wholly at the artful palace, admirable in the builder's art and
finished, the subtlety of which work no valid devising of mortals could imitate. For I fittingly
thought that sagacious nature herself had here singularly feigned, for admiration, so many delights of
her imperceptible workmanship — most apt for convenience, for use, and for grace and glory, and firm
and constant for perpetuity, and lasting.

For which cause I should exceedingly gladly have stayed a little while; but a follower of the appointed
guides and my companions, I could not; true it is that in a swiftest stealing of a glance, in the
border, or frieze, of that door, I saw noted this inscription: HO TES PHYSEOS OLBOS. And as much as I
could draw with my readiest senses, so much most gratefully I received, taking in that passage an
extreme delight, as much as is incredible to say. O happy, then, whoever should be granted to be always
in such a place a citizen, or a dweller.
