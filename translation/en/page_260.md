# Page 260 — Chapter XIX

> **ΤΟΜΟΚΟΥΡΕΙ / ΔΑΡΚΙΑ / ΑΡΤΕΜΕΙΣ / ΛΟΝ…ΧΟ** (TOMOKOUREI · DARKIA · ARTEMEIS · LON…CHO)
> *(A Greek inscription, fragmentary; the reading is uncertain.)*

Excited most-highly by so much comeliness of monuments, searching, to me an epigram somewhat perplexed, candid in
marble, I found — only the inscribed part of a little altar remaining; the rest, broken, lay on the ground:

> **· D ·        · M ·**
> **LYNDIA, THASIUS,**
> **PUELLA,  PUER.**
> **HIC SUM. SINE (TE)**
> **VIVERE NOLUI,**
> **MORI MALUI.**
> **AT, SI NORIS,**
> **SAT EST.**
> **· VALE ·**
>
> "To the shades. Lyndia, a girl; Thasius, a boy. Here I am. Without (thee) I would not live; to die I preferred. But,
> if thou (now) knowest — it is enough. Farewell."

With the greatest delectation and pleasure beholding these noteworthy fragments — avid, still more, searching, to find
other new (things) — thence, then, like an animal questing the ever-more-pleasing pasture, transferring myself through
the embankments of ruins, of huge chunks of columns, and some entire. Of which, wishing to know the proportion, one I
measured, extended on the ground, and from the socle to the contracture (fluting) I found, of its shaft, the tallness
seven diameters of its lowest thickness. There, near, offered itself to me a most-ancient sepulchre, without any
writing, in which, by a fracture, looking in, I saw only the funeral vestments and footwear, petrified. I conjectured
reasonably (it) of the sarcophagus-stone (of such effect) of the Troad of Asia — suspecting (it) of the cadaver of
Darius.

And nearby I saw a noble sepulchre of porphyry, exquisitely carved among sylvan little shrubs, of which offered itself
to me to read an elegant epitaph; and it had the cover in the form of an egregious little temple, scaled scale-wise —
one part of the said, over the ark, remaining, and the other lay cast down on the floor, and inscribed with such an
outstanding title.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium** (the Latin epitaph is secure; the Greek is fragmentary). More tombs: a broken **Greek
inscription**; the tiny paired epitaph of **Lyndia and Thasius** ("without thee I would not live; to die I preferred");
a huge fallen column measured (shaft = 7 diameters); a **body-consuming sarcophagus** (Troad stone) holding petrified
funeral clothes; and a porphyry sepulchre with a temple-shaped lid (its epitaph on p. 261). All secure.

- **Lyndia and Thasius — love-suicide, in miniature.** The shortest romance-epitaph: a girl and a boy, and one voice
  (the survivor, now dead too) saying "without thee I would not live; I chose to die." A love-death of *fidelity* (like
  Leontia & Lollius, p. 258) compressed to five lines. Perfect for the world's polyandrion — brief, buildable, moving.
- **the Troad sarcophagus** — the "flesh-eating stone" (*lapis sarcophagus* of Assos in the Troad, which Pliny says
  consumed bodies in forty days) holding only petrified clothes: Colonna's antiquarian precision, and a grim marvel
  (the stone that ate the corpse but left the shoes). The "cadaver of Darius" comparison places it in imperial legend.
- **the column measured** — Poliphilo the architect pauses even among the tombs to measure a fallen shaft (7:1
  proportion): the antiquarian's habit persists. The ruin is read as architecture and as memorial at once.
- **the temple-lidded sepulchre** — a tomb roofed like a little scaled temple (a *naiskos*): a buildable tomb-type for
  the world's polyandrion, its epitaph (Cornelia Annia) on p. 261.
