And at once, with royal largeness, drawing a golden ring from her ring-finger, with a stone anchitis:
"Take this," she said, "and you shall carry it gladly with you in memory of my friendly munificence."

At this exhortation and precious gift, being become as it were speechless, I certainly knew nothing
equivalent to say, nor how to give thanks. But she, kindly perceiving it, in a matronly way, and with a
native excellence and majestic gravity, turned to two distinguished and notable maidens, sitting near
her imperial throne; and to the one who sat commanding at the right side she said:

"Logistica, you shall be one of the two who go with our guest Poliphilo." And with a holy, religious
and venerable act she turned then to the left side, saying: "Thelemia, and you likewise shall go
together with him, and both give him to understand and to know clearly at which door he ought to stay.
Then, Poliphilo, they will present you to another Queen, very splendid and venerable. Who, if she shows
herself kind and bountiful to you, you shall be blessed; if the contrary, you shall be discontented.
Nevertheless in her face no one can grasp it, because sometimes she shows herself with a noble and
native courtesy and with a most charming pleasantness, and at other times of herself gloomy, malignant
and to be scorned, with an unsteady onset. She it is who ends everything. And for such a dark condition
she is not undeservedly named Queen Telosia; who dwells not in so proud and opulent a mansion as you
now plainly see me inhabit.

"I would have you know, however, that the highest Maker, nor ordered nature, could not give you a
greater treasure than to come to this my divine presence and wide munificence; wherefore artful nature
could not heap up greater riches than to obtain, and to win, my kindly favour, and to be a sharer in so
great a good. Whence, as you may exquisitely judge, no such treasure could ever be found in the world
in comparison with that which truly is found in me, a heavenly gift obtained by mortals.

"But Queen Telosia stays in a clouded place of hiding, and her dwelling has its approaches shut, since
she consents by no leave that men should discern how great and of what kind her beauty is; for it is
not lawful, nor permitted, that a divine beauty should appear to bodily eyes; and for such a reason the
effect of her outcome stands blind. But with a wonderful observance she transforms herself, changing
her skin and of many forms, unwilling to reveal herself unasked.
