Where, towards this building, I saw the gracious little hills of the valley rising ever higher, which
I saw joined to the aforesaid edifice; and it was a closure between the one mountain and the other,
and made an enclosed valley. Judging this thing shrewdly to be most worthy of beholding, I turned my
already hastened journey eagerly towards it without delay. And the nearer I came to it as I went, the
more I discovered a work huge and magnificent, and the desire to look upon it multiplied. For it no
longer appeared a lofty watch-tower, but rather a high Obelisk, founded upon a vast heap of stones.

The height of which incomparably exceeded the summits of the mountains on either side, however
famous a mountain I might reckon Olympus, or Caucasus, or Cyllene, to be. Having come eagerly even to
this deserted place, surrounded with a pleasure beyond imagining at beholding freely so great an
insolence of the builder's art, and a structure so immense, and an eminence so stupendous, I halted
quietly.

Looking upon and considering the whole mass and thickness of this fragmented and half-ruined
structure of white Parian marble: the squared and four-angled blocks fitted together without the glue
of cement, and equally set and placed, so polished and so exquisitely reddened at their edges as
could ever be done — so much so that between the one edge and the other, that is at the joints, no
thinness whatever, however sharpened, could have resisted being thrust in and yet have penetrated in
the least.

Here, then, I found so noble a colonnade, of every figuration, lineament and material as any man
could ever suppose: part broken down, part still in its place, and part preserved unhurt, with their
Architraves and with capitals excellent in device and in rough carving; Cornices, Zophori or
Friezes, arched Beams. Great fractures of statues, many of their bronzed and finished members struck
off. Basins, and Shells, and vases, of Numidian stone and of Porphyry and of various marble and
