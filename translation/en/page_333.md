seat and voluptuous meeting.

For some the hair was reddish, with much-admired wrappings girdling round the head with an
amicable springing floret. Some golden, more than leafy orpiment of outstanding lustre, and of
colour, with a decorous whirl composed tendril-wise to the nymphish and voluptuous adornment,
with a pretty adjunction of various and precious little stones, and to the expression of
extreme delight; some blackish, more than the anthracite plumes of Aesacus, in comely volume

conspicuous and gracious flight, with manifold curled knots, with much resplendence, with art
and artifice that exceeded every wanton study and nymphal voluptuousness — combedly combed,
and most-decorously composed. Little snares baited with the retaining, biting, and noxious
birdlime of amorous hearts. With whorls of inestimable value hung at the pierced ears, and
with most-precious necklaces and segments torqued and girt the straight and snowy throat, with
wicked collars sumptuously begemmed, with more excellent research of adornment, and in
decorating so much womanly affectation as could never subtly be excogitated.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The coiffure-passage: the companion-nymphs' hairstyles catalogued
exhaustively — braided napes, crown-whirls bound with pearl-tufts, fore-blown locks, pendant
foil darts behind the ears, hair flowing to the calves; reddish, golden and raven shades; then
the earrings and jewelled collars. All secure; a costume-ekphrasis of hair and neck-jewels.

- **Hair as a designed object.** Each style is a distinct construction: gathered nape-clumps,
  pointed crown-whirls pinned with pearl-tufts, tendril curls over the temples, veils and
  hairpins for the raven-haired. Colonna treats hairdressing as one of the luxury arts of the
  triumph, on a level with the goldsmithing.
- **"little snares baited with the… birdlime of amorous hearts."** The one interpretive line in
  the catalogue: all this ornament is a *trap* — the coiffure is fowler's lime for lovers'
  hearts. It reminds us the triumph is Cupid's, and its beauty is a weapon (cf. QUIS EVADET /
  NEMO, p. 328).
- **Aesacus** — a son of Priam turned to a diving bird (a "blackish plume"); another
  metamorphosis-simile, for the raven hair. The transformation-imagery persists even into a hair-
  colour comparison.
- Footwear completes the costume on p. 334.
