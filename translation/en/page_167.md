# Page 167 — Chapter XIV

The third heavenly triumph followed, with four turning wheels of Ethiopian chrysolite, flaming with
little sparks of gold — through which, being pierced, the bristle of a little ass puts malignant demons
to flight; welcome to the left hand — with all that is said above of wheels.

Then its slabs, set round about in the manner told above, were of green heliotrope of Cyprus, with
power over the heavenly lights, hiding the one who carries it and giving the gift of divining, spotted
with little bloody drops.

The right-hand tablet offered this history carved. A man notable for royal majesty prayed in a sacred
temple to the divine image, for what should follow from his most shapely daughter. The father, hearing
of his own casting out of the kingdom by her, and that she should not be got with child by any man,
made a fortified building of a lofty tower, and had her shut in it with a solemn guard. In which,
sitting idle, with an excessive solace she saw drops of gold distil into her maiden lap.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The third car, and Danaë shut in the tower. The two stones carry their whole
lapidary tradition into the English correctly, which is worth noting: Ethiopian chrysolite, bored
and strung on an ass's bristle, drives off demons (Pliny 37.42), and the Cyprian heliotrope,
blood-spotted, hides its bearer and gives the gift of divination. The panel itself is accurately
told. Medium because Acrisius is not named, and the oracle he consults — that his daughter's son
will cast him out — is compressed into a clause whose English ("and that she should not be got with
child by any man") reads as part of the prophecy rather than as the father's own intention.

*Confidence stated 2026-09-20 by a second reader, not by the translator (ticket tr-confidence-unstated-180).*
