mole of a harbour nearby. In the fractures of which, and left joints, the salt-loving and littoral *crithmum*

by many uneven steps, one ascended to the suggestum of the propylaeum of the temple. Which building, by voracious
time and by putrid antiquity, and by negligence, to the humid earth collapsed, here and there demolished, had
left without capitals the shaft, or decapitated trunk, of some huge columns of Persian stone of red grain; some,
with mutual alternation of Mygdonian marble; some had the contracture (fluting) broken — the *hypothesis*
(base), nor the *hypotrachelion*, nor Astragal was seen; some, again, I beheld of bronze, of marvellous art

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The lovers come to the ruined **Polyandrion** — the seaside tomb-temple: white-marble
ruins by a wrecked harbour, its beach thick with shore-herbs (samphire, glasswort, sea-wormwood, eryngo), its
decapitated columns of red Persian granite, Mygdonian marble and bronze; Polia explains it was once a great
festival-temple, now abolished, **dedicated to Pluto and holding the tombs of those who died of unhappy love**,
honoured yearly at the Ides of May. All secure; the direct source for the world's polyandrion station.

- **The Polyandrion, in the book's own words.** A "many-men's" tomb (Greek *polyandrion*) by the sea:
  once a thronged festival-temple, now a ruin of headless columns, holding "many little tomb-pits" of those
  "who died of unhappy love," dedicated to Pluto and honoured at the Ides of May. This is exactly the world's
  `_buildPolyandrion` station (folio 242 in the world's notes) — here is its founding description: a
  collapsed marble tomb-temple of failed lovers, the dark counterpart to the Temple of Venus just left.
- **the deliberate contrast.** Colonna sets the ruined tomb of love's *victims* immediately after the temple
  of love's *fulfilment*: the blessed union (pp. 216–233) and the graves of "wicked, unlucky, lugubrious love"
  face each other. The Polyandrion is the memento within the love-story — those who did not persevere (cf.
  Polia's maxim, p. 289). The world's polyandrion carries this meaning.
- **the shore-herbs, catalogued.** Samphire, cakile, glasswort, sea-wormwood, eryngo, purslane, sea-rocket:
  a precise littoral flora for the ruin's setting. Colonna botanises the graveyard as he botanised everything.
- **red Persian granite, Mygdonian marble, bronze columns** — a concrete material palette for the ruin, "of
  decay offended… under the open sky": buildable as weathered coloured-stone stumps. The epitaphs the ruin
  holds occupy chapter XIX (pp. 242+).
