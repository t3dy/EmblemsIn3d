association, one gold twist standing forth above two silken, with exquisite little knotting
where they met. Among which the circular and resplendent gems were variously sewn in order,
according as the variety of the garments is told. Others, a golden diadem divinely binding the
head.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** A long costume-ekphrasis: the companion-nymphs' gems matched to their
dress-colours (emerald on green, ruby, sapphire, diamond, pearl), the range of silks (shorn,
villous, cloth-of-gold, twice-dipped Tyrian, cotton), and the jewelled fillets, hairnets,
tiaras and diadems of their headdresses. All secure; a pure inventory of luxury dress, in the
book's most concentrated jeweller's-catalogue manner.

- **Gems keyed to colours.** Colonna matches each stone to its ground: emeralds on green,
  rubies on leek-green, sapphires on saffron, diamonds on crimson, pearls on amethyst/red — a
  colour-theory of jewels. The nymphs are dressed by a rule, not at random.
- **the fabrics** — a full textile catalogue: pile-velvets in three and four grades, brocaded
  cloth-of-gold-and-silver, zoned (banded) gowns cycling gold/cyan/green/silver, oyster-purple,
  twice-dipped Tyrian, and figured cotton. The company is a walking display of the loom's whole
  range.
- **the six-pearl rose** — a recurring head-ornament: six thick pearls set as a rose around a
  central conoid jewel, worn on the fillet over the forehead. A concrete jewel the world could
  model on the triumph's figures.
- This costume-passage runs on through pp. 333–334 (hair, then footwear); it is descriptive
  rather than architectural, but it establishes the visual key of the whole procession: extreme,
  rule-governed luxury.
