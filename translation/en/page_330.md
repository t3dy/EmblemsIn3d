## Notes

**Confidence: medium** (high for the inscription). The gloved dancers and their gold-thread
work, then the two lead standards of the triumph: the boat's own standard, and the great
**celibar** crowned with a winged, unveiled Cupid drawing his bow and treading an apple,
inscribed on both faces **ΔΟΡΥΚΤΗΤΟΙ** — "the spear-won." All secure; the standard's tiered
ornament is described in exhaustive goldsmith's detail.

- **ΔΟΡΥΚΤΗΤΟΙ — "the spear-won."** The Greek term for captives taken in war as spoils; on
  Cupid's standard it names *us* — Poliphilo and Polia, and all lovers, as Love's prisoners
  of war. It answers the trophies of disarmed gods: they are the arms, we are the captives.
  The world's triumph-standard could carry this Greek title.
- **the lead standard: Cupid drawing the bow, treading the apple.** The image atop the
  *celibar* is the god himself in action — unveiled, shooting, the apple (Venus's fruit)
  underfoot — the emblem the whole procession serves. This is the visual key to the triumph.
- **the gloved dancers** — even the dancers' needle-worked gloves and jewelled arm-bands are
  itemised; the entire train is a single coordinated work of luxury art.
- Psyche, Cupid's divine bride, is presented next (p. 331), leading the file of companions.
