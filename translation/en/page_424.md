sweetest kisses and amorous strainings, with firm and faithful espousal mutually, one to the other, and with much and
festive gladness. Poliphilo, extremely content, went to his journey; and I tended to the desired home-going.

And thus I, of love ardently sub-agitated, with moderate pace, and with the soul acting manifold cupidine operations, at
the contiguous regress at last of the desired palace returned — but of another quality, immuted. And there, hilarious and
most-pleasant, into the conscious and peculiar bedchamber entering, I saw no more the image of the Goddess Diana offer
itself; and, in the imaginative (faculty), it began to vacate. And, introduced the benign effigy of my sweetest Poliphilo,
solely, chiefly, of him I pondered, and, in every corner of my heart fixed, dominant, efficaciously I felt him. Whence
proceeded such an effect:

I, being sole, and my mind consociated, and in amorous captivity partaker finding itself — could cogitate no other, of
this, than of the most-desired Poliphilo. For so much, to my sedentary and accustomed exercises devoted — breathing (upon
me) the inciter Cupid — I set myself, of crimson silk, to form a little Heart, wormwork-sewn, with expression such as, in
that my heart, artfully Love depicted; the border of the circumference of which adorning with lucent pearls; and in the
middle of which, then, with his beautiful and most-pleasing (name), and my sealed and consigned name. This is the first
Greek figures (letters) (asked by him), joined, of millet-pearls, and expressed — so much the more perfectly, as Love,
present impeller, ruled me. And also I made a little torque (chaplet) of gold threads, and of green silk —

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** Polia is **beaten and banished** from Diana's temple (the nymph Algerea seizing her, Polia
fleeing in torn veils), but the lovers, "the enkindled love super-abounding," care nothing for the disgrace; they part with
**mutual vows and kisses**, and Polia returns home **utterly transformed** — Diana's image gone from her mind, Poliphilo's
installed — and begins making **love-tokens** (a crimson-silk heart sewn with pearls and their names). All secure; the
transformation completed.

- **From Diana to Poliphilo — the image swapped.** The clearest sign of her conversion: in her chamber, "the image of the
  Goddess Diana offers itself no more… and, introduced the effigy of my Poliphilo, solely of him I pondered." The idol of
  chastity is replaced by the beloved. Polia is now wholly Venus's/love's. The interior transformation the whole book
  worked toward is complete.
- **love triumphs over disgrace.** Beaten, stripped, exiled, publicly shamed — and Polia does not care, "for the enkindled
  love super-abounded." The love that cost Poliphilo his life (and near-cost hers) now outweighs every punishment. The
  fidelity-thread (his, in Book I) is now hers too: love that endures all shame.
- **the love-tokens.** Polia makes a pearl-sewn crimson heart bearing their entwined names in Greek letters, and a chaplet
  of gold and her own hair (p. 425): the crafts of a woman in love, the reverse of her former cold "sedentary exercises."
  A tender, domestic detail (and the hair-chaplet answers her earlier tearing of her hair in grief, p. 404 — now given in
  love).
- **the vows and parting** — the lovers betroth themselves ("firm and faithful espousal, mutually") and part joyfully:
  the union Book I's temple-rite formalised (p. 218) here has its private origin. Book II is filling in the history behind
  Book I's dream.
