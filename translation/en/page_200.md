# Page 200 — Chapter XVII

In this intervalled void, between one Pillar and the other — or between the jaws of these arches just-
said — upon the left mural plane, excavated was the window. Which vault, as much as it was distant from
the Pier, so much it circled under the first outer cornice, encompassing the first roof. Ten Piers, or
bones, of the building there were, and as many arches in the solid, with the exterior wall — excepting
the face where, set fast, resided the chapel (sacello).

The said cornice, embracing the round sacello, joined it with the temple. Beyond this binding ascended
its roof, which was a blind cupola, distinct and free of the greater one by itself.

Let us return, then, now, to the interstitial binding above the circum-columnation, or Peristyle — of
the straight architrave, fascia and cornice, stretched perpendicularly above the Porphyry columns —
where there were born, for each stretch of the binding, little half-columns of quadrature, striated, of
egregious Ophite. Upon the semi-capital of which went round a cornice of conspicuous lineament, from
which the convex of the lofty cupola began its beginning above.

Between one and the other of the half-quadrangular (pilasters) just described, I saw a proportioned
window, timely situated, and of a plate of Bononia (Bologna) of Gaul, opaque, constituted in a field
gilt, of artful Mosaic. Which spaces, with commensurate distribution and equal partition, I beheld most-
admiredly depicted, of wormwork tessellation: the property of each Month of the year, with its effect;
and above, the Zodiac occurring, with the Sun operating; and, to that of the Moon, the phases — and she
is called, among the monthly (states), the new little crescent, the halved, and the pre-swollen (full) —
and her circuit, by which the months are included. And the windings of the investigating Sun, the
solstices and the winter-turns, circling; and of night and of day the vicissitude; and the quadrifarious
commotion of the seasons; and the nature of the fixed and wandering stars, with their efficacy. I
suspected that such art was here ordained by the most-noble mathematician Petosiris, or by Necepso. The
inspection of which things (was) enough to draw the inspector, with excessive solace, to a choice and
wondrous speculation — not without egregious spectacle, and elegant fiction, and comely distribution of
figures, with defined painting of colouring and shadings, by which the commodulation of the bodies and
requisite lights, to complacency representing themselves with most-worthy expression of the effects, with
laudable and joyous show to the senses of the soul. A work, without doubt, worthy of pondering, as much
as ever one could contemplate. Then, in one division, the content of the meaning of the aforesaid works,
of antiquarian letters, was elegantly inscribed. The spaces interposed between the half-quadrangulars
were hedged round with bands, carved of comely workmanship.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The completion of the wall (ten piers, ten arches, the round sacello with its
own **blind cupola** joined to the temple), and — the page's real subject — the **upper windows glazed
with mosaic depicting the twelve Months, the Zodiac, the Sun and Moon's phases, the solstices, and the
seasons**: a full calendar-astronomy programme in the clerestory, ascribed to the Egyptian astrologers
Petosiris and Nechepso. All secure.

- **The temple's clerestory is a calendar.** The upper windows are gilt-ground mosaic panels, one per
  bay, showing each month with its labour, its zodiac sign, the Sun's course, the Moon's phases, the
  solstices and the four seasons — a complete cosmological cycle ringing the drum, captioned in "antique
  letters." For a temple of *Venus Physizoa* (generative nature), a zodiac-and-months programme is exactly
  right: the goddess of natural generation under the wheel of the year. A ready decorative programme for
  the world's temple drum.
- **Petosiris and Nechepso** — the legendary Egyptian king-and-priest authors of Hellenistic astrology;
  invoking them signals the windows as serious astronomical iconography, not mere ornament. Colonna's
  antiquarian sourcing again (cf. Macrobius for Serapis, p. 344).
- **the double dome** — the round rear sacello has its own "blind cupola," distinct from the temple's
  great dome: a subsidiary domed sanctuary attached to the rotunda. This is the Adytum promised on p. 199,
  the temple's inner holy place.
- **"Bologna glass"** — *lamina de Bononia*, a translucent/opaque plate used as the mosaic ground: a
  real early-modern material reference, grounding the fantasy in known craft.
