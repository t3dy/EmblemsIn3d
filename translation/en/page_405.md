Now, both — the hour being arrived that the whitening Cynthia had left the Lamian Rocks and the condense woods, and put
an end to the solacing huntings — finally (the bedchamber closed and hedged), we went together to the nocturnal
quiescence. And there, the pulsing breast, that still with unquiet beatings frequently beat — scarce, in itself, gathered
the frightened and bewildered little spirits, and, with supreme fatigues and efforts, the large course of the round and
dripping tears re-shut, somewhat interdicted — I began, badly and with difficulty (frequently, by frightful
interruptions, awakened), to sleep.

And, in the deep and first, soporous and soft sleep immersed, the loosened and shattered little body, suffused, slept in

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The day of dread passes; Polia, unable to fathom the vision and afraid to sleep alone, calls her
**nurse** to her bed (the nurse "in place of a parent," her only confidante, since she had lived only "with Diana"); she
falls into a broken sleep and **dreams her chamber's bolts are smashed and doors broken open** — the second vision
beginning. All secure.

- **The nurse — the human helper.** Polia's turn to her nurse is the first step of her rescue: the confidante who will
  give the "useful counsel" (argument, p. 398) that interprets the vision and turns her toward love. The nurse is Book
  II's guide-figure (as the temple-priestess was Book I's). Her presence in the bed makes the dream-terror witnessed and
  survivable.
- **from Diana to human company.** Polia notes she had, till now, been "alone, with my chaste Goddess Diana" — the
  isolation of her vowed chastity. Fear drives her to *human* comfort (the nurse), the first breach in her Diana-bound
  solitude. The conversion from cold chastity to warm love begins socially, before it becomes erotic.
- **the dream-invasion.** The second vision comes as a nightmare (the first was a waking transport): her locked chamber
  is violated, executioners breaking in (p. 406). The doubling (waking vision + dream) intensifies the terror to the
  breaking-point. Colonna structures her conversion as escalating visions, mirroring Poliphilo's escalating wonders.
- **Eumetes's stone** — a heavy sleep-stone (an obscure allusion): her sleep is leaden, then shattered by the dream-din.
