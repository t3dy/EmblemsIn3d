I came to the part of the aforesaid opening of the mouth by climbing another solid and straight
stair, which at the Airy footing of the building, towards the right-hand side flanking the
sheared-off mountain, was excavated within the rock itself, where the interval of the ten paces was.
By which — more curious, certainly, than perhaps was lawful — I climbed. Where, having come to the
going-in by the mouth to the stair, and having at last climbed by innumerable steps or stairs,
circling about, not without grave labour and dizziness of the head, above so unimaginable a
loftiness: my eyes could not fitly bear to look down at the level ground, so much so that everything
below appeared to me imperfect. And for this I did not dare to leave the middle floor.

And there, round about the circular and topmost exit — that is, the end of the winding stair and its
opening — many spindled posts of metal were neatly disposed and fixed in a circuit, the spacing of
which, from centre to centre, divided one foot, and half a pace in height. Girded above with a little
waving cornice going round about, of the said cast material, which surrounded and fenced the lip of
the opening and gape of the upper exit of the said stair — excepting that part by which one went out
onto the surface. Rightly judged, so that no one less cautious should fall headlong into the opening
of the winding cave; seeing that the immoderate height induced a reeling.

Beneath, then, the sloping level of the Obelisk, a bronze tablet was leaded in, lying face upward,
with ancient writing in letters of our own, and in Greek and Arabic; by which I fully understood that
it was dedicated to the highest Sun. And of the whole vast structure the measurement also was
entirely noted and described. And the name of the architect, above the Obelisk, noted in Greek:

LICHAS THE LIBYAN, STONE-BUILDER, SET ME UP.
