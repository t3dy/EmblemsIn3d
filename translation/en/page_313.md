# Page 313 — Chapter XXI

Upon the aforesaid most admirable banks, on one side and the other, were inlay-wise
distributed bitter-oranges, citrons and lemons. From trunk to stock the interval
three paces. Which stocks, a pace high from the ground, began to spread their
branches, meeting one with another, and most excellently entangling, rendered an
arch, from earth to the topmost bend three paces.

The remaining branches then, from one bank to the other over the river, opposite one
another, with artful conjoining and social embrace, vaulting and enveloped, pleasantly
shaded — in a most excellent pergola-work. The surrounding of which, with leafy
density elegantly heaped, with the show of an even shearing, or clipping, one frond
not exceeding another save with utmost grace and loveliness, rendering most placid
shade, and, by the sweet breath of Favonius, tremulous and fanning, full of
nourishment, that made its greenery lustrous, as in the first sprouting they showed
themselves — with the whitening little flowers, and pendent fruits, fit hiding-places
of the plaintive Philomela, ever most sweetly lamenting, singing, perfectly without
echo, by the daughter Glaucopis of high and lightning Jove made clean and most
purged, resounding. Which pergola-work, from the topmost convex down to the raised
water, stood seven paces. Oh, with what allurement and sweetest suggestion to its
contemplation it easily drew the wandering eyes! For in that river ran certain
skiffs and little boats of golden material, delightfully covered, with many long-
haired girls, fair-tressed and Alphesiboean, wreathed with fragrant and various
flowers, rowing — in limpid and crisp and saffron shifts, or smocks, everywhere
edged with gold, over the nymphish nude, clothed and girt with a wanton adornment,
not impeding the sight to offer voluptuously the rosy flesh covered; nay, by the
spring breezes voluptuously impelled and pressed upon the admirable women, it
excellently revealed the delightful little form and any other part according to its
motion, with the whitest and half-appled breasts unclosed to the rounding of the
little nipples with the greatest and voluptuous grace, elegantly girded with gemmed
gold Phrygian bands — with many youths of every warlike nation, gesticulating,
brawling, rushing among themselves in play, laughing at the naval fight, contending
persistently against those attacking them; intently keen on the spoils, they
overturned their skirts and carried them off; the girls remained despoiled and
stripped in the waters without favour, nor made resistance against their harm, but
festively solaced themselves. These being then left, mocking among themselves, made
new fights one with another, submerging the seized little boats; and so the submerged

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The citrus-pergola over the river and the boating-game of
nymphs and youths are secure; the long sentence describing the girls' wind-pressed
shifts is euphemistic-erotic in the original and rendered at the same level.

- **The citrus pergola over the river.** Bitter-orange, citron and lemon planted on
  both banks three paces apart, their branches trained to arch and meet over the
  water into a continuous fruiting tunnel seven paces high — the *vaulted pergola of
  citrus trees trained into architecture* of Segre's plan (GARDENS.md §5), and
  exactly what the world's Cythera renders as the eight citrus-pergola arches over
  the ring-river. Here is its full description: a shaded, fanning, flowering tunnel,
  the nightingale's covert.
- **the mock naval battle of the boating nymphs** — girls in gold-edged shifts row
  golden skiffs while youths capsize them in a playful water-fight, the girls
  "despoiled and stripped in the waters… but festively solaced themselves." A
  Venus-island erotic game, staged on the ring-river. The world's river is empty of
  them; a future pass could add the boats.
- **"Philomela… by the daughter Glaucopis of Jove made clean"** — the nightingale
  (Philomela, tongueless in myth) here sings "without echo," her voice purified by
  Glaucopis (grey-eyed Athena). The transformation-birds recur even here.
- **Favonius** — the west wind of spring; **Alphesiboean** — an epithet from
  Theocritus for beautiful girls "who bring in many oxen" (as bride-price).
