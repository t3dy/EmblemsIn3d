Scarcely had I finished those devout prayers, sincerely made, with the heart united in prayer,
contrite and shaken, and bathed in tears — holding firmly that the gods come to the aid of a good
mind — when without delay I found myself, unawares, outside that narrow, harsh and overshadowing
wood. And as if come forth into a new day out of the humid night, my shadowed eyes could not for a
while endure the lovely light. All wan and mournful and anxious, it seemed to me nonetheless that I
had come to the light I had longed for, as one might come out of a blind prison, stripped of heavy
and troubling chains, and issue from murky darkness.

All thirsting, torn, my face and hands bloodied and blistered by the biting Nettle, feeling myself
half dead, I counted nothing else as anything against the gracious light. So thirsty was I that the
fresh airs could not cool me, nor fitly satisfy my dry heart; most greedy to swallow the empty saliva
that had dried within me. But when I had somewhat reassured myself, and recalled a little spirit into
me — my breast parched with repeated sighs and with anxiety of spirit and with bodily labour — I
resolved by every means to quench that arid thirst. And therefore, searching carefully over that
shore to find some water, and seeking long and very wearily, there offered itself to me most timely a
most joyful spring, with a great vein of water freshly rising up. The place of it the marsh Acorus
and the Silvan's beard disclosed to me, and the flowering Loosestrife, and the musky Masterwort.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The prayer answered, the exit from the wood, and the finding of the spring.
Secure in the main: the *Medioximi* correctly given as the middle gods between those above and
below; Achaemenides begging Aeneas; the stream gathering rills and torrents. Two soft places, both
named rather than smoothed. The four plants at the spring-head (*palustri Achori*, *barba Silvana*,
*fiorita Lisimachia*, *muscariata Imperatoria*) are given by their standard equivalents — sweet
flag, loosestrife, masterwort — but *barba Silvana* is not a settled name and is left as "the
Silvan's beard". And *candidati nel algente monstro de Pana* is rendered literally as "made white in
the freezing monster of Pan"; the monster of Pan is most likely the goat-fish **Capricorn**, i.e.
the mountains white in the depth of winter, which the literal English hides.

*Confidence stated 2026-09-20 by a second reader, not by the translator (ticket tr-confidence-unstated-180).*
