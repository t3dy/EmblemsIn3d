In this same manner were all the hems of the gown, with the tiniest little darts of gold leaf, hanging
unsteadily, most comelily dispensed in many places. Above the one side and the other the gown was
unsewn, or cut apart, and joined in three places by three little pins, which were three thickest pearls
braided with dark blue silk — such as Cleopatra did not have to dissolve in a draught; and with such an
order was that parting bound, showing her shift between the distance of one pearl and another.

About her straight and milk-white neck there went an artful thread of forced gold. Which parted upon
the nymphlike breast, narrowing then sharply to meet across it, woven in fine work, and plentifully
adorned with precious gems. Beneath this garment, as is said above, her thinnest crimped overdress
covered, of white silk of the smallest workmanship, which covered that precious flesh, like purpling
roses, in the parting of her spacious and delicious breast. To my eyes more welcome than the fresh
streams to the tired and hunted stag, more delightful than to Cynthia the fishing boat of Endymion, and
the sweet lyre to Orpheus.

The sleeves of which shift, fittingly wide, near the wrists of her bound hands, were girt in and drawn
by a little golden hem pinned with two thick pearls to each, of an eastern whiteness. Then, beyond all
these most welcome things, I gave stubborn attention, with stealing and busy glances, to gazing with
delight at the unruly little swellings, impatient beneath the pressing of the thinnest garment. Those,
then, I judged not unfittingly, that so great a dignity of notable work the maker had formed most
beautifully with every diligence for himself alone and for his own extreme delight, and had gathered
there every force of love.

Perhaps such were the four winged golden figures bound to the royal palace of Babylon, called the
tongue of the gods, not so forcible to reconcile spirits to the love of the King as I felt in these.
Alas, that they would scarcely have filled the hollow of a hand, with the fairest spacing that the
nature of life could feign.
