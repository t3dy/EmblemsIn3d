At the last I considered discreetly what opposed and obstinate resistance of vaults could ever
sustain or support beneath it, and what Hexagonal and four-square Piers, and what smallness of
columning could firmly, set beneath, endure so great a heaviness and intolerable weighing. By which
reasoning I judged rightly that either the whole remained solid and massive, the mountain being
subject beneath it, or else it was one compacted heap of glued cement and gravel and rough stonework.
By such consideration I explored through the ample gate. And I saw that within there was thick
darkness and hollowness. Which gate, together with the wonderful and proud building — things worthy of
an eternal monument — will be somewhat described in what follows, as it was excellently disposed.
