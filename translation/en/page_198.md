asked — with base and capitals of fine metal caustically gilt and translucent, with elegant *enthesis*,
or swelling. This order, then, of projection was regularly observed throughout all the Porphyry
(columns), balanced and paired, which had to overstand the requisite little wall of the columnation. But
the illustrious Architect, to give the Area a freer expedition, made the intercolumniation passable.

Whence the inflected architraves, with their horns, rested upon the Ophite (serpentine) columns, turned
and lathe-round, illustrable with friction, the due little slab, or plinth, set upon the capital to give
it a solid footing, and not aerial, through the lids insinuated of the said capitals. And under the base
was reserved a half-altar which had to be the little wall, upon which half-altars the bases of all the
Ophite (columns) firmly resided.

The Corinthian (columns) footed upon a sub-column, or truly a sub-base, of the form of a half-Tube, here
and there, with the half-Altars becomingly joined. Which was worked out from two squarings found from the
diameter of the lowest thickness of the Corinthian; there remained a *sesquialter* (one-and-a-half) for
the rest, to little-waves, Tori, Gulets, Knots, and Cymas, or the like lineaments, above and below,
conveniently dispensed, decently conjoined, with the free bases assigned above.

Each Arch stood elegantly decorated of its keystone, with little boys, and alternately of little barks
with flowered foliage. And every triangle (spandrel) specularly shone with a round jasper of varied
colour, its setting egregiously circled, of little-waved foliage translucently gilt.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The temple's inner elevation: **ten arches on serpentine columns**, with
**porphyry Corinthian columns** (nine diameters) between them carrying gilt-bronze capitals and a full
entablature; the columns footed on half-altar sub-bases; and each arch keyed with putti-and-foliage
keystones, each spandrel set with a round gilt jasper. All secure; a Vitruvian order-description of a
domed rotunda's inner ring.

- **The order of the rotunda.** A ring of ten arches on green serpentine columns, paired with red
  porphyry Corinthian columns (gilt-bronze capitals and bases), the intercolumniations left open "to give
  the Area a freer expedition" — a spacious, open-arcaded interior. The green-and-red stone scheme is the
  same as the Cythera theatre-gate (p. 347); the world's temple can use it.
- **jasper roundels in the spandrels.** Every arch-spandrel carries a round gilt-framed jasper disc of
  varied colour — a rhythmic polychrome accent along the arcade. A concrete, renderable ornament.
- **the tenfold division.** The "ten arches / ten columns" realise the ten radial divisions set out on
  p. 197: the temple's whole plan is governed by the decad, as Cythera was by the number seven.
- **Vitruvian vocabulary throughout** — *enthesis* (entasis), *sesquialter* proportion, tori and cymas,
  "the order of projection regularly observed": Colonna writes as an architect, and the description is a
  genuine (if ornate) setting-out. Rendered with the technical terms kept.
