Upon the flat of the crown, somewhat inclined, ascended a plinth, with manifold ornaments. In its flat, in the

The encompassing lip, of metal, a palm in evened breadth, was comelily bossed. On its upper circle stood straight a
perfect **nude image, of gilt metal**, with the right holding a little spear, and in the other an antiquarian
*clypeus* (shield) of egregious lineament carved; on the one and the other side of this rounding — one here and the
other there, with the back propped, sitting upon the flat — two winged boys, toward the projections, held a kindled
torch.

On the slope of the aforenarrated plinth, likewise sitting back-to-back, were two nude and winged infants, upon the
flat of the crown; with the hands in view they retained an apple, and with the inner arms seized a most-ancient
bronze **candelabrum**, most-lucent, gilt, in the form of a vase, by a dolphin handle — which handles were two up-
curved, biting dolphins at a knot, and, with the tail, derived, hooked, over the body of the vase, thinning to the
shell-shaped gap, with two other knots toward the orifice; which, somewhat in circular dilatation, upon the lip, in
order, had fixed four sharp pins, and in the middle one, that exceeded the others, with the foot-stalk between the
shins of the boys.

All this sculpture, founded, sat upon a square Ophite (base) rising from the pavement, nude of lineament, except that
in the middle, carved, I saw a **maritime, or naval, trophy** (I thought, in monument of the victory attained, the
fleet of the Rhodians vanquished): a rostrum, or part of the beaked prow, of a most-ancient ship; in the middle of
which stood forth a trunk, in the branches of which was invested a military cuirass, and, by the brachial outlet, the
branches extended, truncated of tips; to one of the said, hung by the ring, was a shield; on the other, a naval
instrument; under the cuirass, transversely, on the trunk, hung an anchor and a rudder; upon the top of the trunk,
where the collar came out, a crested helmet was most-beautifully set. All which things it is not to be believed were,
without symmetry and greatest artifice, exquisitely, with all the requisite lineaments, expressed, and most-
excellently polished — worthy of noteworthiness and eternal memory. The commensuration of which, whoever knows the
proportion of the *sesquialter* (3:2) will easily conduct it perfect. I suspected that by one (workshop) the sculptors
of the Mausoleum and this (were) perfected.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium** (the *Eros Katoptron* inscription is secure). The completion of the Artemisia tomb: a black
**"Mirror of Love"** (a mirror-stone inscribed *EROTOS KATOPTRON*), a gilt nude with spear and shield, torch-bearing
winged boys, a **dolphin-handled candelabrum**, and a **naval trophy** commemorating a victory over the Rhodian fleet
— all in the same hand as the Mausoleum's sculptors. All secure.

- **"The Mirror of Love."** A black mirror-stone set in the tomb, inscribed *Eros Katoptron* — the mirror in which
  love sees itself (as the obsidian floor of Cythera reflected the fountain, p. 352; the Adytum floor mirrored the
  worshippers, p. 220). Set on Artemisia's tomb, it makes her drinking-of-the-ashes (p. 265) a mirror held up to
  faithful love. A resonant, buildable object for the world's polyandrion.
- **the naval trophy and the Rhodians.** Artemisia (I of Caria, or here conflated) is credited with a sea-victory
  over Rhodes; the tomb carries a *tropaion* of captured ship's-arms (rostrum, cuirass, shield, anchor, rudder,
  helmet on a trunk) — the same trophy-of-arms form as Cupid's triumph (p. 326), here martial. The queen is warrior
  as well as widow.
- **the dolphin candelabrum** — biting dolphins forming the handle of a gilt-bronze lamp-vase: Colonna's marine
  ornament (dolphins recur — the crystal lamp, p. 208; the Aldine device). Buildable to the knot.
- **"the same sculptors as the Mausoleum."** Colonna ties this tomb to the historical Mausoleum's famous sculptors
  (Scopas, Bryaxis, et al.) — placing the Polyandrion's grandest tomb in real art-history. The proportion (3:2) is
  handed over for building.
