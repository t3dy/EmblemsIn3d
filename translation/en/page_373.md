## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The cameo-carving conceit is the difficulty: Venus is
carved from a banded three-colour sardonyx so that the layers of the stone
supply the flesh (white onyx vein), the drapery (red vein), and the seat (dark
sardine vein). The description of which vein does what is dense and continues onto
p. 374; the sense is secure but the exact anatomy of the drapery is intricate.

- **The gushing golden serpent** — a fountain-jet dressed as a snake, deliberately
  coiled so the water is throttled and does not overshoot the basin. A working
  hydraulic detail given a mythic skin, entirely characteristic.
- **Venus carved *puerpera***, as a woman who has just given birth — an unusual
  and pointed epithet at the tomb of Adonis: the goddess of love shown in
  childbed over the grave of her dead lover, birth and death in one image. The
  figure is developed further on p. 374 (she is nursing Cupid).
- **the three-coloured sardonyx** — the ancient cameo material, whose natural
  bands a carver exploits to give figures of one colour on a ground of another.
  Colonna imagines a single stone whose layers become flesh, veil and throne — an
  impossible cameo, and his highest praise. The "secret of nature" the red vein
  veils is, at its plainest, the pudenda; the phrase is the standard learned
  euphemism.
