# Page 220 — Chapter XVII

And there, the golden valves unsealed, together they entered. But I affirmed myself upon the holy and
reverend threshold. And with vigilant eyes, immovably fixed on the most-beloved object, respecting, I saw the
admonitress bidding that my Polia — true *myropolis* (perfume-seller/sweet one) — should genuflect upon the
sumptuous pavement, and, with sincere devotion, prostrate herself.

Which pavement was marvellous, all of gems, stony, orbitally composed, with subtle make, with manifold and
elegant knotting politely distinct — a work bonily tessellated, disposed in greening leaves, and flowers, and
little birds, and other animals, according as opportune was the grateful colour of the precious stones,
splendid, shining, with perfect evening — from which, doubled, it re-showed those who had entered.

Upon this, then, my little-bold Polia, having religiously denuded the milky knees, with the utmost elegance
genuflected — more beautiful than ever Mercy saw (knees) dedicated to herself. For which thing I stood
suspensefully attentive, with silent lips. And, so as not to interrupt the holy offerings, and contaminate the
propitiations, and interrupt the solemn prayers, and the mysterious ministry, and perturb the altar-
ceremonies, the wicked sighs, by valid love inflamed, I duly incarcerated.

Now, before a sanctified Altar — in the middle of the little sanctuary laboriously situated, of divine flame
lucent — genuflected, humbly, she stood.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The inner rite: the priestess and Polia enter the Adytum (Poliphilo kept at the
threshold, watching); the **gem-mosaic pavement** (leaves, flowers, birds and animals in coloured stones, so
polished it mirrors those who enter); and **Polia genuflecting, bare-kneed, before the central altar** of
"divine flame." All secure.

- **The mirror-pavement of the sanctuary.** The Adytum floor is a gem-mosaic of foliage and creatures,
  polished to a mirror that "doubled and re-showed those who had entered" — the same reflective-floor motif as
  Cythera's obsidian (p. 352) and the temple's marble (p. 208). Reflection is the temple's constant device:
  the sacred multiplies itself in mirror-stone.
- **Poliphilo kept at the threshold.** He may not enter the inner sanctuary — only the priestess and Polia
  (the initiate) go in; he watches from the door, holding his breath and "incarcerating his sighs" so as not
  to disturb the rite. The innermost mystery is Polia's alone (as the well-drink was, p. 217). A narrative
  gating the world can honour: the sanctum admits only the beloved.
- **"true myropolis"** — Poliphilo's pun-epithet for Polia (myrrh/perfume-seller, playing on her name and her
  sweetness): the book's habitual name-play. Kept as a gloss.
- **the central flame-altar** — a sanctified altar burning with "divine flame" stands at the sanctuary's
  centre, before which Polia kneels; its elaborate form (a gem-crowned golden platter) is described on p. 221.
  This is the focus of the Adytum, as the well is of the main temple.
