# Page 229 — Chapter XVIII

"O most-holy and inspired **Erothea**, pious mother, and illustrious, unceasing and valid patroness of the
ardent and holy loves, and of the amorous fires, and of the sweetest conjugations untiring helper. If, to
thy divine numen, from her the invoked graces have arrived — by which grateful and accepted be her excessive
ardours, and her already-vowed heart — render thyself pious and yielding to her poured prayers, full of
affectionate and religious espousals and instant entreaty. And remember the exhortatory and divine
persuasions of Neptune to furious Vulcan, by thee sedulously made, when, from the Mulciberian (Vulcan's)
nets, bound with amorous Mars, thou wast loosed unharmed; and to thy supernal clemency it pleased thus to
hear me. And, propitious, grant to fulfil the determined vow and fiery desire of these two.

"Wherefore, by thy blind and winged son — she being, in this her tender and florid age, apt to thy holy and
laudable service, and to thy sacred ministries disposed, separated from the colds of Diana — to thy amorous
and divine fires (conserving nature), with the utmost and integral devotion, she wholly prepares herself. And
already, by that wounding son, her soul pierced through, and out of the chaste breast her soft heart uprooted,
she feeling (it) — he not refusing, but patient, and meekly inclining himself — that (heart) —

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The opening of the **great prayer to Venus** (Erothea): the priestess invokes the
goddess as patroness of "holy loves," recalls Venus's own rescue from Vulcan's net (freed while trapped with
Mars), and presents Polia — separated "from the colds of Diana" (chastity) — as one wholly preparing herself
for love's fires, her heart already "uprooted" by Cupid. All secure; a sustained liturgical invocation.

- **Venus invoked through her own myth.** The prayer's rhetorical strategy is to remind the goddess of her own
  loves: here, the net of Vulcan (*Odyssey* 8 — Venus and Mars caught in Vulcan's invisible net, then freed at
  Neptune's plea). The priestess asks Venus to be as merciful to these lovers as she herself was shown mercy.
  This is the standard hymn-form (*da quia dedisti*), beautifully deployed.
- **"separated from the colds of Diana."** Polia leaves the chastity of Diana (the "chaste college," p. 217)
  for the "warm" service of Venus: the prayer frames the union as a passage from one goddess's service to the
  other's — from cold virginity to fertile love. The temple of *Venus Physizoa* (generative Venus) is exactly
  the place for that transfer.
- **"conserving nature"** — a key phrase: love's fires "conserve nature" (i.e. propagate life). The prayer
  makes explicit the theology of Venus Physizoa — erotic love as the preservation of the natural order, the
  same creed as the door-mottoes (p. 214). The union is sanctioned as natural law.
- The prayer continues on p. 230, recalling Adonis and invoking Venus under her cult-names (Amathuntea,
  Cyprogenia, Autophies).
