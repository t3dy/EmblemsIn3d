# Page 459 — Chapter XXXV

— the third a little less than celestial (as, without fail, I judged) — I beheld, in the open and openly, mysteries and
arcane visions, rarely to mortals and material senses permitted to discern. But I, who, by special grace, and singular
indult, and gracious privilege, was exploring the whole — and diligently and most-accurately contemplated the divine gift
granted, which, wounded, to me graciously offered the fire-born Cupid, which, with certain and secure conjecture, I hoped,
with thee (Polia), to conquer and, attained, amorously to enjoy — and there, scrupulously hallucinated and astonished, that
in one little Nymphal body were, especially, heaped all the elegancies and comely filament of beauties, and, perfectly, every
aggregate shapeliness re-beholding (the present Numina, too, provoked into admiration): I saw, among the other noteworthy and
celestial things, two coruscating and splendid eyes, more clear than the matutinal stars — that thou wouldst say Phoebus
twinned, under those brows, resplendent — scintillating little arrows of gold, without intercalation, into my so most-willing
object, communicating the splendour of every notable virtue pre-nitent; which, no less than the rays of the most-lucent Sun,
made my intent regard to vacillate. Much more, without measure, salutary and gracious than, to the shipwrecked, the near
shore; and more than recuperated health to the sick; and more than were the anxious riches to Darius, the victories to
Alexander; and more than the increment of the rainy Nile to the Egyptian fields... And there the most-beautiful Nymph,
ethereal adornment, to all the other conspicuous beauties sole outstanding, most-comely, amiable, offered herself, with milky
breast, in which Love had made his delicious Orchard, and most-pleasant little garden, a manifest seminary and vestige of
Jove — with golden interweaving of her balled-up tresses, the genial nape encompassing, and choicely constricting, and,
without Ciniflonean (curling-iron) art, crisping, unstable; and part effused, wavy, over the most-white shoulders, which
whitened of nival Candour, adulterated of rosy liquamen. More desirable offering herself than the sacred gold to the
iniquitous Atalanta... nor was so opportune the laurel-chaplet to the baldness of Caesar, nor so salubrious and efficacious was,
to the enamoured Faustina, the gore of the wretched Gladiator, as she, to my furnaced fervour, a most-opportune, salubrious,
and most-efficacious and present remedy. So much, then, is her beauty, that I believe not that of such and so much was Deiopea
promised to Aeolus (by Juno)."

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** Poliphilo's soul, granted "special grace to see arcane visions," **contemplates Polia's divine
beauty** at the throne — her sun-bright eyes shooting golden arrows, her milky breast "Love's own orchard," her golden hair —
"sole outstanding among all beauties," a "most-opportune remedy to my furnaced fervour," beyond every treasure (Darius's
riches, the laurel to Caesar). All secure; the beatific vision of the beloved.

- **The beloved seen among the gods.** Polia, "a little less than celestial," is beheld by Poliphilo's soul at Venus's
  throne as a divine marvel — "all beauties heaped in one Nymphal body," at which "the Numina themselves marvel." His love,
  which deified her on earth (p. 440), sees her deified in heaven. The beatific vision confirms his worship: Polia is
  genuinely a wonder of divine making ("a vestige of Jove").
- **her breast, "Love's orchard."** Polia's breast is "the delicious Orchard where Love made his garden" — the Venus-garden
  imagery (Cythera's orchards, the rose-bush) localised to the beloved's body. She *is* the garden the whole dream sought.
  The world's Cythera and the beloved converge as the same object of desire.
- **the eyes as golden arrows.** Her eyes "shoot scintillating golden arrows" — she is now the *archer*, wounding Poliphilo
  with love (as Cupid's golden arrow, p. 458, wounds her). The lovers now mutually pierce: her converted love shoots back.
  The eyes-as-arrows (p. 427) made reciprocal.
- **the surpassing-catalogue.** Her beauty exceeds Darius's riches, Alexander's victories, the Nile's flood, Caesar's
  laurel, Deiopea (Juno's bribe to Aeolus): the topos at its fullest, measuring the beloved against every prize. Venus's
  divine assurance and the soul's return follow on p. 460, completing the vision.
