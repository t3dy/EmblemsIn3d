Above the topmost shafts sat the choice capitals of the recounted columns, with artful abaci, or

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The capitals and entablature above the arcade, then the great **frieze-relief over
the door**: a Bacchic vase flanked by prostrate bulls, a nude youth riding and about to strike a bull
while embracing its neck, a nude girl seated back-to holding a wind-blown veil, and satyrs with a serpent
and festoons. All secure; a densely-figured antique sacrificial/triumphal relief.

- **The frieze over the theatre-door — a Bacchic bull-scene.** The central frieze-panel is a full
  mythological relief: bulls prostrate at a garlanded vase, a nude youth mounted and raising rods to
  strike, a nude girl clinging back-to-back with a billowing veil, satyrs handling a coiled serpent and
  festoons. It reads as a sacrifice or a Dionysiac triumph — the kind of carved panel the world could hang
  as a relief over the theatre entrance.
- **the veil "blown out under her seat"** — the same wind-caught drapery motif as the boating-nymphs (p.
  313) and the companions (p. 331), now frozen in stone: Colonna's signature moving-veil, carved into the
  relief.
- **the composition is described as a viewer reads it** — "here and there," "next to which," "in prospect,"
  "within": Colonna narrates the relief spatially, figure by figure, the way he narrates the parterres and
  the trophies. The whole theatre-exterior is a surface of legible carved scenes.
- The frieze-relief and the amphitheatre's ornament continue over the next pages, closing chapter XXII
  before the Fountain of Venus (ch. XXIII) opens within.
