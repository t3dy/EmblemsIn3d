# Page 14 — Chapter I

For which cause I began thereafter, and with reason, to suspect and to believe that I had come into
the vastest Hercynian forest; and that there was nothing there but the lairs of harmful beasts, and
the caves of noxious animals and of raging brutes. And therefore, with the greatest terror, I feared
that, being without any defence, I should be torn without warning by some bristled and tusked Boar,
as Charidemus was; or by a raging and starved wild Ox; or by a hissing serpent; or that I should see
my flesh miserably dismembered and gulped down by wolves rushing upon me and howling.

Fearing this, and terrified, I resolved there — all sloth being condemned — to linger no longer, but
to find a way out and escape the perils that offered, and to urge on my steps, already halting and
disordered; stumbling often on the roots laid bare from the earth, wandering here and there astray,
now to the right hand and now to the left, at one time going backwards and at another forwards,
ignorant, and not knowing where to pass; having come into a woodland glade and a thicket and a place
full of briars, all scratched by the branches, and my face injured by the thorny sloes and by their
intractable fruit. And by the sharp-pointed thistles and other thorns my gown was torn, and, held
back, it hindered and made sluggish my attempted flight.
