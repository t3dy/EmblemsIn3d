## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The spirit's speech continues: the revival is "a triumph like our glorious one," Poliphilo now the
"most blessed of the ages" by grace; and his account of the **vision at Venus's throne** — where (Venus reigning, "distant
from the cold Virgin Astraea" and Diana's signs), he **presented himself wounded and complained against Cupid** for
piercing his innocent heart with "more punctures than grains" and tearing him from Polia. All secure.

- **Venus's throne, "far from cold Astraea."** Poliphilo's soul stands before Venus in a realm explicitly *not* Diana's —
  "distant from the cold Virgin Astraea (Justice/the Virgin), remote from Orion's avenger and the Ram (Aries)": the zodiacal
  cold signs banished, only Venus's warmth. The whole Diana-vs-Venus conflict resolved at the divine level: the soul goes
  to Venus, not the cold goddess. Polia's conversion (Venus routing Diana, p. 426) has its cosmic counterpart here.
- **the complaint against Cupid.** Poliphilo arraigns Cupid before Venus — the same "litigation without judge" of p. 452,
  now heard by the goddess herself. His grievance: struck though guiltless, torn from Polia. Venus will answer by
  reconciling the lovers (Cupid's promise, p. 457). The near-death vision is a *tribunal of love* — Poliphilo's case tried
  before the goddess, and won (the "obtained grace").
- **the heart "sieved like grain."** His heart riddled with "more punctures than grains" — the arrow-wound multiplied
  (cf. the quiver girded from his heart, p. 282). The extremity of his love-wound, pleaded before its author's mother.
- **the revival as triumph, restated.** The spirit insists the return is a *triumph* — Poliphilo the most blessed, freed
  into "most-precious liberty." The captivity-of-love (the chain, the fish-trap) is now released into the liberty of
  *mutual* love. His bondage ends where Polia's love begins. Cupid's promise of reconciliation follows on p. 457.
