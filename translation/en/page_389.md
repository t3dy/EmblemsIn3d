# Page 389 — Chapter XXVI

— heart deleted and wiped was every cogitation of that Poliphilo — as if I still had drunk the waters of Lethe, son of
Phlegethon; nor less as if I were ringed with the ring of the good Hebrew (Solomon), as the amorous Ethiopian-woman
oblivious. But he — who, acerbly, of blind fire was, and of stimulating love wounded, and, of the Cupidine lancination,
the breast lancinous — (I understand not how his own intellect could imagine it, or that his favourable fortune benignly
lent him the tufted forehead (opportunity), that he, dolorously consuming himself, and in asperity of love wasting himself,
and, in him, the savage Cupid intemperately domesticating himself) — found me, on the day of my sacred dedication, with
some other little maidens consecrating myself. At which solemnity it is customary for the wanton and juvenile throng
avidly to convene at the sacred spectacles. And, of me clearly aware, totally he lost himself. Then he, for this,
affectionately hoped to have found (a) present and opportune remedy for his inflamed heart — yet ignorant what he ought
to do, save to behold and re-behold, with most-intent gazes, the most-pleasing head, with the adornment of the blond
tresses; in which adornment he had supremely, solidly, collocated and fabricated all his pleasant and delightsome pleasure,
and happy and determined content, and fixed thought.

But because, from then backward — that (having) bound myself, with votive heart, to the espoused vows — by (any) man ever,
or most-rarely, more I let myself be seen; but covertly, and the veiled face over-covered, with most-occult recess and
access to the holy temple, and almost unknown, for a long tract of time, I gave heed to keep myself unconspicuous.
Poliphilo, wretched lover, who prized not gracious life more than frightful death — computing the day as a year, for the
long delay of not being able to re-see me — all anxious and perplexed, but with constant, pertinacious soul, so-little
astutely, and with provident and sleepless disquisition and diligence (like a man bound, thrust into a horrend prison-
house, solely intent, most-vigilant, on the breaking-out to flee; and like a sick man, desirous of health, intent on the
escape) — he knew so corneredly to investigate, exploring (and perhaps, by the flying Love, directed to so watchful a
vigil), that in the temple, one day, where alone I remained to pray — he, blinded of excessive love, and orbed
(blindfolded) by fiery desire (like an animal without discourse, that ponders not the end of its suasive appetite) —
moribund, approached. Whence, no sooner before me did I see him, than promptly, contaminated, my indisposed heart — like
frigid Diamond, which, by burning, alters not, rigid, froze itself — became more freezing than the Porphyry stone; and,
with soul unmild and ferocious, spurned and stupefied every pity, into great hatred against him I converted my soul.
Perhaps more —

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** Poliphilo **finds Polia** at her consecration to Diana and is "totally lost" in her again; she,
now a veiled temple-virgin, hides herself, but he — "like a prisoner planning escape" — searches sleeplessly and at last
finds her **alone, praying, in the temple**, where he approaches "moribund"; and Polia, seeing him, **freezes her heart
into hatred** ("more freezing than porphyry"). All secure; the moment her cruelty turns active.

- **The confrontation — cruelty crystallised.** This is the scene the chapter's argument named: Poliphilo, having tracked
  her down, appears before the praying Polia — and her heart, instead of softening, "froze itself, more freezing than
  porphyry… into great hatred." Consecrated to Diana, she reads his love as an assault on her vow and answers it with
  active loathing. The confrontation (his declaration, her merciless refusal, his swoon) unfolds over the next pages;
  here the *hatred* is born. This is the darkest point of her self-accusation (p. 382, "swinish ferocity").
- **Poliphilo as the sleepless prisoner.** His pursuit of the hidden Polia — "like a man in a prison intent on breaking
  out" — is his constancy (the fidelity-thread) seen from *her* side: what Book I gave as devotion, Book II gives as a
  siege she resented. The double narration lets us see the same love as both worship and unwanted pursuit.
- **the frozen heart.** Polia's coldness is now not absence of feeling but *chosen* hatred, hard as porphyry, unmelted
  by his fire — the exact inverse of the Venus-rite's prayer to "re-kindle her stone-made, gelid heart" (p. 216). Book
  II shows the frozen heart that Book I's rite thawed: the two halves of the same conversion.
- **the ring of Solomon, the Ethiopian, Lethe** — the images of *forgetting* (Solomon's ring, Lethe's water): Polia had
  utterly forgotten Poliphilo (p. 388), and now actively hates the sight of him. Her erudition serves her cruelty as it
  served her chastity.
