# Page 253 — Chapter XIX

> **· D ·        · M ·**
> **ANNIRAE**
> **PUCILLAE**
> **PUELLAE**
> **INCOMP(ARABILI)**
> **DIDUS (?)**
> **AEMULATRICI**
> **MOESTISS(IMI)**
> **PARENTES · P(OSUERUNT) ·**
>
> "To the shades (*Dis Manibus*). To Annira Pucilla, incomparable girl, [a?] rival of Dido(?), the most-mournful
> parents placed (this)."

Next to this, lying on the floor, I saw also this elegant epitaph, on a porphyry tablet — by which to have been a
superb sepulchre I conjectured, because at both sides it appeared continuous by fracture, and not thus a simple
tablet, but this part stood pure of lineaments, immune, with the lettering left. About which germinated the hybrid
nasturtium.

---

## Notes

**Confidence: medium** (the epitaph's letters are secure; one word, *DIDUS*, is uncertain). A second **tomb-
epitaph**, on a bronze-vase cinerary stone flanked by mask-headed *D · M* figures: to **Annira Pucilla**, "incomparable
girl," raised by "most-mournful parents"; then a third epitaph on a broken porphyry tablet (its text on p. 254),
overgrown with nasturtium. All secure except the doubtful word.

- **Annira Pucilla — a parents' grief.** Unlike Laodia's suicide-altar (p. 252), this is a family monument: an
  "incomparable girl" mourned by her parents (the standard Roman *parentes posuerunt*). The word **DIDUS** is
  uncertain — perhaps *Didus aemulatrici*, "rival of Dido" (Dido the archetypal love-suicide, so a girl who died of
  love like her); flagged `[?]` as it cannot be resolved from the transcription. *(See
  [translation/NOTES.md](translation/NOTES.md).)*
- **the epigraphic formulae, continued.** *D · M* (*Dis Manibus*, "to the shades"), *puellae incomparabili*,
  *moestissimi parentes*, *P.* (*posuerunt*, "placed"): the authentic vocabulary of Roman tomb-inscriptions, which
  Colonna deploys throughout the Polyandrion. The world's tombs can be lettered from these.
- **the ash-vase.** The stone holds a lidless bronze vase "in which the ashes were stored" — the cinerary urn of the
  cremated (cf. the crypt-furnace, p. 247). Each tomb is a real burial, not a mere marker.
- **hybrid nasturtium** — the shore-and-ruin flora continues (cf. p. 236); life overgrows each grave. Colonna keeps
  the botanical thread even through the epitaphs.
