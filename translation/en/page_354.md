The wall which the last pergola girded, set fast, was so much raised that the arching of that pergola did
not, to those inspecting in the Area, wholly obstruct the seeing. Within which wall was a trough, or
channel, inter-void, capaciously fabricated — both at the bottom and at the opening a pace and a half,
inclusive, its capacity — with the estimated band, clean and spaced, above the fenestral order.

Of all this discoursed fabric, the interior girdle — that is, the hollow steps — stood made of best
jasper, ponderous, continuous, and oriental, and perhaps Cyprian, of confused colouring, with wavering
little waves most-diverse. The lips of which boxes, and the socle, were most-beautifully fringed, in
continuous circuit, of pure refined Gold, in little waves and gullet-mouldings; and over the cohesions
of the just division of the tablets, with a working transcending human thought, and most-exact
expression, and utmost effort. Here must perish the luxury of the golden vases of Bassus, and that of
Antony; let yield also the glory of Nero, who gilded the Pompeian Theatre. Let Gorgias of Leontini be
put to sleep with his (golden) statue. Nor ever was such (gold) dug up, so outstanding, by the
Scythians, from the ants and Griffins.

The topmost trough, or box, led in a channel and filled and stuffed with earth, had planted cypresses,
two and two, one next (but untouching) to the other, the pairing three paces distant. Which, with even
form and justly, with a fitting rule interlacing mutually their summits and fore-joined together, with
egregious conjugation inflected themselves — with such-made connexion that they appeared one and the
same. That is, the point of the fourth (cypress) with the fourth. The two then of the middle: that on the
right coupled with the fourth on the right order; and the left, by the like, joined itself with the
fourth on the left side, alternating, one below and one above, knotting themselves, so much the better as
could not be told. Between one and the other of the inflected, or the leaning-together, of the collateral
cypresses, resided a densest Box, of spheres in order, diminishing with even decrement, fastigiate — that
is, decimated into manifold rounding — so excellently shorn (as with little shears) that not one leaf
showed itself to offend its order. And between the neighbouring and curved cypresses, rose a straightest
stalk of Juniper, in a most-just sphere, topiaried with spring foliage, which adorned the empty triangle
of the cypress-arches. The remainder of the terrace-top abundantly germinated various and exquisite
aromatic little pot-herbs, copious of their odoriferous flowers, as appears designed at the top of the
Theatre.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The theatre's materials and its crowning garden: the hollow seat-tiers are of
**oriental jasper edged in refined gold**; the topmost tier is a planter carrying an **arcade of paired
cypresses** trained into arches, with clipped box-spheres and topiary junipers between, and aromatic herbs
filling the rest — a living colonnade crowning the cavea. All secure; the gold-edged jasper and the
cypress-arcade are precise, buildable features.

- **The crowning cypress-arcade.** The highest ring of the theatre is planted with pairs of cypresses
  trained to arch into one another, box-spheres and topiary junipers in the gaps, aromatics between: a
  *living arcade* rings the top of the auditorium, echoing in greenery the stone arcades below. Colonna
  even notes it "appears designed at the top of the Theatre" (i.e. in the woodcut). The world's theatre can
  be crowned with a topiary cypress-colonnade.
- **jasper seats edged in gold.** The flower-box tiers are cut from oriental jasper (wave-figured) and
  rimmed in pure gold moulding — the surpassing-topos then heaps Bassus, Antony, Nero's gilded Pompeian
  theatre, and the Scythian griffin-gold, all to be exceeded. The auditorium is jasper-and-gold.
- **"the ants and Griffins" of Scythia** — Herodotus's gold-digging ants and the gold-guarding griffins of
  the far north; even that legendary gold is surpassed. Colonna's habitual erudite hyperbole.
- The three pergola-pavements — the most extravagant surfaces in the building — follow on p. 355.
