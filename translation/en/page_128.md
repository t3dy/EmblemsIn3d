Sitting solaced here beneath it a while, the most witty Thelemia took the lyre that she carried with
her, and with a heavenly melody and unheard-of sweetness of sweet speech began to sing the origin of so
many delights and the rule of her Queen, and with what adornment her companion Logistica's sweet
fellowship was — I marvelling that Apollo did not come there to listen, so excessive was the harmony
she uttered. So that nothing else at present, however longed for, would I have valued at all.

The divine poem being said, at once the god-loving Logistica, taking me by the hand, led me forth from
that place, saying: "Poliphilo, I would have you know that things of the understanding are of a greater
delight to the intellect than things of sense are to the senses. For this, let us enter here, to
satisfy both the receiving faculties."

Who, with a most distinguished company, led me in near this garden to another, where I beheld an arched
colonnade of widely spaced arches, from the floor to the topmost bend five paces high and three in the
opening, all of brick, with a symmetried slope, or spread, in a continuous ring, all most beautifully
clothed and covered with green ivy, no least trace of the wall appearing; and there were a hundred
arches, and they closed in a flowering orchard.

Beneath each one of the arches, then, was set an altar of ruddy porphyry, excellently lined. And upon
each there sat a golden statue of a Nymph with a divine likeness, with varying dress, and the ornament
of the head, and of gesture, each of them reverent towards the middle centre of this garden.

In this middle centre there was mysteriously founded a base of translucent chalcedony in a cubic form.
And above this, within the square compass, there was placed a rounding two feet high, and of a diameter
of a pace and a half, of the ruddiest jasper. Above this then sat a triangle, by the measure of the
figure beneath, of a height of a pace and a half, of the blackest stone. The corners of which triangle
stretched to the edge of the plinth below it.

On the well-polished and free front of each there was fitted a most notable image, in aspect divine,
grave and venerable, with their feet upon the untouched part of the triangular figure of the rounding
pressed beneath, of a tall stature, as tall as the blackest stone, to which they clung with their
backs, with their arms stretched to the left and to the right towards the corners; they held a
horn of plenty packed full, fixed at the blunted, or cut-off, corners, the shortening of their fingers
made even, a foot and a sixth, of purest gold. The horns, thongs and statues shone forth, with their
hands wrapped in the wandering and straggling thongs. Flying along the flat of the stone. And in
