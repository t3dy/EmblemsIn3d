Being therefore somewhat consoled by the kindly light, and the spirits I had lost and renounced being
taken back into me, and my prostrate strength gently restored, my suspended and pathless journey and
flight urged me, striving straight on. For as I drew nearer to it I discerned it multiplying. To which
at last, the heavenly will accompanying me, and most beloved Polia vigorously reigning in my loving
breast, I came anxiously.

Where, blessing the gods undeservedly, and obsequious fortune, and my golden-haired Polia, I found a
wide way out; and thence hastily going forth, and urgently fleeing, waiting not at all. And my arms,
already stretched out to avoid striking against the thickest pillars, now most fittingly adapted
themselves as oars for flight. And thence, striving, Safe, I came into a most pleasant Site and region.

In which, still terrified by that horrible monster, I doubted of the wished-for sitting down and
settling myself, having it so impressed upon my mind that continually and without interval I thought I
felt it at my shoulders. And for such cause I could not so quickly dissolve or displace so great a
terror from there. Whereupon I justly judged, doubting, that it still pursued me. And I was moved also
by manifold persuasions to enter and go forward. First by the pleasantness of that most beautiful
place; then by my disordered mind urging me to flee at once. And chiefly, being always desirous to see
and find things perhaps not customary among mortals.

Equally such considerations provoked me by every means to enter, and to go on as far beyond as I could,
and to remove myself from the way out; where I might quietly calm myself in some place and clear my
mind, and put in oblivion the terror now past — there coming not unwelcome into my retaining memory
the appearance of the white Mouse in the entry of the gate. And this came as a very exhortative
awakening to hearten me, because it was always welcome in the auspices, and a propitious and good Omen.

Therefore it persuaded me opportunely to give effort to keeping myself for the kindness of fortune,
which sometimes may be to me a bountiful and long-haired minister of prosperous and favourable things.
