This delightful and choice disposition of the much-admired flowers succumbed here to no temporal change,
nor order — nor such as in Memphis — but, ever irrigated and fresh, in one vernal being, and never
deciduous, existing.

For which thing, contemplating such majesty of place, with so much grace astonishing, with this elegance
ordered and allotted, and most-pleasant harmony of variation of the flowering colour — such as, in the dewy
dawn, they present themselves fresh — with all the discoursed miracles, astonished and lifeless I wholly
remained. My internal and external senses occupied, and surrounded with ineffable voluptuousness and
delight — beyond the excessive love, which all the while in my lanced heart strongly burned, and the
unceasing siege of the incomparable beauties of my most-comely Polia, my delight — insomuch that I did not
know in what being I was. Finally, into this most-happy place, and most-blessed site, introduced — of a
sudden the two Nymphs who bound us, there, again, unloosed us from the entangled little wreaths. And the
venerable Psyche, most-honorifically, to her beloved Husband, laughing, restored the little golden arrow.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The chapter closes with the **full planting-scheme of the theatre's flower-tiers**
(each ring by species and colour — violets graded white/yellow, narcissus, cyclamen, cornflower, anemone,
and the top ring in ten flower-divisions), all eternally in spring and never fading; then Poliphilo,
overwhelmed "beyond the excessive love… for my most-comely Polia," is **unbound** by the two nymphs, and
**Psyche restores the golden arrow to Cupid** — the last gesture of the triumph. This ends **chapter XXII
(32/32)**. All secure.

- **The theatre is a perpetual spring garden.** Every seat-tier is a named flower-bed — violets by colour,
  narcissus, cyclamen, cornflower, melilot, anemone, and a ten-part crown of primrose, everlasting,
  amaranth, lily-of-the-valley, iris, hyacinth, columbine — "succumbing to no seasonal change… ever in one
  vernal being." A complete planting-key for the world's planted cavea (p. 353), tier by tier.
- **the lovers unbound, the arrow restored.** The triumph ends with release, not captivity: the rose-withes
  are untied, and Psyche hands Cupid back his golden arrow, laughing. The captivity was ceremonial; inside
  the sacred theatre the lovers are freed. A fit close to the world's triumph-sequence — bound at the car
  (p. 341), unbound at the centre.
- **"I did not know in what being I was."** Poliphilo's sensory dissolution — the ekphrastic overwhelm that
  has built through the whole chapter — leaves him "astonished and lifeless," his love for Polia still
  burning under it all. The chapter has held the fidelity-thread from p. 314 to its end.
- **Chapter XXII complete.** The landing, the trophies, the masque of nymphs, the serpent-car, the
  procession, the amphitheatre and its obsidian mirror, planted tiers and jewelled galleries — all
  translated. Chapter XXIII (the Fountain of Venus, already done) stands at the centre of this theatre.
