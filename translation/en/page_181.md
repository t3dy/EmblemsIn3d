Thus, then, most joyfully they went circling round through the whole flowery and most pleasant plain.
Some crowned with laurel, and some with myrtle, and with manifold garlands and varied adornment; with
most solemn prayers, with a religious discourse, divine and triumphal, without term and ending, without
weariness and without labour, with the utmost fullness of every delight, gloriously rejoicing, and
mutually enjoying the divine sights; and the most happy realms, and the holy country, without
interruption they quietly possess, most blessed.

*The Nymph, having fittingly declared to her Poliphilo the triumphal mysteries and the divine love,
invites him to go on further from there, where with the utmost delight he saw countless other Nymphs
with their most welcome lovers, delighting themselves in a thousand solaces among the flowers, and
through the cool shades and clear streams and clearest fountains; and how Poliphilo, strongly stirred
by love, raged there. But tempering himself with hope, he was quieted, gazing upon the sweet face of
his fair Nymph.*

Not only would he reckon himself happy, but he would be blessed above any other, to whom by a special
favour it should continually be granted to see the divine pomps, the heavenly triumphs, and the glorious
sports, and the kindly places, and such goddesses and demi-goddesses and adorned Nymphs, of incredible
beauty and ornament. And to have a particular fellowship with them, and to look upon them unceasingly.

But above all to have beside him, as an equal companion, so renowned a Nymph of chief beauty, with an
exquisite nymphlike and divine adornment, a guide and a sincere leader. And this I judged no small and
scanty part of blessedness. Having really beheld these things, I stayed a great while in thought, and
gladdened beyond esteeming, and marvelling beyond measure.
