# Page 117 — Chapter X

*Poliphilo goes on to tell of an elegant dance after so great a banquet, which was a game. And how the
Queen committed him to two excellent maidens of hers. Who led him to behold delicious and great things,
and, talking with him, plainly instructed him upon certain doubts. At last they came to the three
doors. And how he stayed at the middle door, among the amorous Nymphs.*

So excessive and incomparable a glory and triumph, and unthinkable treasure, and frugal delights, and
the highest pomp, and solemn feast, and most splendid and sumptuous banquet of this most happy and most
opulent Queen having been recounted — if I have not distinctly and to the end worthily expressed her
chief dignity, let the curious little crowd not marvel at it; for anyone of sharp and ready wit, and
adorned and plentiful with a prodigal and most fertile tongue, could not satisfy this plainly, nor
altogether.

But much less I, who continually suffered, in every inward hiding-place of my burning heart, the
unceasing battle — however absent — of my lady Polia, the occupier and wasting plunderer of all my
powers. Beyond, the many marvels, unheard of in their excellence and diversity, things unaccustomed and
unlike, inestimable and not human; therefore dazzled and all alike weighed down in every sense of mine,
my excessive contemplation drawn apart by the notable variety, I should not know how to describe it
perfectly point by point, nor worthily to set it forth.
