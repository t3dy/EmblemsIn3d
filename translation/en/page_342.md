Towing, then, this superb vehicle of triumphant Love drew two scaly serpents, **skinks**, beyond
their nature great in this place, for this amorous office and mystery and conveyance valid and
vigorous, inflamed with three-forked and quadruped vibrations; with their necks stretched out,
becomingly entwining their scaly breasts with marvellous knotwork of thongs and retainers interwoven
of gold train, with little swelling bosses standing forth, of various and excellent gold chasing
alternated with gems, most-excellently, with irradiating coruscation, buckled in gold buckles and
most-choice clasps they passed through; and thickly adorned with thick little stones, at the drawing
not swiftly, but with a temperament of triumphal procession.

This divine triumph all the triumphal Nymphs preceded, as before is described: the shrine-bearers,
then the trophy-bearers, then the torch-bearers, the torches banded with little bundles of gold; next
the light-bearers with golden lamps, brands, and tapers of whitening and purest material, luminous
with clear candour. Continuing, followed the scent-bearers (Osmiferae) and Sweet-smellers (Euosmiae),
with golden scent-vessels of unseen make, and with golden censers, or incense-caskets, scattering
wondrous fragrance beyond that which the happy place diffusedly breathed everywhere. Some with little
golden vases, with the narrowest outlet, sprinkling a most-odorous inconstant liquor drop by drop,
bedewing all. Very many others, with a heavenly order, held in their longish hands sounding
instruments: sweet symphonies, pierceable pipes, tibiae, sounded with sweet measures; and twisted gold
horns, most-preciously gemmed. Some together, with sweetest consent, harmonically, in the Lydian mode,
singing, with angelic utterance, and a voice rendering no familiar sound, and reiterating the comely
songs. Some others with the tinkling Cymbals. Others with rattling and raucous Drums, suspended from

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The carved diamond on Psyche's shoulder (**Cupid wounding himself while Psyche
handles the arrow** — the myth's peripeteia in miniature); Psyche's lamp and gold-wefted chlamys; the
car **drawn by two winged skink-serpents** in jewelled harness at a slow triumphal pace; and the full
marshalling of the procession (shrine-, trophy-, torch-, light-, and scent-bearers, then the
musicians). All secure.

- **The self-wounding Cupid on the gem.** The chlamys-diamond shows Cupid pricked by his own arrow as
  Psyche handles it — the moment in Apuleius when Psyche's lamp-oil and Cupid's own arrow undo him. Love
  wounded by Love, watched by the Soul: the emblem that governs the whole triumph, worn on Psyche's
  shoulder. Colonna makes the god's power recoil on himself.
- **the serpent-drawn car.** Two great winged skinks (lizard-serpents) in gold-and-gem harness draw the
  chariot "not swiftly, but with a temperament of triumphal procession." A concrete, buildable image for
  the world's triumph-car: serpent-draught, jewelled tack, ceremonial slowness.
- **Psyche's lamp** — she bears the kindled lamp of the myth (by which she first saw Cupid), here made a
  triumphal attribute. Her arrow ("her firebrand") and lamp are the two instruments of the story.
- **the procession's ranks, named** — Colonna gives the marching order as a liturgy: pastophorae,
  trophigerae, faceferariae, splendophorae, osmiferae, euosmiae, then the musicians. The triumph is
  organised as a sacred rite (continued p. 343).
