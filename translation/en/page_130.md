"The Sun, with a most joyful light, can do all things, and its nature is attributed to God. The second
is the rudder, which expresses the provident government of the universe, with infinite wisdom. The
third is the fiery vessel, and I feel of it a sharing of love. And though the three images are

one, kindly communicating its good — as one may see by the horns of plenty at the elbows of the
figure."

And continuing her fate-telling, Logistica said further: "At the image of the Sun, mark this Greek
word: ADIEGETOS. At that of the rudder, look at that Greek noting: ADIACHORISTOS. At that of the fire
was carved: ADIEREUNETOS. By such effects, then, those three animals are set below, subject to the
golden obelisk lying above them, which are three great and famous opinions of such figures. And as the
human likeness effectually surpasses the others, so, neither more nor less, does the opinion.

"On the pyramid are three flat sides lined with three circles, each one signifying a time: past,
present, and to come. Understand that no other figure could hold those three circles unchanging save
that one. And none of mortals could perfectly discern nor see two sides of the said figure together,
save one wholly, which is the present. And therefore most wisely were those three characters stamped:
OON.

"For which cause, Poliphilo — not accusing me of being long-winded, but most ready to set you right in
such a telling — know that the first base figure is known only to itself, and to one so far as human
and translucent; but to us not in full clearness. Then he who is gifted with wit climbs above and
skilfully considers the colouring of the figure. Searching further, they climb to the third figure,
which by its colouring is dark and shut up in those three images of gold, and walled about.

"Last, climbing higher still, they consider a figure in a threefold aspect; and thence, the more they
climb contemplating towards the sharpest narrowing, and there, however well instructed, they have
nevertheless gained nothing else than that one sees *that it is*; but what it may be, they remain
ignorant, unavailing, and feeble."
