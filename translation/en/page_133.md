"The porphyry stone stands with a notable mystery for such an expression. For it is affirmed to be of
such a nature that not only does it not burn in the furnace, but it also makes the other stones
standing near it unburnable. Such does true patience show itself: that not only is it not kindled, but
it renders those that are kindled quenched. The stone of ophite has its property well known, fitting to
that saying.

"So that, Poliphilo, I commend you in this part, because you are eager for such inquiry; for to weigh
everything, and consider, and measure it, shows itself praiseworthy." Here I highly praised the wisdom
of the most eloquent lady, saying countless thanks.

And thus, with honest and most approved discourse, we came most festively to a most charming river.
Upon the banks of which I saw a gracious plane-grove, besides other greenest little trees, and water
shoots excellently set and placed, with lotuses set between. Where there crossed a proud stone bridge of
three arches, with its ends upon the banks above the firmest foundations, with the piers keeled at
their two fronts to hold the structure most firm, and with most noble parapets.

In which, at the middle curve of the wedge beneath the arch, here and there, there stood out, raised
and well polished, a porphyry square with a gable, holding a sunk carving of hieroglyphs. On the right
at our crossing I saw a matron bound with a serpent, sitting upon one haunch only, and with the other
leg in the act of rising; with the hand on the side she sat on she held a pair of wings, and in the
other, on the rising side, a tortoise. Opposite there was a circle, the centre of which two little
spirits held, turning their backs with their little breasts to the circumference.
