# Page 140 — Chapter X

I was gladdened and took an extreme comfort.

Thelemia, then, perceiving that such a matron with her company, and the place and its condition, were
to my pleasure and content, and her own kindness, kissing me like a dove and embracing me closely,
asked leave of me and departed.

And the metal valves being closed again, I remained shut in at once among those distinguished Nymphs,
who began most charmingly and wantonly to sport about me; and, hedged in by the voluptuous crowd of
them, to provoke me to enticing longings, alluring and persuasive.

Whence I felt a beginning of itching, their wanton looks fostering an increase of an amorous and
wounding fire. So that Phryne would perhaps have warmed the cold and superstitious Xenocrates with so
great an onset of love, and moved him and made him fall into luxury, nor would he have been called a
statue by her, if she had been one of these. With wanton faces, and forward breasts, coaxing eyes
sparkling and playful in their rosy brows.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** Thelemia's dove-kiss and departure, and Poliphilo shut in with Philtronia's
nymphs. Short and mostly secure, and the closing allusion is exactly right and complete: Phryne
would have warmed the cold Xenocrates if she had been one of these, and he would not have been
called a statue by her — which keeps the point of the anecdote ("I wanted a man, not a statue")
rather than paraphrasing it away. Medium for two words, *lacescente foco* rendered "wounding fire"
where the sense is provoking, and *petulci* flattened to "wanton" in a sentence where it is already
doing that work twice.
