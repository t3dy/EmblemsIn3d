Within the compass of the crown I beheld a shaggy and teeming she-goat suckling a little boy, who sat
beneath her, with his fleshy little Shins, the one thrust out and one drawn somewhat towards himself.
Holding himself with his little arms to the hanging and rough wool, and with his face intent upon the
swelling udders, sucking them dry.

And another of the Nymphs then, coaxing, bending down, held raised one foot of the goat with her left
hand; and with the other she held out to him the distended and heavy udders, to the sucking kiss of the
milk-drinking infant. Beneath which was read AMALTHEA. And a Nymph standing at the head of the beast
went dutifully about its neck with one arm, and with the other hand shrewdly bridled it by the horns.

A third stood besides in the middle, with foliage in one hand, and in the other holding a most ancient
Cup with exquisite little handles. At the feet of this one was inscribed MELISSA.

Two Nymphs then, between the one and the other of the three described above, danced nimbly leaping
with Corybantic instruments, each in Nymph-like habits expressly imitating the movement of the agitated
form. O how artificially they fulfilled their mystery of illustrious making! Chased work without fail
not of the stone-carver Polyclitus, nor of Phidias, nor of Lysippus; and to the pious Artemisia, Queen
of Caria besides, there were afforded, of such excellent mastery of the chisel, the relief-workers
Scopas, Bryaxis, Timotheus and Leochares and Theon. For this was most skilfully made beyond human
genius and any relief-work whatever.

Finally in the temple-like pinnacle, or frontispiece, beneath the order of the upper cornice, in the
flat of it there appeared in carving these two words in perfect Attic capitals: *Of aegis-bearing
Zeus.*

Of this conspicuous and most notable gate such a wonderful composition and excellent disposition was
to be seen. Of which, if its every measured proportion has not been particularly explained, the blame
must be laid upon regard for prolixity, and upon the lack of the proper words of description. And
because gnawing time had left this one alone untouched, therefore I did not approve of passing on
without having said and treated something especially of it.
