In the right hand, at the object of her gaze, she held an artificial horn of plenty, stuffed with
every good thing, turned towards the earth. And the other hand she held pressed and closed upon her
naked breast. This statue, then, turned easily at whatever breeze blew, with such a whirring of the
rubbing of the hollow metal engine as was never heard from the Roman treasury. And where the figure
rested with its feet, rubbing upon the little altar beneath, there sounded such a ringing as the
Bell at the magnificent Baths of Hadrian never gave out, nor that of the five Pyramids standing upon
the square.

Which most lofty Obelisk allows me still not the least belief that another showed any conformity to
it, or any likeness. Not the Vatican one. Not the Alexandrian. Not the Babylonian. It held in itself
such a heaping-up of wonder that I stood senseless with stupefaction at the considering of it. And
far more still, the immensity of the work, and the excess of the subtlety of that opulent and most
acute genius, and the great care and exquisite diligence of the Architect.

With what daring invention of art, then; with what power and human strength, and order, and
incredible expense, was so great a weight carried up and set so high in the air, in emulation of the
heavens? With what Windlass, and with what wheeled Pulleys, and with what Crabs, or Block-and-tackle,
and other hauling Engines, and framed Scaffoldings? Let every other incredible and greatest structure
keep silence here.
