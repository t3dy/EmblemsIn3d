Now, having left this leafy and stopped-up gate, going down by a certain slope, I came to a thick
Chestnut thicket at the foot of the mountain — a station, as I supposed, of Pan or Silvanus — with
moist pastures and pleasant shade. Passing beneath which with pleasure, I found a marble and most
ancient bridge of one very great and high arch. Upon which, on the several sides of the parapets, seats
were most conveniently built. Which, although they offered themselves fittingly to my weariness as I
came out, none the less I then valued them not at all, in my excited going forward.

In the middle of which parapets there rose somewhat, level with the top of the keystone of the arch
beneath, a Porphyry square, with an excellent cymatium of polished lineament — one on the one side, and
one of like form on the other, but of Ophite stone. On the right of my way I saw most noble Egyptian
hieroglyphs of this expression: an antique helmet crested with a dog's head; a bare ox's head with two
tree-branches bound to the horns with small fronds; and an ancient lamp. Which hieroglyphs — the
branches excepted, for I did not know whether they were of fir, or pine, or larch, or juniper, or the
like — I interpreted thus.

On the other side I beheld this elegant carving: a circle; an anchor, about whose shank a dolphin
wound itself. And these I interpreted most excellently thus: *Always make haste slowly.*
