Where with careful diligence I explored the little pebbly shores wiped clean by a
kindly wash, of scattered gems of varied form and colouring, shining. There too,
dispersedly, appeared an abundance of the fragrant issue of the monstrous whales,
carried in by the fruitful tides. A most adorned island, then, of most gratifying
and new and perennial covering of springlike greenery, admirable everywhere over
the flat.

But first, upon the bare shore-banks close by, I beheld the coeval and tall
cypresses with their close-set and cracked cones, persevering, and durable for
weighty roofs — timbers not savoury to the gnawing woodworm — and the spacing
between cypresses of three paces, one separated from the other. This regular
order, turned round in a ring, was observed throughout the whole outer circle of
the island. Then, in a circle, there went round a most delightful and flowery
myrtle-grove, loving the talkative shores; which stands votive and dedicated to the
divine mother of the amorous fires. Compacted and most densely reduced and shaped
in the manner of a walled fence, a pace and a half tall, enclosing within it the
trunk-stocks of the straightest cypresses, the beginning of its foliage rising two
feet from the levelled top, or flat, of the myrtle-grove. Thus this greenery,
so made, walled about the shore-banks, with fitting passages left and distributed
at the seemly places. Which fence showed the least wood, but, protected by the
delightful and flowery foliage — no top exceeding another, no leaf — kept its
summit and its circling shorn with exceptional evenness.

Within this walled-about myrtle and green hedge (which might be, from it toward the
centre of the island, about a semitertio of a mile), I saw, by the drawing of lines
from the centre to the shore-circumference, in equal partition twenty, each of the
immensity of a stadium, with a fifth part added — imitating the outer enclosure of
the myrtle. In each division was a little grove of diverse meadows variously
grassed, and of tree-plot the like; distributed specifically according to the
required aspect of the kindly sky. Let Dodona's wood yield here. Which divisions,

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The description is secure; the closing geometrical
construction (how to lay out the twenty radial divisions) is compressed and
technical, translated literally rather than smoothed. **A reviewer should check the
decagon construction against the facsimile diagram.**

- **This is the outer ring of Cythera — the cypress rim, the myrtle wall, and the
  twenty radial compartments** that Segre's reconstruction (GARDENS.md §5) turns
  into the *bosco*, and that the world's `_buildCytheraIsle` renders as twelve
  wedges (we simplified twenty to twelve). Here is Colonna's own count: **twenty
  divisions, "each of the immensity of a stadium with a fifth part added,"** drawn
  as radii from centre to shore, the outer edge a ring of cypresses three paces
  apart backed by a shorn myrtle hedge a pace and a half high, sacred to Venus.
  The next pages catalogue what grows in each.
- **the geometry** — Colonna gives an actual construction for dividing the circle
  into a decagon (then doubled to twenty): the classic straightedge method using a
  half-diameter and a quarter-diameter. He is showing his mathematical hand, as at
  the Great Portal. Kept literal.
- **"the fragrant issue of the monstrous whales"** — ambergris, washed up on the
  shore among the gems. A real luxury commodity given its mythic-sounding origin.
- **Plotae, Niobe, Dodona** — the Plotae (Strophades) are storm-beaten islands;
  Niobe was turned to weeping rock with her slain children; Dodona's oak-wood was
  the oracle. Each named only to be surpassed or excluded — the island has none of
  their roughness or grief.
