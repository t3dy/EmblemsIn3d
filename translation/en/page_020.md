# Page 20 — Chapter III

POLIPHILO HERE RELATES THAT IT SEEMED TO HIM THAT HE SLEPT AGAIN, AND THAT ELSEWHERE, IN SLEEP, HE
FOUND HIMSELF IN A VALLEY WHICH AT ITS END WAS CLOSED BY A MARVELLOUS BARRIER WITH A PORTENTOUS
PYRAMID, WORTHY OF ADMIRATION, AND A LOFTY OBELISK ABOVE IT; WHICH WITH DILIGENCE AND PLEASURE HE
CONSIDERED SUBTLY.

THE FEARFUL WOOD AND THE CROWDED grove being escaped, and those first other places left behind
through the sweet sleep that had spread itself over my broken and prostrate limbs, I found myself
anew in a place far more delightful than the one before. It was not girdled about with horrid
mountains and sheer crags, nor scythed with knotted ridges, but was composed of pleasant little
hills of no great height; wooded with young oaklings, with oaks, ashes and Hornbeams, and with leafy
Italian oaks and Holm-oaks, and with tender Hazels, and Alders, and Limes, and Maples, and with
barren wild Olives, disposed according to the aspect of those tree-bearing Hills. And down on the
