By which brightness I could not steadily behold the choice workmanship and reliefs, by the reflection of
the flickering light, and by the flashing of the most precious little stones.

About which divine triumph, with much and solemn devotion and the greatest pomp and religion, countless
Maenad Nymphs with their hair loose and scattered. Some naked, with nymphlike mantles flowing from
their shoulders, and some in fawnskins — that is, clothed in a pelt varied with the colour of a young
deer — without the other sex, cymbal-players and flute-players, held the sacred rites, crying out with
shouts, and dances, as at the three-yearly feasts, with thyrsi of fronds of cone-bearing trees, and
girt and crowned with vine fronds twisted over the bare body, running and leaping.

There followed immediately the triumph old Silenus riding his little ass; then, immediately behind this
rider, they festively led a shaggy goat adorned with the pomp of sacrifice. And one of his followers
carried a wicker fan, with disordered laughter and frantic gestures. With this most ancient and holy
rite they gloriously extolled this fourth triumph; and with a reverent discourse, crying out
confusedly with a loud voice to Bacchus, there followed, keeping festival, the Mimallones, satyrs,
bacchantes, Lenae, Thyiads, Naiads, Tityri and Nymphs.
