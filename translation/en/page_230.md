## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The body of the **prayer to Venus**: Polia's heart offered in the altar-fire; the
petition that the lovers "navigate to thy triumphal realm" (Cythera); Venus invoked under her cult-names
(**Amathuntea**, **Autophies** "self-born," **Cyprogenia**), reminded of prayers she heard before (Aeacus,
Pygmalion, Hippomenes) and of **Adonis, whose blood she turned to the rose**. All secure; the theological and
mythological heart of the temple sequence.

- **The prayer looks forward to Cythera.** The priestess asks that the lovers "navigate and cross over, by thy
  potent son, to thy delicious triumphal and glorious realm" — i.e. to **Cythera** (chs. XX–XXIV, already
  translated). This prayer at the Temple of Venus Physizoa is the request that is *granted* in the second half
  of Book I: the crossing to the island and the triumph. The two great Venus-episodes are joined here.
- **Venus's own loves as precedent.** The prayer recalls, in turn, Pygmalion (his ivory bride made flesh),
  Hippomenes (the golden apples), Aeacus (the Myrmidons), and — climactically — **Adonis**, "the boy-shepherd
  beaten by jealous Mars," whose divine blood Venus "scattered in a rosy flower." This last is the myth that
  the *miraculous rose-bush* about to spring from the sacrifice (pp. 231+) enacts: the prayer names the
  precedent for its own answering miracle.
- **the cult-names.** Amathusia (Cyprus), Cyprogenia ("Cyprus-born"), Autophies ("self-generated") — Colonna
  heaps Venus's epithets, each a facet of the generative goddess. *Autophies* especially fits Venus Physizoa:
  the self-born mother of nature.
- **"the young man… an impavid and strenuous Athlete."** The priestess certifies Poliphilo's constancy — he
  has been, "many days now," a steadfast "athlete" of love (the fidelity-thread again). His and Polia's mutual
  vow of service to Venus's laws is the ground of the petition. The prayer completes on p. 231.
