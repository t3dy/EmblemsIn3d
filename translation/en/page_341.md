# Page 341 — Chapter XXII

> *(The inscription on the smoking vase, continued:)*
> **PANTA** [BAIA BIOU]

Received finally the offered mysterious and fateful love-tokens and fitting insignia, the divine
boy set himself to sit upon a golden and antique car, or triumphal vehicle, there prepared — all
of golden plates, an embroidered band a *dodrans* wide going round all the fringes. In which,
sun-like, shone encloistered ostentatious gems of bigness and unthinkable price, and of
imperceptible artifice and rarest invention, divinely disposed, and orderly destined. With two
vehicular wheels having the circumference of gold, and the spokes proceeding from the central
axle, in which the pole lay; and, fixed in the middle, they were fashioned longish, in balustered
figment, of precious stones various of fulgurant colouring.

Sitting, then, he — without protraction of time — both taken and captured were we, Polia and I, by
the outstanding Nymphs **Plexaura** and **Ganoma**, at the imperial nod of the triumphing, dominant
little boy; and, both our arms thrown back behind, and our hands bound at the back, like captives
Polia and I were ensnared and bound, connected and re-netted with tresses and little wreaths of
roses and various flowering. And behind the pompous and divined car of the prize-bearer and greatest
triumphator we were drawn, softly and willingly, by **Synesia** (Understanding), a most-outstanding
Nymph.

For which thing I began almost to tremble. But because the Nymphs, with my world-key Polia, laughed
facetiously, of a sudden I was reassured. There followed immediately after us the curious Psyche.
And behind her were following the girl-damsels who, venerating, had made offering. Which Psyche was
bordered, or clad over, with a matronal and most-ambitious mantle, or chlamys, of gold — such as
Silophontes did not give to Darius, nor could Numa, first king and inventor, use such-made. Over the
right shoulder, foldedly cast, and eyeleted in pressed gold circlets, where among thick carbuncles
most-purged and touchstone-tried, and coruscating with splendour, she held encloistered a diamond an
inch-and-a-half long lengthwise and flattened, and a thumb in breadth, and of iron-like scintillation,
full of wondrous stupor, with inestimable adornment standing forth — such that of so great a price
was not the gift given by Gyges to —

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** Cupid takes his seat on the golden triumphal car (all gold-plate, jewelled,
two gold-rimmed wheels with balustered spokes); **Poliphilo and Polia are bound as captives** behind
it by the nymphs Plexaura and Ganoma, garlanded with roses, and led by **Synesia** (Understanding);
and **Psyche** follows in her golden chlamys pinned with a great diamond. All secure; the names are
meaningful Greek and are glossed.

- **The triumphal car of Love.** A gold chariot bordered with a jewelled band, two gold-rimmed wheels
  on balustered spokes of coloured gemstone — the vehicle at the centre of the world's approach to the
  theatre. Colonna describes it as a goldsmith's object, sun-like with encloistered gems.
- **the lovers bound as captives — the emotional climax of the triumph.** Poliphilo and Polia have
  their arms bound behind them with *rose-garlands* and are led behind the car as Love's *doryktetoi*
  (p. 330): captives, but bound in flowers, "softly and willingly." Poliphilo trembles; Polia and the
  nymphs laugh; he is reassured. This is the image the whole procession was building to — willing
  captivity to Love. The world's triumph should show the two bound in roses behind the car.
- **Synesia leads them** — "Understanding" draws the captive lovers: even in bondage to Love, reason
  attends. Colonna's allegory keeps its balance (cf. Synesia's opposite, blind Typhlote, p. 339).
- **Psyche's diamond** — carved (p. 342) with Cupid wounding himself: the chlamys-jewel that turns the
  Cupid-and-Psyche myth back on the god. "Not Pyrgoteles" (Alexander's gem-engraver) but a divine hand
  cut it.
