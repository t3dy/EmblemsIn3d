*(The title page. Latin.)*

THE HYPNEROTOMACHIA OF POLIPHILO, WHEREIN HE TEACHES THAT ALL HUMAN THINGS ARE NOTHING BUT A

## Notes

**Confidence: high.** Nineteen words of Latin and a five-line printing privilege; nothing here
is uncertain. Read against the facsimile by a second reader, 2026-09-20.

- **The title, and what it promises.** *Hypnerotomachia* is Colonna's own coinage from three
  Greek words — *hypnos* (sleep), *eros* (love), *machē* (strife) — so "the strife of love in a
  dream", which is exactly how Robert Dallington rendered it on his 1592 title page. The Latin
  tag under it is the book's thesis in one line: **that all human things are nothing but a
  dream**. A reader who takes the thousand pages of marble and nymphs that follow as a
  celebration of the world should be held to this sentence, printed first.
- **"Along the way."** *Obiter* — in passing, incidentally. The 1499 sets it as one word, here
  and again on the half-title of page 9; the Wikisource transcription splits it into *ob iter*.
  Either way it is the ordinary classical adverb. The antiquities, the orders of
  columns, the hieroglyphs, the gardens: the title page files them all as digression. It is a
  joke, or a defence, or both.
- **ILL. S. V.** — *Illustrissima Signoria Veneta*, the Most Illustrious Signory of Venice. The
  privilege is a copyright of a kind: the Republic forbids anyone else printing the book within
  its territory. Aldus Manutius printed it in December 1499 at the expense of Leonardo Crasso,
  whose dedication follows on the next page and who paid for the privilege as well as the paper.
- **The star-shaped colophon.** Six asterisks tapering 3–2–1, mirroring the tapering
  lines of the title above them. The whole page is set as a downward-pointing triangle. That
  shape — text cut to a figure rather than a measure — is the book's typographic signature and
  Aldus's most imitated page.
- **Second reader, 2026-09-20.** Collated letter by letter against the facsimile — Siena,
  Biblioteca Comunale degli Intronati, O.III.38, f. 1r, in the corpus at
  `site/images/siena/O.III.38_0001r.jpg`. Two things the transcription quietly tidies. The
  title reads **ATQVE OBITER**, one word. And the privilege's last word is broken
  **IMPPRIME / RE**, with a doubled p — a slip, or a compositor filling out a short line, on
  the most copied title page in Europe. Modern texts print *imprimere*; we record what the
  page prints.
