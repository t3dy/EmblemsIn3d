## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The founder's *tour de force* — casting an **unbroken bronze chain** in a piece-
mould (the lost-wax/piece-casting method described); the wind-rung **bells** with enclosed steel balls that
ring against the sphere in harmony with the great triangle, "beyond the sound of the Temple of Jerusalem";
and the start of the temple's final dimensioning (the eight-window wall 1½ feet thick). All secure; the
casting-note is a real foundry technique.

- **The wind-organ finial.** The lantern's chains hang bronze bells (steel balls sealed inside) that the
  wind swings against the great hollow triangle: a self-sounding aeolian chime crowning the temple, "sweet
  and great," surpassing the fabled sounding-chains of the Jerusalem Temple. The world's temple could carry
  a wind-rung chime at its lantern — sound as well as light and motion.
- **casting a chain without solder** — Colonna details the foundry trick (a four-part mould with a central
  hole, links cast one into the next): a genuine, admiring note on the caster's art, of a piece with the
  vine-dome (p. 201) and hollow eagles (p. 204). The temple showcases metallurgy at every scale.
- **the temple's three self-animating systems** — winds (weathervanes, p. 210), sound (these bells), and
  light (the crystal lamp, p. 208): the building moves, rings and glows on its own. A rich brief for a
  living, animated temple in the world.
- The final dimensioning (wall and pier thicknesses) is completed on p. 212, which then reaches the **door**.
