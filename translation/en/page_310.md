# Page 310 — Chapter XXI

Now among the flower-bearing and tender little fronds, innumerable birds adorned with
excellent plumage, of small and middling stature, and various little birds and
fowls, flitting unstably here and there, wandering, now up, now down, leaping with
delightful chatter, the sweet sonority of their song resounding everywhere — which
would have had virtue to provoke any woodland and inept heart to pleasure, joy and
solace, making festival with their little wings and feathers. Here the plaintive
nightingale, Daedalion weeping the death of Lycaon's daughter; the spotted blackbirds,
and the singing crested lark, or *galerita*, and the *terraneola*, sparrow, or lark;
the solitary sparrows; the most eloquent parrot, of manifold clothing — green, white,
yellow, crimson, and yellow with green; the singular (but not here) and marvellous
Phoenix; most white turtle-doves; Picus, husband of Pomona, betokening the swollen
angers of Circe; Aedon, tearful for her beloved husband Itylus; Asterie with feet
shod in rose; and the two magpies, Procne the roof-dweller; and pious Antigone, too
beautiful, without a tongue; Itys, doleful and funereal table; the gluttonous
*icteris*; Tereus the rock-dweller, keeping the royal pomp in his feathers, seeking
*pou pou, pu, pu* in his song, and in his head a bearer, marked with the military
crest; and, by Syrinx, the drowsy shepherd; and the birds of Palamedes, and the teal,
and the wanton partridge and *porphyrio*; Periclymenus, whose form Jupiter freely used
for his loves; and the *sygolis*, or *melancoryphos*, or blackcap, changeable in
autumn; likewise the robin, or redstart, and innumerable others of a lengthy telling.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** A bird-catalogue, the aviary counterpart to the plant-lists,
secure in its identifications; every bird is a metamorphosis-myth, which is the point
— an aviary of transformed humans. The Greek-Latin bird-names are Pliny's and
Ovid's.

- **An aviary of the transformed.** Colonna's birds are almost all people turned to
  birds: **Daedalion** (grieving, cast himself down and became a hawk); **Picus**
  (turned to a woodpecker by Circe — cf. p. 365, his faithfulness to Canens);
  **Aedon**, **Procne**, **Itys**, **Tereus** (the whole Ovidian nightingale-and-
  hoopoe family, Tereus keeping his crown as the hoopoe's crest and crying *pou
  pou*); **Antigone** turned to a stork; **Periclymenus** the shape-shifter. It is
  the transformation-key of the fountain chapter (ch. XXIII) extended into a garden
  of shapes — the island of love is populated by the metamorphosed.
- **the Phoenix "singular, but not here"** — a nice aside: the unique phoenix cannot
  be *among* the many birds of the aviary, since there is only one of it. Colonna's
  wit.
- **Tereus's cry "pou pou, pu, pu"** — the hoopoe's call, rendered as the Greek
  *ποῦ* ("where?"), as if Tereus forever asks where his victims are. Kept
  phonetically.
- This aviary closes the *prati* description; the river and the crossing to the
  inner terraces follow.
