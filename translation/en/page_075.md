Behold, I hear suddenly behind me a great crashing and din, like the shaking of the bony wings of the
dragon; and behind me, alas, I heard a sounding of a Trumpet. At once, wretched and gasping, I turned
myself about, and I see on one side many trees of Egyptian Carob, with their ripened, very long fruits
hanging and shaking, which by the wind had somewhat struck one against another. Whereupon, coming
swiftly to myself, and falling into laughter at a case so befallen, I moved on.

For which cause I then religiously invoked the kindly gods Jugatinus, the goddess Collatina, and
Vallonia, that as I journeyed through their consecrated places they might show themselves propitious to
me. Seeing that I had almost feared a warlike army because of the trumpet's sound; but, considering, I
judged it to be of shepherds' trumpets of bark, whereupon I rather reassured myself than mistrusted
otherwise.

But after this there was no long delay before I hear coming, singing, a company (by their voices of
tender and flowering age) of damsels graceful (as I judged) and beautiful, sporting and taking their
solace among the flowering herbs and in the gracious cool shades, free of any retarding suspicion, and
journeying with great applause among the comely flowers. The incredible sweetness of their modulated
voice, carried by the temperate and dew-bearing airs, spread itself through that delightful place, and
was brought back with the sweetest sound of an accompanying lyre.

At which novelty, searching about and bending down beneath the low branches, I saw them coming towards
me with a gesturing step. With their maidenly heads gathered in most notable fillets of threads of gold,
wrapped and above bound and garlanded with flowering Myrtle and with manifold flowers. And over their
snowy brows the yellow and trembling forelocks curled like vine-tendrils. And then down over their most
white and comely shoulders flowed the long tresses, composed with nymph-like neatness and elegant art.

Clothed in a Carpathian habit of silk most adorned, and of various colouring and texture. There were
three tunics, the one shorter than the other and distinct. The lowest of shellfish purple; above
followed the silken one of most green colour woven through with gold. The topmost of finest cotton,
saffroned and crimped, girt with a golden collar beneath the bound of their round little breasts.

Their outstretched arms were clothed by the last tunic, and copiously covered by the cotton one, which
granted with the highest grace the colour beneath. And near their swelling little hands they were fitly
bound with little cords of fine silk, with little golden loops, of a pleasurable artifice. And some of
them had their little feet excellently laced with double soles, with manifold binding of threads of gold
and of purpling silk. Some shod then over stretched hose of scarlet and greening cloth, and some over
the naked, of soft and gracious shining leather, and others of chamois dyed in most beautiful
colourings, without accusing the toes.
