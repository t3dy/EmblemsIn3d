This triumphal car six white elephants, yoked two by two, drew — such as would not be found in the land
of Agisymba, nor among the Gandari; nor were such subdued to the African triumph of great Pompey, nor
were such set to draw the triumph of Father Liber when India was conquered. With their trunks armed with
ivory and deadly tusks, and with a sweet trumpeting, they drew fittingly, with traces of finest silk of
a dark blue tincture, most beautifully twisted with threads of gold and of silver mingled, in tightest
knots woven four-square like ears of corn, such as are seen on Mount Gargano; with golden breastplates
filled with a multitude of most flashing and differing gems, with golden rings fitted, in which the
binding of all six ran.

Six most tender girls rode likewise in the manner of the first, with other unlike instruments,
excellently sharing in one sound; and all that the others did, these did in like manner. Two clothed in
crimson, two in a shining yellow, that inner colour of the flower of the crowfoot, and two in violet
purple.

The drawing elephants were covered with a covering of gold fringed with thick pearls, and pompously
adorned with other gems. And their necks encircled with round and thick jewels; and above their wide
foreheads there hung an unsteady little apple of marvellous pearls, with a long little beard of various
silk and golden threads, unsteady at every movement.
