Between the line A.B. and the last line of the master square M.N. I found that it was taken up by a
third — that is, divided into four partitions, three of which are attributed to the straight Beam, the
Zophorus, and the cornice. Which cornice claimed one part more than the Beam and the Frieze; that is
to say, if five portions are assigned to the Beam and as many equally to the Zophorus, the cornice
deservedly usurps six. And it exceeded this limit by so much the more, in that the discreet and
skilled craftsman had made a sloping licking-over above the margin determined for the Cyma of the
aforesaid cornice, half a foot remaining chamfered.

And this is not found to be observed in vain, because the lowest of the carved works set above are not
hidden by the coming-out, or projection, of the cornice. Although one may so much the more magnify the
part following above of the ornaments — as happens with the Zophorus, exceeding for this cause the
assigned Symmetry. Then above this first crowning there followed a perfect square, by this rule.

As much as the projection of the Zophorus was above the perpendicular of the columns, so much was that
one thrust out. Which, divided into two partitions, one part as it was found, so much thickness was
attributed to the topmost cornice. These two squares being constituted, one to a side: the remainder
therefore set between, at the perpendicular of the opening of the gate, divided into seven portions,
the middle one was reserved for a throne, or Niche, where the Nymph-like statue resided. Three then,
on this side and on that, were left to the flanking parts.

The coming-out of the projection of the upper cornice offers itself easily: making of the line of its
thickness a Four-square, divided by the Diagonal, the just rule of the projection is found.
