Above this measure there grew a form of the vessel aforesaid, raised a foot, and began to widen for a
palm and a half. Which half was given to the exquisite border of wound fronds and flowers, almost all
torn free of that jacinth. The diameter two quarters and a half. Beneath this little border there stood
out round about certain slices of a modest rise and a tempered keeling, somewhat thick at the top, and
narrowing away at the bottom.

Then it rose most beautifully to the mouth two quarters and a half. Distinguishedly hollowed with
twisted little hollows. The mouth of a little shell with wide rims, beneath the body with an elegant
moulding, and at the grasp of the vessel with turned gorges, waves and rounds. Of such lineaments were
the borders set both below and above.

At the border of the ewer below, within the said enclosure, there were held two cut half-rings, pressed
down by their figure, set crosswise opposite with their hooked ends one to the other. Which were held in
the biting jaws of two lizards, or little dragons. And the little dragons, left perfectly of a vein of
emerald, the rest crusted away, lay with four lizard-like feet upon the ridge of the lower vessel;
which ridge, between the ewer and the lower vessel, had a rise of a quarter. And from its lofty
narrowing, descending, it ended with a lineament of an inverted moulding at the encircled rim of the
body, where the surrounding border was.

Which sloping ridge was most diligently scaled of the jacinth. Only the little dragons left of the
emerald vein, with their serpent bellies held above the scaling, and so the four little feet. Which
little dragons, one at each side, at the cut end of the fall of the said ridge, above the cut of the
little cornice, made with their turning tails a round and ready coil towards the spine. And then they
made another like it below. These coils were for the handles. The lower whirl, where it was joined with
the vessel, cut into two parts, one here and the other there, passed into a marvellous foliage. And at
the right and left side they entered half a foot with an elegant polish into the border. Which fronds
were seen almost wholly in relief; and the ground, that is the solid flat lying beneath the body, was
of the jacinth. The whole circumference of the body was taken up by these leafy tails, or rather a
girding band of two feet.

It remains to speak of the space that stands a foot and a half to each side. The body of the vessel
from the girding downward I judged a stupendous work, and rather divine; I beheld the said vessel
covered all over with an exact vine of carving. Of which the stems, or shoots, with vine-leaves, with
little tendrils and ringed shoots, of a vein fitted to the devising, of topaz. Such as would not be
found in the island Ophiodes.
