Sitting, then, these solacing, comely and outstanding little Nymphs upon the sandalwood thwarts, paired, turned toward the
divine lord, and to us the delicate back — the divine helmsman, the smooth wings distended, explicating, having called
with odoriferous breath to himself the sweet zephyr, wind-blowing rendered the holy feathers (more than the ardent
carbuncle to the pre-shining torches, coruscating lightnings), and, the plumate wings filled with flower-bearing air, we
began to abandon the garrulous shores, and to navigate upon the deep and spacious sea, with grateful calm tranquil. And,
of great and fearful veneration, and of singular sweetness and alacrity, my amorous and contumacious heart being stuffed,
and all shakeable, distinctly with my genius I cogitated: what heart so inhuman could ever be found — or one of such
hardness, so vivid and so vigorous, however more rough than the bark of the palm to imagine — that, to such-made beautiful
and concupiscible presences, and divine objects, to all gentleness most-tender and most-soft, and mortified, invalid, and
most-lenient, of a sudden would not have shown itself?

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The boat **departs the shore**: the six nymphs turn to face Cupid on the prow (backs to the
lovers), Polia names them "the readiest handmaids" of the god, and Cupid, spreading his wings and calling the **zephyr**,
sails them out onto the calm deep. Poliphilo reflects that no heart, however hard, could resist such beauty. All secure.

- **Cupid the helmsman, wind in his wings.** The god does not row: he *is* the propulsion — spreading his wings, calling
  the zephyr, and filling them with "flower-bearing air" to drive the boat. The crossing is powered by Love's own wings
  and the spring wind. A vivid, buildable image for the world's ferry (Cupid at the prow, wings as sail).
- **the nymphs as fuel.** Colonna's simile-chain casts the nymphs' beauty as "fuel to the ardent flame" — the necessary
  matter of Love's fire, as sulphur to Vulcan, fright to the Furies. Their sensuousness serves "our amorous mystery": the
  crossing to Cythera is an erotic initiation, its crew its instruments.
- **the six named handmaids** — Polia confirms the six rowers (Aselgia, Cypria, etc., p. 277) are Cupid's *pedissequae*
  (handmaids): the boat is crewed by the aspects of Venus's service, ferrying the initiates. The world's crossing carries
  this meaning.
- Poliphilo's reflection on love's irresistibility continues (p. 279), then the sea-gods come to honour Cupid (p. 280).
