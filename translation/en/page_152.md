For this cause I was strongly disposed with all my strength to conquer and shut in my loose and
wandering appetite, and my wavering mind, and to overcome my immodest will, judging by now that it
could not be otherwise than so.

At last, thinking upon it in the secret of my kindled heart, I began to think that without fail I could
match my present and continual pain to that of wicked Tantalus, to whose dry and thirsting mouth the
cold and purest waters offer themselves, tasteable and joyful, and to whose raging appetite the sweet
fruits present themselves most welcomely up to his gaping mouth — and he remains at last unfed and
fasting of the one and of the other.

Alas for me: in no other manner was a most comely Nymph, notable in form, of a flowering age, adorned
more than can be said with angelic manners and famous for a chief honesty, presented most kindly before
my eyes — the sight of whom exceeded every exquisite and delightful human content; and I at her side,
she full of everything that avails solacingly to love and provokes to longing, and drawing the
intellect away from every other working, heaping it in herself alone — and yet it did not succour my
panting and delighting desire.

Now, since by such a way the burning longings were not put out, as much as I could I quieted my
languishing heart, inflamed beyond measure, by slackening it with an amorous and solacing hope. And
with this reasoning: that no coal is ever found so far quenched that, being near a kindled one, it does
not likewise, by the fitting disposition, take fire. But my unbridled eyes, more than at any hour,
inflamed that heart, defenceless and unarmed against any power, with a more uncommon desire for her
chief and divine beauties. Ever more beautiful, more elegant, more comely, more to be longed for, and
extremely apt and excellent to love, showing herself plainly with a marvellous increase of sweet
pleasure.

Then I thought sincerely: if by chance the highest gods perceive that I desire and wickedly long for
and presume upon forbidden delights — perhaps in this holy place, and of such a person, reasonably
forbidden — might it not easily befall me, as a profane man, as it did to many others who have
shamelessly offended: the cold and unbroken angers justly used upon bold and most confident Ixion? And
