Behind her head, then, was a leafy Strawberry-tree of unfalling leaves, copious with its soft and round
fruits, and with little birds that seemed to chatter and to be a cause of sweet sleep. At her feet stood
a Satyr, itching with wantonness and all stirred, standing on his goat's feet. With his muzzle joined
to a nose goat-like and snub; with the beard on his chin parted into two curlings of Goatish twists,
and so likewise at his shaggy flanks and in this same manner at his head, with hairy ears, and crowned
with fronds, with a face adulterated between goat and human. I judged that to his most acute genius the
stone-carver had most skilfully, and at his pleasure, had the workmanship of nature present in his Idea.

The said Satyr had violently seized the Arbutus tree by the branches with his left hand, and bending it
by his strength above the sleeping Nymph, showed that he made her a gracious shade. And with the other
arm he drew the end of a little curtain, which was knotted to the branches nearest the trunk. Between
the strawberry-tree and the Satyr sat two infant Satyrs: one with a vase in his hands, and the other
with his hands wrapped about by two coiling serpents.
