# Page 215 — Chapter XVII

And lo, with the utmost veneration, maturely, one carried, with registered procession, the ritual book,
duly invested with velvet of Cyan silk, of most-round pearls-in-union, in the form of a flying little
dove nobly of padded re-weaving, with little gold handles — each of them (the virgins) marked with
Pancarpies (all-fruit garlands) on the comely head. Another brought two most-subtle little veils,
lily-decked, and two purple Tutuli (fillet-caps). The third had the holy brine in a golden vase. The
fourth held the *secespita* (sacrificial knife) with an oblong ivory handle, round and solid, joined at
the pommel with silver and gold, and nailed with Cyprian copper; and, as well, a *praefericulum* (ritual
jug). The fifth was bearer of a hyacinthine *lepista* (bowl), most-eyeing (beautiful), full of fountain
water. The sixth bore a golden Mitre, with richest Lemnisci (ribbons) pendent, throughout ornated
copiously with precious and most-refulgent gems. All these, a little priestess, candle-bearer, preceded,
with a Candle never yet kindled, of candid, most-purged, and virgin material. These delicate virgins,
taught to do the sacred and divine things, and scrupulous at the ministries — more expert than the
Etruscan discipline — and, at the sacrosanct sacrifices, with ancient institute apt and most-observant,
to the pontifical Priestess, with obstinate religion reverent, presented themselves.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The **ritual implements** borne in procession by the seven virgins for the rite of
Venus Physizoa: the ritual book in a **dove-shaped cyan-velvet cover**, veils and purple caps, the holy
brine, the sacrificial knife (*secespita*) and jug (*praefericulum*), a bowl of fountain water, a gemmed
gold mitre, and an unlit virgin candle — all handled by virgins "more expert than the Etruscan discipline."
All secure; a precise reconstruction of antique sacrificial apparatus.

- **The apparatus of the rite.** Colonna itemises the sacred vessels with antiquarian exactness — the
  *secespita* (the priest's iron sacrificial knife), the *praefericulum* (the ritual ewer), the *lepista*
  (libation-bowl), the *tutuli* (priestly caps), the *lemnisci* (mitre-ribbons), and the *murie* (sacred
  brine). This is a learned dictionary of Roman cult-objects, staged as a live liturgy. A ready propertied-
  set for the world's temple-rite (and the visual-novel's temple scene).
- **the dove-book.** The ritual book is bound in cyan velvet worked into the shape of a **dove** (Venus's
  bird), sewn with pearls, with gold clasps: a beautiful, buildable object. The book of the rite is itself
  an emblem of the goddess.
- **the unlit virgin candle.** The candle-bearer carries a pure candle "never yet kindled" — it will be lit
  at the rite's climax (the drawing of sacred fire from the well, ch. XVIII). Its unlit state is deliberate:
  the ceremony's central act is the first kindling.
- **"more expert than the Etruscan discipline"** — the *disciplina Etrusca* was the Romans' benchmark for
  ritual correctness (haruspicy, augury); the virgins surpass it. The surpassing-topos, on liturgy.
- The rite itself (the priestess's actions at the well, the kindling, the drawing of water) follows through
  the remaining pages of the chapter into ch. XVIII.
