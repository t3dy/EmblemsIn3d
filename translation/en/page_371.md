excellently woven and intertwined rose-bushes of the hundred-petalled kind,
little Greek autumn ones, and small crowns of purpling flower straying twined
along the lattice; with leaves not to fall, of unthinkable fragrance, of most
admirable freshness.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** A long ekphrasis of the Adonis fountain's setting, secure
throughout; the measurements are given plainly and the botany is precise. The one
oddity is `xii passi` (twelve paces) for the surrounding measure of a hexagon then
also said to be thirty-six in circumference — the text gives both figures and I
translate both rather than reconcile them, as with the Great Portal's numbers.

- **The hexagonal fountain** — note that this second sacred fountain is
  *hexagonal*, where the theatre's fountain of Venus was heptagonal (the seven
  columns). The change of figure is deliberate: six is the number of the tomb it
  crowns (Adonis, whose story the next page tells), not of the planets.
- **the citrus cloister** — orange, lemon and citron in an "alternating marriage,"
  a ring of fruit trees enclosing the spring: the same trained-citrus architecture
  the Cythera reconstruction gives the river-pergola (GARDENS.md §5). Here it is a
  *claustro*, a cloister, which is what Colonna calls it.
- **the gold pergola with real roses** — the same figure Hunt reads at the fountain
  of Venus (GARDENS.md §7): the structure is gold, the roses are living. Colonna
  repeats it here, which strengthens the case that the enclosure we built for the
  Cythera fountain is right.
- **`centifolie… graecule autumnale`** — the hundred-petalled rose and the "little
  Greek autumn" rose are both real Plinian varieties.
