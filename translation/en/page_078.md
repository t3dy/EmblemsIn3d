And she added: "Here, in fine, is a wholesome Field, most wide in its bounds, various in herbs and
pleasant to the sight in its plants, fruitful of all crops. Fortified with famous hills. Filled with
all harmless animals, and conspicuous and most crowded with every pleasure. Copious of every fruit,
with a universal exuberance, and adorned with the purest springs."

Another said: "Hold all this fixed and firm, dear guest. This happy territory is more fertile than the
fruitful Mount Taurus on its northern aspect, of which wandering fame declares that the vine's cluster
is of two cubits, and that one fig-tree produces seventy measures of its fruit."

Then the third, festive, added: "This sacred shore exceeds the richness of the Hyperborean island lying
in the Indian Ocean; nor are the Lusitanian lands such, nor Talge on the Caspian mountain."

Continuing, the fourth said, affirming with more fervour: "The Egyptian abundance is empty in
comparison with ours, though it be called the public Granary of the world."

Lastly, one alluring in aspect to every headlong fall added with elegant pronouncing: "In this kindly
country there would be found no encumbrance of overflowing marshes vexing us with troublesome air, nor
any shutting-in by most rugged mountains, but by most adorned little hills. And on the outward side it
is strongly walled about with harsh and pathless precipices. And so, in this manner, every sadness being
eliminated, here is everything that can bring delight, and a refuge of the gods, with blessed security of
mind.

"Beyond all these things said, we are the followers of a renowned and distinguished queen, most
munificent and of most lavish generosity, called Eleuterylida, of wonderful clemency and most merciful;
who governs here with the highest and strongest wisdom, and rules with a most ample dominion, and
commands, fortunate and happy, with heaped-up glory. And it will greatly please her when we shall bring
you to her venerable presence and majestic sight. And if by chance the others, her fellow-servants and
courtiers, perceive it, they would come running here in troops to look upon what is rarely seen here.
Therefore drive from you and shut out every afflicting sadness, and compose your mind, taking your
comfort festively with us, and give yourself to solace and to pleasure, every trembling being driven
away."
