## Notes

**Confidence: medium** (the date 1462 and the historical frame are secure). Polia reaches **herself**: born of the Lelian
line under **Venetian rule** ("the Marine Lion"), named for **Lucretia** ("the chaste Roman"), and grown to youth by the
year **1462**. Then the **origin of the whole love**: combing her golden hair at her window, she is seen by **Poliphilo**,
who is **struck instantly and violently in love** (like a lightning-blasted oak), and begins his daily vigils beneath her
windows. All secure; the founding event of the book, in Polia's own telling.

- **1462 — the book's date.** Polia dates her youth to 1462, and the events (Poliphilo's love, her plague, the dream) to
  that time — the Hypnerotomachia's own internal chronology, and a key anchor (the woodcut-inscriptions and the acrostic
  point to a real 1460s–1490s composition). The love begins under the Venetian Lion, in a real year, in a real Treviso.
- **The first sight — the love's true beginning.** Here, at last, is *how it started*, from Polia's side: she at the
  window combing her hair (the Renaissance topos of the golden-haired beauty), Poliphilo passing, "his heart shattered
  at the first pure regard" like a lightning-struck oak. The whole vast dream of Book I flowers from this ordinary
  street-glimpse. Colonna grounds the cosmic romance in a domestic instant.
- **Polia's candid vanity.** She admits she feared becoming a **Narcissus** at her own mirror, and defends saying so —
  "as to feign the false is a vice, no less to conceal the true." Her self-aware near-vanity is the seed of the *cruelty*
  she confessed (p. 382): the beauty that dooms her family (the Venus-comparison) lives in her too. Book II is her
  reckoning with it.
- **Perseus/Andromeda, Lucius/Photis** — her hair surpasses Andromeda's (whom Perseus loved) and Photis's (Lucius's lover
  in Apuleius): the surpassing-topos, now in Polia's mouth, and the Apuleian frame once more (cf. p. 282). Her voice keeps
  the book's learning while telling a girl's-eye story.
