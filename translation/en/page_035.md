# Page 35 — Chapter IV

Not a little stupefied, therefore, turning it over and looking with the greatest curious delight upon
that huge machine cast into an animal by human genius, most worthily imagined — so that in every
member it shared unfailingly the excellent harmony and joining — there came into my retaining memory
the ill-fated horse of Seianus.

Then, dazzled by such an artificial mystery, there offered itself to my eyes a spectacle no less
wonderful, a very great Elephant, and with it the greatest desire to hasten towards it. But behold, I
hear from another quarter a human groan as of sickness. I stood at once, my hair rising, and without
further deliberation, hurrying towards the groan, I climbed a heap of ruins of great fractures and
marble cuttings. And having gone forward fitly from there — behold, I see a most Vast and wonderful
colossus, with the feet hollowed out and without soles, and all the Shin-bones open and empty.

And coming from there to the head, gazing with horror, I conjectured that the air let in through the
gaping soles, by a divine invention, caused the groan to be moderately expressed. Which lay silent,
reclining on its back, cast of metal by a wonderful artifice, of middle age, holding its head raised a
little upon a cushion. With the semblance of a sick man, with the mouth showing that it sighed and
groaned. Gaping, of a tallness of sixty paces. And by the hairs one could climb up over the breast,
and by the matted and tormented hairs of the thick beard, to the lamenting mouth. Which by passages
was all void and empty. Through that, then, driven by the stimulus of curious searching, without other
