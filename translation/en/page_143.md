For which cause, if I had seen this one as a fourth among the three quarrelling goddesses, and had been
appointed judge by Jove above, as the Phrygian shepherd was in the shadowing woods of Mesaulon, without
doubt I should justly have judged her far more of an admirable form, and without equal more worthy of
the inscribed apple, and without any respect to the others. Whom at the first sight I suspected to be
certainly Polia; but the condition of the unaccustomed dress and place dissuaded me. And by this
lawful discerning, staying doubtful, I kept myself in a reverent suspense.

This sun-born Nymph, then, clothed her maidenly and divine little body in a most fine cloth of green
silk woven with a warp of gold (that most joyful colouring of the little feathers of a duck's neck)
mingled, over a white and crimped shift of cotton, covering her cleanest and most delicate flesh and
milky skin, such as the inventress Pamphila, daughter of Plates, first knew how to weave in the island
of Cos. Which shift graciously seemed to cover whitest and flesh-coloured roses.

But the gown above this, elegantly worn with the smallest little folds, or wrinkles, clinging, and
above the wide flanks girt most tightly with a little cord of gold, holding down the folds of the
thinnest cloth over the delicately swelling breast — above this first girdle the excess of the long
garment was drawn up, the fringed hem remaining evenly as far as her fleshy ankles.

Then this raised and tucked-up garment was fastened at the first golden cord with the sacred girdle of
holy Cytherea. Which gathered raising, curved about and elegantly composed, went round above the modest
waist, with a welcome swelling, and above the firm and quivering haunches; and the remainder of the
garment let down veiled the round and small belly with the finest wrinklings, unsteady at the breathing
of the soft breezes and by the movement of her body, falling as far as her milky hams.
