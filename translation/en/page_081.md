Now, in the face opposite the most beautiful Nymph of the fountain, I saw the entrance, with a most
polished door. All the artifice of that excellent Stone-carver who, I thought, had carved the sleeping
Nymph; in the frieze of which I saw this title in Greek characters: ASAMINTHOS. Such baths, then, the
amplitude of Tatius does not surpass.

Within, seats went round in four stone steps, girt with a continuous joining, all minutely inlaid with
jasper and Chalcedonies of every colouring. Two of the steps the tepid water covered, up to the margin,
or surface, of the third. In the angles there stood at each a free and rounded little Corinthian
column, of various colouring, with straggling waves of jasper — the more welcome, the more the artful
nature is wont to make it — with a decent base. And the capitals most excellently composed beneath a
beam; beyond which lay the Zophorus of naked little boys playing in the waters, with little
water-monsters, with wrestling and infant contests, with apt strivings of a readiness fitting to their
age, and lively movements and games; and it girt it round most beautifully with a crown set above.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The weathervane explained (the wind turns the vane, the hollow nape takes
the draught, the trumpet sounds — and so the carob-trees had frightened him), and the bath's
interior begun. ASAMINTHOS is rightly kept in the Greek, and it is the Homeric word for a
bathing-tub, which is the joke. The four inlaid steps with the water covering two of them, and the
Corinthian colonnettes at the angles, are clear enough to model. One reference is left unresolved:
*tale therme non suppedita l'amplitudine di Tacio* is rendered "the amplitude of Tatius does not
surpass", keeping a name that in this context is almost certainly the baths of Titus.

*Confidence stated 2026-09-20 by a second reader, not by the translator (ticket tr-confidence-unstated-180).*
