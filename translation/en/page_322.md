# Page 322 — Chapter XXI

— in the middle square a porphyry altar resided, at the angles of which, near the little
cornice, stood forth four horned wethers' heads, snail-wise and smoothly carved. From which
hung down incurving little bundles, with all the accessories recited of the round one. Upon
which lay an antique amphora-vase, with four handles equally divided, of finest sardonyx most
beautifully conjoined with its familiar agate, expressed with wondrous artifice. Out of which
came forth a most-polished box, with its lower sphere, or rounding, of one pace its diameter. On
the top of which were four stalks, equally a foot apart and distinct, and each a proportioned
sphere; upon each a peacock, with its tail let down, resided, with its head in a salver upon a
middle stalk exceeding the four spheres. Out of the salver ascended a stalk again, with four
branches, and each sustained a sphere. In the middle, raised, the stalk held another sphere. Upon
which grew an egg-shaped circle, spreading two branches to a side, and each had a sphere, and the
like at its top. Such a disposition was ordinarily observed in every vase, uniform — the place, the
box, altar, vase, lineaments.

NEXT follows, and the other seven steps. Upon the height it encompassed, in the manner of a wall,
of greenest myrtle, with towers — like the others fashioned, with cypresses, and with all the rest,
with a fleet-like feathering most excellently heaped up. Within this cloister likewise were two
squares, alternated of kitchen-garden figuring, with such a plotting. There were two quadrangles
banded with symmetrical knotwork, and with the enclosing circle, in the manner of the quadrangle
first described. In which circling, egregiously painted, was seen an **Eagle** with outspread wings,
comprehending the circular space. In place of the acanthine foliage, there were majuscule (capital
letters); and first, at the left side, between the two bands, in the space excluded by the circles:
in the first were two, **AL**. In the other excluded, four, **ESMA**. Toward the hedge, in the first
space between the circles, three, **GNA**. And in the following, four, **DICA** —

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The altar-and-vase at the parterre's centre (a porphyry altar with four
ram-heads, a sardonyx-and-agate amphora, box topiary tiered with peacocks) — a standard template
"observed in every vase." Then a **seventh** flight of steps to a myrtle-hedged cloister whose
parterres carry a spread-winged **eagle** and, planted in majuscule letters, the beginning of an
inscription: **AL · ESMA · GNA · DICA**. All secure; the letters are read straight from the woodcut.

- **The altar-vase template.** Every parterre-centre carries the same furniture: a carved altar
  (bucrania/ram-heads and festoons), a great semiprecious amphora on top, and out of it a tiered box
  topiary studded with spheres and roosting peacocks. Colonna states it is "uniform… in every vase" —
  so the world can model one and instance it at each parterre-centre.
- **the letters in the beds — an inscription grows out of the garden.** The next cloister's parterres
  abandon acanthus for planted capital letters: **AL / ESMA / GNA / DICA…**, laid out around a
  spread-winged eagle. Colonna is spelling a Latin motto in living herbs (it continues on p. 323).
  The eagle + the emerging letters point to a Jovian dedicatory inscription — the garden writes.
- **sardonyx "with its familiar agate"** — sardonyx and agate as banded companion-stones; the
  vase-carver exploits the natural banding, the same connoisseurship of stone as the columns of p. 309.
- The world's Cythera could render this ring as the *inscribed parterre*: an eagle roundel and herb-set
  majuscules, the reveal that the terraces themselves bear a text.
