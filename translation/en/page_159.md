# Page 159 — Chapter XIV

The left-hand tablet held that same trusting Nymph, who had climbed upon the meek and white bull. And
he carried her, timid, across the swelling sea.

On the front face I saw Cupid, with a countless crowd of both kinds of people wounded, marvelling that
he should draw his bow towards high Olympus. On the hinder face I beheld Mars before the throne of
great Jove, complaining that his son had torn his impenetrable breastplate. And the kindly lord showed
him his own wounded breast. And in the other hand, his arm stretched out, he held written: NO ONE.

---

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The four panels of the first car: Europa crowning the bulls, Europa carried
over the sea, Cupid shooting at Olympus, and Mars complaining to Jove. All four are correctly
described. Two editorial losses to flag. The Italian carries the woodcut captions — PRIMA TABELLA,
SECUNDA SINISTRA — and the English drops them, so the reader cannot tell which panel is which. And
the word in Jove's outstretched hand, NEMO, is replaced by "NO ONE": that is the point of the panel
(Jove shows his own wounded breast and the legend says *no one* is exempt), and it is an inscription
on the car, not narration.

*Confidence stated 2026-09-20 by a second reader, not by the translator (ticket tr-confidence-unstated-180).*
