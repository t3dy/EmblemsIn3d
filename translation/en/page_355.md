The first, then, lower pergola, was covered of odoriferous myrtle and full of flower, laboriously compacted
of little gold rods above, in arched curvature, led round to the cornice, upon the little arches of the
golden columnlets. Which resided above, with the little bases, on the surface of the inner lip of the fourth
box, led round with most-exquisite chasing.

The levelled ground under the first pergola — not so much to stupefy the intellect, as to un-sense the
sense. It was, then, all paved, in a ring, of odorous ladanum, with a graded mixture of Amber, Musk, Storax,
and Benzoin, condensed in a dusky colouring. In which most-fragrant mixture were then, elegantly, whitest
pearls fixed, like tessellation, reduced into a circumvolved embroidered band of antique foliage, with
olive-fructification; and within the loops the flowers, or within their calyxes little animals and little
birds — a divine and incomparable working, and pressable by divine feet — pavemented as never Zenodorus
availed to find.

The covering of the second levelling of the flatness under the pergola, of manifold roses opulently
disseminated, and with the accessories of the first, was of a paste of Coral, which in the grinding had kept
its redness. And in this fulgurated, comelily depicted, a marvellous embroidery of most-ancient fronds of
finest emeralds, and the flowers of most-even sapphires.

The third walkway, likewise of minute and flowering myrtle was the pergola, of a most-precious composite of
lapis lazuli ground small, ponderous, with its cyan colour somewhat adulterating toward green, marvellously
flagged. In which, with excellent glue, were commixed fragments, or minutiae massed, or cuttings, of all the
jewels universally that fostering nature has known to produce; and with points of gold most-beautifully, in
an evening to the level, most-terse, pre-shining, pavemented. Think how much voluptuousness, how much
pleasure, and solace, and allurement of so many delights, it incredibly conferred on the human senses — that
to the blessed spirits it offered itself marvellous.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The three pergola-gallery pavements — the most extravagant surfaces in the book: the
first of **perfumed paste** (ladanum, amber, musk, storax, benzoin) inlaid with pearls; the second of
**ground coral** with emerald fronds and sapphire flowers; the third of **powdered lapis lazuli** studded
with fragments of every gem and points of gold — under myrtle pergolas on gold-arched peristyles. All secure;
a climax of the material imagination.

- **Floors you can smell.** The first gallery is paved not in stone but in a hardened *perfume-paste* —
  ladanum, amber, musk, storax, benzoin — set with pearls in a foliage pattern, so the floor releases scent
  underfoot. Coral and lapis pave the others. Colonna's theatre engages every sense; the pavements are the
  extreme case. The world can suggest them as glowing red-coral and deep-blue lapis rings under the pergolas
  (scent it cannot render, but the colour and gem-inlay it can).
- **the gold peristyle of the galleries.** Each pergola is carried on a ring of little gold columns and
  arches, the spandrels filled with flat-inlaid chalcedony, agate and jasper: a jewelled miniature peristyle
  running round the cavea at three levels. A buildable ornament for the theatre's inner face.
- **"pressable by divine feet"… "Zenodorus"** — the pavements are fit only for gods to walk on; Zenodorus
  (the great bronze-founder of Nero's Colossus) could not have made them. The surpassing-topos on flooring.
- With this the theatre's fabric is complete; the last pages of ch. XXII (356–357) finish the interior and
  bring the party to the Fountain of Venus at the centre, which ch. XXIII describes.
