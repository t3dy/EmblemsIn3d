It is manifest that the remainder of the aforesaid closure, on the one side and the other, was a great
display of stupendous building; and this was clearly understood from certain workings preserved
untouched here and there. As in the lower parts certain Dwarf columns designed to resist the
unbearable weight; others Corinthian with unnoticed swelling, pregnant with so moderate a polish, and
just as Symmetry required, and as the exigency of the heaviness and of the ornament wished — the
artificial reasoning being drawn as it were from human likeness, and shrewdly gained. As a man to whom
it belongs to sustain a heavy weight must have broad soles beneath robust legs, so in the measured
fabric the Dwarf columns are attributed to the weight-bearing, and thereafter, for the adorning, the
slender Corinthian and Ionic.

And according to the requirement of the harmony of the building, so all the parts stood with approved
elegance. With a decent sharing of the colouring of the marbles and a pleasing distinction, with a
gracious sharing towards the object: of Porphyry, of Ophite, Numidian, Alabaster, fire-flecked,
Laconian, and of white marble waved with veins, and dark-red degenerate with whitest markings. Others
of manifold colouring confusedly mixed. And from the circumference its rising was found by another rule
than from the diameter of the lower thickness.

I found also a rare form of Cushioned base, which above the Plinth had two trochili, separated by an
interposition of little necks and Astragals, with the topmost Torus.

And in divers parts there was a hiding by the hanging and thick ivy, creeping from the ground upward —
the wood whose cup divides Bacchus from Thetis — with its scattered fruitful Clusters of black berries,
and with green down, taking up here and there many places of the ancient building, together with many
other wall-shrubs. And in the cracks grew the lively little finger-fern; and elsewhere hung the
Navelwort, and Houseleek, to which its pleasing name belongs, hanging low in the eaves. And in other
breakages the Pellitory, and diuretic Chickweed, and Polypody, and Maidenhair, and the fringed
Spleenwort with its rusty back, and the jointed lesser Moonwort, and other House-leeks that love the
antiquity of walls and of stones, and the Hair-moss, and the green Olive-grove that dwells in ruins.
Whereupon many worthy works were clothed and covered with this and much other greenery.
