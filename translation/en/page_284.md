foliage, with the utmost most-comely adornment, three hieroglyphs: an antique little vase, in the mouth-
gap of which burned a little flame; and then was the world; joined together with a little branch of osier,
at the soft breaths of the spring-bearing and obsequious zephyr wind-blown, flutteringly volant, and
choicely inconstant. The interpretation of which I made thus:

> **AMOR VINCIT OMNIA** — "Love conquers all."

## Notes

**Confidence: high** (the hieroglyph and its reading are secure); medium for the surrounding prose. The
description of **Cupid's boat** (prow-for-stern, "where all the mysteries of love breathed") and its
**standard bearing the rebus AMOR VINCIT OMNIA** — a flaming vase + the world + an osier-withy, which
Poliphilo reads as "Love conquers all." All secure; one of the book's most famous hieroglyphs.

- **AMOR VINCIT OMNIA — the emblem of the crossing.** The boat to Cythera flies a cyan-silk standard on
  which three picture-signs (a vase with a flame, the globe of the world, a binding osier-withy) spell
  Virgil's *omnia vincit Amor* (Eclogue X). This is the motto of the whole Cythera sequence, and the
  natural inscription for the world's ferry-boat / approach-standard. The world could carry this hieroglyph
  on the sail or a banner.
- **the reversible boat "with prow for stern."** Cupid's vessel is built prow-for-poop — a paradoxical
  craft "where all the mysteries of love breathed," made by Venus for her son. A distinctive, renderable
  boat-form for the digit-0 sailing.
- **Poliphilo "like a murmuring bee" gathering Polia's delights** — the bee-in-the-meadow simile for the
  senses feeding on beauty (cf. the bee/elephant hieroglyph of the polyandrion, and the "murmuring bee" of
  the lover's eyes). Consistent imagery of the heart as forager.
- The reading of hieroglyphs is the book's signature interpretive game; here Poliphilo performs it in
  miniature on the sail before the singing begins (p. 285).
