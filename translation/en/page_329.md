# Page 329 — Chapter XXII

Consequent was another most noble trophy, borne aloft. On the high spear-shaft a sphere
stood forth upon a *pyrronium*, between an inflected lunar-crescent of two little wings,
subtly leafed with gold foil, one leaf pressed page-wise upon the other.

The remainder of which, forced into a circling, rendered a crown held by a little band,
through the middle of which the spear, slightly balustered, passed perpendicularly. Under
the crown a little basin, with the bottom of an ewer-vase descending upon the oarage of
two conjoined wings. Then an egg-shaped figure, with a byssus-boss coruscating in the
navel. Fixed under this was a sphere, scored melon-wise, with flying little bundles
opportunely bound in.

Many others, of prolix telling: the standard-shafts of which — some of ebony, others of
red, citrine and white sandalwood, and of whitest ivory, and gilt, and covered with
silver, and of other precious woods. Every thing skilfully fashioned of most tenuous gold,
silver, and of polished material, and formed of green silk, and of every other gracious
colouring, with pleasant flowering. Multiply adorned with gems in the fit places, every
thing harmoniously destined and conspicuously applied, with pendent vertebrae —

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** Another emblematic trophy (crescent-wings, crown, ewer, byssus-boss,
melon-scored sphere), then a general account of the trophies' precious materials — ebony,
tricolour sandalwood, ivory, gold, silver, silk, gems. All secure; a passage of pure
material inventory.

- **The trophy-standards are luxury objects.** Colonna pauses to say the whole set is made
  of ebony and sandalwood shafts, ivory, gilt and silvered metal, green silk and gems — the
  triumph of Love is also a display of the goldsmith's and joiner's art, "every thing
  harmoniously destined." The world could render the trophy-standards as jewelled poles.
- **the crescent-and-crown trophy** — winged crescent, a crown held by a band, an ewer on
  paired wings, a gemmed egg: another disarmed-panoply device in the series, its meaning left
  to the emblem rather than an inscription.
- **byssus** — sea-silk; a boss of it "coruscating in the navel" of the egg-figure. Colonna's
  taste for rare materials extends to the smallest ornament.
- The next standard (p. 330) carries a winged Cupid treading an apple and the Greek title
  **DORYKTETOI** — "the spear-won."
