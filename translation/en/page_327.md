# Page 327 — Chapter XXII

Another was bearing yet another trophy. On the point was a little band of laurel;
beneath it, a pair of wings of blackest eagle, outspread; and then, set below, lay the
face of a most noble boy. There followed, and then, crosswise, two thunderbolts bound
together with little bundles of gold and woven silk, flying; and, likewise bound
crosswise to the spear, a sceptre, holding suspended a superb robe.

A bearer, too, was another, of a trophy: of a helmet, with a buffalo's head; and beneath
it an antique cuirass, with two shields knotted at each brachial outlet; between which
two little bands, one to a side, hung pendent, holding the Cleonean skin, with the
knobbed and gland-knotted, sinewy club.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** Two more trophies of arms: the second Jovian (laurel, black-eagle
wings, thunderbolts, sceptre and robe — the disarmed panoply of Jupiter); the third
Herculean (buffalo-headed helmet, cuirass, twin shields, and the Nemean lion-skin with
the knotted club). All secure; the attributes identify the gods whose arms Love has taken.

- **Love disarms the gods.** The trophies are a systematic inventory of surrendered divine
  weapons: Mars's cuirass (p. 326), now **Jupiter's** thunderbolts, sceptre and eagle-
  wings, and **Hercules's** lion-skin and club. Cupid triumphs over the strongest of the
  gods — the *omnia vincit Amor* topos made into a parade of captured arms.
- **the Cleonean skin and knobbed club** — the Nemean lion (killed near Cleonae) and
  Hercules's olive-wood club, his unmistakable attributes; here hung as spoils. Even the
  hero of the twelve labours is disarmed by Love.
- **"a sceptre holding suspended a superb robe"** — the royal insignia (Jupiter's, or a
  king's) reduced to a trophy-ornament: sovereignty itself hung on Love's spear.
- Short page (the woodcut of these trophies takes most of the leaf); the emblematic
  trophies with inscriptions follow on p. 328.
