## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The exact herb-planting of the first knot-parterre — every band,
rhomb, circle and rose named by its species and colour (marjoram, southernwood,
ground-pine, thyme, germander, amethyst and sweet violets, rue, primrose, cyclamen,
hollyhock, hyssop) — then the start of a second companion square built of nine sub-squares
that interlock into octagons. All secure; the plant-names are Pliny's and the herbalists'.

- **A planting-plan precise enough to build.** Colonna specifies not only the knot's
  geometry but which herb fills each cell and its colour: marjoram / southernwood /
  ground-pine in the three bands, mountain thyme in the rhomb, germander in its inner
  circle, amethyst violets in the central rose, and so on. This is a literal parterre
  planting-key — the world could render one bed exactly from this page.
- **the hollyhock finials and hyssop spheres** — three-cubit purple hollyhocks rise at the
  diagonal-square centres, box-spheres of hyssop elsewhere: vertical accents punctuating
  the flat knot, "an even law kept in all."
- **the second, nine-square parterre begins** — nine interlocking sub-squares generating
  octagons, the companion pattern that alternates with the first around the ring; its
  planting is given on p. 321.
- This is the densest horticultural page of the whole island; it establishes that Cythera's
  parterres are meant as real, plantable designs, not mere ornament.
