illustrious Nymphs, with the other sex, were hiding — at a discreet pleasure apart from the
others — and singing, with antique instruments, fugitive from sweet Cupid, and intent on the most
opaque shades and rustic doings. They were dressed shapelessly in silken habits, subtle and crisp
and pleated, of half-saffron tincture, and the greater crowd of sweet-scented and marigold and
galbanum-yellow, and some of colossine colour, with little sandals and nymphish footwear.

Now all the dwellers of these voluptuous places, feeling the triumphant advent of the arrow-shooting
lord, without delay suddenly, festively venerating, presented themselves to meet him — the newest
excepted. Then to their peculiar solace and continual delight they returned.

Lastly, beyond the recorded little grove, without any intercalation, and the other newest gradation
of seven steps following, with the observed norm, had upon the high step a much-admired close of a
little colonnade, like the first after the aforesaid river, of artifice and material. And there was
walled round a sectile, spacious, unencumbered and levelled area, with wondrous invention of
tessellated emblem-work, and with knotworks of little circles, triangles, small squares, and conoid
figures, and almond-shaped, and half-round, and rhomboid and scalene, most beautifully fashioned into
manifold designs coinciding, and with mirror-like illumination, and with egregious distinction of
various and choice colouring.

Finally, the middle third of the mile, from the river to the centre, was in such measurement
mosaic-wise distributed. Which semitertio, then, stood (as aforesaid) at 166 paces and a half.
Wherefore to the river are given 12 paces; to the meadow beyond the river, 10. All the steps
occupied, lengthwise, 8 paces and 2 feet, and in universal height as much. The little path, 6 feet.
The first garden, 33 paces; the second, 27; the third, 23. The grove, 25. The area encompassing the
theatre, 16. The theatre, from its middle to the centre, contained 16 paces. Of the island's
commensuration, enough.

## Notes

**Confidence: high** (for the closing measurement-table, which is arithmetic); medium for the grove
and nymphs above it. This page ends chapter XXI, and its final paragraph is **the canonical radial
breakdown of Cythera from the ring-river to the exact centre** — the companion to p. 311's whole-plan
diagram, and the single most useful page for checking the world's ring radii.

- **The island's radial measure, ring by ring (from the river inward).** The inner semitertio =
  166½ paces, distributed as: **river 12 · meadow-beyond 10 · all seven terrace-flights together 8
  paces 2 feet · path 6 feet · first garden 33 · second garden 27 · third garden 23 · grove 25 ·
  theatre-surround 16 · theatre-to-centre 16.** Together with p. 311 (shore-to-river = the outer
  semitertio) this closes the full one-mile diameter. The world's `_buildCytheraIsle` ring radii
  should be checked directly against these numbers.
- **the aromatic grove and the hidden nymphs** — the innermost planting before the theatre is the
  spice-grove, watered by "sacred founts," where nymphs hide singing "fugitive from sweet Cupid":
  the last, most secret ring before the centre, reserved and shaded. The "newest excepted" nymphs
  who do *not* come out to greet Cupid are the shy initiates — a small narrative touch.
- **the innermost colonnade and the emblematic pavement** — the seventh flight tops out at a
  colonnade (matching the first, by the river) enclosing a *sectile emblem-pavement* of circles,
  triangles, almonds, rhombs and scalene figures: the polychrome-marble floor of the theatre-
  precinct at the island's heart. The theatre of Venus itself opens in ch. XXII.
- **Chapter XXI complete (36/36).** The entire Cythera ekphrasis — shore, meadows, river, terraces,
  parterres, grove, and the measured plan twice over — is now translated.
