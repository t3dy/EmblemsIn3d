Now upon the flat aforesaid there lay a fateful, whitest and kindly bull, adorned with many flowers, and
with the pomp of an ox for sacrifice. Upon him sat a royal maiden with her wide shoulders, with her
long and bare arms, as if frightened, holding and embracing his hanging dewlaps. Clothed most
exquisitely in a fine cloth of green silk and gold, of a marvellous weaving, in nymphlike dress, with
the ends of a veil tucked up at her ankles, filling like a sail, not without a plenty of various jewels
for adornment, with a crown of gold pressing down a most elegant and golden head of hair, neat and
shining.

Such a triumph was drawn by six wanton centaurs, sons of the falling seed of bold Ixion. With flat
little chains of gold exquisitely looped at their strong and horse-like flanks, the rings knotting one
with another close together, and held in golden clasps and joinings, and then running in the fitted
rings, all six drawing evenly. Nor did Erichthonius find a like manner in the joining of fierce horses
to his flying four-horse cars.

Each was ridden by a notable Nymph, sitting with their shoulders turned one to another, three with
their notable faces turned to the right, and three to the left, with musical instruments sharing
together in a heavenly harmony; with most abundant and yellow hair spread down over their white necks,
their heads adorned with garlands of all fruits; the two nearest the triumph clothed in dark blue silk,
of that bright and choice colouring of the little feathers in a peacock's neck.

The two in the middle in flashing crimson. And the first, going before, of satin cloth of a green
emerald colouring. Not without the nymphlike additions and adornments, singing with their rounded
little mouths, and playing with so great a sweetness of melody as to keep the soul unfed and ever
living.

The centaurs were crowned with mistletoe. In their hands, the one at the lower part, and embracing it
with the other, the two nearest the car carried vessels of antique shape, of topaz of Arabia, with its
flashing golden colour, welcome to Lucina, and at which the waves grow quiet. Slender at their bottoms,

scattering a fragrance beyond all esteeming. Those that followed blew golden trumpets, with a hanging
