## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The most concentrated jewel-catalogue in the costume passage: the
amethyst-satin cuirasses of the most-splendid nymphs, their pearl-covered antique-foliage
backs, the green and crimson gold-wefted skirts beneath, and the merlon-edged embroidered
bands studded with rhomboid gems, fluttering gold darts, and pendent tablets. All secure; a
page of pure begemmed detail.

- **The cuirass-bodice.** The grandest nymphs wear a fitted silk cuirass over the flanks,
  padded with antique foliage and sewn with pearls, its edges bound with embroidered gold
  bands hung with pearls and little pear-drops. Beneath fall two gold-wefted skirts (green over
  crimson). This is a buildable costume for the world's most important triumph-figures.
- **the merlon-edged band** — the embroidered borders are serrated like little battlements
  ("merlons"), each merlon hung with a stone, gold darts fluttering between: a jeweller's
  crenellation. Colonna's ornament is architectural even at the scale of a hem.
- The catalogue continues on p. 336 (sleeves and arm-ornaments) before the text finally turns,
  on p. 337, from description to Poliphilo's reflection on what all this beauty *does*.
