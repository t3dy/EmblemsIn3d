Whence, with mosaic distance, and with long and solemn progress, was accompanied the triumphant boy;

Finally, with this triumphal and mysterious discourse, and with the quiver-bearing pomps and amorous
enticements, among fragrant flowers successively proceeding — the standard-bearing of the notable
victory preceding, with the fore-singings of the most-joyous nymphal youth, with flowing gait and
fore-dances, and with coruscating torches pre-shining, and with clear light pre-glittering, they went
before — and among fragrant pot-herbs, sweet-smelling and living trees, odoriferous fruits, odorous
air and clement and most-liquid sky. The road hedged, planted with every kind of shrubbery, and in
every part covered with little herbs, and with green grass. Not lacking, step by step, the roses, and
the plenty of flowers — every thing fragrant, and a place happy, blessed, delightful, and most-
pleasant. And with so great and divine a following of the miscellaneous throng of Nymphs, with
peculiar pomp, with religious progress — nay, triumphal — the one accompanied the other, with stature
and determined distance, advancing under the rose-pergola of every manner, with new and spring fronds
and leaves. Which, with sweet noises, whistled softly.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The procession moves, under a **roofed rose-pergola**, over a marble pavement
strewn ankle-deep with flowers, to the amphitheatre; Poliphilo and Polia bound in rose-withes among the
blandishing nymphs; the whole train bearing thyrses and olive/laurel/myrtle branches with tame birds
perched singing. All secure; a page of pure processional atmosphere.

- **The roofed rose-way.** The triumph proceeds down a *rose-pergola* over a tessellated marble pavement
  strewn with rose-petals, orange-flower, violets, jasmine, lily and myrtle — "the ground fertilely
  overwhelmed" with flowers. This is the approach-avenue to the theatre of Venus, and a vivid, buildable
  scene: a flower-carpeted marble road under an arched rose-tunnel.
- **the lovers still bound, still soothed** — Colonna keeps returning to Poliphilo and Polia's willing
  captivity: bound in rose-withes, the nymphs making "amorous blandishments… with genuine hilarity." The
  captivity is gentle, festive, consensual — Love's triumph is not cruelty but glad surrender.
- **thyrses and tame birds** — the Bacchic thyrsus (fennel-wand) joins the olive, laurel and myrtle
  branches; the birds perch "familiarly," fearless, as on the island generally (p. 312). The whole natural
  order participates in the triumph.
- The gate of the amphitheatre is reached on p. 346.
