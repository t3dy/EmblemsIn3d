# Page 42 — Chapter IV

Leaving this most excellent and mysterious and unimaginable making, I returned again to look upon the
prodigious Horse. Which had a bony head, and lean, and proportionately small; it appeared an excellent
figuring of standing unquiet and impatient of delay, and one saw almost the trembling of its flesh,
and it was more alive than feigned. With a Greek word hollowed in its brow: GENEA.

Then many other great pieces and fragments of every lineament, among the greatest broken heaps of
ruin. And of all of them, devouring and flying time had kindly spared these four stupendous things
only, untouched: the Gate, the Horse, the colossus, and the Elephant. O holy fathers, ancient
craftsmen, what savagery invaded so great a virtue of yours, that you carried with you into the
sepulchre the disinheriting of us from such riches?

Having come, then, to this most ancient gate, of work most notable, and marvellously constructed with
exquisite rule and art, and with illustrious ornaments of carving and of various lineament — for all
which things, being studious and inflamed with the desire to understand the fertile understanding and
the keen searching-out of that perspicacious Architect, both his dimension and his lineament and his
practice — I set about examining it subtly, and did thus.

I diligently measured a square set beneath the columns, two to a side. From which measuring I easily
grasped the whole symmetry of the aforesaid gate. Which, explaining, I shall run over briefly. A
four-sided figure A.B.C.D., divided by three straight lines and three cross lines equally distant,
will be sixteen squares. Then, adding to the figure as much as its half, and dividing the addition by
those same partitions, there are found twenty-four squares. This figure of little cords offers itself
ready to hand, and how useful and fitting it is for reducing to a smaller scale, in cutting, or in
fine work and in painting.

Drawing again in the first figure A.B.C.D. two diagonals; and marking in it besides two lines, a
straight and a cross one, mutually intersecting, four squares were made. Likewise in that empty space
above the equal sides, four middle points being made, and lines drawn from one to the other, the
Rhombus is constituted.

The aforesaid figures being described in such a way, I considered fitly: what reason have the blind
moderns, who esteem themselves in the art of building, not knowing what the thing is? So enormously
still they order their false little buildings, sacred as well as profane, public and private,
dishonouring the parts that proceed from the middle, neglectful of that which nature teaches by
showing. It being a golden and heavenly saying and lesson that in that consists virtue — and the poet
sings of blessedness — which, being deserted and neglected, must needs come out disordered, and
everything with it.
