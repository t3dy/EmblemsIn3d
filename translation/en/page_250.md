Without interval, other dolorous souls attempted this same, successively, with vain and impotent wish, and by no
means could they attain the desired effect. Those most-wretched souls, then, that hastened without quiet to flee,
by furious horror and rabies agitated — the incendious flames — could not, by relieving, come and refresh themselves
in the ice. And those likewise, that gave assiduous heed to fleeing the hardest cold, and entering into the ardent
flames, frustrated of the greatest desire, could not. And this, to those, was inescapable, and a pain unceasing —
ever more desirous, losing every hope. Which so much the more ardent avidity increased, as, upon the bridge, the one
and the other felt — those (of the fire) the reflex of its cold, and those (frozen) the heat — the one with the
other meeting at its term (the bridge's midpoint). And this, in the affect, was the greatest quickening of pain and
of torment. For which thing, with so obstinate an art of colouring, and of simulated gestures and expressed
efforts, I saw such a painting skilfully-made, and exquisitely perfect, as ever could be made and shown. And the
title, indicating, was inscribed: that, in the burning flames, were condemned the souls that, for too much fire of
love, killed their very selves; and, in the horrid ice, were immersed those who, rigid and chilly to Love and
refusing, had obstinately shown themselves.

Finally, with such disposition I beheld this odious, frightful and to-be-avoided Barathrum (pit), that where the
lakes met — that is, the frigorific with the most-ardent — by the contrariety they must make, with eternal
controversy, a terrible thundering; because then, meeting, both immersed themselves in an abrupt precipice, effused
into a dark, vast and most-deep passage and immense abyss. Where the profundity was so artfully feigned by the
artificer, that, by the colouring, those demonstrations feigned (themselves) to be true, and (one seemed) to see a
most-absorbing vortex, with wondrous emulation of the colourings, and of lineal symmetry of prospect, and of the
figures the elegance, and copious invention, and artful designation, and with incredible sharpness — such that
Parrhasius of Ephesus, notable painter, could never first glory himself of a like device.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The **meaning of the Hell-mosaic**, made explicit by its inscription: **the fire punishes
those who killed themselves for too much love; the ice punishes those who were cold and refused Love** — and each
is tormented by longing for the other's element, kept from it by "divine justice," feeling at the bridge's midpoint
the very relief they cannot reach. The two lakes meet in a thundering abyss, painted with a trompe-l'oeil depth
surpassing Parrhasius. All secure; the thematic key of the whole Polyandrion.

- **The two sins against Love — the Polyandrion's judgement.** The fresco names the tomb's whole moral: **excess**
  (self-slaughter for love, the fire) and **frigidity** (cold refusal of love, the ice) are the two damnations,
  each eternally denied its opposite's relief. This is the theology the Polyandrion enforces over its dead, and the
  exact counter-image to the Temple of Venus's sanctioned, measured love. For the world/novel, this is the meaning
  to inscribe over the polyandrion: love neither too hot nor too cold — the mean the whole book (and Polia's
  "perseverance," p. 289) commends.
- **the contrapasso.** The torment is *desire itself*, frustrated: the burning long for ice, the frozen for fire,
  and feel at the bridge the relief forever withheld. Colonna makes Hell a machine of unquenchable longing — an
  image of unfulfilled love raised to eternal punishment. Powerful material for the world's polyandrion ceiling.
- **surpassing Parrhasius.** The great Greek illusionist (of the painted curtain) is exceeded by this fresco's
  feigned abyssal depth: the surpassing-topos, on painting (cf. Apelles/Aristides, p. 287). The Polyandrion's art
  makes its doctrine unbearably vivid.
- **the fire-and-ice abyss** — where the lakes meet they thunder and pour into "an immense abyss" of feigned depth:
  a buildable, dramatic centre for the mosaic. The world could render the whole tribune-vault as this fire/ice Hell.
