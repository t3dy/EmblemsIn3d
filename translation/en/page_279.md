# Page 279 — Chapter XIX

And what imprisoned and extinguished concupiscence — glacial and depraved appetite — that would not here have vigorously
unbroken the tenacious cloisters and biting snares, and most-aptly restored itself to the comely, most-beautiful and
amorous spectacles, and would not have turned itself into flame-bearing Etna? And what Diana would have spurned so benign
fires — (fires) to contaminate the chaste Hippolytus, and make lascivious the most-modest Orithyia? But what must those
have felt, who wholly were apt, near, and uniformly disposed (to love)? I found myself like the little fish born in
boiling water — thence extracted, and in other waters set to boil, it never cooks (again). Beyond, then, stupidly I
beheld that divine little spirit, and, in his over-gilt feathers, some restless little plumules, tender and delicate,
such as are on the immature wings of the young hawk not yet flying from the nest, and, at the dewy winds, tremulous
resulting. O how gracious, and how most-pleasant to the senses, the little golden-crimson feathers presented themselves!
Which golden colour, in some feathers, refulged, and (some) of phoenician colouring; some of glaucous colour, and of
emerald tincture, and of mallow colouring, and of cyan, and (some), more than the icteric little bird, yellow — in a
golden colour most-admiredly coming together, and, with harmonic dispensation, most-decorously, through the divine
wings.

For which thing, all the jewels of fecund nature, distributed there, participatingly, to coruscate, I easily judged —
because they irradiated like mobile and most-tenuous foils of most-pure gold, suspended in the breeze, and, in the clear
sun, wind-blowing, comelily painting the waters of the most-placid colours; then torn (reflected) by the unstable and
crisping little waves, in a great emulating gyre. I beheld also the incomparable beauty of the divine composite of Polia,
from hour to hour ever more delicious and shapely presenting herself. Then I beheld also the most-purged and serene air,
the moderate and placid weather, and the saline cerulean waters — like perspicuous crystal, most-limpid, seen even to the
most-open shallows — and, here and there, many wooded rocks; and, ornated of vernal verdure, the scattered (Sporad)
little isles, thick of leafy greening shrubs, and most-pleasantly shady; and many vague places, from the senses, afar,
lost themselves; and in the flattened (waters), like spots, they appeared. Likewise the frondiferous trees over-shadowed
the littoral little banks, and the greenish reflex, then, in the most-nitid and mirror-like waters, as the proper (thing),
was discerned.

Proceeding, then, our solacing and triumphant navigation — where presided the empire and divine monarchy of potent Love;
where resided that lord who, in extreme sweetness, renders himself austere, and, in austerity, feigns himself so sweetly
sweet, and, in sweetness, so bitter, and, in bitterness, presents himself so pleasant. O happy, therefore, whoever,
navigating, should feel his feathered wings prosperous and favourable! Finding myself, then, thus, between two so pleasing
lords — the one inflamed me, the other consumed me — lo, that the marine gods: **Nereus** with the most-pleasing **Chloris**,

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** Poliphilo's reflection on Love's irresistibility (no chastity — Diana's, Hippolytus's, Orithyia's
— could withstand it; he is "the fish born in boiling water"), a rapt study of Cupid's **rainbow wings** (gold, phoenician,
glaucous, emerald, mallow, cyan, yellow, painting the sea), the beauty of Polia and of the crystalline, isle-scattered sea;
then the arrival of the **marine gods** (Nereus, Chloris) to honour Cupid. All secure; the sea-idyll before the homage.

- **Love's monarchy over the sea.** The crossing is "the divine monarchy of potent Love" — the god "who in extreme sweetness
  renders himself austere… in bitterness so pleasant": Colonna's chiastic definition of Love's paradox (sweet/bitter,
  austere/pleasant), the theme of the whole voyage. A quotable line for the world/novel.
- **Cupid's wings paint the sea.** The god's feathers, "like foils of pure gold in the sun," cast shifting colour on the
  water, "torn by the crisping waves" — a luminous optical image (Colonna's caustics again, cf. the crystal lamp, p. 208).
  A rendering cue for the world's crossing: rainbow light off Cupid's wings scattering on the sea.
- **the Sporades-idyll** — crystal water to the shallows, green shrubby islets, shaded banks mirrored in the sea: the
  perfect calm passage to Cythera. Buildable as the world's approach seascape.
- **"between two lords, one inflamed, the other consumed me"** — Cupid and (implicitly) Poliphilo's own heart, or Cupid and
  Venus: he is caught between the two powers of love. The marine procession honouring Cupid (p. 280) confirms Love's reign
  over even the sea-gods.
