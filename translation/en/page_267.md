## Notes

**Confidence: high** (the two inscriptions are secure). A woodcut leaf — the illustration of Artemisia's tomb — bearing
only its two Greek labels: the **Mirror of Love** and **the ashes of Queen Artemisia**. No prose to render; the two
inscriptions (already given on pp. 265–266) are repeated here as the image's captions. Noted so the page-count stays
honest.

- **The tomb pictured.** This is the woodcut of the monument whose architecture and sculpture pp. 264–266 describe —
  the porphyry niche, the ashes-drinking queen, Mausolus's borne portrait, the Mirror of Love, the naval trophy. The
  world's polyandrion could take its principal-tomb design directly from this image and its text.
- The two Greek inscriptions are the tomb's identity: *Artemisia's ashes*, seen in *the mirror of Love*. Together they
  make the monument an emblem of love faithful past death — the Polyandrion's answer, in its grandest tomb, to the two
  damnations of the Hell-fresco (p. 250).
