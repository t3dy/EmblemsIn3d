Whose abundant and yellow hair is to me a knotted snare stretched and set about my hedged heart; her
wide and smooth brow, whitening like lilies, twists me like a rod bound in a garland; the arrow-shooting
glances of her life hang me up, sweet awakenings to afflict me; her rosy cheeks invite me sweetly to
sharpen my pain; her cinnamon mouth makes me long for a sweet torment.

Then her delicious breast, white as winter snow on the northern mountains (which, being in itself an
extreme sweetness), is to me a bitter and hurtful scourge. And her more than human semblances, and her
comely person, drawing my appetite to an imagined delight, cruelly waste me.

And against all these insulting martyrdoms, and against this so perilous a contest, and against impious
and treacherous Cupid, with all these insulting parts of that smooth little body, my provoked heart
vigorously throwing itself in as a strenuous wrestler — nevertheless it could never resist, but, like
Milo beside her, I find myself torn apart, every strength put to sleep, nor can I turn aside from
there, as if I had entered incautiously into the Babylonian marsh.

So the only worthy and fulfilling present remedy and fitting medicine that would offer itself, however
much I felt myself accepted, with all these harshest and intolerable pains of mine, would be that this
goddess should be Polia — she who has secretly set me on fire and burns me without delay, and burns me
all over with the flames of hard Cupid, neither more nor less than Minerva kindled the figure of
Prometheus, the burning fire snatched with a light reed from the running wheels of shining Phoebus.

O Tityus, I should ill persuade myself that my torment were less than yours, while the sword-like
vultures split your warm breast, and thence, without staying, your living heart, torn out and smoking,
and seizing it with their taloned feet, and cruelly tearing it limb from limb with their hooked beaks,
they devour it; and in a little while afterwards, it being restored, they return swiftly to that same
butchery. And the sorrowful carnage begins again from the top.

Likewise, my inflamed breast being opened, two most thieving eyes without pity harshly scatter and tear
my loving heart, and, tearing it, bitingly devour it. Then it is not long in time before her festive and
most joyful aspect heals it again, as if it felt no hurt; and after a little instant, beginning again,
they return to its stricken wounds.
