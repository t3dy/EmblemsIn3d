## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The Hell-mosaic in full: an iron bridge between a **burning lake** (sparks, boiling fire,
sulphur-mountain, steam-vents) and a **frozen lake** (glacial, clay-muddy); **Taenarus's bronze door** guarded by
three-headed **Cerberus**; and the three Furies — **Tisiphone**, **Megaera**, **Alecto** — driving the souls back
and forth between fire and ice so neither the burning may reach the cold nor the frozen the flames. All secure; a
vivid, Dante-adjacent infernal ekphrasis.

- **The contrapasso of lovers.** The mosaic's torment (spelled out on p. 250) is that the fire-damned *long for
  the ice* and the ice-damned *long for the fire*, and the Furies keep each from the relief of its opposite — an
  eternal frustration. It is the punishment of love's two failures: those who burned themselves with love, and
  those who were cold to it. The Polyandrion's dead are judged by this fresco overhead. A powerful, thematically-
  exact ceiling for the world's polyandrion.
- **the classical machinery.** Taenarus (the Peloponnesian cave-mouth of Hades), Cerberus, the three Furies
  (daughters of Acheron and Night), the burning and freezing lakes: Colonna assembles the full ancient underworld,
  painted "feigned shrewdly." The world can render it as a fire-and-ice mosaic with the bridge, the dog, and the
  serpent-haired Furies.
- **the painting as trompe-l'oeil** — Colonna stresses the artifice ("feigned shrewdly," sparks "like atoms in
  the sunbeams"): the fresco's realism is itself the marvel, surpassing Parrhasius (p. 250). The Polyandrion's art
  makes Hell visible.
- The torment's logic and the surpassing of Parrhasius complete on p. 250.
