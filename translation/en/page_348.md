next to the altar, two little boys, one to a side, with a little vase apiece. Likewise two lascivious
Satyrs, with a token of vociferating, with a fist tightly raised toward the Nymphs, with snaky
entanglement. Which (Nymphs), with the free arm, gripped those of the satyrs — who, with the hand of
the other arm, stopped the orifice of a leaky vase, forbidding the touch — and, inclined, with the
other held the little pipe, intent and unmoving at its office. Some other columns of this same form,
with the two-thirds of twisted fluting, and the lowest one altared, as is said, changed, doubled in
lineament. Such had, among swelling ropes of fronds and fruits incurving, playful little boys. Some,
manifold trophies, sculptures egregiously made; many had carved heaps of spoils. Others, occupied
with set figures of applauding goddesses, and little boys, and copies of victories, and titles, and
other most-congruous ornaments.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The amphitheatre's fabric: **diaphanous alabaster walls with gilt-bronze
entablature**, an outer face of **two superposed orders of arches** between striated half-columns, each
half-column footed by a ram-skull-and-festoon altar whose festoon frames a tiny carved relief (a
satyric sacrifice, boys and trophies). All secure; the double-arcade is a Roman amphitheatre-elevation
(the Colosseum's) rendered in precious materials.

- **The theatre's elevation, buildable.** Two stacked orders of arcades in alabaster, framed by fluted
  half-columns, entablature of gilt bronze — Colonna describes the standard Roman amphitheatre exterior
  (superposed arcaded orders) but in translucent alabaster and gold. This is the direct architectural
  source for the world's central theatre; the world can raise a two-tier alabaster arcade with a gilt
  cornice.
- **the ram-skull altars with micro-reliefs.** Each half-column stands on a little altar carved with
  bucrania and festoons (as on the parterres, p. 319), and *within* the festoon a miniature scene: a
  satyric sacrifice (nymphs piping at a boiling tripod, satyrs grasping a leaky vase). The theatre is
  encrusted with tiny narrative reliefs — a texture the world could suggest with a carved-panel material.
- **"nor did M. Scaurus"** — M. Aemilius Scaurus built the most extravagant temporary theatre of the
  Roman Republic (58 BC, 3,000 bronze statues); the amphitheatre of Venus surpasses even it. The
  surpassing-topos, applied to theatre-building.
- **the leaky vase the satyr stops** — a recurring erotic-emblem detail (the *futile* vase that cannot
  hold water); here the satyrs stop it while the nymphs tend the fire. The reliefs carry the same
  fertility-and-restraint theme as the whole triumph.
