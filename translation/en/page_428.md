# Page 428 — Chapter XXX

*(Chapter argument:)*

> POLIA HAVING ACCUSED HERSELF, BEFORE THE TEMPLE-PRIESTESS, OF HER TRANSACTED IMPIETY — AND (SHOWN) THAT, AT
> PRESENT, ALL WAS SUFFUSED OF ARDENT LOVE — DEMONSTRATING POLIPHILO STANDING BY, THE RELIGIOUS MATRON CALLED HIM TO
> HER. WHO, SUPPLICATING (FOR) THE STABILITY OF BOTH IN ONE RATIFIED PURPOSE — POLIA, FROM IMPATIENT LOVE STRENGTHENED
> IN HER, INTERRUPTED THE RESPONSE.

Appearing, sedulous, without delay, to the venerable Priestess, Poliphilo, expeditely, and with devout inclination,
presented himself there being; and I, with affectionate and thundering sighs (which, in the vaulted Temple sounding —
lo — to our hidden ears the emulous (echo) sent them back), and with the eyes only on him fixed — then, nude and
divested of every cold hardness, but most-mild and meek and outstanding, I laid open to him the gaping opening of my
enkindled heart; and, of his enticing and festive and intent eyes, I most-often imprecated (invoked) a fated domicile
and delicious lodging. And I, as desirous, courteously made it (my heart) only of that worthy and meritorious Lord — an
adjunct, and, together, to possess all my life, and my very self, to what he arbitrarily should will. Who, to me, then,
so much the more grateful presented himself, as, more, before, hateful and displeasing I had held him — a more gracious
and most-efficacious remedy to my ardent love opportunely offering himself, and much more salutary (than) — to the
navigators, upon the estuous sea with the rainy sky — appear the lucid stars of Castor and his brother Gemini, on the
right part of the Charioteer above Orion collocated, and even the desired and secure ports.

Whence, in the last degree of love wounded, I beheld him immovably to take (in), with a gracious intuition; and this was
a sweet congeries and heap of invasive fire in my breast — and my soul, thereby, from every other solicitude excluded:
only him it pleased (me); only him, most-pleasing, it desired; only him, a solace, offered itself; and, to my
insatiable and desirous regards, a delectable object — from the vacation of which impatient, and, of avidity,
stimulated, and, by an immodest appetite impelled, and, by such amorous gratifications, taken and possessed, that
almost externated (beside myself), and in ecstasy, immovable, I beheld him — whence, already beyond measure unbridled
being my eyes — and, because I felt and experienced what thing was the new love, I, miserating, reasonably, to those
(his eyes) pardoned their scrutatory importunity. But Poliphilo, who —

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The opening of **chapter XXX**: its argument (Poliphilo, called forward, supplicates for the
lovers' union; Polia interrupts his response); and Polia — now "nude of every cold hardness," in "the last degree of love"
— gazing on Poliphilo "in ecstasy," her heart wholly his, welcoming the very gaze she once loathed. All secure; the
reunion at the priestess's tribunal.

- **Polia's total reversal, seen in her gaze.** She who refused Poliphilo one word (p. 396) now "lays open the gaping
  opening of her enkindled heart," invokes his eyes as "a fated domicile," and gives him "all my life and my very self."
  The cold votary is now the ecstatic lover — "the last degree of love." Her welcome of his scrutinising gaze (which she
  once "held hateful") is the exact measure of the change.
- **the eyes reversed.** The whole drama has run on eyes — his loving, hers refusing (chs. XXVI); now both gaze insatiably,
  "in ecstasy." The mutual wound is complete. Polia's narration of her own ecstasy is the emotional peak of her
  conversion, told from within.
- **Castor and Pollux, the safe harbour.** Poliphilo is to her love "more salutary than Gemini's stars to storm-tossed
  sailors" — the guiding lights and the "desired and secure port." She casts him as her salvation, exactly as he cast her
  as his (the sun, the elm, pp. 391): the imagery finally symmetrical, both lovers each other's harbour.
- Poliphilo's plea to the priestess (that the two be made one) follows on pp. 429–430.
