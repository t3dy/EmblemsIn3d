Then I said: "What do those two very unsuited things mean that he holds in his divine hands?" Thelemia,
witty, answered: "By his infinite goodness the immortal Jupiter makes a show to earth-born men that
they may have at their wish that of the two hands which pleases them, freely to choose."

And without delay I added: "Since our pleasant talking has fallen here, most welcome companions — my
kindled desire to understand not being yet wholly fulfilled, and since my boldness did not displease
you — tell me this, I pray you. Before my horrible fright I saw, of a daring greatness and art, a stone
monster, which is an elephant. Entering, then, into its empty belly, I found two tombs, with writings
of an ambiguous meaning, about finding treasure: that I should scorn the body and carry away the head."

Logistica, without any other thinking, expounding it, answered cheerfully: "Poliphilo, I know fully
what you ask; I would however have you know that not without a great admiration of human wit, and with
an ardent study and incredible diligence, was that huge engine made, with a perplexity of the intellect
to understand its divine conceit. Take heed that above its front there hangs the ornament with that
double-headed writing, which in the mother and common tongue says: *labour and industry*. For in the
world, whoever living would have treasure, let him leave rotting idleness, signified by the great body,
and take the adorned head, which is that writing — and you shall have treasure by toiling with
industry."

No sooner were her soothing and effectual words ended than, being perfectly taught the whole, I gave
thanks for her affable kindness; yet, being still most eager to search out all that I had before
imperfectly grasped, and having made myself familiar with them, I made a third request: "Most wise
Nymph, in my coming out of the underground caverns I found an antique and elegant bridge. Which, on
both its parapets, in porphyry stone on the one side, and on the other of ophite, I saw certain
hieroglyphs carved. And of both I was the interpreter; but I remained ignorant only of the branches,
not knowing them, which were bound to the horns, and then why in porphyry stone, and not in the like of
the other side."
