Then, whoever accurately considered such an expression could easily conjecture (it) to be thus; because the maker,
most-rich of shrewdness, and of outstanding cogitation, had here exquisitely feigned the souls to a corporeal
expression — which shades cannot appear save (as) concreted air, and condensed in that effigy, by which the effect
is comprehended. And therefore many souls, stopping their ears; others (covering their eyes with the little palms)
dared not look into the terrific and gulfy abyss, full of frightful, terrible and various monsters; others, pale,
to express the torpid cold, straining their arms to the breast; some, to demonstrate the ardour, blew out smoky
breath; others, to indicate the mourning sadness and dolorous pain, the fingers of the hands joined comb-wise, wept.
Whence, upon the limited bridge, in the diagonal compacture, meeting in droves, the one and the other of the first
butted (against each other), and could not proceed, by the treading-down of those following. Then the bridge, by

others unceasingly attempted, successively, that (bridge) —

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The souls of the Hell-mosaic, rendered as "concreted air" (shades made visible), each in a
gesture of its torment — stopping ears, hiding eyes, arms clenched against the cold, blowing smoke of the heat,
weeping — jammed on the bridge that eternally separates and rejects them. All secure; the close reading of the
fresco's figures.

- **shades as "concreted air."** Colonna's aesthetic note: the painter can only show incorporeal souls by
  condensing them into visible bodies ("concreted air"), a knowing account of how painting represents the immaterial.
  His interest is always double — the horror *and* the artifice of showing it.
- **the gestures of torment** — ears stopped, eyes hidden, arms hugged against cold, smoke breathed, hands wrung:
  a repertoire of expressive poses. For the world's polyandrion mosaic, these give the figures their attitudes.
- The first **tomb-epitaphs** begin on p. 252 — the graves of individual love-dead, which fill the rest of the
  chapter.
