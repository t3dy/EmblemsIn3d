all the work from the arch upward being of six feet, the aforesaid box excepted. No
indication showed itself in all this excellent topiary of woodwork, except the
straight stocks; but all was covered with most propped foliage and most evenly
shorn, with diligence and art clipped.

Between one and the other vase, in the chest, was seen a box without a stock, in
onion-form [?cepacea], a pace wide, two feet and a half tall. In the middle of which was led a
pear-tree, raised four feet, with the pointed top. Above the tapering it held a flat
circular figure, its diameter four feet. In the middle of this lens-like round
sprang forth a little stock holding an egg-shaped form, tall as the pear beneath.

## Notes

**Confidence: low.** Like p. 300, this is a single unbroken account of one topiary
structure — the box-arch springing from the two towers held by the giant — in
Colonna's most compressed manner, with the plant-parts named anatomically (trunks,
pommels, shells, a lily-basket) so that the mechanics are hard to hold. The overall
form is recoverable but individual clauses are provisional. **A reviewer should
work from the woodcut.** I have rendered literally rather than smoothing, and marked
the confidence accordingly, though there is no single corrupt word to bracket — the
difficulty is the syntax, not the lexicon.

- **The box-arch topiary continued.** The two towers the giant holds each sprout a
  ball, from which trained box-trunks arch together into a masonry-like arch,
  carrying further balls, a shell-basin, a lily-basket, and a finial of eight
  diminishing balls — all in living clipped box, "no woodwork showing except the
  straight stocks." It is the most elaborate single topiary in the book, and the
  reason the world keeps the *prati* topiaries deliberately abstract rather than
  attempting this.
- **the onion-box with a pear-tree** — between the two vases, a stockless box
  clipped to an onion, from which a pear rises carrying a flat disc and an egg
  finial. Another trained-fruit topiary, more legible than the arch.
- Confidence is marked **low** for the whole page on the strength of the syntax;
  the reader of the parallel text should weigh it against the Italian and the
  facsimile rather than take the English as settled.
