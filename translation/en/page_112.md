In the branches of which little tree there were artfully set certain open flowerets, shaped into a
five-petalled rose. Some of shining sapphire, and some of bright jacinth, or beryl. In five of these
flowers there were fitted within five little apples, as big as a sorb-apple and more, and of that same
painting, upon a pointed golden spike that stood out from the centre of the flower, so that they
feigned to have been produced there.

Which young girl, reverent and kneeling to the ground with her right knee, keeping the other raised,
held this notable coral fitly upon it. Which, besides the little branches taken up by the precious
flowers, had at the tips of the others monstrous pearls curiously fixed.

Another of these had the cup, with a precious liquor, such as proud Cleopatra did not give to the Roman
captain. The three others carried out the service lying upon them, as customary above. Plucking off,
then, one after another with the little golden fork those small fruits (unknown to me, and never seen),
and offering them to us, we tasted them most savourily. But the unthinkable sweetness of taste that I
felt was no otherwise than as if in the gracious matter, set apart, there were the entering in of the
longed-for form. And here were given back the round golden apples recited above.
