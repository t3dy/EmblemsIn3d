reflexed with incredible symphony under the obtuse cupola, around the incensed Altar, thus rhythmically
saying:

> "O holy fire of odour: thaw the ice of every heart; placate Venus with love, and grant us her ardour."

In this mysterious manner singing, and tibia-sounding, with most-elegant chorus, orbitally they gyred, while
the sacrifice burned. And, extinguishing thus, the little flame smoked. I think that those odours were to
suffocate the stench of the roasted flesh, beyond the (main) purpose. Then, no sooner was it extinguished,
than of a sudden, to the pavement, all silently (the Priestess excepted) they prostrated themselves. For
which thing it was not long time, that I openly, out of the holy smoke, saw a most-beautiful little spirit,
oracular, and of form other than human, as beautiful as, with shrewd discourse and investigation, one could
imagine. And at the divine shoulder-blades a pair of arched little wings it had, with an unseen and unwonted
light — which, not without some lesion of my eyes regarding it, most-avidly, the lost heart truly felt. With
so vehement an impetus, more than the thunderbolt created of water —

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The core of the rite: Polia kindles the incense and myrtle, **sacrifices the two
turtledoves** in the fire (reserving their blood in the *praefericulum*), and the priestess and virgins
**sing and dance a hymn to Venus** around the altar to Lydian pipes; then, as the smoke rises, a **beautiful
winged spirit** (a little Cupid/genius) appears out of the holy smoke. All secure; the hymn and the apparition
are the chapter's centre.

- **The hymn to Venus.** A short, singable quatrain — *"O holy fire of odour: thaw the ice of every heart;
  placate Venus with love, and grant us her ardour."* The prayer of the whole temple in four lines: the fire
  of scent melting the cold heart (the same image as the well-torch, p. 216 — quench the cold to kindle love).
  Prime liturgy to surface in the world's temple-rite and the visual-novel; it rhymes in the original
  (*odore / core / amore / ardore*).
- **the dove-sacrifice.** The turtledoves (Venus's birds, p. 219) are the offering, burned with myrtle
  (Venus's tree) amid the aromatics; their blood is kept in the ritual ewer. The rite is precisely Venerean and
  precisely antique.
- **the spirit from the smoke.** A winged genius of "unwonted light" rises from the sacrifice — the epiphany
  the chapter's argument (p. 224) promised. Its attributes (myrtle-crown, fire-arrow, diamond crown) and its
  dissolution follow on p. 227. A luminous, animatable apparition for the world's rite.
- **the wry practical aside** — Poliphilo suspects the heavy incense is partly to mask the "stench of roasted
  flesh": Colonna's realism intrudes even here (cf. the latrine-note, p. 202). The sacred and the practical
  side by side.
