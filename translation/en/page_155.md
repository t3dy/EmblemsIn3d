By all which most delightful things, chained in the difficult and inextricable knots of a vehement
love, more unloosable than the Herculean knot, and more than that which Alexander the Great undid with
the sword; and most amorously netted in the most tangled nets; and my enslaved heart in most ardent and
troublesome thoughts and most fervent desires, mailed, gripping me wherever I turned, feeling more
prickings and more little nails in that loving heart than faithful Regulus in Africa, carried within
the nailed barrel.

So that with nothing else did I cool my grieving spirits from the amorous burning, and from the most
exquisite torments sharpened, which burned in my thundering breast, save by swallowing my busy sobs,
gaping like a hunted fawn.

Being then plunged headlong into frequent anxieties, and seeing myself wholly carried away to a most
violent love of this woman, I said from myself to myself:

O Poliphilo, how can you leave the love once inseparably kindled in your honeyed Polia, for any other?
And here, wanting with all my strength to untie myself from this biting noose, which drew me more
sharply than the claws of the gripping crab, it was not a work that could be done. Whence nothing else
did I feel that more troublesomely ensnared my tormented soul in affection for this one than the true
likeness of every bodily thread of hers, the comely semblance, and the excellent gestures of my sweetest
Polia.

But above all this there offered itself to me a most cruel torment: how could I ever give up my most
acceptable Polia? And at once, the warm tears called up in my moist eyes, it seemed to me despicable
and very hard to renew my wasted heart, and to bring in a new and unknown lord, and impiously to
banish its ancient one.

Then, comforting myself, I said: perhaps this is she, according to the divine oracle and the high and
true promise of Queen Eleuterylida, but she does not reveal it; for, if I do not err, this one
infallibly seems so to me. And this amorous and running thought being made, and this persuading
presupposition, and being gone out of every other desire, I only pondered, with my heart and with my
