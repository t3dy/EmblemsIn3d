In the last place, immediately, in front, next to the drawing serpents, two aegipans and wanton
satyrs, with goatish beards, with goat-feet and cloven, proceeded. Which lewd ones, joyous, with
little wreaths of flowering *satyrion*, and of flowers of *helenium* and of *cynosorchis* (orchid),
pressing down the unkempt hair over the wrinkled forehead, crowned. Each bore a monster, rudely cut
in wood, and gilt, effigied human, clothed: from the three-heads to the diaphragm only; the rest, in
a square, tapering to the lower part, passed off into a basal gullet-moulding, with a little slab,
with an antique foliage at the arm-site, with an apple at the breast. And in the middle of the
square, in the broadest part, appeared the ithyphallic sign.

Before whom, immediately, preceded a polished Nymph, on whose snowy face the porphyry cheeks
reddened, with the ivy-crowned forehead of corymbs, clad in a segmented pall, quivered here and
there by the soft zephyr-breaths, the lappets of the reflexed bosom; she bore a golden little vase,
rounded in the manner of a nipple, from which, by the narrowest channel, sprinkling milk, she
libated.

Likewise, again, two Nymphs preceding, wreathed the one of leaves of the female Mercury-plant, and
the other of male *Hermopoane*, were bearers: the one, of a whole gilt little boy in the right hand,
and in the other another, mutilated of the head, little arms, and neck. The other, with special
honour and obstinate superstition, bore the simulacrum venerated by the Egyptians, of Serapis. Which
was a head of a lion; to the right jutted a fawning dog's head; and to the left, a head of a rapacious
wolf. Which effigy was all contained and surrounded in a coil of a dragon, emitting fore-sharpened
rays; which dragon, with the head, derived to the right part of the simulacrum, most-excellently
gilt.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The rear guard of the triumph: **satyrs and aegipans** bearing gilt ithyphallic
herm-idols; a nymph **libating milk** from a nipple-shaped vase; and two nymphs bearing gilt boy-idols
and the **tricephalic Serapis simulacrum** (lion, dog, wolf heads wrapped in a rayed dragon). All secure;
the Serapis image is drawn straight from Macrobius.

- **The Serapis-of-the-three-heads.** Colonna's Serapis — a lion flanked by dog and wolf heads, coiled by
  a rayed serpent — is the exact allegory Macrobius gives (*Saturnalia* I.20): the three heads are present,
  past and future, the beasts of Time; the serpent is eternity. Set at the tail of Love's triumph, it reads
  Love under the sign of Time — the same *memento* as PANTA BAIA BIOU (p. 340). A striking image the world's
  triumph could carry.
- **the satyrs and the ithyphallic herms** — the fertility-底 of the procession: goat-footed satyrs crowned
  in orchids (*satyrion*, *cynosorchis*, "dog's-testicle" — aphrodisiac plants) bearing priapic idols. The
  triumph of Love spans the whole range from Psyche's soul-love to raw generation.
- **the milk-libation** — a nymph pours milk from a breast-shaped vase: a nurture/fertility rite opening the
  rear file, balancing the incense and torches of the front.
- This completes the marshalling of the whole procession; pp. 345–346 bring it, under the roofed rose-way,
  to the amphitheatre gate.
