And looking again upon the plain, I saw it everywhere most green, adorned and painted with a variety of
scattered flowers: the yellows of the buttercup, and of frog's-foot or ox-eye; and the peacock-blues of
the Satyrion, of the lesser Centaury, and of the garland Melilot; and the small ones of the Eyebright,
and the golden of the Shepherd's-needle, and the flowering little Turnips, and the azures of the Clary,
and of the cornfield Gladioli; and of Strawberries with flowers and fruits; and the small Yarrow with
its white little tufts, and the Sawwort, and Pancuculus, and infinite other most beautiful little
flowers.

Whereupon, lost in a wonderful pleasantness, I felt myself consoled. And here and there, with measured
and ordered distance and interval, with gracious spaces, composedly and to the level, were the
green-bearing Orange and lemon trees and Adam's-apple trees, with their branches levelled and hung one
pace from the ground, thick with fronds — such as the glassy colour appears, of a turbinate form, that
is, of a pinnacled length and rounded at the base — with an abundance of their flowers and fruits,
breathing a most sweet odour. By which my closed heart felt itself not sparingly refreshed to the
utmost (having perhaps been invaded by the pestilent stench and wasting snaky breath).

For which cause I stood long pondering, suspended and full of stupefaction, in what place I now found
myself, so delightful to my senses — having especially considered carefully that marvellous fountain,
the variety of herbs, the colouring of the flowers, the place planted with trees; the noble and most
convenient disposition of the site, the sweet and unresting song of the birds, the temperateness and
the most purged air. For all this I should have counted myself content, if I had found any inhabitant
there.

And the eagerness to go forward pressed me somewhat, the kindly place offering itself to me ever more
joyful — although the past terror had not yet altogether unstuck itself from my seizing memory, nor
been rooted out. And for this cause alone I stopped, doubtful, not knowing where and by what way to go
and set out.
