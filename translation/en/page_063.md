Where I firmly held that I had come into the inextricable fabric of the sagacious Daedalus, or of
Porsena, containing so many inexplicable goings and returnings with frequent doors to cheat the exit,
and to come back into those same errors; or into the chambered cave of the terrifying Cyclops, and into
the foul Cavern of thieving Cacus. So that, however much my eyes were somewhat accustomed to the
darkness, none the less by no means could I, wretched, discern anything.

But with my arms stretched out before my face, so as not to run butting into some pillar, I went, those
doing the office of my darkened eyes. As the Snail, bearer of its own governance, putting forth its
soft little horns and drawing them back, and feeling out the way and contracting them at obstacles. And
I likewise, groping, so as not to strike against those greatest supports of the mountain and Pyramid.
And turning towards the gate to see whether the cruel and fearful dragon came behind me, the light was
wholly spent.

I found myself, then, in the blind bowels and pathless passages of those shadowy caverns, and in
greater terror and mortal trouble than Mercury making himself an Ibis, and Apollo in Thrace, and Diana
in Chlomone a little bird, and Pan in his twofold shaping; and in greater than those of Oedipus, of
Cyrus, of Croesus and of Perseus; and in greater fright and destruction than the bear-shaped robber
Thrasyleon; and in greater straits than Psyche, and in more laborious perils than Lucius made an ass —
and when he heard the robbers' counsel of his own destruction, without any choice to be counselled,
truly ignorant and desperate.

At that point, made most fearful and anxious above all the fears, terrors and frights named, there came
besides the frequent flight of light-shunning owls about my head, to redouble the fearful straitness.
And sometimes, at their screech, without delay I believed myself to be among the pointed teeth of the
venomous Dragon, and held fast between its gripping jaws like a toothed saw. Without delay there
followed upon this a redoubling both of my perilous and grave terror and of the truth of my heart's
grief: there came into my mind the wolf I had seen, whether by chance it had been a pernicious portent
to me and a messenger of my wretched outcome.

Wandering and running here and there, like the corn-gathering ant that loses the scent of its track and
strays, with wakeful ears to perceive whether the horrendous monster had come upon me, sorrowful, with
the peril of its Lernaean and strong venom and its horrible sword and most foul devouring. And
therefore everything that offered itself to me in that first approach I suspected to be that very
thing.
