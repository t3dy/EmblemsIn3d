In the middle of the shaft of the seventh, the beryl column, on the inner face,
there was marvellously carved out of that same stone — as though wrenched free
of the sculpture — a little Hermaphrodite child, held in a socket. The three
shining columns of the right-hand order had likewise, each of them, an infant
boy caught in a kind of receptacle; and so too on the shaft of each of the
precious columns of the left there hung, set into it, the female sex. And this,
by a mystical artifice, was expressed naturally at the middle of the columns,
with such a scintillation of polish as neither the biting of whetstone nor emery
with licking Tripoli chalk can give.

The bases, the capitals, the Phrygian architrave and the cornice stood out in
the purest gold. The arches, and all the solid between one column and the next,
were of the wrought stone of one of the columns of the surrounding order — that
is, of sapphire toward the emerald, and of emerald toward the turquoise — and so
on in sequence, the whole arcade was marvellously constructed.

At the angles of the crown, above the true central perpendicular of each column
beneath, there stood a small altar, and rising from it projected the image of a
planet with its proper attribute, its size exactly a third of the column below
it, symmetrically, and of the purest gold. On the front face, at the right, sat
scythe-bearing Saturn, and at the left the night-shining Cynthia; and beginning
from the first and going round in order they ended at Selene. Beneath these, in
the frieze running round, chased with the most exquisite artifice, were to be
seen the twelve signs of the zodiac, with their governing impressions and
characters, expressed in outstanding carving.

The crown then of this most marvellous fountain, and its roofing, blazed with an
extraordinary cupola of the finest crystal, veinless, utterly pure and
transparent. Xenocrates saw none such; none like it was found in Cyprus, nor
produced in Asia, nor in Germany — without rust or roughness, without a clouded
speck, no core of salt, nor any hairline flaw to be seen. Nor did Nero shatter
such a one. But pure, surpassing, and unalloyed, it was girdled with a raised
work, in a fitting and even binding of foliage issuing from certain little
monsters, with small boys wonderfully interwoven playing among them; and it was
of excellent bulk and convexity.

At the tapering of its topmost peak was fixed a miraculous show: in a golden and
most cunningly wrought [?setting], a carbuncle flashing lightning on every side,
egg-shaped, and of the thickness of an ostrich's.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The architecture, the planetary order and the crystal
cupola are secure. Two soft places: `Lovo` in the last paragraph is unresolved
and marked (below), and the sequence of planetary figures does not quite close
— Colonna starts at Saturn and Cynthia and says the round ends at Selene, but
Cynthia and Selene are both the Moon. I have translated what the page says
rather than repairing it. **A reviewer should start with that planetary
sequence.**

- **`in uno aureo et faberrimo Lovo`** — unresolved. Probably *l'ovo*, "the
  egg," which would suit a carbuncle described in the same breath as
  egg-shaped; possibly a corruption of a word for the setting or socket. Marked
  `[?setting]` rather than guessed. **Check against the facsimile.**
- **`di crassitudine strutiocamela`** — *struthiocamelus*, the ostrich. The
  carbuncle is the size of an ostrich egg, which for a gemstone is an absurdity
  Colonna clearly enjoys.
- **The sexual program of the columns** is the alchemical heart of the fountain
  and is stated plainly: infant boys set into the three right-hand columns, the
  female sex into the three left, and a hermaphrodite child in the seventh,
  which stands alone, is hexagonal where the others are round, and faces the
  midpoint between the first two. That is the *coniunctio* — the union of male
  and female in the hermaphrodite — built as architecture, and it is the same
  figure the Atalanta Fugiens draws in Emblem XXXIII.
- **`trabe Phrygio`** — the Phrygian architrave; Colonna uses the ethnic
  adjective for a decorative order rather than for provenance.
- **Xenocrates and Nero** are both from Pliny's chapters on rock crystal: the
  authority on it, and the emperor who smashed two crystal cups in a rage so no
  one else could have them.
- **`emusicatamente`** — literally "musically," used of mosaic or inlaid work;
  the sentence breaks off here and continues on p. 361.
