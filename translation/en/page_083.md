The most black stones of the wall were cut through and diligently set again with a most notable
composition of interlacing, or bindings, of antique leaves and flowers, of shining Cytherean little
Shells, as most acceptable to the eyes as ever one could set forth.

Above the door, in a space of Gallactite stone, I saw a dolphin bending among the calm waves, and a
youth sitting upon it and sounding a lyre. Opposite, above the laughter-making fountain, likewise
another dolphin swimming, with Poseidon riding upon it and with his pointed trident. These little
histories were made exactly from the bounds of the same stone, and carried upon a most black flat.
Here I deservedly praised that illustrious architect, and no less the statuary.

On the other hand, the comely dignity of those shapely and pleasing girls exalted itself. For I did not
know how to compare, between the terror now past and this unthought-of and chance solace, the excess of
it; but without doubt I found myself in extreme delight and pleasure.

And there, having most joyfully entered into such a fragrance as could never sprout in Arabia, they
stripped upon the stone seats in place of a dressing-room, and laid aside their silken garments, their
blond tresses most beautifully wrapped beneath netted Fillets woven of threads of gold, and most
worthily bound. And without any regard they allowed their shapely and delicate persons, all naked, to
be seen freely and particularly discerned — honesty reserved — flesh without fail delicate, rosy, and
suffused with ripe snow.

Alas, I felt my stirred heart leaping to open itself and to be wholly taken up with a pleasurable
gladness; whereupon I esteemed myself happy then, only in beholding such delights. Because most
pertinaciously I could not withstand the most burning fires hurtfully assaulting and troubling me in my
furnace-like heart. And for this, sometimes for my better refuge, I did not dare to look so much at
those enkindling beauties heaped up in those divine little bodies.

And they, perceiving it, laughed at my simple gestures, taking girlish sport. And I for this stood with
a sincere and contented mind, at being pleasing and welcome to them. And sitting in the midst of such
burning, I bore no ordinary patience. But yet with tolerance I stood, bashful and forbearing, knowing
myself unequal to so fair and such a company. And being invited also, although struggling and having
excused myself, none the less I entered into the bath — like a crow among white doves; for which cause
I stood apart, blushing, with my eyes unsteady, searchingly allured by objects so excellent.
