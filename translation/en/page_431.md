# Page 431 — Chapter XXX

Already the eloquent and inflamed Poliphilo fell silent from his sweet and most-accepted narration, with most-pleasant
and sweet little words; and that most-sweet mouth soothed my soul. And, taken and circum-obsessed by his mellifluous
tongue, in me I felt not the soul, but, among the rosy lips transmigrated, delightfully to enjoy I experienced. Whose

fulfil (it); and, by the henceforth not-simulated pity that, at present, of him I had, all commoved to satisfy — my heart

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The end of **chapter XXX**: Poliphilo's plea done, Polia — "taken by his mellifluous tongue," her
soul "transmigrated among his rosy lips," her love so kindled she "would burst" if she could not release it —
**interrupts the priestess's response** to answer Poliphilo directly. All secure; the overflow of her love.

- **Love that cannot wait.** Polia's love so overflows that she cuts off the priestess before she can rule, unable to
  contain it — the argument's "impatient love strengthened in her interrupted the response" (p. 428). Her eagerness is the
  exact opposite of her former frozen silence (she refused Poliphilo one word, p. 396; now she cannot stop her love from
  speaking). The reversal is total and dramatised in the very structure (interruption).
- **"my soul transmigrated among his lips."** Her soul leaves her and lives in Poliphilo's mouth as he speaks — the
  mutual self-loss of love (he "never found himself mine but all hers," p. 430; she now "feels not the soul in me"). The
  two selves dissolve into each other: the "two hearts coming into one" Poliphilo prayed for, already accomplished in her.
- **the answer preempts the priestess.** Polia's declaration (pp. 432–433) answers Poliphilo's petition before the
  priestess can — Venus's grace working through Polia's own overflowing love. Her speech and the kiss-pledge follow, the
  climax of Book II's reunion.
- Chapter XXXI opens with Polia's declaration of love.
