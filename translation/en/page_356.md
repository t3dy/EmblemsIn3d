There, beneath, restlessly, choral dances they led, and continually many most-joyous Nymphs — ever

golden trumpets, and four sweetest *epiphonia*, *antiphonia*, *messophona*, and *chamaephona* (upper-,
counter-, middle-, and ground-toned instruments), turned of red, citrine and white sandalwood, and of
blackest Ebony, not without manifold ornaments of gold and of gems. With a sonority most-sweetly
united-in-tone, and with briefest utterances, one explicated symphony, in this supremely-delightful and
most-happy enclosure, becomingly reflecting together — and the choristers, not with a broken tumult,
jubilated. Where was perceived a wondrous variety of voice, and an inexperienced equability of
proportion, and sweetness, and harmony, which extremely delighted my mind, and, rapt, held it soothing.

The middle Nymphs, nude, with their whitening and most-shapely bodies, proceeded. The others, most-
exquisitely comely, with much-admired adornment of their divine little bodies, clad in byssus robes and
veiling shifts, with virginal gestures and motions, and with girlish delight. As many again, in the
wall, specularly feigning, the blackest stone.

In prospect, then, of the door of our entrance, corresponded a stair of seven steps, of the stone of the
boxes. By which one ascended to the plane of the first pergola; and, over against the stair, under the
pergola, was built into the wall a slender door. Through which, then, one could — by interstitial stairs,
and passages, and goings — wander and go, through the fornices and vaults, and conveniently through all
the building. And under the other pergolas too, level with the first, were likewise little doors of gold,

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The theatre in use: **nymphs dancing choral rounds** toward the fountain in three
choruses (a middle and two revolving flanks) to four gold trumpets and four ranged instruments; the nude
and the byssus-clad dancers doubled in the black-mirror walls; a **stair of seven steps** opposite the
gate giving access, through little gold doors, to the whole system of internal passages; and the flower-
planting of the tiers begins. All secure.

- **The theatre alive with dance.** Colonna finally peoples his auditorium: choruses of nymphs weave
  reverencing rounds toward the central fountain, a three-part choreography (middle chorus, two counter-
  rotating flanks) synchronised to gold trumpets and toned instruments. The world's theatre could animate
  its centre with circling dancers reflected in the obsidian.
- **the seven-step stair and the honeycomb of passages.** Opposite the gate, a stair of seven mounts to the
  first gallery, and little gold doors lead into interstitial stairs and vaulted corridors threading the
  whole structure. The theatre is a working building with backstage circulation — a concrete plan-detail.
- **the dancers doubled in the wall** — nude and clothed dancers alike are reflected in the black-mirror
  gallery walls (p. 353), so the chorus appears twice: real and reflected. The mirror-motif governs even
  the performance.
- The planting of every tier is enumerated on p. 357, which closes the chapter.
