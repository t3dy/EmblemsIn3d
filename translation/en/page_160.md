# Page 160 — Chapter XIV

This figure of a car was four-cornered, of two perfect squares in length, of six feet, three high and
as much wide, with the required cornice above and beneath the plinth; and from here upward, a foot and
a half, there was a flat two feet and a half wide, five and a half long, with a slope towards the
cornice, all scaled with most precious stones, with an alternating meeting and order of colouring.

And at the four corners were fitted horns of plenty turned about, with their mouths upturned, above the
corner projection of the cornice, packed with many fruits and flowers of thick and manifold gems,
sprouting among the varied foliage of gold. Which horns I saw clothed with a distinguished showing of
leaves of the horned poppy, and with twisted hollows, and with their slenderness wound at the end of
the flat. Which broke off into a fringed antique leaf, that most beautifully ran over the back of the
elegant horn of the said material.

At each corner, from the plinth towards the cornice, at the projection there was made fast a harpy's
foot, with a moderate curving, and with an excellent turning here and there into acanthus foliage.

The wheels were covered within the car, half of them appearing; and the plinth — that is, the outermost
part of that engine — in the forward part, near the harpy feet, rising somewhat and neatly narrowing,
turned into a snail-like scroll. In which the nooses, or holdfasts for drawing, were fittingly
committed. And where the axle was fixed and turned, fitted to that plinth there hung a pointed piece,
of as great a breadth at the joining of the plinth as was twice the distance from the turning hub to
the top. And here there began exquisitely two foliages, which, dividing, ran down beneath the plinth;
