almost all their rotundity was free, appearing wholly pendent below. Which little lamps were excavated of
precious stone, an incomparable work: one of Balas-ruby; the other of Sapphire; the third of Emerald; the
last of Topaz.

The greater lamp, as above is said, was spherical, of most-clean crystal — nor at the lathe would such

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The suspension of the temple's great **hanging lamp**: four monster-girls turning to
acanthus and harpy-wings hold the chains; from a pierced plate hang **four hollow gem-lamps** (ruby,
sapphire, emerald, topaz) and, in the centre, the **great crystal sphere-lamp** (its "burning water" is on
p. 208). All secure; the lamp is a jeweller's fantasy of light.

- **The five-lamp chandelier.** Below the Medusa-oculus (p. 206) hangs a spherical crystal lamp a cubit
  across, ringed by four smaller lamps hollowed from single ruby, sapphire, emerald and topaz gems — the
  whole suspended by chains from four acanthus-harpy girls. Lit (p. 208), it throws coloured light through
  the whole temple. A spectacular, buildable centrepiece: a crystal-and-gem chandelier over the sacred well.
- **the four gem-lamps as coloured light-sources** — red, blue, green and gold, each a single carved stone:
  the temple is lit in the four jewel-colours, which mix on the marble (p. 208). A precise lighting scheme
  for the world's temple interior.
- **the girls dissolving into acanthus** — the caryatid-girls turn to foliage at the thigh (the "inhabited
  scroll" again, cf. the theatre bulls, p. 350), and grip the chains with harpy-wings: ornament and figure
  fused, holding up the lamp.
