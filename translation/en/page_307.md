## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The summary of the *prati*'s perpetual fecundity, the
rose-catalogue, and the beginning of the peristyle that closes the middle ring are
all secure.

- **The perpetual garden.** Colonna states the paradise-condition plainly: the
  fruits and flowers here are evergreen, "not subject to the lunar changes nor the
  paling of Phoebus," fruiting and flowering in one unchanging state — the fruit
  trees bear whatever the season, which is precisely the property Hunt singles out
  at Cythera (GARDENS.md §6, "Cythera's fruit trees bear fruit whatever the
  season"). This is the textual root of that reading.
- **the rose-catalogue** — Damascene, Praenestine, five-petalled, Campanian,
  Milesian, and the rest: all Pliny's rose-varieties, "nor sooner did one fall than
  another succeeded" — the self-renewing rose that the Adonia rite (ch. XXIV) will
  make miraculous.
- **the eustyle peristyle** closing the *prati* — a bellied colonnade with a
  latticework wall bounds the middle ring from the inner. *Eustyle* is Vitruvius's
  best intercolumniation (2¼ diameters); Colonna names the order precisely, as
  always. The description of this dividing colonnade continues on the next pages,
  leading to the river and the inner terraces.
- **the jasper chests "mirroring air, fronds and flowers"** — the polished stone
  planters reflect the garden, so that art holds nature's image: the paragone once
  more, and a neat closing image for the *prati*.
