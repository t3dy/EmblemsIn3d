Out of the little chests, or platforms, beneath the socle of the solid columns,
sprouted rose-bushes, their height not exceeding a pace. Which between one column
and the other made a delightful fence; to each column, on this side and on that,
perpendicularly raised, in the middle of the inner column adhered, most straight, a
rod of rose-bush. Which — beyond the most noble architrave extended over the said
columniation, of red stone like most gleaming coral — without other support the
rose-bushes made the topiary bend. The summit of the said pergola, with the little
chests, columns, and straight beam, stood five paces in height. From which summit
the little domes began to round, or swell, in bubble-form; these alone covered with
yellow roses. The pergolas along their length were roofed with all kinds of white
roses, and the transverse ones with vermilion roses of every condition, with
perennial fronds and flowering in the utmost multitude, and a breathing of
fragrance; and out of the little chests sprouted every generation of flowers and of
aromatic herbs.

The first surrounding pergola continued with the cloister of the orange-grove,
which had a window of the opening of the arch terminating at the cloister of the
pergola, directed toward the centre, less open from the ground up by one pace,
equal to the intercolumnar fence.

Each meadow had four gates, in the middle of the enclosing columniation, the
little chests left pervious. Which gates, through all the meadows, uniformly
corresponded to one another, level.

In the middle of the olive-set and flower-bearing meadows, an elegant work of
excellent distribution and especial polish, I saw. First, in the first meadows, I
beheld a most celebrated fabric of a gushing fountain, set beneath a little watch-
tower of box greenery of shrewd device. Which was found uniform throughout the
whole primary order, and in the circuit of the meadows, in this manner.

In the middle of each, with equal distribution, three steps, turned in a rounding,
were built up. Of the topmost, in the levelled surface, the diameter was two paces
and a half, with a peristyle of eight little columns raised above, with the little
bases in the circling round of the upper step, and with the capitals. Which usurped

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The construction of the *prati* fountains — a stepped round
base, an eight-column Doric peristyle, and antique vases sprouting box — is secure
in its architecture; the sentence-long anatomy of a single vase is intricate and
continues onto p. 300.

- **The meadow fountains and their box-topiary canopies.** Each of the first ring's
  meadows has, at its centre, this fountain under a "little watch-tower of box
  greenery." The world's Cythera renders exactly this: pool-and-topiary
  centrepieces at the *prati* (GARDENS.md §5). Here is the full apparatus — a
  three-stepped round base, eight Doric columns with bellied arches, and antique
  vases from which the box is trained.
- **the four gates per meadow, uniformly corresponding** — again the openings the
  world's radial roads pass through, stated for the middle ring as for the outer.
- **the rose-pergolas roofed white along their length, vermilion across** — the
  colour-coding of the pergola-roofs (yellow domes, white lengthways, red
  transverse) is a precise decorative scheme; the "topiary bend" of the arches is
  made of the rose-rods themselves, without other support.
