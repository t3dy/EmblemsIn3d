# Page 222 — Chapter XVII

At the margin of the soffit of the inverted flat, in the rim, evenly distant, were contained four most-
beautiful handles, deriving, and solid with the Trochlea; their volutes were under the inverted flat, and
ended above the Trochlea, with the cochleate whirl, spreading with the slope of the little slab. Then,
moderately, they inverted themselves toward the soffit, at a grateful up-curve, submitting themselves to it
with the whirl resupine, and the other supine.

This marvellous sculptile was all of one solid of finest jasper, of manifold mixture of colouring most-
admiredly coming together, and in whatever part with incredible and most-exquisite lineaments. A work, most-
certainly, not worked out by force of chisel, or scalpel, but with unknown artifice marvellously expressed.

From the marble and stepped footing, up to the beginning of the stylus exclusive, it was raised a cubit; the
stalk as much; the remainder, up to the golden platter, a foot and a half. Thence, above, a quadrant: from
one upper volute to the other spread gold threads, through the middle of which were threaded, in longish
berries, coruscating balas-rubies, bored, and of most-refulgent Sapphires, and of scintillating Diamonds,
and of springing Emeralds, with gracious and amicable colour-alternation strung, with inestimable and
monstrous pearls — such that, without doubt, such a gift Octavian did not make to Capitoline Jove.

In the inversion of the lips of the golden platter, quadrifariously, perpendicularly, hung orbiculate gems,
and pierced, through which, pervious, let in, a gold thread, threaded, they were retained, suspended, and
bound to a clasp, free in the grapples — larger than a hazelnut, seven to a thread. At the extreme of the
thread, interdicted (finished) by an elegant little floret, with the little spirals of varied silken thread,
gold, and silver, intermixed. Again, from one clasp to the other likewise knotted, gold threads, gemmed,
spread in the aforesaid manner —

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The altar completed: its four scrolled handles (all carved with it from **one solid
of jasper**, "not by chisel but by unknown artifice"), its exact dimensions, and the **festoons of gemstones**
(balas, sapphire, diamond, emerald and monstrous pearls) hung between its volutes and from its golden platter,
surpassing Octavian's gift to Capitoline Jove. All secure; the altar is a single-block jewelled marvel.

- **The altar carved from one stone.** The whole moulded, handled, scrolled object is one block of many-
  coloured jasper, worked "not by chisel… but by unknown artifice" — the topos of the seemingly impossible
  single-piece carving (cf. the one-pour vine-dome, p. 201). Colonna's marvels are always *made*, and always
  beyond ordinary craft.
- **the gem-festoons.** Between the volutes and from the platter hang strings of bored balas-rubies,
  sapphires, diamonds, emeralds and hazelnut-sized pearls — the altar dressed in jewels like the nymphs' dress
  (p. 335). A dazzling, buildable centrepiece: a jasper altar draped in gemstone garlands under its fire-dish.
- **surpassing Octavian's gift to Jove** — Augustus's famous dedications on the Capitol; the altar's jewels
  exceed them. The surpassing-topos, on votive treasure.
- The altar description closes here; the rite resumes on p. 223 with the invocation of the three Graces.
