In the opening, then, of this vessel there stood out a most precious little hill, marvellously heaped
of countless round gems pressed close one upon another, which with an unequal, or rough, shaping made
the stony little hill most charmingly, with the flashing of various lightnings of colour, with a
proportioned prominence.

At the top, or summit, of this little hill there grew a little tree of pomegranates, of trunk, or stem,
and of branches, and likewise this whole composition, of shining gold. The leaves set on of sparkling
emerald. The fruits, of their natural size, scattered here and there, with the golden rind widely
split, and in the place of the seeds there burned the brightest rubies, brightest beyond any touchstone
and of the thickness of a bean.

Then the ingenious maker of this inestimable work, being plentiful in his imagining, had marked off, in
the place of the pith, the seeds with the thinnest silver foil. Beyond this, and reasonably, he had
feigned certain other apples split open, but unripe in their seeding, where he had composed, with a
bold exquisiteness, thick pearls of eastern whiteness. Skilfully too he had feigned the pomegranate
flowers made of perfect coral in calyxes full of golden points.

Beyond this, out of the top of the pipe-like hollow stem there came a turning and free shaft, the lower
pivot of which was fixed in a socketed head, or rather made fast upon the middle of the axle. And it
rose up through the open and hollowed trunk.
