# Page 79 — Chapter VIII

ELEUTERYLIDA, HE SAW ALONG THE WAY, AND AT THE PALACE, EXCELLENT THINGS, AND A CHOICE WORK OF A
FOUNTAIN.

BEING AFFABLY RECEIVED AND HIGHLY reassured by those merciful Nymphs, and altogether comforted by those
coaxing girls, and my fled spirits not a little refreshed — showing myself readily willing in everything
that I could judge welcome and pleasing to them — I set myself freely familiar and most devoted.

And because they had alabaster boxes of perfumed powder, and little cleansing vessels of gold and of
fine stones, and shining mirrors, and golden hair-partings in their delicate hands, and white folded
veils of silk, and bathing shifts, and offered me to be their carrier, they told me, refusing, that
their coming to this place was because they came to the bath. And immediately they added: "We wish you
to come with us. It is there before you, where a fountain pours. Did you not see it?"

I answered reverently: "Most comely Nymphs, if a thousand and various tongues were found in me, I should
not fitly know how to render the thanks I owe, and to thank so homely a kindness — for you have most
opportunely brought me to life again. Therefore not to accept such a gracious and Nymph-like invitation
would be reckoned rustic baseness. And for so much I should esteem myself happier being a servant with
you than ruling elsewhere; because, so far as I can conjecture, you are the dwellers and companions of
every delight and true good.

"You must know that I saw the marvellous fountain, and considered it with a shrewd sense; and I confess
and affirm it a work more illustrious than ever presented itself to my eyes. And I so occupied my
shrewd mind, allured, intently gazing upon it and drinking greedily, wholesomely quenching my heavy and
day-long thirst, that I went no further to explore."

One of them answered, saying placidly: "Give me your hand. Now you are safe and welcome. We are at
present five companions together, as you see; and I am called Aphea. And this one, who carries the
little boxes and the whitest linens, is named Osfressia. And this other, who is the bearer of the
shining mirror — our delight — Orassia is her name. This one who holds the sounding lyre is called
Achoë. This last, who bears this vessel of most precious liquor, has the name Geussia. And let us go
together to these temperate Baths, for pleasure and delight. Whereupon shortly you too (since your
