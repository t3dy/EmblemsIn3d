This greatest beast stood therefore in fitting composition, so nobly figured, upon the levelled plane
of a vast base of hardest Porphyry, most smoothly lined. With two great projecting tusks of a most
white and shining stone, set on and fastened. And from the bronze saddle there hung, buckled, an
excellent breast-piece of various ornament, of the material of the saddle, in the middle of which was
written in the Latin tongue: *The brain is in the head.*

And likewise, carried round by the end of the neck and joined to the great head, there went a
masterly binding. From which an ambitious ornament, most notable, of bronze, hung thrown over its most
ample brow, composed of two squares, with elegant lineaments. In the flat of which — surrounded with
waving foliage — I saw certain Ionic and Arabic letters.

Now its voracious trunk did not keep to the level of the base, but raised and hanging it stood, turned
somewhat towards the front, with the furrowed ears very widely let down, or barred. Which image in its
vastness showed itself not one whit less than the natural. And in the oblong circuit of the base were
carved hieroglyphs, or Egyptian characters. Decently polished, with the due base-course, with the
block, gorge, torus and roundel, with their Astragals or little bindings, with the reversed Cyma at
the footing. And above no less, with the projecting Cyma turned back, and the collar of the scotia and
the dentils with the Astragals; excellently Symmetried according as the thickness required. The
length, breadth and height: twelve paces, five, and three. The extremities of which were formed in a
half-circle.

In the hinder half-round part of the base just described, I found a stepped ascent of seven steps
carved for climbing upon the flat surface. By which, eager for novelty, I mounted. And towards the
reserved quadrangle, set beneath the perpendicular of the saddle-cloth, I saw a little door hollowed
out. A thing of great admiration, that in so stubborn a material so apt a hollow was afforded; so that
