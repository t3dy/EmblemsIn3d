and weeping for an importunate and unprospering love, I turned over point by point what a thing
unequal love is; and how one may fitly love who does not love in return; and with what protection the
fluctuating soul, besieged by unaccustomed and repeated assaults and surrounded by hostile battle,
may resist at all, being so unarmed — the seditious battle being above all an inward one, and
ceaselessly ensnared by anxious, unstable and ever-new thoughts.

Having grieved bitterly and at length over a state so made and so wretched, and the wandering spirits
being already worn out with thinking to no purpose, and fed upon a false and feigned pleasure — yet
rightly and without fail upon an object not mortal but rather divine, upon Polia, whose venerable
Idea lives deeply impressed in me and more inwardly carved still, and holds me. And already the
trembling and glittering stars were beginning to pale their splendour, when, the tongue falling
silent, that desired enemy from whom proceeds this so great and unceasing contest, impatiently urging
the wounded heart, called upon it tirelessly for a profitable and effectual remedy — which was
nothing other than the renewal of my torment, without interval, cruel.

Musing too upon the quality of wretched lovers, by what condition they choose sweetly to die in order
to please another, and, pleasing themselves, to live ill; and to feed a spear-like desire upon
laborious and sigh-laden imagining, and upon nothing else. Then, like a man weary after long labours,
just so and no otherwise, the sorrowful outward weeping being scarcely at all abated, and the course
of the streaming tears shut in, my cheeks hollowed with the languor of love, I now desired the
natural and timely quiet. Now, my wet eyes a little closed within their reddened lids, without
lingering between bitter life and gentle death, that part was invaded and possessed and pressed down
by a sweet sleep which is not united with the mind, nor with the loving and wakeful spirits, nor
shares in operations so high.
