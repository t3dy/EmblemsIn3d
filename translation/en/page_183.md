Others had set themselves upon the green grass and the coloured flowers, at a welcome sitting near the
adorned banks, not tangled with little reeds, but adorned with various flowers. In which the melting
waters, clearer than the Axius in Mygdonia, sounded, breaking at the feet of the crimson oleander; and
beneath the shadowing trees they were combed one with another, like the viper locks of Medusa, and more
than the tangled dodder, in delightful embraces, and gripping more closely together than the creeping
ivy to the ancient elms and to the most aged buildings.

And to their reverent lovers not fierce, not refusing, but purely with a companionable love kindly and
affable, and yielding themselves to their desires, consenting with their bare and full breasts. Which
gave themselves to the eyes beyond measure welcome, with loving gestures more delightful and gracious
than flowing tears to cruel and pitiless Cupid. And much more than the cool little streams to the
grassy meadows, and the morning dew. And more than the longed-for form to matter.

Some sang fitly amorous verses, with tired voices taken up with little sighs in their inflamed breasts,
full of sweet accents, able to make sweetly enamoured the fierce hearts of stone, and to tame the
harshness of the pathless Caucasus. And to hinder all that the lyre of Orpheus did, and the accursed
face of Medusa. And to call back any horrible monster, pleasant and to be handled. And to quiet the
continual goad of raging Scylla.

Some lay idly set in the chaste laps of the seated girls, and told the pleasant jests of high Jove; and
they, sharp-witted, went about their curled hair with garlands of savoury flowers, and of fragrant
little herbs, and crowned them with the utmost solace.

Likewise some of them were lovingly and feignedly refused, pretending to flee what both one and the
other intently desired. And here, following, they ran one after the other with their little mouths open,
full of laughing and womanly cries, with their blondest tresses poured down over their milky shoulders,
veiling them, shining like a threading of gold, pressed by garlands of green myrtle. And some had them
knotted elegantly with nymphlike care, with flying veils. Some with hair-fillets woven of a golden
plaiting, adorned with gems.
