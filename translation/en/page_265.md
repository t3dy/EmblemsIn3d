On the lower plane of the niche was a plinth of the narrated material, in aspect most-beautifully adorned with

antiquarian little ark, with lineaments of notable expression. Upon the cover was a bench (throne), to the compass of
the covering, feigned covered with silken cloth, with an encompassing fringe.

Sat upon (it) a **Matron-Queen**, with regal cultivation, and majestic vestment, on the breast fibulated over a fitted
garment; which, from the collar down through the breast, and transversely in the girding, went round a little fascia,
and, over the belly, dilated in the form of a *tetraphyllon* (four-leaf), that is, in a figure of four hemicycles; in
which, of Greek majuscules, was thus annotated:

> **ΜΑΥΣΩΛΕΙΟΝ ΑΤΙΜΗΤΟΝ** (MAUSOLEION ATIMETON) — "The priceless Mausoleum."

With the right (hand) she held a **chalice to the mouth, drinking**; and in the other she held a little rod, or
sceptre; with the poured (loose) hair, over the head wreathed with a somewhat-fastigiate crown, tapering to another
crownlet at the nape; from which, becomingly and combed, descended the hair.

Upon the keystone of the arched architrave stood forth an oval figure, flattened, of a height up to under the

## Notes

**Confidence: high** (the two Greek inscriptions are secure and famous). The **tomb of Artemisia and Mausolus** — the
original Mausoleum: a porphyry niche-monument inscribed *"The ashes of Queen Artemisia"* and *"The priceless
Mausoleum,"* with a seated queen-figure **drinking from a chalice** (Artemisia drinking her husband's ashes), a
lion-clawed ark, and Mausolus's bearded portrait held aloft by winged spirits. All secure; the archetype of the love-
monument.

- **Artemisia drinking the ashes — the emblem of conjugal love.** The queen who, out of grief, *drank the ashes of her
  husband Mausolus* and built him the Mausoleum (one of the Seven Wonders): Colonna sets her tomb at the Polyandrion's
  centre as the supreme image of love faithful beyond death (cf. Cornelia Annia, p. 261). The figure with the chalice
  at her lips is the drinking of the ashes made visible. A powerful, buildable centerpiece for the world's polyandrion.
- **"The priceless Mausoleum."** *Mausoleion atimeton* — the tomb names itself the Mausoleum, the monument so great it
  gave its builder's name to all great tombs. The Polyandrion, a ruin of humble love-graves, holds at its heart the
  grandest tomb of all — love's memorial raised to a Wonder of the World.
- **the drinking queen, the borne portrait** — Artemisia with chalice and sceptre; Mausolus's crowned, bearded face
  held by two winged genii: a complete iconographic group. The world's polyandrion could render this as its principal
  tomb-tableau (the widow drinking, the husband's imago aloft).
- **lion-claw feet, tetraphyllon breastplate** — the precise ornament (gilt lion's-paw ark-feet, the four-lobed breast-
  fascia bearing the inscription): Colonna's exact sculptural detail, buildable to the letter.
