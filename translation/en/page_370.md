## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** Descriptive garden prose; secure in sense. Two soft
points: the marble lattice's thickness (`sextante semisso` — a fraction of a foot,
the exact value uncertain, marked) and `Polycarpia` as the name of the garland
(from καρπός, fruit — "the many-fruited," a coinage kept as a name).

- **Polia weaving a garland of her own hair and flowers** — the *Polycarpia*. The
  detail of her uprooting long golden hairs to bind the flowers is one of the
  book's tender-strange moments; kept literal.
- **The banks "not mossy… but Macedonian marble"** — Colonna's habitual figure:
  he names the natural thing (moss, maidenhair, spleenwort — real streamside
  ferns) only to deny it in favour of the artificial. The paragone again.
- **`vincapervinca`** — periwinkle, the flower of the walks; **manna-ash**
  (*ornus*) the trees over the rivulet. The botany is precise and Plinian.
- The description runs on to the laurel-shaded fountain of Adonis on p. 371.
