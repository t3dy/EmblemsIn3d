Not without grave injury, damage and loss (and, by the ventilating impetus, the water pours through the
walls, rendering them then putrid and earthy, and through the windows leaking, exhausted and vanished
every rendering): by the sprinkling of which, there grows in the joints herbage — cotyledon, or cymbalaria,
maidenhair, digitello, pellitory, and polypody — and, ready to produce little shrubs and wild figs, a mural
ruin, with populous and reviving fibres, or roots, rendering the walls inconstant and rift.

Let us return. The band of the first wall derived its height to the level of the summit, of the thickness
of the inflected Architraves, or Arches, above the columnation, and those of the vaulting, from the wall,
opposite the Corinthian column. Above the surface of which wall — that is, of the binding of the circum-led
cornice — was a little channel excavated. Near the lip of which, toward the temple, terminated the scaled
slope of the bronze and gilt roof. The topmost of which summit, above the curved (dome), evenly began,
opposite the sublime line of the cornice, of the Frieze and inner Architrave. In the said little channel,
above the wall, in the flat of the cornice excavated, the waters that by rain ran down the sloped roof were
poured into the receivers of the Piers. And by this way the rivulets of the conceived rain guttered,
precipitating to the bottom; and by hidden pipes, or subterranean passages, flowed into the Cistern. Which
had a Devourer (overflow-drain), by which the superfluous of the waters went out, and sufficiently for the
sacred things remained.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** A largely **engineering** page: the temple's rainwater system (roof-channel →
piers-as-downpipes → subterranean pipes → cistern with an overflow), framed by a little essay on *why*
open gutters and stairs are bad (slipping, fouling, and — the vivid part — water eroding foundations and
weeds rooting in wet joints). All secure; Colonna the practical architect.

- **The temple's water is designed, not decorative.** The gilt scaled roof drains into a cornice-channel,
  which feeds the hollow piers (p. 201), which run to a cistern with an overflow that keeps "sufficient for
  the sacred things." Colonna specifies a complete concealed drainage system — the same rigor he gives to
  Cythera's ring-river (p. 312). The temple is engineered to stay dry.
- **the catalogue of wall-weeds** — cotyledon, cymbalaria, maidenhair, pellitory, polypody, and self-sown
  wild figs: the plants that ruin damp masonry. Even his warning against water-damage becomes a botanical
  list. Characteristic of the book that a plumbing note carries a herbarium.
- **the earthy realism** — the aside about the man who "makes water next to his own feet" on an open stair
  is a startling drop into the practical and the crude, amid the marble and gold: Colonna's range runs from
  the Muses to the latrine on one page. Rendered plainly, at its own register.
- Structurally this page confirms the temple as a real building — roof pitch, cornice line, dome springing,
  cistern — not a dream-vision facade. The world can treat it as constructable.
