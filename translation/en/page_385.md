# Page 385 — Chapter XXV

Being, then, from this feeble and pitiable case, the only survivor escaped — the first-born **Lelio Mauro** — who, being
in his boyhood invited by his kinsmen, the lords of the Altinates, to a solemn anniversary funeral (outside the Manian
gate, where it was named "ad Manes," in which place were buried the cadavers of the whole city, and still, the vocable
corrupted, kept, one says "alli Mani"): the pompous obsequies made, with most-ancient rites and patrician custom, he
remained with some youths at leisure. It befell that, finding themselves at the maritime places, near the watch-tower,
or shrine, of the port of Altino, named **Turricella** (from which, at present, is called the noble town **Turricello**,
there founded) — in that his puberty, then, by invading **Pirates** they were captured. Whence he was, by lot, into the
ancient Bruttian people conducted, into a famous city, which at present is called **Teramo**. Who, adopted for his
ingenuous character by a noble and magnificent man, **Theodoro**, grew up patricianly; and, after sufficient learning,
to the military exercises, assiduous, strenuously gave heed. Whereupon, being come to the virile age, and, in progress
of time, for his things most-excellently done — and, with a soul strong, exalted, generous, robust — most-constantly
various victories attained, and all the military honours discharged (like Bellerophon exalted from Heurie): for this
reason, no more **Lelio Maurio**, but, for the egregious condition and outstanding effects, was named **Calo Maurio** —
to extinguish, with the choice virtues, the ill-omened name.

For all which things, from the most-holy Roman Senate, with military prefecture, with the *paludamentum* (general's
cloak) designated, he came, deputed to dwell — by chance — where he was born, and whence he was descended, keeping his
fatherland secure and guarded from the barbaric invasions. Which place, of shade and air most-pleasing, and of rivers
and founts pleasant, chosen, was named by him **Calo Mario** — so that, by the benefit of the site, more promptly, amid
the molesting assiduity of the infesting foe, he might some time avail to delight himself, where was the verdant area,
clad of herbs and of flowers.

There, then, when he was made *poliucho* (city-protector) — round about, in serviceable memory, and of his dearest
mother, an eternal monument — a noble and great city of municipal people (named from the hill **Taurisana**), and of
literal and military study, and of site most-rich and pleasant, and of most-ancient cult, and of sanctity and religion
a most-true hostess, upon the hastening and swift Father **Sile** — and, given it the name of the Pious Mother
**Trivisia** — whence, even to this day, the maternal name it retains (**Treviso**).

Which, with auspicious increment, a long time, with affluence, with togate peace, and social federations of the
neighbours, with life led, pacate, he, happily dominating, possessed; and, of him, the successors —

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The survival and career of **Lelio Mauro** (the one Lelius spared the metamorphosis): captured
by pirates as a boy, taken to Teramo, adopted, made a soldier, renamed **Calo Maurio**, and sent by the Roman Senate as
military prefect back to his birthplace — where he **founds Treviso** on the river Sile, naming it after his mother
Trivisia. All secure; the foundation-legend of the city.

- **The founding of Treviso, completed.** Colonna gives the city a full Roman foundation-legend: the pirate-captured
  heir returns as a Roman prefect and builds Tarvisium/Treviso on the Sile (his own transformed father), naming it for
  his mother Trivisia Pia — so "the maternal name it retains to this day." The dream-romance is anchored to a real city
  and its real river, with an antiquarian etymology. (The author's Trevisan connection again — cf. p. 383.)
- **the name-change as redemption.** *Lelio Maurio* ("the dusky," the ill-omened name that marked the doomed family)
  becomes *Calo Maurio* ("the good"), his virtue "extinguishing the ill-omened name" — a small pattern of a cursed
  lineage redeemed by merit, prefiguring Polia's own arc (her cruelty expiated into love). Names carry fate in Book II.
- **"the holy and fierce Marine Lion."** (Set up here; named on p. 386.) The city's history will run down to Venetian
  rule — the **Lion of St. Mark** — placing Polia's own time under the Venetian Republic. Colonna dates and localises
  his heroine precisely.
- **Bellerophon, the paludamentum, poliucho** — Colonna's learned apparatus (the hero exalted, the Roman general's
  cloak, the Greek "city-protector"): the foundation-legend is told in full antiquarian dress even in Polia's plainer
  Book-II voice.
