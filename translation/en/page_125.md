"I think, Poliphilo, that of this wondrous site you do not understand its appointed lot. Attend. He who
enters cannot go back. But, as you plainly see, those watch-towers set here and there stand seven
circuits apart one from another; and the extreme harm that falls to those who enter is this: that in
that central tower, in the open gap of its entry, a deadly dragon dwells, most devouring and invisible.
And this is most harmful on the one hand, and on the other quiet: not to be able to see it, and utterly
terrible not to be able to shun it. Which, both at the entry and in the going forward, wherever it
chances and is appointed to wish, devours those who have entered. And if between one tower and another
it does not kill them, they pass safely through the whole sevenfold circuit as far as the next tower.

"Those, then, who enter here by that first tower (look at the title written in Greek noting, and think
most carefully upon it: DOXA KOSMIKE HOS POMPHOLYX) go with the little boat with the current, and
without any care or labour the fruits and flowers fall into the skiff, and with the utmost pleasure and
sport they run through the seven windings as far as the second tower. And consider, Poliphilo, how
great the brightness of the air is in this beginning, growing to the middle tower, and thence little by
little, about the centre, darkening and growing gloomy and lightless.

"In that first tower there dwells eternally, presiding, a most pious matron and kindly giver, before
whom there stands most steadily a most ancient urn of lots and of dispensing, adorned with seven Greek
letters as you see thus: THESPION, packed with fated tokens; and to each of those who enter, most
charmingly and munificently she gives one of those, without respect of condition, but according as the
chance disposition falls out. These, being received, coming forth they begin to sail in the labyrinth,
the passages hedged with roses and fruit-bearing trees.

"The first long circuit of the seven windings being passed, from the Ram to the last fishy tail, coming
to the second tower, they find countless girls of divers condition, who ask of all the showing of their
tokens; and it being shown to them, they, most skilled, know the proper and disposed token, and
embracing him they receive him as a guest, and invite him with them to wander through the other seven
circuits; and according to his bent readiness, and with divers exercise, inseparably they lead them as
far as the third tower. In this place whoever will persevere with his companion, she never abandons nor
