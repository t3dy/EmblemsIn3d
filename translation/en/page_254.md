> "To the shades. To my gladiator, by my love — for whom, burned to the utmost, I lay down in death's languor; but
> by his blood (alas, wretched me!), unpurified, I recovered. Leaving a pious monument [to divine Faustus
> Augustus?], that yearly, with the blood of turtledoves amid the sacrifice of the arcane religion, this [torch] be
> dipped, with forty-nine kindled torches; and that the weeping girls be loosed (in mourning), and, for the token
> of so great grief, funeral lamentation — with hair torn and let flowing — redden their breasts and face a whole
> day; and, the shades propitiated, they busy themselves about the sepulchre yearly, in perpetuity to be repeated.
> By her testament she ordered it made." *(Several abbreviations are conjecturally expanded.)*

Then, when I had these two epitaphs accurately perused, and most-pleasingly seen, solicitous, inquiring, turning
the eyes — lo, that I see a **historiated sepulchre**. To which, without tarrying, I went. On the anterior part of
which, fixed, was a little altar, together with such (figures) expressed. Upon the flat of it, of wondrous
artifice, was set a head of a sylvan goat, held by one of the horns by a little old man, with the head of hair, in
the old manner, tormented (dressed), confused; who was clad, over the nude, in a pall thrown back over the right
shoulder, coming under from the left, returning over the right, and at the back pendent; near, he was clothed in
goat-skin — one (skin) in front and the other behind at the back, over the shoulders, the feet of both skins
knotted, and the others hanging between the thighs with the rough hair turned to the flesh; and girt with a torque,
or fillet, made of *tanus*, or black vine, with its leaves.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium** (the epitaph is elaborate and several abbreviations are conjecturally expanded). A remarkable
**foundation-epitaph**: a woman to her dead **gladiator**-lover — she nearly died of love, "recovered by his blood,"
and by her will endowed a **perpetual annual rite** (dove's-blood sacrifice, forty-nine torches, girls loosing their
hair and reddening their breasts in mourning) at his tomb. Then a **historiated Bacchic sepulchre** (a goat-headed
sylvan altar with an old satyr). All secure in outline; some Latin flagged.

- **The gladiator's endowment — a lover's perpetual mourning.** The most elaborate epitaph yet: a *foundation*, by
  testament, of a yearly funeral rite for the beloved gladiator — an antique instance of the endowed anniversary
  (like the Polyandrion's own festival, p. 237). It shows love's grief institutionalised: the mourner buys eternal
  lamentation. A rich model for the world/novel's polyandrion (a tomb with its own perpetual rite inscribed).
- **"recovered by his blood."** The macabre detail — she was healed of love-sickness by her dead lover's blood —
  ties the epitaph to the Polyandrion's blood-rites (pp. 237, 248). Love, illness, blood and death intertwined.
- **the Bacchic tomb-relief** — the historiated sepulchre carries a satyr-and-goat pastoral (continued p. 255), the
  Dionysiac imagery of death-and-fertility that runs through the tombs (cf. the theatre friezes, p. 349). The
  world's polyandrion tombs can carry such carved panels.
- **forty-nine torches** — the oddly specific number (7×7) marks the rite as arcane and numerological, fitting the
  "arcane religion" the epitaph names. Colonna loves such exact ritual detail.
