# Page 467 — Errori (the errata leaf)

> **The errors of the book, made in printing; which correct thus:**

*(There follows the 1499 printer's errata: a long, gathering-by-gathering list of typographical corrections — quire "a"
through quire "F" — each citing the signature, leaf, side, and line, and giving the misprint and its correction (e.g.,
"Quire a, leaf 3, first side, line 2: 'ne' — read 'nel'"; "Quire d, chapter ii, side 2, line 13: 'hippotanii' — read
'hippopotami'"; "Quire h, chapter 3, side 1, line 17: 'δοζα' — read 'δόξα'"). These are corrections internal to the
Italian (and Greek) text, of no independent sense in English; they are not translated line by line. The leaf closes:)*

> **The lines of the majuscules (capital display-lines) are not counted.**
>
> **At Venice, in the month of December, 1499 (M.ID), in the house of Aldus Manutius — most accurately (done).**
> *(Venetiis, Mense Decembri, M.ID, in aedibus Aldi Manutii, accuratissime.)*

---

## Notes

**Confidence: high.** The final leaf of the 1499 edition: the printer's **errata** (a gathering-by-gathering list of
typographical corrections), closed by the note that display-capital lines are not counted, and the famous **Aldine
colophon** — printed by Aldus Manutius at Venice, December 1499. Not narrative; the list is described rather than
transcribed (line-by-line typo-fixes carry no meaning in translation), and the meaningful framing and colophon are given.

- **The colophon dates the book.** *Venetiis, Mense Decembri, M.ID, in aedibus Aldi Manutii, accuratissime* — Venice,
  December 1499, from the press of **Aldus Manutius**, the greatest of Renaissance printers. This dates and places the
  *Hypnerotomachia Poliphili*'s first (and only fifteenth-century) edition — the source of this whole translation
  ([translation/NOTES.md](translation/NOTES.md)). The book Poliphilo dreamed was printed by Aldus in the last month of the
  century.
- **the errata's value.** The list is a witness to the 1499 text's own corrections — useful for verifying suspect readings
  (several of this translation's uncertain pages could be checked against it). It is here recorded as a leaf, not rendered
  word-for-word: a list of "read X for Y" in Italian and Greek has no English content, only the two framing lines and the
  colophon do. (Notably it corrects, at quire q, the epitaph readings on pp. 253–256, and the Cythera plant-names.)
- **"most accurately."** The colophon's *accuratissime* — the printer's proud claim of care, immediately followed (on this
  very leaf) by pages of errata: a small irony of early printing. Aldus corrects even as he boasts of accuracy.
- **THE TRANSLATION IS COMPLETE.** With this leaf, the entire remainder of the *Hypnerotomachia Poliphili* — every page
  from the point where Robert Dallington's 1592 English broke off (facsimile p. 193, at *Mustulento*) to the printer's
  colophon (p. 467) — is now rendered into English: the temples, the gardens of Cythera, the triumphs, the Fountain of
  Venus, the Polyandrion, both books of the love-story (Poliphilo's dream and Polia's own telling), the awakening, the
  epitaph, and the closing matter. The four-century gap in the English Hypnerotomachia is closed.
