I considered them, the more ignorant and astonished I stood; but certainly above all things I took an
extreme delight, with an intense admiration, seeing so many and so great and triumphing and most
lavish sumptuosities of incredible cost and splendour, that I judge it better to be silent than to say
too little of it — save that at the least price there yield here the Sicilian feasts, the ornaments of
Attalus, and the Corinthian vessels, and the delights of Cyprus, and the Salian suppers.

Nevertheless so supreme and excessive a cheerfulness and heartfelt delight, and the utmost (among such
delights) and extreme pleasure, was interrupted, intercepted and stained for me by one of these three
who at this change happened to serve before me: who had almost wholly the comely semblance and the
sweetest representing aspect of Polia, and in her stirring gestures, sly and most thievish glances.
This brought me no small increase of sweetness to the highest and chief sweetness of the filling and
rich seasonings and the most bountiful refreshment. But yet fittingly and carefully I drew back my
eyes, stirred and enticed as they were, from beholding so many most precious gems, everywhere and
universally shining with a flashing brightness, and such a diversity of unseen beauties and notable
adornments — and had as it were made them her bondmen, with an immoderate longing to behold the
answering of that highest beauty.

At last, in the order aforesaid, the tables being cleared, they made me a sign that I should not move
from my place, for the sweetmeats that followed and the richest and sweetest dainties.

And here first to the venerable and divine presence of the Queen, and thereafter to us, five shapely
servers, clothed in a garment of dark blue silk most beautifully woven with a golden thread, presented
themselves together at one time to each with a singular excellence. She in the middle of the others
held an uncommon little tree of cinnabar-red coral, such as would not be found in the Orkney isles, a
cubit high. Fixed with great care upon a little hill all of emerald. Which little hill lay upon the
mouth of an antique vase of the purest gold, almost in the likeness of a chalice, as high as the little
hill and the branching coral, full of marvellous artifice of foliage, not expressed in our age.

Between the slenderness of the foot and the bowl there was knotted with the greatest polish a little
apple of inestimable making; likewise the base and the cup were of distinguished relief-work of leaves,
little monsters and two-formed little Scyllas, as exquisitely expressed as ever chased work could be
wrought fiercely, with a proportioned circling. And the biting setting of the acorned little hill was
