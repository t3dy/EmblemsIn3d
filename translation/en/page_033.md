# Page 33 — Chapter IV

In the surface of the base there was fixed and leaded a plate of the same cast material — as much as
the shoes stood held fast and the young men thrown down — the whole one composition and mass cast
together, by a wonderful art of founding. In the end one could not tell whether any rider of such a
swift horsemanship was yet content, so far as I could judge. For which cause the statues appeared
sorrowful and wearied, without lament — which was not heard because they lacked it: only because the
maker could not breathe the breath of life into them, so excellently did they imitate the truth of
nature. Let the sharp genius of the imprudent Perillus give way here, and of Hiram the Jew, and of
every founder of statues whatsoever. It gave one to understand that those striplings were being
brought so ill towards the opened gate.

The Pedestal, or marvellous base, was of solid marble — of a thickness, height and length proportioned
to sustaining the machine fixed upon it — of flooding veins many-coloured, and of pleasing markings
grateful to the eyes, disposed confusedly in infinite mixtures.

In the front of the aforesaid stone, towards the gate, I saw set a crown of green marble of leaves of
bitter Parsley, with mixed fennel-like leaves of Hog's-fennel. Within which there was moreover
fastened a rounding of white stone, in which, carved, it held a writing in Latin capitals.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The cast group of youths thrown from the horse, Perillus and Hiram, and the
pedestal with its first two garlanded roundels. The founders are correctly identified (Perillus of
the brazen bull; Hiram, the bronze-caster of 1 Kings 7, here called *iudaeo* as Colonna calls him).
Two problems for a reviewer. First, an unevenness in the apparatus: the first roundel's inscription
is missing from the source transcription and is marked as such by an editorial note, but the second
— *in una corona di foglie di mortifero Aconito cusì annotato* — is missing too and is left
unmarked, so the English simply stops. Both readings are recoverable from the corpus: hp.db
`woodcut_catalog` nos. 7 and 8, on this leaf, record them as the garland with **D.AMBIG.D.D.** and
the garland of orpine with **Equus infalicitatis**. Second, the clause *il quale non si sentiva per
essere prive, perche il significo solamente non gli pote l'aura vitale inspirare* is rendered into
an English sentence that does not parse; *significo* is the unresolved word.

*Confidence stated 2026-09-20 by a second reader, not by the translator (ticket tr-confidence-unstated-180).*
