# Page 273 — Chapter XIX

And that thus, to her, the trident-bearing Neptune might not violently do (as to Medusa)! Alas, at this point I
certainly experienced what is the affliction of spirit, and the quality of true lovers — extremely more trembling,
terrified, and stupefied, and finding myself in greater anguish, and into more miserable trepidation deduced, than
when I saw myself almost a putrescible little morsel among the gaping and horrend jaws of the venefic dragon, at the
next-door demise. With so much terror that I could judge (myself), through the priest and the removed simulacrum of
the Pellenean temple, to have every serenity struck from the mind, re-cruding every hour, the more, the formidable
grief in the fluctuating heart.

For which thing, impelled by the coquent cares and urgent anguishes, without any delay, most-swiftly brazen-footed —
leaving so noble an undertaking, and illustrious inquisition, and laudable lustration, and virtuous solace — suddenly,
then, through those windings and infested bushes, and rough shrubs, and through the place made narrow of stones and of
thorns, and through those prolapses of marbles, and formless heap of rudiments and fragments, uneven, scrupulous, and
among caustic nettles and pathless ruins, I hasten, with hostile course, by rough ways and innumerable stumbling-
blocks, with prompt velocity — not esteeming the laceration of my ragged toga, here and there held by thorny fronds,
and all full of burs and down, and thistle-tufts, and goat's-beard, and sowthistle, in some part entangled by the
rapacious seeds of hound's-tongue — firmly holding to have arrived at the newest troubles, and formidable perils, and
ultimate and deadly privation of all my hoped good, to my sweetest and most-precious Polia: not wholly alive, but half-
dead, like an asthmatic drawing the breath (not by other way than if the grave odour of the Babylonian gulf I had
drawn), with the eyes wet with tearful dew, near her, having prostrated myself, spasming, with incredible heart-grief
and swooning, scarce I arrived.

And she, with the soft soul moved, with serene forehead, and propitiated and soothing face — thus lurid and afflicted,
with assiduous pulse in my dolorous breast, with bloodless pallor trembling — miserating, grieving, with amorous pity,
marvelled at it; raising me, pretty, into her sweet embraces; and, with a genuine sweetness, and with a most-tenuous
saffron veil, my dirty face, dewy in most-rich sweat, with amorous obsequy lightly rubbed, dried me — both most-
officious and, with blandishment, sponging me. Whence, the cause and accident of so much bitter and troublous anguish
eagerly inquiring, and most-benignly blandishing me —

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** Poliphilo's **panic-flight back to Polia**: fearing Neptune might ravish her (as he did
Medusa), more terrified than when the dragon nearly ate him (ch. VII), he crashes back through the thorny ruins (his
toga shredded, burr-covered) and collapses **half-dead at Polia's feet**; she, moved, gently raises him, wipes his
sweat-soaked face with a saffron veil, and asks what has happened. All secure; the reunion.

- **The lover's terror as the "quality of true lovers."** Colonna frames Poliphilo's panic as proof of true love —
  the fear of losing the beloved is worse than mortal danger to oneself ("more terrified than… among the jaws of the
  dragon"). The Polyandrion, meant to *cool* his ardour (p. 242), has instead confirmed its depth: the tombs of the
  love-dead make him dread being one of them. The therapy overshoots into revelation.
- **the toga shredded on the thorns** — a vivid, human detail: the antiquarian who wandered the ruins in delight now
  flees them, robe torn, burr- and thistle-covered. His scholarly composure is stripped as literally as his clothes.
  A stageable beat for the world/novel.
- **Polia's tender care.** She raises him, dries his face with her own veil, and asks the cause — the reciprocal
  tenderness that answers his love (cf. her soothing, pp. 235, 241). The reunion is gentle; his fear was groundless,
  and her presence restores him. Her question sets up his confession and her reassurance (p. 274).
- **"the Babylonian gulf"** — the deadly cave/vapour of Babylon (Pliny's Charonium): Poliphilo's breath as if poisoned.
  The erudite simile even in extremity — his mind is antiquarian to the last.
