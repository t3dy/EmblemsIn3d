"Poliphilo, I would have you know that true and virtuous love has no respect for outward things; and
therefore your dress neither diminishes nor lessens your spirit, perhaps magnanimous and gentle, and
deservedly worthy to behold these marvellous and holy places and realms, and the wondrous triumphs. For
which cause do not let any fear at all take up your mind, but look carefully at what realms those
crowned by holy Venus possess — those who have manfully striven, and persevering, serving at her
amorous altars and sacred fires, have lawfully gained her most ready favour."

And now, ending her shrewd and sweet talk, we both moved our steps, neither hurried nor yet slow, but
at a modest pace; and, discussing it within myself, I considered, saying:

O most valiant Perseus, for this woman you would have fought more strenuously with the horrible
monster, to win her sweetest love, than for your Andromeda. And then:

O Jason, if the lawful marriage of this woman had been set before you — with far greater peril (I swear
by Jove) than was that of winning the golden fleece — I reasonably judge that, setting that aside, you
would have contended fiercely for this one, judging her above all the jewels and precious treasures of
the spacious world, and above even those of the richest Queen Eleuterylida, most precious, and of a
greater worth and incomparable price.

Every hour and continually she appeared more shapely, more comely in aspect, with a cleanest adornment.
Nor does plentiful gold show itself so welcome to Hippodamia, and to the ravening and anxious
misers. Nor does the entrance of a quiet and safe harbour so offer itself to sailors, nor a cable or a
mooring-post for their fastening when they are shaken in the winter storms. Nor was the falling rain so
longed-for and timely at the pyre of Croesus, as the delicious Nymph offered herself, most acceptable
to love. More joyful and dearer to me than the bloody battles to raging Mars; than to Dionysus the
mysteries of great Crete.

And than to unshorn Apollo the chattering lyre. More welcome still than the fruitful clods, and the
thick ears of corn, and the sacred first-fruits and the Thesmophoria to Demeter.
