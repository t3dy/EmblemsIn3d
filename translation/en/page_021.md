Then, a little beyond its middle, I came upon a sandy place, or gravelled stretch, though in some
parts scattered with some tufts of herbage. There a most joyful Palm-grove presented itself to my
eyes, with leaves of knife-like point, of such usefulness to the Egyptians, fruitful and abounding in
their most sweet fruit. Among which clustered palms, some were small, and many middling, and the
others were straight and lofty — the chosen Sign of victory, because of the way they resist a
pressing weight.

Here again in this place I found no inhabitant, nor any other animal whatever. But wandering
solitary among those palms — not thickly set, but standing apart, and most worth the seeing — I
thought that the palms of Rachelais, of Phaselis and of Libya were perhaps not comparable to these.
Behold, then, a famished and flesh-eating wolf appeared to me on the right hand, with its mouth full.
