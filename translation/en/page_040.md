At so great a novelty, worthy of the telling, and reading over the riddles again and again, I remained
wholly ignorant, both of the interpretation and of the sophistical meaning, which was very ambiguous.
Nor did I dare therefore to attempt anything. But being struck as it were with fear in this dark and
unlighted place — for all that there was the lamp's light there — nevertheless the anxious desire to
contemplate the triumphal gate, urging me on, was a more lawful cause for my not lingering there than
any other.

Whereupon, without doing anything else, with the thought and purpose of returning there once more by
every means after the contemplation of that wonderful gate, and of considering more tranquilly such a
magnificence of invention from human wits, I came most swiftly to the opening. And going down I came
out of the disembowelled monster.
