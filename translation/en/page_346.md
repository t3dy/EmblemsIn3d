## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The procession arrives, with pyrrhic dance and hymns to Venus ("the divine
Genetrix") and Cupid, at the **proscenium-gate of the amphitheatre** — the great theatre of Venus at the
island's centre. The building is introduced with the full superlative-and-inexpressibility apparatus, and
its gate begins to be described: built of *litharmenon*, a golden-flecked oriental marble. All secure.

- **The theatre of Venus, at last.** The whole triumph has been the approach to this: the central
  amphitheatre of Cythera, the "circo" and "theatro" of the measured plans (pp. 311, 325), where the
  Fountain of Venus stands and where ch. XXIII will tear the curtain and reveal the goddess. Chapter XXII's
  procession delivers the lovers to its gate. In the world, this is the climax-station the whole island is
  built to walk toward.
- **"neither in Atella nor any famous place"** — Atella (the Oscan town that gave Rome its farces) stands
  for the theatre's origins; the amphitheatre surpasses them all. The surpassing-topos, now applied to
  architecture, ushering in the building's description.
- **the golden marble of the gate** — *litharmenon* flecked with gold "like scattered filings": the theatre
  is faced in a stone that glitters. Its architecture is itemised over the following pages (347+), the last
  stretch of chapter XXII before the Fountain of Venus.
- **the misting pipes again** (cf. p. 324) — the processionaries are perfumed by fine golden spray-pipes at
  the gate: the atmospheric misting that recurs across the island.
