Beyond this, then, there ended a marvellous making of three paces of most noble knot-work of jaspers,
prasines, chalcedonies, agates and of other notable manners and kinds of fine stones. Along the walls
of the aforesaid area I considered certain most worthy seats of sandalwood, red and citron, most
diligently made, and covered over with the greenest velvet. Stuffed, or cushioned, with a moderate
downy convexity, or of a soft material that yielded most comfortably to the sitter; and the said silken
pile fixed along the rims of the benches with little golden nails with studded heads, upon a silver and
stretched braid, or flat little cord.

The splendid facing of the enclosing walls I beheld, all clothed with plates of purest and shining
gold, with chasings answering to it, and most fitting to that precious material. In the levelled and
most burnished flat, then, of the said plates — divided by certain little pilasters, or squares, of a
most fitting measure and correspondence — there appeared in the middle of each, clearly, round jewels
fitted in, moderately swelling in the form of the torus of a base, of a thickness suited to the
capacity of the space, covered over with fringed leaves pressed one against another. Girt about with
thongs, with the ends flowing, they perfectly adorned the jewel round about. Where there came in, most
aptly among the foliage in a fitting distribution, a fruitage of shining and various jewels, notably
shaped to various expressed lineaments.

In the remaining space, then, walled round with these swelling jewels, painted perfectly with beauty in
encaustic work, I beheld with great pleasure the seven planets with their inborn properties. The
remaining level, outside the roundness of the jewels, was wrought fiercely in endless and elegant
expressions of silversmith's work. With an adorned scattering of manifold and inestimable gems I saw it
wonderfully with its ornament.

At the left wall, or moulded partition, it was likewise. And with the spaces and jewels of the same
form of ornament and of the same number as the aforesaid — that is, in seven roundings — I contemplated
seven triumphs of those subject to the ruling planets, most skilfully made in the said painting. And on
the right side I saw likewise seven harmonies of them, and the passage of the soul, with its
qualitative receiving of the degrees of the circles, with an incredible history of the celestial
operations that come with it.
