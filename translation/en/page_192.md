# Page 192 — Chapter XVII

And the other carried a trophy of certain buds and green shoots joined together, and country
instruments; leaping with an ancient rite and applause, solemnly circling, and going round a sacred
four-cornered altar in the middle of the tufted and flower-bearing meadow, watered by the clearest
springs, religiously set up. Which, with all the exquisite lineaments of a most excellent making, was
distinguishedly carved in white and shining marble.

On each front of which there stood out an incredible relief of an elegant image, almost wholly free of
the ground. The first was a most beautiful goddess with flying tresses girt with roses and other
flowers, with a most thin overgarment rivalling the comely limbs beneath; with her right hand she
devoutly scattered flowers and roses upon a little sacrifice, a little flame leaping out of an antique
three-footed pot; and in the other she held a little branch of fragrant and berried myrtle. Beside her
there stood a winged and most beautiful little boy, laughing, with his wounding tokens, and two doves
likewise. Beneath the feet of which figure was inscribed: *sacred to the flowering spring*.
