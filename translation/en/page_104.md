Next there followed a comely and nimble girl with a wide golden vessel heaped with flowers of violets —
amethyst-coloured, pale yellow and white, such as are fragrant in the new spring — which she most
readily scattered and strewed over all the tables (except the Queen's, which was always bare).

The sacred majesty of the Queen had put off the pompous royal mantle, such as Lollia Paulina of Rome
never saw, and remained in a most sumptuous figured garment of purple crimson pile, woven all over with
manifold birds and other little animals, fronds and flowers, of an ordered knot-work, and the work
somewhat swelling with the most illustrious pearls, with a very thin overgarment above. Tinged with a
little of a saffron silken colour, showing through, by its clear thinness, the covered work and the
crimson beneath. Which habit was in the highest degree graceful and comely and imperial.

Then there came two elegant girls. Who carried an artful fountain that poured continually, artfully
taking up again the water that it scattered; and it was of shining gold, founded in a basin of most
beautiful workmanship, and before all else they set it before the Queen. And no sooner was it presented
upon the golden table than together they made a graceful bow, bending likewise their adorned heads. And
kneeling within a thumb's breadth of reaching the burnished pavement. Like reverences, in an ordered
way and at one time, the attendant servers who waited made — following one another, and observing them
both before and after the offering of anything whatever, and at its removal.

Three other excellent young women followed after those. One with a golden ewer. And the other with a
basin, or hand-bowl, most shining. The third with a delicate towel of the whitest silk.

The divine Queen having washed her hands, she that bore the golden bowl received the washing, so that
it should not go back and be taken up again into the fountain. And she that carried the ewer poured in
as much scented water as was poured out, so that the fountain of wonderful devising should not empty
itself. The third lightly dried her hands.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The violets strewn, the Queen's changed dress, and the self-replenishing
hand-washing fountain. Accurate throughout, and three words are handled well: *Lolia Paulina* is the
Lollia Paulina of Pliny 9 whose jewels were proverbial; *investitura belluata* is correctly a
garment figured with beasts, and the English says so in the following clause rather than in the
word; *malluvio* is the hand-bowl. Medium for the page's form rather than its content — it is a
single unbroken inventory of ceremony, divided into English paragraphs at the translator's
discretion, and the choreography of who pours, who catches and who dries is easier to follow in
English than the Italian actually allows.
