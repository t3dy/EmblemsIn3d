POLIPHILO, FEARING THE PERIL OF THE DARK WOOD, MADE PRAYER TO DIESPITER; HE CAME FORTH ANXIOUS AND
THIRSTING; AND, WISHING TO RESTORE HIMSELF WITH WATER, HE HEARD A SWEET SINGING, WHICH, FOLLOWING AND
REFUSING THE WATERS, HE CAME INTO GREATER ANXIETY STILL.

MY UNDERSTANDING HAVING NOW BEGUN to be darkened, so that it could not discern, and my senses to be
clouded as to which choice I ought to take — whether to meet hateful death, or to hope for safety
wavering in that shadowy and dark grove — running this way and that, I bent all my strength and
striving to get out. Into which, the further I penetrated in my wandering, the darker it grew. And
being already made feeble by great terror, I only waited, fearing it from some quarter or other, for
some most savage beast to fall upon me and begin to devour me; or else, stumbling blindly and without
forethought, to fall into some abysmal pit and trench, or to be thrown headlong into some vast gape
of the earth; and by now to end this wearisome life like Amphiaraus, or like Curtius swallowed by the
mephitic gulf of the earth, and to fall from a greater height than the witless Pyreneus did.
