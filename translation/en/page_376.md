After the divine Lady, as is her custom, has washed herself in the fountain and
come out of it, again in commemoration and amorous memorial of the dear and
beloved Adonis, beaten by Mars, with moist eyes she throws herself embracing over
the sepulchre, weeping, her rosy cheeks bedewed with flowing tears; and all we,
with a lamentable piping, piteously weep, because on such a day the divine calf —
of the little foot we kissed — was pricked by the thorns of these roses. And
therefore on that same day the cover of the holy repository is solemnly unsealed
by her and revealed, and with venerable ceremony all we, glad and exulting and
singing — the son having received it, carries the oyster-shell with the divine
gore, and she, the priestess and newest bearer of the bundle of roses unwilted of
their greenness, with serene grace, most festive.

No sooner is the precious liquor drawn out than at once all the whitest roses, as
at present they appear, are re-dyed in purple colour. And in such order, three
times pompously lustrating this fountain, and she alone weeping, her eyes wet,
wipes them with the handful of roses; at the third circling, the sacred things
laid back in their place, all that most celebrated day is solemnly dispensed to
pleasures alone, to dances, sounds and songs, most dedicated. And on such a day
her grace is easily obtained.

Opposite the sepulchre, at the fountain, there were five steps of the bordering
stone, sloping down to the flat bottom — not rough, not gravelly, but of precious
and vermiculate inlaid paving. The channel of the emissary rivulet flowed on,
underground, beyond the lattices, carrying the succeeding water.

The maiden nymphs having domestically and eloquently narrated so memorable and so
curious a mystery, began again to sound, and rhythmically, most sweetly and with
the greatest pleasure, to sing the recounted little histories and the past
happenings. And dancing in a circle about the fountain for a long while, then all
kneeling and seated and interlaced, in such most acceptable pleasantness and most
delightful greenery — I then, particularly stripped and freed of every holding-
back respect, in that unwonted fragrance of my dainty Polia, which still breathed
from her most elegant and cleanly self, and being all perfumed with a recent
exhalation of that fragrant sweetness of her delicate garments dewing balsam — I
placed myself amorously and freely in her lap, eagerly kissing both her milky
hands and that snowy breast, ivory and lustrous shining; and then mutually — not
ungrateful but voluptuously vying — being in her sight, such impulses of love

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The completion of the Adonia rite is secure; the closing
turn to the lovers dallying in the grass is one long periodic sentence, broken up
in English, its sense clear.

- **The white roses re-dyed purple by the blood** — the climax of the rite: the
  gore drawn from the tomb turns the freshly-bloomed white roses red, re-enacting
  the origin of the red rose (or anemone) from Adonis's blood every year. The
  fountain's whole meaning is this annual dying-and-reddening, love and death in
  the one flower.
- **"her grace is easily obtained" on that day** — the rite has a practical
  religious payoff: petitions to Venus succeed on the Adonia. Colonna gives his
  invented cult a working liturgical logic.
- The song of the nymphs turns, on the next page, into their request that Polia
  tell her own story — the doorway to Book II.
