## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The architecture of the closing peristyle — its
intercolumniation, its gate, its earth-filled hollow entablature sprouting flowers,
and the box-and-juniper topiary along it — is secure. The catalogue of coloured
column-stones begins here and runs onto p. 309.

- **The peristyle that divides the *prati* from the inner ring.** This is the
  bellied *eustyle* colonnade begun on p. 307, now detailed: a ring of columns
  whose entablature is hollowed like a planter and filled with earth, so that
  flowers grow along the top of the colonnade itself, with box balls and stepped
  junipers alternating at each column. Architecture that is also a garden — the
  book's constant fusion.
- **the gates where the roads cross** — the colonnade "loosens" (opens) where the
  radial ways pass through it, with a noble arched gate at each: the same
  road-crossing openings the world renders on the terraces (GARDENS.md §5, the
  gated crossroads).
- **alabaster without pumice-polishing** — the wall-stone is naturally lustrous,
  needing no abrasive finish (Theban earth and pumice were the ancient polishing
  agents, from Pliny). The columns' varied stones are catalogued next.
