POLIPHILO, HAVING ENTERED SOMEWHAT INTO THE DESCRIBED GATE, WITH GREAT PLEASURE STILL BEHELD THE
WONDERFUL ORNAMENT OF ITS ENTRANCE. AND WISHING AFTERWARDS TO TURN BACK, HE SAW THE MONSTROUS DRAGON;
AND HE, TERRIFIED BEYOND BELIEF, TOOK FLIGHT THROUGH UNDERGROUND PLACES. AT LAST, FINDING A MOST
LONGED-FOR WAY OUT, HE CAME INTO A PLEASANT PLACE.

A GREAT AND PRAISEWORTHY THING WITHOUT fail it would be to be able easily to narrate, and to set out
point by point, the incredible making and unimaginable composition of that vastest structure, and the
greatness of so much building, and of that most notable gate, conveniently situated in a high and
conspicuous place.

Whereupon the delight of contemplating exceeded my great admiration; for I judged that to Jove among
the gods no making whatever is hard, suspecting almost that by no craftsman and by no human knowledge
could so great a vastness be composed, and such most ample conceits expressed, and so much novelty
devised, and adorned with such elegance, and disposed with such singular symmetry, and perfectly
defined without supplement and correction — the illustrious and unimaginable display of that aforesaid
structure.

For which reason I should not doubt in the least that, if the natural historian had beheld or
understood this, he would have scorned Egypt, and the industry and genius of its craftsmen at which he
wondered — those who, distributed in distinct and various workshops, chose a part to be carved and
were assigned its length; and then with so much Symmetry all agreed with their own piece, and to the
composition of a huge colossus, as exactly as if it had been polished by one craftsman alone. And the
sagacious shrewdness of Satyrus the architect and of the other famous ones. And chiefly, for
Simandius, the wonderful work of the excellent Memnon, of three great statues of highest Jove carved
in one solid rock, of which one, seated, its feet or sole exceeded seven cubits. He would with reason
have made little of them.
