## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The close of the third-ring topiary and its herb-and-fruit
corners is secure; the last sentence of the fruit-list is damaged in the source
(the `{{Pt||}}` gaps again, marked `[?…]`), so the tail is provisional.

- **The third ring's corners** repeat the four-step planter, now three-cornered
  and faced in **chrysolectrum** (a yellow-gold amber), planted with the fragrant
  resins and herbs: *saliunca* (Celtic valerian), mountain *polion*, labdanum and
  cistus, and *ambrosia*. The three rings' corner-planters thus run jasper→apples,
  amber→pears+herbs, chrysolectrum→plums+resins — an ascending order of both stone
  and scent toward the centre.
- **the third order's fruit is plums** — Damascene plums, pistachios, tamarinds,
  myrobalans: matching Segre's third order (apples, pears, then plums with
  pistachios, GARDENS.md §5). The world's fruit-tree rings come from exactly this
  sequence.
- **chrysolectrum / Citros / the Meleagrides** — all amber-lore from Pliny: *Citros*
  (an amber-island of "Germany"), and the Meleagrides (Meleager's sisters, turned to
  guinea-fowl, whose tears were also said to become amber). Colonna's stones always
  carry their myth of origin.
- **the box "far more than a feigned picture"** — Colonna's recurring claim that the
  living topiary surpasses painting: the paragone of art and nature stated once more.
