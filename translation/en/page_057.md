I saw innumerable Trophies, spoils of war, and infinite ornaments; and heads of oxen and of horses
disposed at due interval, and from their horns ropes of fronds with fruits, stalks and pods, and other
fruits hanging in their bulk, with little boys riding upon them in play.

For all which things it was rightly judged how copious the thought of that many-knowing Architect
showed itself, in care, in study and in industry. And with how much vigilance his fertile understanding
declared itself. And with how much pleasure he had laboriously set forth the effect of his purpose.
And how great was the Eurythmy, publishing the subtlety of the stone-cutter's art; and how much art
the carving showed in the stones — certainly with such facility that it seemed the material afforded
had been not marble, however hard, but soft clay and potter's earth. And with how great a closing the
stones were joined and composed, to the norm, the rule and the level.

This was the true art, which discovers our confident ignorance, and detestable presumption, and public
and most damaging error. This is that clear light which sweetly invites us to its contemplation, to
illuminate our darkened eyes. For no one, save he who struggling flees from it, remains blind with
open eyes. This is that which accuses wicked avarice, the ravening consumer of every virtue, the worm
continually gnawing the heart of him who is its captive; accursed obstacle and barrier to well-disposed
wits, mortal enemy of good architecture; execrable idol of the present age, so unworthily and
damagingly venerated. Deadly poison, that makes wretched whoever is hurt by you — how many magnificent
works are ruined and in part forbidden?

For which cause, being seized and taken with delight and unimaginable solace, and by that holy and
venerable antiquity, with such grace and admiration, I found myself with undetermined, unstable and
unfed gazings. Looking gladly here and there, and crowded with admiration, and encircled in my mind, I
examined, running over what the carved histories signified, gazing fixedly at it with a willing
pleasure. Intent with parted lips for a long stretch, none the less my greedy eyes and insatiable
appetite could not be satisfied with looking and looking again at those excellent and most ancient
workings.
