HYPNEROTOMACHIA POLIPHILI

DESCRIPTION OF THE DAWN

At that hour when Phoebus, issuing forth, was whitening the brow of Matuta Leucothea, he did not yet
show, risen clear of Ocean's waves, his rolling wheels held aloft. But diligent with his winged
horses — Pyrous first, and Eous somewhat appearing — he did not delay in painting the pale-lit
four-horse chariot of the daughter of vermilion roses, following after her most swiftly. And already,
glittering above the cerulean and restless wavelets, his beams came curling. At which arrival, in
that instant, the un-horned Cynthia gave herself over to her setting, urging on the two horses of her
vehicle together with the Mule — the one white, the other dusky — drawing her to the last Horizon
that divides the Hemispheres; and having come there, the day being kindled by the star that runs
before it, she yielded and fled.

In that season when the Riphaean mountains were at peace, and the chill and frost-bearing Eurus no
longer shook the tender branches with such rigour in its sidelong blowing, nor troubled the swaying
rushes and the pointed reeds and the frail Cyperus, nor harried the pliant withies, nor stirred the
slow willows, nor bowed down the brittle fir beneath the horns of the wanton Bull — as much, that is,
as it is wont to blow in the season of winter. And likewise the boastful Orion, weeping, left off
pursuing the adorned shoulder of the Bull and the seven sisters.
