In the triangular space between the rhomb and the square interval, upon the diagonal
lines, as a filling, was a free circle. Within the rhomb was a circle, unknotted to the
capacity of the rhombic figure. In the middle of which circle was an eight-petalled
rose. In the centre of which was set an empty altar, rounded, of stone of tawny Numidian

carving, hung little bundles of fronds and fruits, swelling in the middle, with wandering
thongs bound in knots about the heads, and girding the bundles, with choice lineament —
at the socle and the abacus, with most beautiful cyma-moulding, and other adorning
ornaments. Out of which altar grew a savin, in compact cypress-form, the opening of the
altar stuffed with manifold chervil.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The full working-out of the box knot-parterre — bands passing
over and under, squares ringed corner-to-corner, a rhomb inside a square, an
eight-petalled rose at the centre — and, at the very centre of the figure, a small round
sacrificial altar (tawny Numidian marble, three ox-skulls, festoons) with a savin
growing from it. All secure; the bucrania-and-festoon altar is a precise antique type.

- **The knot-parterre, fully specified.** A square whose bands weave over-and-under, a
  concentric ring, a second square set diagonally, a rhomb, and a central circle holding
  an *eight-petalled rose* — Colonna gives the complete construction of one knot, band by
  band. This is buildable as an exact parterre pattern; it is the ancestor of every
  Renaissance knot-garden engraving.
- **the bucrania altar at the knot's centre** — a small round altar of yellow Numidian
  marble carved with three ox-skulls (*bucrania*) joined by fruit-and-frond festoons on
  knotted thongs, moulded with cyma at socle and abacus, and a savin growing out of it.
  The ox-skull-and-garland is the standard antique sacrificial-frieze motif (as on the
  Ara Pacis); Colonna plants one at the heart of the garden-knot.
- **savin again** — the same *Juniperus sabina* of p. 317 ("used by the divine mother to
  hide the calumny"), here growing from the altar: the plant of Venus at the sacred
  centre of the parterre.
- The knot-garden description continues into p. 320.
