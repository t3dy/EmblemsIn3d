At last, having taken counsel with myself to make the venture and to risk whatever might follow — it
persuading me all the while that by no conjecture could there be found in these any inhumanity or
savagery, and above all that the innocent carries his protection with him — I stirred up my lukewarm
spirit, still reined in by a numbing shame, knowing myself to have come unworthily into this perhaps
holy place, and solacing assembly of most delicate and divine Nymphs.

And still it did not persuade me with a sincere and tranquil mind that I had entered and come there,
rashly perhaps into forbidden places and a prohibited country, and with wicked daring. Turning over
therefore such thoughts within myself — behold, one of these, more confident and bold, said: "Who are
you, then?"

Then, all confounded between my familiar fear and sudden shame, I did not know what to say nor what to
answer; and, my voice together with my spirit being forbidden me, I remained half alive, and like a
statue. But those virtuous girls, perceiving that in me was a real and human shape, but terrified and
fearful, all drew near, saying: "O young man, whoever you are here, our looks and presences should
never make you afraid here; therefore do not doubt in the least. For here no savagery whatever is
used, nor will you find displeasure in any way. Who are you, then? Speak, do not fear."

At this request, having somewhat taken back my voice, stirred by those alluring and nymph-like looks
and recalled by their sweet speaking, answering I said to them: "Divine Nymphs, I am the most
graceless and unhappy lover that ever could be found in the world. I love; and she whom I so ardently
love and heartily desire, I know not where she or I may be. And by the greatest and most mortal peril
that I could ever express, I have been led and come here."

And the piteous tears being already called to my eyes, and bowing to the earth and casting myself at
their maidenly feet, sighing I cried out, beseeching pity by the most high God. At once, all shaken in
their soft hearts with compassion and piteous sweetness, and moved almost likewise to little tears,
dutifully and striving with one another to draw me by the arms from the ground, they raised me up; and
with sweetest and most coaxing speech they said to me pleasantly:

"We think, poor little one — indeed it is so — that by the way you have entered here, wretch, few can
escape. But above all things praise highly the divine power and the kindness of your star, for you have
now escaped an extreme peril. But at present there is nothing more to fear that is disturbing, nor any
troublesome assault; for by this way you might perhaps easily find yourself blessed. Settle yourself
therefore, and be tranquil again, and comfort your mind. For here, as you plainly see, is a place of
