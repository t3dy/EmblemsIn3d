# Page 271 — Chapter XIX

> **· D · DITI ET PROSER(PINAE) · S ·**
> **· V · F ·**
> **TREBIAE Q(UINTAE),**
> **L(UCII) S(EX?) TREBII FILIAE.**
>
> "Sacred to Dis and Proserpina. (This) monument of love and of piety Aulus Fibustius, her husband, (raised) — with
> whom, with the utmost longing, delightfully she lived one month, three days. She, my wife, most-loving to me, most-
> unhappy, left me tears and eternal mourning. Perturbed by an extreme jealousy — when she suspected that I had lain
> with another woman — her sweetest love converted into fury, with the iron (sword), through the middle of the breast
> driven, she killed herself. Alas, wife — why this? My dear consort, thou oughtest to have removed from thy lover not
> only the deed, but even the suspicion. Farewell freely. But I, uncertain, unhappy and trembling, my life loosed
> (undone), rest.
>
> — THE INEVITABLE STATE OF NATURE THE STEPMOTHER.
> — THE BENIGN EDICT OF NATURE THE MOTHER."

---

## Notes

**Confidence: high** (the epitaph is secure; a few abbreviations conjecturally expanded). A **jealousy-suicide**:
**Trebia Quinta**, wife of Aulus Fibustius for barely a month, killed herself with a sword through the breast on the
mere *suspicion* that her husband had lain with another — "her sweetest love converted into fury." The husband speaks,
grieving and reproaching her; the tomb closes with the paired mottoes on **Nature the stepmother** (death) and
**Nature the mother** (the benign law). All secure.

- **Trebia — love turned to fatal jealousy.** A new variety of love-death: not refusal, excess, fidelity, or accident,
  but *jealousy on a false suspicion*. The husband's reproach — "thou oughtest to have removed from thy lover not only
  the deed but even the suspicion" — is a small marital tragedy, and a warning against jealousy's rashness. His closing
  "I, uncertain, unhappy and trembling… rest" is bleak and human. Voiceable material.
- **the two Natures.** The tomb's twin mottoes state the book's cosmology: **Nature the stepmother** (*noverca*)
  imposes death's "inevitable state"; **Nature the mother** (*mater*) gives the "benign edict" (life, generation). It
  is the Polyandrion's philosophical frame — the same double Nature that Venus Physizoa ("life-giving Nature")
  embodies, here shown with its death-face. A resonant inscribed pair for the world's polyandrion (life and death as
  Nature's two faces).
- **the marriage of one month, three days** — the exact, pitifully short span (like Vibius's "19 years, 2 months, 9
  days," p. 259): Colonna's authentic epigraphic precision, the brevity of the happiness measured against the
  "eternal mourning."
- **the epitaphs' cumulative taxonomy** — refusal (Nevia), fidelity destroyed (Leontia, Sertullius), excess (Vibius),
  guilt (Lopidia), and now jealousy (Trebia): the Polyandrion has catalogued nearly every way love kills. The rising
  pathos builds toward the Rape-of-Proserpina relief that ends Poliphilo's visit (pp. 280+).
