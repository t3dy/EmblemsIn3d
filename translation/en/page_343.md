Wreathed with diadems of jewels, flowering with a spring-crown, among the varied flowers gold foil
becomingly intercalated — chiefly of amethystine violets, and of sky-blue flowers, and of purpling
amaranth, and everlasting, and of cyclamen, and with little wreaths of melilot, with yellow and
white violets, measuredly composed, and mixedly inserted, and of other coronary florets, and with
whitening pearls and other gems, accessorily their ornate locks girdled round and woven in. And
some, most-elegant, of beautiful voice, dispensed so much soothing harmony, communicating it to the
hearers, as perhaps Apollo — nay, without hesitating — playing the lyre to the Heliconian Muses does
not render, nor offer. Nor such and so great a sweetness of sound did the Tyrrhenian sailors hear;
nor did Arion, with so much sound, upon the dolphin his conveyer, make the crossing to Taenarum. Which
women musicians were not in one place, but disposed in ordered procession, and processional division
at their requisite and constituted place, to great extolling and triumphal glory and victorine
praise, in redundant troops.

Through which divine things, I firmly persuade myself, every fecund and eloquent tongue would prove
vain, striving to express a particle of the sweetest sounds, of the sweetest canticles, of the solemn
and playful dances and feasts, of the divine Nymphs and notable girls, of their singular and
incomparable beauties. Of the excellent and illustrious and most-elegant adornment — enough to
interrupt and amputate gracious life, and to dissipate and distract every hard and obstinate heart —
rejoicing with various and triumphal insignia, and with alacrity —

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The musical procession in full: every antique instrument named (pierced tibia,
ivory-beaten drum, twin tibiae "let Marsyas yield," lyre, cithara, sistrum, steel triangle, horn,
trumpet); the flower-and-jewel crowned singer-nymphs surpassing Apollo, the Muses and Arion; and
Poliphilo's closing *inexpressibility topos* — no eloquent tongue could render a particle of it. All
secure; a catalogue of ancient music and the chapter's rhetorical culmination.

- **The orchestra of the triumph.** Colonna assembles the entire classical instrumentarium — reeds
  (tibia, twin auloi), strings (lyre, cithara), percussion (drum, sistrum, triangle, cymbal), brass
  (horn, trumpet) — played by flower-crowned nymphs "in ordered procession." The world's triumph can
  draw its sound-world directly from this list.
- **surpassing Apollo and Arion** — the singers out-sing Apollo before the Muses, and Arion (saved by
  the music-loving dolphin): the surpassing-topos applied to music, as it was to gardens (Alcinous, p.
  317) and textiles (p. 331). Cythera exceeds every classical superlative in turn.
- **the inexpressibility topos** — Poliphilo ends by declaring the scene beyond any tongue: the standard
  close to a great ekphrasis, here marking the summit of the triumph before it reaches the amphitheatre
  gate. The beauty is again named as lethal — "enough to amputate gracious life."
- **the flower-crowns, keyed** — amethyst violets, amaranth, everlasting, cyclamen, melilot, yellow and
  white violets, with gold foil and pearls: a specific garland-recipe for the musician-nymphs.
