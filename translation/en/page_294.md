These twenty divisions were, by most noble fences, diversely latticed with fitting
and convenient marble openwork, two inches thick, between the measured placing of
most polished little pilasters, of whitening marble, and the rest most lustrously
reddening; clothed with a varied delight of creeping plants, one shut off and
separated from another. In the middle of the fence there opened, level, in each,
a gate — seven feet in the opening, nine high up to the arching of its topmost
curve. Which latticings were of such rhombs, and little squares, and such
degenerate from the quadrangle, and by other most beautiful devices. Along these
serpentined the periclymenon; others the jasmine; some of bindweed; some of hops;
and some of black bryony, or black vine; others of convolvulus, with the
lily-like half-azure bells; some all white; some of momordica — so each was varied.
Some of Jove's flammula; of smilax, which for love of beautiful Crocus made itself
Autophoros, adorned with a white fragrant lily-flower, with a thorny and
ivy-like leaf; of bittersweet; of the little vine, with the three-cornered
bladder-campion, its seed spotted with whiteness; and of many others which,
serpentining on high, are unknown to me by name.

In the first partition the grove was a daphnon, or laurel-grove, of manifold
laurels: here I saw the Delphic, the Cyprian, the Mustax with its greatest and
whitening leaf, and the wild, the Cinos, and the Royal or Bacchalian, the Taxa,
the Spadonic, and Chamaedaphne — such as never on Mount Parnassus, most admired
and most pleasing to Apollo, most noble gift sent to the Romans. Nor did such
laurel-bearing earth kiss Brutus, most pleasing to Tiberius; nor did Drusilla see
such, borne by the white hen; nor were such born of the augur's command, planted
in the villa of the Caesars, triumphal ornament — chiefly the Sterile. I saw also
the Daphnoides, or Pelasgian, or Euthalos, fragrant with frankincense; nor did
Peneus's daughter make such a perpetual green of so much beauty, with whose leaves
Apollo was wont to adorn both his lyre and his quiver. Let the airy Sicilian
mountains yield here, both as to their sweetest springs and as to their
loveliness — however the most beautiful son of Mercury delighted himself there with
Diana — which are not prescribed by the wrath of most high Jove, offering
themselves so pleasing to cover the bald head of Caesar, mixed with many Comari in
the most pleasant soil.

## Notes

**Confidence: medium.** The geometrical opening and the two plant-catalogues
(climbers on the lattices; laurel varieties in the first compartment) are secure in
substance; the botanical nomenclature is Pliny's and Theophrastus's, much of it in
Colonna's Latinised-Greek forms, and a botanist could refine several identifications.

- **The first of the twenty compartments is a laurel-grove** — the *daphnon* —
  and this begins the species-by-species catalogue of Cythera's *bosco* that runs
  through the next pages. The world's island plants twelve wedges "of alternating
  kinds"; Colonna's are twenty, each a single named genus, exactly as Segre's plan
  reconstructs.
- **the twenty gates, seven feet wide and nine high** — each compartment's marble
  lattice has an arched gate. These are the openings the world renders where the
  radial roads meet the rings.
- **the climbers** — periclymenon (honeysuckle), jasmine, bindweed, hops, black
  bryony, convolvulus, smilax, bittersweet, bladder-campion: real garden creepers,
  each trained on a compartment's lattice.
- **the laurel of Peneus's daughter** — Daphne, turned to the laurel Apollo then
  wore. The whole grove is Apollo's tree, and Colonna piles the Roman laurel-lore:
  the white hen that dropped a laurel-sprig into Livia's (Drusilla's) lap by
  augury, from which the triumphal laurels of the Caesars' villa were grown, one
  of them "the Sterile"; the laurel that crowned Caesar's baldness. All from Pliny
  and Suetonius. Left unglossed in the text.
