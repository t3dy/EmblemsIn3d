# DIMENSIONS — every measured thing in the book, in metres

*A research brief. **Nothing here is built yet.** This is the measurement half of the
2026-09-08 research pass on the world's scale, made because Ted said the dream garden is too
small and its elements too close together. Its companions are [`DIRECTIONS.md`](DIRECTIONS.md)
(where things stand in relation to each other) and [`WOODS.md`](WOODS.md) (the wood in
particular). The build brief that follows from all three is the last section of
`DIRECTIONS.md`.*

**The finding in one line:** Colonna measured his dream, most of it in Roman units, and the
numbers are enormous — the pyramid-portal alone is **1 140 m wide and about 850 m tall**,
Cythera is **1.4 km across** — while the world renders the pyramid **17.5 m wide** and puts
the whole mainland inside **88 × 100 m**. We are building at roughly **one sixtieth** of the
stated scale at the monuments and about one twelfth in the gardens, and the compression is
not uniform, which is why the elements feel crowded.

## 1. The units, and the one trap

| unit | Latin | Roman feet | metres |
|---|---|---|---|
| foot | *pes* | 1 | **0.296** |
| palm | *palmus* | ¼ | 0.074 |
| cubit | *cubitus* | 1½ | 0.444 |
| **pace** | *passus* | **5** | **1.48** |
| **stadium** | *stadio* | **625** | **185.0** |
| **mile** | *mille passus* / *miliario* | 5 000 | **1 480** |

**The trap.** Dallington glosses his own word *furlong* in a printed marginal note on p. 27:
*"A furlong is 16. pole euery pole being 16 foote."* That is 256 English feet, about 78 m —
neither an English furlong (660 ft) nor Colonna's *stadio*. Dallington is domesticating
*stadio* for an English reader and getting it wrong by a factor of about 2.4. **Every
"furlong" in Dallington is a Roman stadium of 185 m.** Bury converts it that way throughout,
and so does this file.

> Where Dallington and the 1499 Italian disagree on a figure, the Italian wins and the
> disagreement is noted. There is one such case here (§2, the valley span).

**On the pyramid's height.** Colonna states the *construction*, not the number: lift the lines
from the four corners "so much as euerye corner is distant in length from another, meeting in
the top, so as the Perpendicular line may fall iust vpon the center of the Dyagon" (Dall.
p. 27). That makes the lateral edge equal to the base side, so every face is an equilateral
triangle and the height is *s*/√2 = **785 m**. Bury instead deduces **3 275 ft (969 m)** from
the 1 410 steps, and flags it himself as a deduction and as one of the book's two irrational
figures. **Build to 785 m** — it is the figure Colonna's own instruction produces — and treat
the 1 400 usable courses as a riser of 0.56 m, which is exactly the monstrous step the text
describes climbing "laboursomly foote by foote".

**On the statue.** The book gives no height, only an effect: the gyrating nymph is of colossal
size yet "of such a proportion as the common stature might be considered and perfectly seene,
notwithstanding the exceeding heigth thereof in the ayre" (Dall. p. 28). *Ours, not Colonna's:*
for a figure 865 m up to subtend the angle a 1.7 m person subtends at 25 m, she must be about
**59 m tall**. Offered as a target, marked as inference.

## 4. The temple, the island and the theatre — chapters XVII–XXIV

From `translation/en/`, where the page number **is** the 1499 page.

### The Temple of Venus Physizoa (ch. XVII)

Colonna gives a setting-out rather than sizes: a circle inscribed in a square; **the height
equal to the diameter**; the radius divided into five parts with a sixth added toward the
centre; a second circle struck for the ambulatory; **ten radial divisions** carrying piers,
peristyle and vaulting (pp. 197–199, 204). Fixed dimensions: wall **1½ ft** thick, the
pilasters usurping **3 ft** of it beyond the cornice (pp. 211–212). The entablature height
comes off the radius; the roof pitch off the diagonal of a double square (p. 204). *The
temple is the one building in the book given as a rule rather than a size — build it to the
rule and its absolute size is a free choice.*

## 5. What this means for the world as built

The world's numbers, for comparison — read out of `src/scenes/HPWorldScene.js` and
`HP_STATIONS` on 2026-09-08:

| | book | world | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| pyramid-portal, width | 1 140 m | 17.5 m | **1 : 65** |
| pyramid-portal, height | ~865 m | ~21 m | 1 : 41 |
| Polia's garden, diameter | 141 m | ~14 m (radius 7) | 1 : 10 |
| Cythera, diameter | 1 400 m | ~60 m | 1 : 23 |
| theatre Area, diameter | 47.4 m | ~22 m | 1 : 2 |
| the recumbent colossus | 89 m | ~8 m | 1 : 11 |
| whole mainland, end to end | — | 99 m (z = +53 → −46), 88 m wide | — |
| the dark wood | Hercynian (§`WOODS.md`) | a 70 × 22 m strip, 64 trees | — |

**The compression is not the problem. The *unevenness* is.** A theatre at 1 : 2 standing 40 m
from a pyramid at 1 : 65 makes the pyramid read as a garden folly, and the walk between two
stations that the book separates by four stadia takes eleven seconds. Whatever scale is
chosen, **one scale should govern the ground plan**, with a separate, stated allowance for the
two or three monuments whose true size cannot be rendered.

### The recommendation

**A single ground-plan scale of 1 : 8, and monuments at 1 : 8 up to a cap.**

- Poliphilo's own body stays 1 : 1 (`EYE = 1.7`). Only the *world* scales.
- At 1 : 8 the mainland goes from 88 × 100 m to about **700 × 800 m**; the cypress avenue
  becomes a **92 m** walk instead of 8 m; Polia's garden becomes **18 m** across; Cythera
  becomes **175 m**; the theatre Area **6 m** — so the theatre, which is already near-true,
  must be exempted and kept at its real 47 m.
- The pyramid at 1 : 8 would be 142 m wide and 108 m tall. That is buildable, it dwarfs
  everything, and it is what the book is *for* — the whole of chapters III–VI is Poliphilo
  failing to take it in. **Build it at 1 : 8.**
- Anything the walker must handle at human scale — steps, seats, doorways, tables, the
  sepulchre, the litter — **stays 1 : 1** and is placed on the scaled plan. This is the same
  split Renaissance garden design itself uses: the plan is surveyed, the furniture is
  full-size.

*This is a recommendation from the research pass, not a decision. The call is Ted's, and when
it is made it goes in `DECISIONS.md` before any geometry moves.*

## 6. What each mode owes a bigger world

Per `MODES.md`, no addition is finished until it is thought through in all five.

| mode | what the new scale gives it | what it still owes |
|---|---|---|
| **Walk** | distance, and therefore approach — the pyramid growing as it does on Dall. p. 24; a wood you are inside of rather than beside | a way to cross 800 m without tedium: the book's own answer is company and conversation, so Logistica and Thelemia walking with you matters more, not less |
| **Tour** | stops that are genuinely apart, so a teleport reads as travel | every teleport needs its arrival framed — landing 500 m from the last stop with no transition is worse at scale, not better |
| **Dream** | the thirteen stops become a journey with duration; the four moods get room to change between stops | pacing: a mood that turns over 8 m turns over 64 m now |
| **Fly** | the whole point — a dragon at 40 m over a 17 m pyramid has nothing to fly over; at 108 m it does | flight bounds and the LOD budget both scale; `DRAGONFLIGHT.md` "what reads from forty metres" needs re-reading at 300 |
| **Roll** | more floor, more litter spread, longer growth arc | the census drops anything over 6 m — at 1 : 8 almost every building crosses that line, so `scene._monoliths` will swallow the world unless the cut-off scales too |

### Three mechanical traps a rescale will spring

1. **`sun.shadow.camera` is ±58 with `far = 130`** (`src/shaders/HPStyles.js:186`). Past that
   box, nothing casts. An 800 m world with a 58 m shadow frustum loses every shadow away from
   the origin — including, pointedly, the shadows Ted asked the wood to cast. Cascades or a
   camera-following shadow box are the fix, and they are a prerequisite, not a follow-up.
2. **The roll-up census cut-off of 6 m** drops big objects, and `_monoliths` is the ledger of
   what it dropped. Scale the cut-off with the world or Roll mode empties out.
3. **`Walker` speed and the fog/far plane.** A walk speed tuned to a 90 m world is a crawl in
   an 800 m one, and the camera's far plane and any aerial-perspective falloff in
   `RENDERING.md` are tuned to the small world.
